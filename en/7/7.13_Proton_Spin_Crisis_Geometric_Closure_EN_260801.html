Chapter 1　Introduction: 35-Year Unsolved Case

1.1　EMC Impact and Subsequent Confirmations

In 1987, EMC at CERN measured polarized muon-proton deep inelastic scattering and discovered the quark spin contribution:

\[\frac{1}{2}\Delta\Sigma \approx 0.30 \pm 0.06\]

Subsequent experiments SMC (0.33±0.06), COMPASS (0.30±0.04), and HERMES (0.33±0.04) all confirmed it. RHIC polarized proton collisions extracted gluon spin ΔG≈0.20±0.07, but this is only part of the gluon contribution to spin. The remaining ~50% orbital angular momentum has not been directly measured.

For 35 years, no theory has been able to simultaneously predict all four quantities from first principles. Lattice QCD is limited by computational complexity, and phenomenological models disagree among themselves.

1.2　Unique Position of Conjugate Spectral Geometry

Conjugate Spectral Geometry provides a perspective orthogonal to all existing approaches: the proton is an N=3 color singlet of the Cl(3) semisimple splitting (theorem corollary), and its internal degrees of freedom are rigorously characterized by the odd-even decomposition of the J_σ operator at the ℳ sector Birkhoff fixed point (Theorem 7.8.9.07). The spin decomposition does not come from guesswork about the quark-gluon picture, but from the phase space measure distribution on the constrained manifold.

The quantum state density on the constrained manifold at σ* is determined by the phase space measure μ. In the semiclassical limit, the phase space measure of each canonical degree of freedom is the Gaussian integration weight on that degree of freedom:

\[\mu_i = \int d\theta_i \, dp_i \, \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}H_{ij}\theta_i\theta_j\right) \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det H_i}}\]

where H_i is the Hessian matrix block in that degree-of-freedom direction. For a 1-dimensional degree of freedom (odd subspace), μ_odd ∝ 1/√λ_odd; for a d-dimensional block, μ_block ∝ (det H_block)^(-1/2) ∝ (∏ λ_i)^(-1/2).

Key insight: The odd and even subspaces lie on the same constrained manifold, and quantization uses the same quantum unit h. Hence the quantum unit cancels in the odd-even measure ratio — this is the core of bypassing ℏ_phys^eff:

\[\frac{\mu_{\text{odd}}}{\mu_{\text{even}}} = \frac{1/\sqrt{\lambda_2^{\text{eff}}}}{1/\sqrt{\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \cdot \omega_B^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \cdot \omega_B^2}{\lambda_2^{\text{eff}}}}\]

\[\boxed{\frac{S_{\text{odd}}}{S_{\text{total}}} = \frac{1}{1+\mathcal{R}}}\]

Factor 1: g_even/g_odd (degeneracy ratio)

Thus g_even/g_odd = 8/2 = 4.

  • s_even = 1: unit spin of boson modes (gluon spin 1, fundamental quantum of orbital angular momentum)

Thus s_even/s_odd = 1/(½) = 2.

\[\boxed{\delta_{\sigma} = \frac{\sigma^*_C}{\sigma^*_M} \cdot \frac{\Delta\Theta}{\Lambda} = \frac{0.210603}{0.777912} \cdot \frac{5}{3} = 0.2707 \times 1.6667 = 0.4512}\]

\[\frac{S_{\text{odd}}}{S_{\text{total}}} = \frac{1}{1+\mathcal{R} \cdot (1 - \delta_{\sigma} \cdot \eta)}\]

Physically, the coupling between the causal boundary and the material world (ℳ-𝒞 mixing) causes part of the odd subspace spin to “leak” into the even subspace. The leakage amount is proportional to the degree of incomplete material-world dominance of the encoding weights. The η factor reflects the finite frequency ratio correction between slow and fast modes — when λ₁ᵉᶠᶠ/λ₂ᵉᶠᶠ → 0 (infinite spectral gap), η→0 and the leakage shuts off.

Physical meaning: the coupling between the causal boundary and the material world (ℳ-𝒞 mixing) causes part of the constrained manifold measure to flow back from the even subspace to the odd subspace, raising the odd subspace spin fraction from 28.6% to 31.5%.

\[\boxed{\Delta G = 0.714 \times 0.6667 \times \frac{1}{2} = 0.238}\]
