§1 Geometric Origin of Mass

1.1 Mass and Spectral Gap

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, particle mass is not an external parameter——it is a geometric output of the coding framework. The mass \(m_i\) is proportional to the Spectral Gap \(\Delta\lambda_i\) of the Dirac Operator:

\[m_i \propto \Delta\lambda_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}.\]

By the Born Rule (Theorem 1.3.4.01), the Spectral Gap \(\Delta\lambda_i = C \sqrt{\sigma_i}\), where \(C\) is the Algebraic Normalization constant (Theorem 1.4.3.01). Hence:

\[m_i = m_0 \cdot \sqrt{\sigma_i},\]

where \(m_0\) is the mass scale, determined by the coding ground state \(N_1 = 6000\).

1.2 Mass Scale

Theorem 3.2.1.01 (Mass Scale Theorem). The mass scale \(m_0\) is determined by the coding ground state:

\[m_0 = \frac{\hbar c}{r_M} \cdot \frac{N_1^{1/3}}{2\pi} = \frac{\hbar c}{r_M} \cdot \frac{6000^{1/3}}{2\pi}.\]

Proof. The ground state Eigenvalue of the Dirac Operator on \(S^3(r_M)\) is \(\lambda_0 = 1/r_M\). The energy \(E_0 = \hbar c \lambda_0 = \hbar c / r_M\). The coding ground state \(N_1 = 6000\) determines the “lattice point count” on \(S^3\), and the effective resolution \(N_1^{1/3}\) corrects the continuum limit. ∎

Numerically, \(6000^{1/3} \approx 18.17\), so the mass scale is:

\[m_0 \approx \frac{\hbar c}{r_M} \cdot 2.892.\]

If \(r_M\) were taken to be of Planck-length order, then \(m_0\) would be far larger than known particle masses——this indicates that \(r_M\) in physical units is not the Planck length; its calibration will be determined in Volume 7 by comparison with the electron mass.

The mass ratios of different particles are entirely determined by the distribution of Coding Weights \(\sigma_i\)——independent of the mass scale \(m_0\):
