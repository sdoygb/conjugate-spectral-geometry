1. Introduction

In recent years, mainstream physics has begun to explore the concept of “time entanglement” — namely, that quantum systems at two distinct instants can exhibit nonlocal correlations, analogous to quantum entanglement of spatially separated particles. Such studies typically rely on pseudo-density matrices (PDM) and temporal CHSH inequalities for theoretical analysis, but lack a clear geometric picture.

Geometric Theory reduces time to the unidirectional advancement of the causal field \(C^3\) (\(\partial S/\partial\eta < 0\)), and reduces information storage to the coding orbit encoding of the information realm \(I^3\). On this foundation, time entanglement is naturally interpreted as follows: systems at different causal times share the same information field coordinate \(\xi\), thereby generating nonlocal correlations on the coding orbit. This paper provides a complete mathematical formulation of this picture.

4. Geometric Criterion for Time Entanglement

4.1 Core Criterion (Geometric Model Theorem)

Theorem 10.18.1.01 (Time Entanglement Criterion): Let the states of a system at causal times \(\tau_1\) and \(\tau_2\) be described respectively by coding orbits \(\Sigma_{\tau_1}\) and \(\Sigma_{\tau_2}\), with coordinates \((\xi_1, \eta_1)\) and \((\xi_2, \eta_2)\). From Theorem 1.3.4.01, the information field direction \(\xi\) is the Dirac spectral direction of \(\lambda_2\); sections at different \(\xi\) are exponentially suppressed by \(\exp[-\lambda_2(\Delta\xi)^2/2]\) in information field fluctuations and thus decoupled. \[\xi_1 = \xi_2.\]

4.2 Entanglement Entropy Formula (Geometric Model Formula)

When \(\xi_1 = \xi_2\), the \(\eta\)-directional distance between the two coding orbits is \(\Delta\eta = |\eta_1 - \eta_2|\). In the strongly anisotropic limit of spectral gap direction versus Dirac spectral direction (\(\lambda_2/\lambda_1 \approx 150\)), off-diagonal coupling terms are suppressed by the \(\lambda_2\) scale, and \((\xi, \eta)\) are approximately the principal-axis coordinates of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix. From Theorem 1.3.4.01 Birkhoff Mixing Matrix structure, the two-point information overlap kernel on the joint section is \(\exp[-(1/2)(\lambda_1(\Delta\eta)^2 + \lambda_2(\Delta\xi)^2)]\).

Taking the logarithm of the effective degrees of freedom and introducing coding orbit baseline regularization (model regularization constant \(1\)), we obtain:

Theorem 10.18.1.02 (Time Entanglement Entropy Formula): \[S_{\text{ent}}(\tau_1, \tau_2) = \frac{1}{2}\log\left(1 + \frac{1}{\lambda_1 (\Delta\eta)^2 + \lambda_2 (\Delta\xi)^2}\right)\]

[Geometric Model Formula: A deduction based on Theorem 1.3.4.01 Birkhoff Mixing Matrix structure; the \(1\) regularization and the coefficient \(1/2\) are both model constructions of this paper.]

Dimensional and Regularization Remarks: In the formula, \(\Delta\eta\) and \(\Delta\xi\) are both in units of \(\text{rad}\), and \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2\) have units of \(\text{rad}^{-2}\), so the product is dimensionless. The constant term \(1\) originates from the baseline entanglement entropy regularization of the coding orbit (model regularization constant), ensuring that the entropy tends to zero when \(\Delta\xi^2\Delta\eta^2 \to \infty\), i.e., zero entanglement entropy at complete causal independence.

Self-Consistency Analysis:

  • When \(\Delta\xi = 0\) and \(\Delta\eta \to 0\) (same information field coordinate and coincident causal times), the denominator tends to zero, \(S_{\text{ent}} \to \infty\), characterizing complete entanglement.
  • When \(\Delta\xi \neq 0\) (different information field coordinates), even if \(\Delta\eta = 0\), the denominator is dominated by \(\lambda_2(\Delta\xi)^2\), and \(S_{\text{ent}}\) is finite, decaying to zero as \(\Delta\xi\) increases.
  • When \(\Delta\eta \to \infty\) (causal times fully separated), \(S_{\text{ent}} \to 0\), reducing to classical causal independence.

Numerical Example: Taking \(\Delta\eta = 1.0\ \text{rad}\), \(\Delta\xi = 1/\sqrt{\lambda_2} = 0.00413\ \text{rad}\) (the minimally resolvable scale in the Dirac spectral direction), \(\lambda_1 = 392.21\), \(\lambda_2 = 58760.77\), we have:

\[\lambda_1 (\Delta\eta)^2 + \lambda_2 (\Delta\xi)^2 = 392.21 + 58760.77 \times (0.00413)^2 = 392.21 + 1.000 = 393.21,\]

\[S_{\text{ent}} = \frac{1}{2}\log\left(1 + \frac{1}{393.21}\right) \approx \frac{1}{2} \times 2.54 \times 10^{-3} \approx 1.27 \times 10^{-3}.\]

This indicates that time entanglement is generally extremely weak, unless \(\Delta\eta\) is very small (i.e., the two causal times nearly coincide).
