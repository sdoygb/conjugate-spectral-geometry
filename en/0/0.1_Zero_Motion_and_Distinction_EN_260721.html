§1 Undifferentiated Potential

Definition 1 (Undifferentiated Potential). Let \(\mathcal{Z}\) be a set (a legitimate mathematical object within ZFC). \(\delta: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{Z}\) is a non-trivial self-map — \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \mathcal{Z}\), i.e., \(\delta\) is not the identity map.

1.1 Mathematical Positioning of \(\mathcal{Z}\)

\(\mathcal{Z}\) is a set within ZFC. The framework draws an analogy between the “undifferentiated” nature of \(\mathcal{Z}\) and the indiscrete topology: the indiscrete topology (which can be assigned to any set) is “the most structureless” topology, under which the space cannot be non-trivially partitioned. This analogy provides a geometric picture of \(\mathcal{Z}\), but serves only as a philosophical motivation — the mathematical derivations of the framework do not rely on any topological properties of the indiscrete topology.

\(\mathcal{Z}\) is not “prior to sets” — it is a set within ZFC. The motivation for starting from \(\mathcal{Z}\) is: the non-trivial self-map \(\delta\) introduces the First Distinction upon a structureless starting point.

1.2 Distinction between \(\mathcal{Z}\) and “Nothing”

Several seemingly similar concepts must be rigorously distinguished:

  • Nothingness: a philosophical concept, already conceptualized, hence already distinguished.
  • The empty set \(\emptyset\): a set-theoretic object, already bearing the property of “emptiness.”
  • \(\mathcal{Z}\): prior to all conceptualization, prior to all attribution of properties.

\(\mathcal{Z}\) is not “there is nothing” — the concept of “thing” has not yet been established within \(\mathcal{Z}\). \(\mathcal{Z}\) is a primitive potential “capable of producing things,” but it itself contains no “thing.”

§2 Zero Motion

Axiom (Zero Motion). There exists a non-trivial self-map \(\delta: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{Z}\) on \(\mathcal{Z}\), satisfying \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \subsetneq \mathcal{Z}\).

This is the sole axiom of the entire system. All subsequent structures — Clifford Algebra, Bott Periodicity, three Structure Constants, physical constants — are derived from this single axiom.

This axiom possesses the following features:

  1. Non-creativity: \(\delta\) introduces nothing from outside — it is a self-map on \(\mathcal{Z}\).
  2. Non-triviality: \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \mathcal{Z}\) — motion produces difference.
  3. Landscape generativity: \(\delta\) generates the landscape of all algebraic realizations satisfying “non-trivial finite-period self-map.”

First Distinction. The first structure introduced by \(\delta\) is: \[\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \mathcal{Z}.\]

This produces the most primitive duality — “before motion” vs. “after motion.” This is not presupposed, but an inevitable consequence of \(\delta\)’s non-triviality. From this moment on, distinction occurs.

2.1 Precise Meaning of Non-Triviality

The non-triviality of \(\delta\) is not merely \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \mathcal{Z}\) — it carries a deeper meaning. Let \(\text{id}_\mathcal{Z}\) be the trivial self-map (identity map) on \(\mathcal{Z}\); then the non-triviality of \(\delta\) can be decomposed as:

  1. \(\delta \neq \text{id}_\mathcal{Z}\): \(\delta\) is not the identity — it does something.
  2. \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \subseteq \mathcal{Z}\): the output of \(\delta\) still belongs to \(\mathcal{Z}\) — \(\mathcal{Z}\) is closed (no external escape).
  3. \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \mathcal{Z}\): the output of \(\delta\) is strictly smaller than \(\mathcal{Z}\) — motion produces a remainder.

Point 3 is key: if \(\delta\) were surjective, then \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) = \mathcal{Z}\), and \(\delta\) would be “motion without remainder” — such motion leaves no trace and is equivalent to no motion at all. \(\delta\) can produce structure precisely because it is not surjective — it leaves untouched potential, so that the next iteration \(\delta\) can continue to operate on the remaining potential.

2.3 The Dual Meaning of “Zero”: Total Action is Zero

The “Zero” in “Zero Motion” carries a dual meaning — they are not two independent zeros, but two faces of the same zero:

  1. Ontological Zero: the undifferentiated nature of \(\mathcal{Z}\) — prior to all distinction. This is the “zero” discussed in §1: \(\mathcal{Z}\) contains no distinguished structure and is pure undifferentiated potential.

  2. Dynamical Zero: total action \(S_{\text{total}} = 0\) — after all distinctions have occurred, the sum of coding contributions from all sectors is precisely zero. This is an inevitable corollary of \(\delta\) being non-surjective (\(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \subsetneq \mathcal{Z}\)): \(\delta\) injects nothing from outside and outputs nothing to the outside. It only redistributes within \(\mathcal{Z}\) — hence total action is conserved, and the conserved value is zero (since the initial state of \(\mathcal{Z}\) is structureless, with zero initial action).

The unity of the two: \(\delta\) starts from zero (\(\mathcal{Z}\) is structureless), produces distinctions and structures within \(\mathcal{Z}\), but the total balance always remains zero — positive contributions from distinctions must be exactly canceled by negative contributions elsewhere. This is precisely the complete meaning of “Zero Motion”: motion, initiated from zero, ends in zero — but the intermediate process produces rich structures. Zero is not “nothing”; zero is a constraint — it constrains how structures can emerge.

§3 Iterative Properties of \(\delta\)

\(\delta\) is not a one-time operation — it is iterable. The iterative properties of \(\delta\) are the source of the entire system’s structure. This section elaborates in detail on the iterative characteristics of \(\delta\).

3.1 Non-Idempotence

Proposition 0.1.3.01 (Non-Idempotence). \(\delta \circ \delta \neq \delta\) — \(\delta\) is not idempotent.

Argument. Suppose \(\delta \circ \delta = \delta\). Then the second iteration of \(\delta\) produces the same result as the first iteration, i.e., \(\delta(\delta(\mathcal{Z})) = \delta(\mathcal{Z})\). This means that \(\delta\) “stops doing anything” after the first iteration — all subsequent iterations merely repeat the result of the first, producing no new distinctions.

But the non-triviality of \(\delta\) (\(\delta \neq \text{id}\)) means that \(\delta\) does something — it produces a distinction. If \(\delta\) stops doing anything at the second iteration, then the distinction produced by the first iteration is not processed by subsequent operations, and structures cannot accumulate. This contradicts “\(\delta\) is the source of structural emergence” — structural emergence requires sustained operations, not a one-time distinction.

Therefore \(\delta \circ \delta \neq \delta\). Each iteration of \(\delta\) does new work. ∎

An important corollary of non-idempotence: the orbit of \(\delta\) is not single-step — it is a path, not a point. The iteration sequence of \(\delta\) \[\mathcal{Z}, \quad \delta(\mathcal{Z}), \quad \delta^2(\mathcal{Z}), \quad \delta^3(\mathcal{Z}), \quad \dots\] has each term different from the previous one. This is the essence of \(\delta\) as “motion” — motion is not a state, motion is change, and change is sequential.

3.5 Three-Layer Self-Referential Closure

§3.1–§3.4 established the iterative properties of \(\delta\): non-idempotent, non-surjective, descending chain. This section proceeds from these properties to prove that the ternary structure is not a philosophical presupposition, but an inevitable product of \(\delta\)’s iterations.

Definition 3.5.1 (\(\delta\)-domain type \(\sim\)). For subsets \(A, B \subseteq \mathcal{Z}\) of \(\mathcal{Z}\), \(A\) and \(B\) are defined to have the same \(\delta\)-domain type, denoted \(A \sim B\), if and only if there exist bijections \(\phi: A \to B\) and \(\psi: \delta(A) \to \delta(B)\) such that the following diagram commutes:

\[\psi \circ \delta|_A = \delta|_B \circ \phi\]

Domain type characterizes the “behavioral pattern” of \(\delta\) on a subset — two subsets behave “the same” under the action of \(\delta\) (up to renaming) if and only if they are of the same type.

Lemma 3.5.2 (The first three domain types are mutually distinct).

\[\text{type}(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \text{type}(\delta(\mathcal{Z})) \neq \text{type}(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z})) \neq \text{type}(\mathcal{Z})\]

Argument: \(\mathcal{Z}\) is completely undifferentiated — before \(\delta\) acts, \(\mathcal{Z}\) has no internal structure. When \(\delta\) acts on \(\mathcal{Z}\), the behavior of \(\delta\) is entirely determined by \(\delta\) itself, unconstrained by any pre-existing structure. This is the “primitive operation.” \(\delta(\mathcal{Z})\) is different — it bears the imprint of \(\delta\) (non-surjectivity guarantees \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \subsetneq \mathcal{Z}\)). Suppose \(\text{type}(\mathcal{Z}) = \text{type}(\delta(\mathcal{Z}))\); then there exists a bijection \(\phi: \mathcal{Z} \to \delta(\mathcal{Z})\) such that \(\delta\) behaves identically on both. But \(\delta\) on \(\mathcal{Z}\) is the first distinction (producing \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \subsetneq \mathcal{Z}\)), while \(\delta\) on \(\delta(\mathcal{Z})\) is the second distinction (producing \(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z}) \subsetneq \delta(\mathcal{Z})\)). The “freshness” of the two differs — no element of \(\mathcal{Z}\) bears the \(\delta\) imprint, whereas all elements of \(\delta(\mathcal{Z})\) bear the \(\delta\) imprint. Hence no such bijection exists. A similar argument yields \(\text{type}(\delta(\mathcal{Z})) \neq \text{type}(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z}))\). ∎

Lemma 3.5.3 (Binary cycles produce no structural accumulation). Binary mutual conjugation — \(A\) constrains \(B\), \(B\) constrains \(A\) — cannot produce irreversible structural accumulation.

Argument: Let the binary cycle be \(A\) = Suppression (first distinction), \(B\) = Trace (memory distinction). \(B\) remembers the negation of \(A\), but the fact that “\(B\) remembers \(A\)” is not objectified by a third party. In \(\delta\) iterations: if there were only two layers, then after \(\delta^2\), \(\delta^3\) should \(= \delta\) (nothing new), implying \(\delta^2 = \text{id}\), contradicting the non-idempotence of \(\delta\) (Proposition 3.1.1). Therefore binary cycles are not self-consistent within the \(\delta\) framework. The non-idempotence of \(\delta\) forces the existence of a third layer. ∎

Theorem 3.5.4 (The self-referential property of \(\delta^3\)). \(\delta^3\) is the first iteration among the iterations of \(\delta\) whose operation domain contains the history of \(\delta\) itself.

Argument: \(\delta^3 = \delta \circ \delta^2\). \(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z}) = \delta(\delta(\mathcal{Z}))\), whose internal distinctions — \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \setminus \delta^2(\mathcal{Z})\) (the “pure Trace” touched the first time but not re-touched the second time) and \(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z}) \setminus \delta^3(\mathcal{Z})\) (newly touched the second time) — are entirely defined by the previous two operations of \(\delta\). When \(\delta\) acts on \(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z})\), its operational behavior is constrained by the internal structure of \(\delta^2(\mathcal{Z})\) — and this structure is the encoding of \(\delta\)’s own history. This is the mathematical meaning of self-reference: the operation domain of \(\delta^3\) is a complete record of the previous two operations of \(\delta\); \(\delta\) can no longer pretend that this is the first time — the imprint affects \(\delta\)’s behavior. The operation domains of \(\delta^1\) and \(\delta^2\) (\(\mathcal{Z}\) and \(\delta(\mathcal{Z})\)) do not contain the complete history of \(\delta\), hence \(\delta^3\) is the first iteration to achieve self-reference. ∎

§4 Ternary Intrinsic Structure of \(\delta\)

From the Three-Layer Self-Referential Closure Theorem of §3.5, the iterations of \(\delta\) achieve self-reference at the third step, and the ternary structure — Suppression, Trace, Emergence — unfolds as an inevitable result. 4.1 Suppression

Suppression is the negational face of \(\delta\). The \(\neq\) in \(\delta(\mathcal{Z}) \neq \mathcal{Z}\) is Suppression — it says “this is not that.” Without this negation, there is no distinction, and everything remains within \(\mathcal{Z}\).

Suppression is not “destruction” — \(\mathcal{Z}\) cannot be destroyed. Suppression is “drawing a line”: within the undifferentiated, a line is drawn, and the two sides of the line are no longer the same.

4.2 Trace

Trace is the memorial face of \(\delta\). If a distinction occurs but is not remembered, then in the next moment \(\delta\) would have to start over from \(\mathcal{Z}\) — which is equivalent to nothing having happened. Trace means: the distinction of the preceding step affects the behavior of the subsequent step.

The iterativity of \(\delta\) — \(\delta \circ \delta \circ \delta \cdots\) — is precisely the mathematical expression of Trace. Each \(\delta\) operates on the basis of the previous step, rather than starting from scratch.

4.3 Emergence

Emergence is the pattern-forming face of \(\delta\). A single distinction is merely duality (this-vs-that), but the sustained iteration of \(\delta\) accumulates irreversible patterns — structures. Emergence is not “something new injected from outside \(\delta\),” but rather “the cumulative effect of \(\delta\)’s iterations.”

Key point: Emergence is irreversible. Once a pattern forms, it constrains subsequent \(\delta\) operations — not all distinctions are permitted; only those self-consistent with existing patterns can survive. This is the germ of Self-consistency Filtering.

§5 Mutual Conjugate Balance

Suppression-Trace-Emergence do not occur sequentially. They are simultaneous — distinguishing, remembering the distinction, and pattern emergence are three faces of a single event.

Definition 2 (Mutual Conjugation). The state in which the three dimensions constrain and complete one another is called mutual conjugation: - Suppression is precisely remembered by Trace (otherwise Suppression is ineffective) - Trace is precisely patterned by Emergence (otherwise Trace is mere rote memorization) - Emergence is precisely constrained by Suppression (otherwise Emergence expands without bound)

If any of the three is missing, the distinction collapses: - No Suppression → no distinction → \(\delta\) is trivial - No Trace → distinction is instantaneous → no accumulation - No Emergence → back-and-forth oscillation → no structure

The hallmark of mutual conjugate balance is closure: the loop returns to itself. When \(\delta\) iterates sufficiently many times, if the loop can close — returning to the starting point but carrying accumulated patterns — then mutual conjugate balance is achieved. Closure = Consummation.

The mathematical expression of mutual conjugate balance is total action equals zero:

\[\boxed{S_\mathcal{M} + S_\mathcal{C} + S_\mathcal{I} = 0}\]

This is not an externally imposed constraint — it is an intrinsic property of \(\delta\) as a non-surjective self-map (§2.3). Suppression contributes positively (negation consumes free energy), Trace records negatively (memory is energy release), and the pattern closure of Emergence returns to zero — the coding contributions of the three are mutually locked under the zero-sum constraint. Zero is not “nothing at all”; zero is perfect balance — all the activity of the three sectors on the Coding Orbit sums precisely to zero. This is precisely the ultimate meaning of “Zero Motion”: starting from zero, ending in zero, but experiencing all possible distinctions in between.

This is the semantic-layer concept of Completeness — at this stage it is still merely a qualitative judgment: “closure means consummation.” In [0.3], this will acquire a precise mathematical expression: Berry Phase \(= 2\pi\).
