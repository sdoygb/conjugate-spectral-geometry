§1 Sector Parameter Space as a Berry=2π Entity

1.1 Definition of the Parameter Space

From the Born Rule (Theorem 1.4.3.01) and the sector structure (Theorem 1.1.5.01–15), the coding weights of the three sectors \(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I}\) satisfy the normalization constraint:

\[\sigma_\mathcal{M}\sigma_\mathcal{C}\sigma_\mathcal{I} = 1\]

Under the parameterization of the Birkhoff mixing matrix (Theorem 2.1.4.01), this is equivalently expressed in angular parameters:

\[\theta_\mathcal{M} + \theta_\mathcal{C} + \theta_\mathcal{I} = \frac{\pi}{2}\]

Define the sector parameter space \(\Sigma\) as the 2-dimensional submanifold satisfying this condition, embedded in the three-sector angular parameter space \((\theta_\mathcal{M}, \theta_\mathcal{C}, \theta_\mathcal{I}) \in (0, \pi/2)^3\).

1.2 Symplectic Structure of Σ — Proof of Berry=2π

On \(\Sigma\), from the algebraic structure of the Born Rule (Theorem 1.4.3.01), there exists a symplectic form:

\[\omega = \left(\frac{1}{\sin^2\theta_\mathcal{M}} + \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta_\mathcal{C}} + \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta_\mathcal{I}}\right) \mathrm{d}\theta_\mathcal{M} \wedge \mathrm{d}\theta_\mathcal{C}\]

\((\Sigma, \omega)\) is a symplectic manifold.

Berry phase of Σ as a global geometric entity: Consider a closed loop \(\gamma\) on \(\Sigma\) encircling \(\theta_\mathcal{I}\) from \(0 \to \pi/2\) (traversing the complete domain of definition of the \(\mathcal{I}\)-sector). The Berry phase accumulated by \(\gamma\) on the spinor bundle is \(2\pi\) — because \(\gamma\) encloses the topologically nontrivial region of the \(\mathcal{I}\)-sector, the loop passes through two singularities, and its Gauss-Bonnet integral is \(2\pi\).

Conclusion: \(\Sigma\) is a global geometric entity with Berry \(= 2\pi\). This is consistent with the Completeness Criterion — the Bott periodicity \(\delta^8 = 2\pi\) Berry phase projected onto the sector parameter space.

§2 Definition of δ₀ — Pure Calculus, Independent of Any Physical Quantity

Definition

On the sector parameter space \(\Sigma\), at any point \((\theta_\mathcal{M}, \theta_\mathcal{C}, \theta_\mathcal{I})\), the projection narrowing \(\delta_0\) is defined as the relative change of the \(\mathcal{I}\)-sector projection \(\sin\theta_\mathcal{I}\):

\[\delta_0 \equiv \frac{|\Delta\sin\theta_\mathcal{I}|}{\sin\theta_\mathcal{I}}\]

Lemma 2.5.2.01 (Small Fluctuation Approximation)

For small fluctuations \(\delta\theta_\mathcal{I} \ll 1\):

\[\delta_0 = |\delta\theta_\mathcal{I}| \cdot \cot\theta_\mathcal{I} + O(\delta\theta_\mathcal{I}^2)\]

Proof (pure calculus, no physical assumptions needed):

\[\sin(\theta_\mathcal{I} + \delta\theta_\mathcal{I}) = \sin\theta_\mathcal{I}\cos\delta\theta_\mathcal{I} + \cos\theta_\mathcal{I}\sin\delta\theta_\mathcal{I}\]

\(\cos\delta\theta_\mathcal{I} \approx 1\), \(\sin\delta\theta_\mathcal{I} \approx \delta\theta_\mathcal{I}\):

\[\Delta\sin\theta_\mathcal{I} \approx \cos\theta_\mathcal{I} \cdot \delta\theta_\mathcal{I}\]

\[\delta_0 = \frac{|\cos\theta_\mathcal{I} \cdot \delta\theta_\mathcal{I}|}{\sin\theta_\mathcal{I}} = |\delta\theta_\mathcal{I}| \cdot \cot\theta_\mathcal{I} \quad \square\]

Key point: \(\cot\theta_\mathcal{I}\) appears in the formula solely because the definition of \(\delta_0\) chooses \(\sin\theta_\mathcal{I}\) as the projected quantity. This is a parameterization choice of the \(\mathcal{I}\)-sector coordinate on \(\Sigma\) — a purely geometric convention, independent of any fermion.

§3 Soft Mode Zero-Point Width — Pure Symplectic Geometry

3.1 Second-Order Structure at the Birkhoff Fixed Point and the Soft Mode

At the symmetric reference point \((30°, 30°, 30°)\) of \(\Sigma\), the constrained second-order structure matrix of the algebraic action \(S(\sigma)\) (Theorem 1.6.4.01) is:

\[H_{(\xi,\eta)} = \begin{pmatrix} H_{\xi\xi} & H_{\xi\eta} \\ H_{\eta\xi} & H_{\eta\eta} \end{pmatrix}\]

Eigenvalues: soft mode \(\lambda_1\) (\(\eta\) direction), hard mode \(\lambda_2\) (\(\xi\) direction).

\[\lambda_2 / \lambda_1 \approx 150 = k_0 \times \Lambda \times (\Delta\Theta)^2 = 2 \times 3 \times 5^2\]

This ratio is the geometric curvature asymmetry of \(\Sigma\) — a purely geometric quantity, uniquely determined by the second-order derivative structure of the algebraic action at the fixed point. It is consistent with the \(\Lambda_H\) structure identity (2.3 §2.7), given by the product of the three structure constants \(k_0=2\), \(\Lambda=3\), \(\Delta\Theta=5\).

3.2 Harmonic Oscillator Zero-Point Width

In the Darboux neighborhood of the fixed point, the Hamiltonian in the soft mode direction \(\eta\) is:

\[H_{\text{soft}} = \frac{1}{2}\lambda_1 \cdot \eta^2\]

In geometric units \(\hbar=1\), the harmonic oscillator ground state wavefunction:

\[\psi_0(\eta) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_1}}{\pi}\right)^{1/4} \exp\left(-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_1}}{2}\eta^2\right)\]

Zero-point width (root-mean-square):

\[\langle\eta^2\rangle_0 = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda_1}}\]

\[\sigma_\eta = \sqrt{\langle\eta^2\rangle_0} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\lambda_1^{1/4}}\]

This is a purely symplectic geometric quantity on \(\Sigma\) — depending only on the soft mode eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\) and Darboux linearization (the local coordinate transformation uniquely guaranteed by the symplectic structure).

3.3 Projection of the Soft Mode in the \(\mathcal{I}\)-Direction

The soft mode coordinate \(\eta = (\theta_\mathcal{I} + \theta_\mathcal{C}) - (\theta_\mathcal{I}^0 + \theta_\mathcal{C}^0)\). Near the symmetric reference point, the soft mode eigenvector has components \((1/\sqrt{2}, 1/\sqrt{2})\) in the \((\theta_\mathcal{I}, \theta_\mathcal{C})\) plane (symmetry).

The amplitude of the soft mode zero-point fluctuation projected onto the \(\theta_\mathcal{I}\) direction:

\[\sigma_{\theta_\mathcal{I}} = \sigma_\eta \cdot |v_\mathcal{I}| = \frac{|v_\mathcal{I}|}{\sqrt{2}\,\lambda_1^{1/4}}\]

where \(|v_\mathcal{I}|\) is the projection component of the soft mode eigenvector in the \(\theta_\mathcal{I}\) direction. At the symmetric reference point, \(|v_\mathcal{I}| = 1/\sqrt{2}\).

§4 Theorem 49: Spectral Geometry Formula for δ₀^single

Statement

Theorem 2.5.4.01 (Spectral Geometry Formula for the Single-Chain Zero-Point Width). On the sector parameter space \(\Sigma\), at a point with \(\mathcal{I}\)-sector angular coordinate \(\theta_\mathcal{I}\) and soft mode eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\), the single-chain zero-point width is:

\[\delta_0^{\text{single}}(\theta_\mathcal{I}, \lambda_1) = \frac{|v_\mathcal{I}|}{\sqrt{2}\,\lambda_1^{1/4}} \cdot \cot\theta_\mathcal{I}.\]

Derivation Chain (each step supported by a theorem)

  1. \(\delta_0 = |\delta\theta_\mathcal{I}| \cdot \cot\theta_\mathcal{I}\) (Lemma 2.5.2.01, small fluctuation approximation)
  2. \(|\delta\theta_\mathcal{I}| = \sigma_{\theta_\mathcal{I}}\) (the quantum fluctuation in \(\theta_\mathcal{I}\) is the projection of the soft mode zero-point width)
  3. \(\sigma_{\theta_\mathcal{I}} = \sigma_\eta \cdot |v_\mathcal{I}|\) (projection of the soft mode onto the \(\mathcal{I}\)-direction)
  4. \(\sigma_\eta = 1/(\sqrt{2}\,\lambda_1^{1/4})\) (harmonic oscillator zero-point width, §3.2)
  5. Therefore \(\delta_0^{\text{single}} = (|v_\mathcal{I}|/(\sqrt{2}\,\lambda_1^{1/4})) \cdot \cot\theta_\mathcal{I}\).

Remarks

The formula involves only geometric quantities: \(\theta_\mathcal{I}\) (coordinate on \(\Sigma\)), \(\lambda_1\) (soft mode eigenvalue), \(|v_\mathcal{I}|\) (eigenvector projection). No physical mass, coupling constant, or fermion identity appears anywhere in the derivation.
