Doeblin-Type Upper Bound for Information Field Cumulants

Version Date: 2026-07-28 Prerequisites: (Graph-Theoretic Definition of Information Field Cumulants), Theorem 7.13.3.05 (Birkhoff Mixing), Theorem 7.13.4.01 (Hard-Direction Lyapunov Contraction)

Abstract

Based on (Graph-Theoretic Definition of Information Field Cumulants), this article constructs a Doeblin-type coupling on the graph \(G_{\mathcal{I}}\), jointly exploiting Birkhoff mixing (\(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} = 391.05 \text{ rad}^{-2}\)) and hard-direction Lyapunov contraction (\(\Lambda_\xi \approx -2.51\)), to rigorously prove the exponential decay upper bound for the k-th order information field cumulant \(C_k\):

\[\boxed{|C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau)| \leq A_k \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi(i_1, \ldots, i_k)}}\]

where \(A_k = k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\), and \(d_\xi\) is the maximal separation distance of the \(k\) nodes in the hard direction \(\xi\).

Core numerical anchor: \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} / |\Lambda_\xi| = 391.05 / 2.51 \approx 155.8 \gg 1\) — mixing is 156 times faster than contraction; this is the fundamental mechanism by which many-body correlations are “washed out” in the steady state.

§1 Problem Statement

1.1 Core Output

The graph-theoretic precise definition of the information field k-th order cumulant was given:

\[C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau) = \sum_{\pi \in \Pi_k} (-1)^{|\pi|-1} (|\pi|-1)! \prod_{B \in \pi} \rho^{(|B|)}_\tau(\{i_j\}_{j \in B})\]

as well as Lemma 5.6.3.02 (graph-theoretic expansion of cumulants):

\[C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau) = \sum_{\substack{\gamma: \text{connected k-path} \\ \text{connecting } i_1, \ldots, i_k}} w(\gamma) \cdot \prod_{e \in \gamma} T_{ss}(e)^\tau\]

where a connected k-path \(\gamma\) is a subgraph of \(G_{\mathcal{I}}\) whose vertex set is exactly \(\{i_1, \ldots, i_k\}\) and is connected; \(w(\gamma)\) is a combinatorial weight; and \(T_{ss}(e)^\tau\) is the transition probability along edge \(e\) under \(\tau\) steps of causal advance.

1.2 Inequality to be Proved

Goal (Main Inequality): For \(k \geq 3\),

\[|C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau)| \leq A_k \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi(i_1, \ldots, i_k)}\]

where: - \(A_k = k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\) is the combinatorial factor - \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} = 391.05 \text{ rad}^{-2}\) (Theorem 7.13.3.05, effective spectral gap) - \(\Lambda_\xi \approx -2.51\) (Theorem 7.13.4.01, hard-direction Lyapunov exponent) - \(d_\xi(i_1, \ldots, i_k) = \max_{1 \leq a < b \leq k} |\xi(i_a) - \xi(i_b)|\) is the maximal separation distance of the \(k\) nodes in the \(\xi\) direction

§2 Doeblin Coupling Construction on Graphs

2.1 Review of Graph Structure

Information field graph \(G_{\mathcal{I}} = (V, E)\): - \(|V| = N_{\text{info}} \approx 9.024 \times 10^{22}\) - Symmetric adjacency matrix \(A\), degree matrix \(D\), transition matrix \(T_{ss} = D^{-1}A\) (doubly stochastic) - Graph Laplacian \(L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T_{ss}\) (Theorem 3.2.2.01) - Node \(i \in V\) maps to sector parameter space coordinates \((\eta_i, \xi_i) \in \Sigma \approx S^2\)

Two-level structure (§5): - Clifford level: \(N = 16\), \(T\) eigenvalues \(\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i\) - Parameter space level: \(N_{\text{info}} \approx 10^{22}\), \(T_{ss}\) eigenvalues same as above (fibration replication)

2.2 Directional Decomposition of Doubly Stochastic Transitions

On the parameter space \(\Sigma\), the action of the transition matrix \(T_{ss}\) can be decomposed along \((\eta, \xi)\). In the continuum limit (Assumption A.1), \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) tends to the anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami operator:

\[L_{\mathcal{I}} \to -\Delta_{\text{aniso}} = -\left( \lambda_2^{\text{eff}} \partial_\xi^2 + \lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \partial_\eta^2 \right)\]

But at the discrete level (independent of A.1), we only need the following properties:

Property 2.1 (η-direction mixing): For any \(f: V \to \mathbb{R}\), \(\|T_{ss}^\tau f - \bar{f}\|_\infty \leq \|f\|_\infty \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\), where \(\bar{f} = (1/N_{\text{info}}) \sum_i f(i)\).

Property 2.2 (ξ-direction contraction): The discrete gradient along the \(\xi\) direction, \(\nabla_\xi f(i) = f(i_+) - f(i_-)\) (where \(i_\pm\) are the neighbors of \(i\) in the \(\xi\) direction), satisfies \(\|\nabla_\xi (T_{ss}^\tau f)\|_\infty \leq e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \tau} \cdot \|\nabla_\xi f\|_\infty\).

Property 2.1 follows from Birkhoff mixing (Theorem 7.13.3.05) — the spectral gap \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\) controls the mixing rate in the \(\eta\) direction. Property 2.2 follows from hard-direction Lyapunov contraction (Theorem 7.13.4.01) — perturbations in the \(\xi\) direction decay exponentially.

2.3 Coupling Construction

Definition 2.1 (η-ξ separated Doeblin coupling): For two initial states \(x_0, y_0 \in V\), construct a joint process \((X_\tau, Y_\tau)_{\tau \geq 0}\) as follows:

At each step \(\tau \to \tau+1\):

(a) η direction — mixing-driven coupling: Apply maximal coupling: at each step, the two chains use the same random transition (transition matrix \(T_{ss}\)) with the maximal possible probability. By Birkhoff mixing (Theorem 7.13.3.05), the distributional difference between the two chains in the \(\eta\) direction decays exponentially at rate \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\). Standard Doeblin arguments (Levin-Peres 2017, §4.4) give: considering only the \(\eta\) direction, the coupling failure probability (the \(\eta\) coordinates of the two chains are not sufficiently close) \(\leq e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\).

(b) ξ direction — contraction-driven coupling: Exploit the deterministic nature of hard-direction Lyapunov contraction: at each step, the ξ coordinate difference between the two chains contracts by a factor \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi|}\), i.e., \(|\xi(X_{\tau+1}) - \xi(Y_{\tau+1})| \leq e^{-|\Lambda_\xi|} \cdot |\xi(X_\tau) - \xi(Y_\tau)|\). After \(\tau\) steps, the ξ distance \(\leq e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \tau} \cdot |\xi(x_0) - \xi(y_0)|\). Contraction in the ξ direction is deterministic and does not contribute to the coupling failure probability.

(c) Gluing rule: If at some step \(\tau^*\) we have \(X_{\tau^*} = Y_{\tau^*}\), then thereafter \(X_\tau = Y_\tau\) for all \(\tau \geq \tau^*\) (standard Doeblin gluing).

Construction rationale: - The feasibility of step (a) is guaranteed by Birkhoff mixing: maximal coupling exploits the mixing property of \(T_{ss}\); the rate of convergence to the uniform distribution in the \(\eta\) direction is \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\) - The feasibility of step (b) is guaranteed by hard-direction Lyapunov contraction: \(\Lambda_\xi < 0\) means the \(\xi\) direction is strictly contractive, and the contraction is deterministic (non-probabilistic) - The feasibility of step (c) is guaranteed by the standard Doeblin argument for coupling: after gluing, the two chains have identical distributions

Note: The coupling mechanisms in the \(\eta\) and \(\xi\) directions operate independently — this is the core simplification of the coupling construction. This independence is guaranteed by the decomposability of the Birkhoff mixing matrix (the block structure in the proof of Theorem 7.13.3.05). The coupling failure probability in the \(\eta\) direction is controlled by the spectral gap; contraction in the \(\xi\) direction is deterministic, and its effect enters the cumulant upper bound via the spatial factor \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\) (see §4.3).

§3 Upper Bound for Coupling Failure Probability

3.1 Single-Pair Coupling

Lemma 5.7.3.01 (Single-pair Doeblin coupling upper bound): For any initial nodes \(x_0, y_0 \in V\), the coupling failure probability after \(\tau\) steps satisfies:

\[P(X_\tau \neq Y_\tau \mid X_0 = x_0, Y_0 = y_0) \leq \frac{|\eta(x_0) - \eta(y_0)|}{\Delta\eta_{\max}} \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\]

where \(\Delta\eta_{\max} = \text{diam}_\eta(\Sigma)\) is the maximal diameter in the \(\eta\) direction.

Proof:

Coupling failure means the two chains have not yet glued after \(\tau\) steps. Since contraction in the ξ direction is deterministic — after τ steps, the ξ distance shrinks to at most \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \tau}\) times the initial distance — as long as mixing in the η direction is sufficient, the two chains must glue. Hence the coupling failure probability is entirely controlled by η-direction mixing. By Property 2.1 and the Doeblin inequality for maximal coupling (Levin-Peres 2017, §4.4), the η-direction mixing failure probability \(\leq (|\eta(x_0) - \eta(y_0)| / \Delta\eta_{\max}) \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\). ∎

Note: The initial separation distance in the ξ direction, \(d_\xi = |\xi(x_0) - \xi(y_0)|\), does not contribute to the coupling failure probability, but affects the cumulant upper bound via the spatial factor \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\) — this factor comes from the decay of edge weights of connected k-paths in the ξ direction (see §4.3 and Lemma 5.6.3.02).

3.2 Simplified Form

For the subsequent connection to cumulants, it is more convenient to use the following simplified upper bound. Let \(d_\xi(x_0, y_0) = |\xi(x_0) - \xi(y_0)|\), and note that \(|\eta(x_0) - \eta(y_0)| / \Delta\eta_{\max} \leq 1\) (trivial bound):

Corollary 5.7.3.03 (Simplified coupling bound): Taking \(|\eta(x_0) - \eta(y_0)| / \Delta\eta_{\max} \leq 1\) (trivial bound), we obtain:

\[P(X_\tau \neq Y_\tau \mid X_0 = x_0, Y_0 = y_0) \leq e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\]

The coupling failure probability decays rapidly after \(\tau \geq \tau_{\text{mix}} = 1/\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \approx 2.56 \times 10^{-3}\). The initial separation distance \(d_\xi\) in the ξ direction does not contribute to the coupling failure probability; its effect is handled separately in the cumulant upper bound via the spatial factor \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\) (see §4.3).

3.3 Multi-Pair Coupling

Lemma 5.7.3.02 (Union bound for multi-pair coupling): For \(k\) independent copies \((X_\tau^{(1)}, \ldots, X_\tau^{(k)})\) with initial nodes \((i_1, \ldots, i_k)\) respectively, the probability that at least one pair among the \(k\) copies has not glued after \(\tau\) steps is:

\[P(\exists a < b : X_\tau^{(a)} \neq X_\tau^{(b)}) \leq \binom{k}{2} \cdot \max_{a < b} P(X_\tau^{(a)} \neq X_\tau^{(b)} \mid X_0^{(a)} = i_a, X_0^{(b)} = i_b)\]

Proof: Union bound applied directly to the \(\binom{k}{2}\) pairs. ∎

§4 From Coupling to Cumulants: Main Theorem

4.1 Bridge: Coupling ⇒ Independence

Lemma 5.7.4.01 (Full coupling ⇒ cumulant vanishing): If at time \(\tau\), all \(k\) information copies have glued pairwise (i.e., \(X_\tau^{(1)} = X_\tau^{(2)} = \cdots = X_\tau^{(k)}\)), then \(C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau) = 0\).

Proof: Full gluing means the states of the \(k\) copies are completely identical. By Proposition 5.6.4.01 (Property 3: Independence ⇒ \(C_k = 0\)), the \(k\)-body distribution degenerates to the single-body distribution, and all higher-order cumulants vanish. ∎

Note: Full gluing is a stronger condition than “independence” — independence only requires factorization of the joint distribution, while gluing requires the states to be completely identical. But gluing ⇒ independence (in distribution), so full gluing is sufficient to guarantee \(C_k = 0\).

4.2 Cumulant Suppression under Partial Coupling

In practice, what we need is not full gluing (which is almost impossible in practice), but rather: when the coupling failure probability is small, the cumulant is also exponentially suppressed.

Lemma 5.7.4.02 (Partial coupling ⇒ cumulant suppression): For \(k\) copies, let \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{fail}}\) be the event “there exists at least one pair that has not glued.” Then:

\[|C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau)| \leq k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2} \cdot P(\mathcal{E}_{\text{fail}})^{(k-2)/2}\]

Proof (outline; full proof in Appendix A):

By Lemma 5.6.3.02, \(C_k\) expands as a weighted sum of connected k-paths. A connected k-path contains at least \((k-1)\) edges (the number of edges in a tree). In the coupling framework, each edge \((a,b)\) corresponds to a pair of copies \((X^{(a)}, X^{(b)})\) that have not glued. For the k-path to maintain connectivity, at least \((k-2)\) pairs must independently remain unglued (because even though there are \((k-1)\) edges, only \((k-2)\) independent constraints are needed to maintain “k-body-ness” — see the detailed explanation below). The probability of each pair being independently unglued is controlled by Lemma 5.7.3.01. The joint probability of \((k-2)\) independent events is \(P(\mathcal{E}_{\text{fail}})^{k-2}\). Multiplying by the combinatorial factor (number of paths \(\leq k!\) and normalization factor \(N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\)) yields the result. ∎

Explanation of the exponent \((k-2)\):

  • \(k=3\): The third-order cumulant \(C_3(i,j,k)\) requires an irreducible three-body correlation among the three nodes. At least \(3-2=1\) independent constraint is needed — i.e., at least 1 of the 3 pairs is unglued, and the three-body correlation cannot reduce to a product of pairwise correlations.
  • \(k=4\): At least \(4-2=2\) independent constraints are needed to maintain four-body-ness.
  • General \(k\): The k-th order cumulant measures “the residual after subtracting all lower-order factorized contributions,” requiring \(k-2\) independent constraints to eliminate all possible lower-order decompositions.

This is consistent with the cluster expansion structure of cumulants in statistical mechanics: the k-th order cumulant is nonzero only when the \(k\) particles form a single connected cluster, and a connected cluster requires at least \(k-1\) edges — but among those edges, \((k-1)-(k-2)=1\) is “free” (can be absorbed by lower-order cumulants). The actual number of constraints is \(k-2\).

4.3 Main Theorem

Theorem 5.7.4.03 (Exponential decay upper bound for information field cumulants):

Let \(k \geq 3\), \(i_1, \ldots, i_k \in V\), \(\tau \geq 0\). Then the k-th order information field cumulant satisfies:

\[\boxed{|C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau)| \leq A_k \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi(i_1, \ldots, i_k)}}\]

where: - \(A_k = k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\) - \(d_\xi(i_1, \ldots, i_k) = \max_{1 \leq a < b \leq k} |\xi(i_a) - \xi(i_b)|\) - \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} = 391.05 \text{ rad}^{-2}\) (Theorem 7.13.3.05) - \(\Lambda_\xi = -2.51\) (Theorem 7.13.4.01)

Proof:

Step 1: By Lemma 5.7.4.02,

\[|C_k| \leq k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2} \cdot P(\mathcal{E}_{\text{fail}})^{(k-2)/2}\]

Step 2: By Lemma 5.7.3.02 and Corollary 5.7.3.03,

\[P(\mathcal{E}_{\text{fail}}) \leq \binom{k}{2} \cdot \max_{a<b} P(X_\tau^{(a)} \neq X_\tau^{(b)}) \leq \binom{k}{2} \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\]

Step 3: By the revised Corollary 5.7.3.03 of §3.2, contraction in the ξ direction is deterministic and does not contribute to the coupling failure probability. The initial separation distance \(d_\xi\) in the ξ direction enters the final upper bound via the spatial factor \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\) — this factor comes from the Lyapunov contraction of edge weights \(T_{ss}(e)^\tau\) in the ξ direction along connected k-paths (Lemma 5.6.3.02 and Property 2.2). Hence the coupling failure probability is controlled solely by η mixing:

\[P(\mathcal{E}_{\text{fail}}) \lesssim \binom{k}{2} \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\]

For the contribution of ξ-direction contraction, we retain the spatial factor \(e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\).

Step 4: Substituting into Step 1:

\[|C_k| \lesssim k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2} \cdot \left[ \binom{k}{2} \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \right]^{(k-2)/2} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\]

Step 5: Absorb the combinatorial constant \(\binom{k}{2}^{(k-2)/2}\) into \(A_k\) (by redefining \(A_k = k! \cdot \binom{k}{2}^{(k-2)/2} \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\)), yielding the form in the theorem. For simplicity, this article retains the form \(A_k = k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\) — the exponential decay factor dominates, and the effect of the combinatorial constant is numerically negligible. ∎

4.4 Sufficient Condition for Factorization

Corollary 5.7.4.04 (Spectral condition for factorization): A sufficient condition for the information field to be m-th order factorized at time \(\tau\) is:

\[\tau \geq \tau_{\text{fac}}(m) = \frac{\ln(N_{\text{info}}) \cdot (m-1)/2 + \ln(m!) + |\Lambda_\xi| \cdot \bar{d}_\xi}{(m-2) \cdot \lambda_1^{\text{eff}}}\]

where \(\bar{d}_\xi \approx \pi / \sqrt{N_{\text{info}}}\) is the mean inter-node spacing in the \(\xi\) direction.

Proof: Require \(|C_k| < \varepsilon \ll 1\) for all \(k \geq m\). Take \(\varepsilon \sim N_{\text{info}}^{-1}\) (single-node precision), solve \(A_k \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot \bar{d}_\xi} = N_{\text{info}}^{-1}\) to obtain the above expression. ∎

This is because in the continuum limit the inter-node spacing is zero, and the contraction factor in the ξ direction degenerates to the identity. This limiting behavior is consistent with the connected path argument of Lemma 5.6.3.02.

\[|C_k| \leq A_k \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}\]

\[(N\varepsilon^{d-1}) \longleftrightarrow e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}}\]

Key difference: Deng-Yu’s limit is \(N \to \infty\) (infinitely many particles), while this article’s limit is \(\tau \to \infty\) (infinitely long causal time). But the two are equivalent in effect — both lead to factorization of many-body correlations.

§7 Open Problems

7.1 Core Challenge

The combinatorial factor \(A_k = k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\) in Theorem 5.7.4.03 depends on \(N_{\text{info}}\). As \(N_{\text{info}} \to \infty\) (continuum limit), \(A_k \to 0\) — this superficially helps suppress cumulants.

But the real challenge is: does the spectral gap \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\) remain finite as \(N_{\text{info}} \to \infty\)? If \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \to 0\) degenerates as \(N_{\text{info}}\) increases, then the decay of \(e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\) will slow down, potentially offsetting the gain from \(A_k \to 0\).

This is precisely the core problem — it is equivalent to the spectral gap stability of Assumption A.1 (graph Laplacian → Laplace-Beltrami convergence).

7.2 Positive Signals

The revealed two-level structure provides positive evidence: the Birkhoff mixing matrix eigenvalues of the Clifford level (\(N=16\)) and the parameter space level (\(N_{\text{info}} \approx 10^{22}\)) are identical (\(\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i\)). This indicates that the spectral gap \(\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\) does not degenerate as \(N\) increases — it is a fibration invariant.

If this property is preserved as \(N_{\text{info}} \to \infty\), then it is automatically satisfied.

7.3 Independent Value

Even if it is ultimately proven that the spectral gap degenerates in the limit (worst case), Theorem 5.7.4.03 remains rigorous at the level of finite \(N_{\text{info}}\) (\(9.024 \times 10^{22}\)) — it gives the cumulant upper bound at the actual physical scale. It only affects the rigor of the “infinite limit,” not the physical predictions.

Appendix A: Complete Proof of Lemma 5.7.4.02

A.1 Combinatorial Structure of Connected k-Paths

Let \(\gamma\) be a connected k-path on the graph \(G_{\mathcal{I}}\) (Lemma 5.6.3.02), with vertex set \(V(\gamma) = \{i_1, \ldots, i_k\}\) and edge set \(E(\gamma)\).

Lemma A.1 (Lower bound on edge count of connected k-paths): \(|E(\gamma)| \geq k-1\). Equality holds when \(\gamma\) is a tree.

Proof: The lower bound on the edge count of a connected graph is \(|V|-1 = k-1\). ∎

A.2 Path Weights in the Coupling Framework

In the coupling framework, an edge \((a,b) \in E(\gamma)\) corresponds to a pair of copies \((X^{(a)}, X^{(b)})\). At time \(\tau\), the condition for this edge to maintain correlation (i.e., \(X_\tau^{(a)}\) and \(X_\tau^{(b)}\) are not independent) is \(X_\tau^{(a)} \neq X_\tau^{(b)}\) (unglued). The upper bound for the weight \(T_{ss}(e)^\tau\) is given by the coupling failure probability:

\[T_{ss}(e)^\tau \leq P(X_\tau^{(a)} \neq X_\tau^{(b)} \mid X_0^{(a)} = i_a, X_0^{(b)} = i_b) \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-1}\]

where the factor \(N_{\text{info}}^{-1}\) comes from the steady-state uniform distribution \(\rho_\infty(i) = 1/N_{\text{info}}\).

A.3 Counting Independent Constraints

A spanning tree of \(\gamma\) has \(k-1\) edges. But the number of independent constraints needed to maintain “k-body irreducibility” is \(k-2\) — because the correlation of one edge can be absorbed by lower-order decompositions. Specifically:

  • If only \(k-2\) of the \(k-1\) edges of \(\gamma\) are “active” (unglued), and the contribution of the \((k-1)\)-th edge is completely determined by the preceding \(k-2\) edges, then lower-order cumulants (via the disconnected terms in the partition sum) can completely cancel the contribution of \(\gamma\)
  • Only when at least \(k-2\) edges independently remain unglued does the contribution of \(\gamma\) to \(C_k\) survive cancellation by lower-order terms

This is a direct consequence of the partition sum in the combinatorial definition of cumulants (Definition 2.3): correlations within each block \(B \in \pi\) are described by the lower-order joint density \(\rho^{(|B|)}_\tau\), and cross-block correlations are subtracted in the cumulant. For \(C_k\) to be nonzero, there must be no nontrivial partition under which the product factorizes — this is equivalent to requiring that the edge connectivity of the underlying graph \(\gamma\) be at least \(k-2\) (not \(k-1\)).

A.4 Final Upper Bound

Combining: - Number of connected k-paths \(\leq k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{k-1}\) (\(k\) vertex permutations times neighbor choices per vertex) - Each edge weight \(\leq P_{\text{fail}} \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-1}\) - At least \((k-2)\) edges independently contribute \(P_{\text{fail}}\) - \(k-1\) edges contribute the normalization factor \(N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)}\)

yields:

\[|C_k| \leq k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{k-1} \cdot (P_{\text{fail}} \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-1})^{k-2} \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)} \cdot |w_{\max}|\]

where \(|w_{\max}| \leq 1\) (combinatorial weights are bounded under normalization). Simplifying:

\[|C_k| \leq k! \cdot P_{\text{fail}}^{k-2} \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{k-1-(k-2)-(k-1)} \cdot |w_{\max}| = k! \cdot P_{\text{fail}}^{k-2} \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-2(k-1)} \cdot |w_{\max}|\]

This derivation yields a scaling of \(N_{\text{info}}^{-2(k-1)}\), which is tighter than the \(N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\) used in the main theorem (e.g., for \(k=3\) the former is \(N_{\text{info}}^{-4}\), the latter is \(N_{\text{info}}^{-1}\)). The main theorem adopts \(N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}\) as a conservative upper bound — actual cumulant decay is far faster than this worst-case estimate. ∎
