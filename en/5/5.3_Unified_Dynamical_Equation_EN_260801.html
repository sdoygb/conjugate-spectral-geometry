5.3 Unified Dynamical Equation for the Information Field

Version: 260721.2

Abstract

This article establishes the unified dynamical equation for the information field — a diffusion-response-steady-state three-layer framework. The evolution of the information field is described by three levels: fast diffusion (network propagation), intermediate-speed response (causal field driving), and slow steady-state (global equilibrium). This article derives in detail the coupling term structure of the three-field joint equations, analyzes the analytic solutions of the equations, and discusses the physical meaning of information conservation.

§1 Three-Layer Framework

1.1 Diffusion Layer

Rapid diffusion of the information field on the network \(G_{\mathcal{I}}\):

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{\text{diff}} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}},\]

where \(D_{\mathcal{I}} = 1/\tau_I\) is the diffusion coefficient and \(L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T\) is the Graph Laplacian.

Physical meaning of the diffusion layer:

  • Network propagation: Information propagates among the 16 nodes of the information network
  • Rate: Determined by the diffusion coefficient \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107\) — a fast process
  • Mechanism: The spectrum of the Graph Laplacian \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) determines the propagation modes

The diffusion layer is the fastest level of information field evolution — information rapidly homogenizes within the network.

Detailed derivation. The derivation of the diffusion equation is based on continuous-time random walks on a graph. Let \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(i, t)\) be the information density at node \(i\) at time \(t\). The rate at which information flows from node \(i\) to node \(j\) is proportional to the density difference \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(j) - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(i)\) and the transition probability \(T_{ij}\) (elements of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix). Hence:

\[\frac{d\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(i)}{dt} = D_{\mathcal{I}} \sum_{j} T_{ij} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(j) - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(i)) = D_{\mathcal{I}} \sum_{j} (T_{ij} - \delta_{ij}) \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(j) = -D_{\mathcal{I}} \sum_{j} (I - T)_{ij} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(j).\]

Written in vector form: \(\partial_t \rho_{\mathcal{I}} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\).

The diffusion coefficient \(D_{\mathcal{I}} = 1/\tau_I = \Delta\lambda_{\mathcal{I}} = C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}\) is determined by the Born Rule. The physical meaning of \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107\): within one coding step, the diffusion distance of information in the network is approximately \(\sqrt{D_{\mathcal{I}}} \approx 0.327\) nodes. After \(N\) steps, the diffusion distance is \(\sqrt{D_{\mathcal{I}} N} \approx 0.327 \sqrt{N}\) nodes. Reaching network-wide homogenization (covering 16 nodes) requires approximately \((16/0.327)^2 \approx 2400\) steps — this seems to contradict the rapid equilibration \(\tau_I \approx 9.34\), but \(\tau_I\) is the characteristic time scale, while network-wide homogenization is dominated by the slowest mode \(\mu_1\), with the actual homogenization time being approximately \(1/(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1) \approx 1/(0.107 \times 0.369) \approx 25.3\) steps.

Numerical verification. Verify the computation of the diffusion coefficient \(D_{\mathcal{I}}\): \[D_{\mathcal{I}} = C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*} = 0.882 \times \sqrt{0.011484} = 0.882 \times 0.1072 = 0.0945.\]

The simplified value \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107\) in the table uses a reduced \(C\) (taking \(C = 1\) as reference). The two choices do not affect the structure of the diffusion layer — the diffusion coefficient only changes the time scale, not the mode structure.

Comparative discussion. The form of the diffusion layer has a formal correspondence with the classical diffusion equation (Fick’s law) \(\partial_t u = D \nabla^2 u\). The Graph Laplacian \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) is the discrete analog of the continuous Laplace operator \(\nabla^2\). Key difference: in classical diffusion, \(D\) is a phenomenological parameter (determined by the microscopic properties of the medium); in Conjugate Spectral Geometry, \(D_{\mathcal{I}}\) is a geometric parameter (determined by the Born Rule and \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*\)).

Cross-volume references. The definition of the Graph Laplacian \(L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T\) is found in Theorem 5.1.1.01. The Born Rule origin of the diffusion coefficient \(D_{\mathcal{I}} = C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}\) is found in Theorem 3.2.2.01.

1.2 Response Layer

Response of the information field to causal field driving:

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{\text{resp}} = -\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]),\]

where \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = 1/\sqrt{\xi_{\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{C}}}\) is the response rate and \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\) is the equilibrium distribution given the state of the causal field.

Physical meaning of the response layer:

  • Causal driving: The causal field drives the information field through 𝒞-ℐ coupling
  • Rate: Determined by the response rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234\) — an intermediate-speed process
  • Mechanism: The information field tends toward the equilibrium distribution determined by the causal field

The response layer is the intermediate-speed level of information field evolution — the information field adjusts following changes in the causal field.

Detailed derivation. The derivation of the response term is based on linear response theory. The causal field \(\rho_{\mathcal{C}}\) drives the information field through 𝒞-ℐ coupling (\(Z_2\) transposition \(P_{\sigma_2}\), weight \(\theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 0.009\)). Given the causal field \(\rho_{\mathcal{C}}\), the equilibrium distribution of the information field is \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]\). When deviating from equilibrium, the information field relaxes toward equilibrium at rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\).

The response rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = 1/\sqrt{\xi_{\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{C}}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*/\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*}\) is determined by the coding weight ratio. The physical meaning of \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234\): when the information field deviates from equilibrium, it relaxes toward equilibrium at a rate of approximately 0.234, with characteristic relaxation time \(1/\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 4.28\) steps. This time scale lies between the diffusion layer’s \(\tau_I \approx 9.34\) and the causal field’s \(\tau_C \approx 2.18\) — the response layer is the “intermediate-speed” layer.

Numerical verification. Verify the computation of the response rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\): \[\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*/\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*} = \sqrt{0.011484/0.210603} = \sqrt{0.05453} = 0.2335.\]

Comparison of the response rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234\) with the diffusion coefficient \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107\): \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} / D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234 / 0.107 \approx 2.19\). Response (external driving) is approximately 2.2 times faster than diffusion (internal propagation) — the information field responds faster to external causal changes than to internal network propagation.

Comparative discussion. The form of the response layer has a formal correspondence with linear response theory (Kubo formula). In standard statistical mechanics, the response of a system to external perturbations is described by the response function \(\chi(t)\), \(\chi(t) \propto e^{-t/\tau}\) (exponential relaxation). In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the response of the information field to causal field driving is also exponential relaxation, but the relaxation time \(\tau = 1/\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) is determined by the coding weight ratio, not by dynamical correlation functions.

Cross-volume references. The derivation of the slowdown factor \(\xi_{\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{C}} \approx 18.3\) is found in Theorem 5.2.1.01. The \(Z_2\) transposition structure of 𝒞-ℐ coupling is found in Theorem 6.5.1.01.

1.3 Steady-State Layer

Global steady-state condition:

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{\text{ss}} = 0 \implies \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^*].\]

Physical meaning of the steady-state layer:

  • Global equilibrium: The information field reaches the equilibrium distribution determined by the causal field
  • Time scale: Determined by the slowdown factor \(\xi \approx 67.6\) — a slow process
  • Mechanism: Free energy minimization drives the system toward the Gibbs steady state

Detailed derivation. The steady-state layer is the ultimate destination of the three-layer framework. When both diffusion and response are complete, \(\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}} / \partial t = 0\), and the information field reaches the steady state \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\). The steady state is determined by two conditions: 1. Diffusion equilibrium: \(L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 0\) (network uniform distribution) 2. Response equilibrium: \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^*]\) (equilibrium distribution determined by the causal field)

Both conditions are simultaneously satisfied, requiring \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\) to be both uniform and equal to the causal equilibrium distribution. At the Birkhoff Fixed Point \(\sigma^*\), these two conditions coincide — \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \Lambda \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 3 \times 0.011484 \approx 0.0345\), and \(L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 0\) (because \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\) is proportional to the eigenvector \(v_1 = (1,1,\ldots,1)^T\) of \(T\)).

Numerical verification. Verify the steady-state condition: \[\begin{aligned} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* &= (I - T) \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* - T\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 0 \quad (\text{since } T \text{ is doubly stochastic}), \\ \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* &= \Lambda \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 3 \times 0.011484 = 0.03445. \end{aligned}\]

Cross-volume references. The relationship between the steady state \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\) and the Birkhoff Fixed Point \(\sigma^*\) is found in Theorem 3.3.1.01 and Theorem 3.3.2.02.

1.4 Coupling Structure of the Three-Layer Framework

Coupling structure of the three-layer framework:

Level Driving source Rate Time scale
Diffusion layer Internal (network) D_ℐ ≈ 0.107 τ_I ≈ 9.34
Response layer External (causal field) γ_ℐ ≈ 0.234 1/γ ≈ 4.28
Steady-state layer Global (free energy) Γ_geo ≈ 0.023 1/Γ ≈ 43.5

Time-scale separation of the three layers: diffusion is fastest, response is intermediate, steady-state is slowest. This separation guarantees the applicability of the adiabatic approximation.

Detailed derivation. The ordering of the three-layer time scales requires careful analysis. Superficially \(\tau_I \approx 9.34\) (diffusion) > \(1/\gamma \approx 4.28\) (response), but “diffusion is fastest” here refers to the local propagation speed within the diffusion process (\(D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107\) is the diffusion coefficient, not the global equilibration time). The global diffusion equilibration time is determined by \(1/(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1) \approx 25.3\), the response relaxation time by \(1/\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 4.28\), and the steady-state convergence time by \(1/\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 43.5\). Thus the time ordering is:

\[\tau_{\text{resp}} \approx 4.28 < \tau_{\text{diff}} \approx 25.3 < \tau_{\text{ss}} \approx 43.5.\]

Response is fastest (rapid relaxation under external driving), diffusion is intermediate (gradual homogenization within the network), and steady-state is slowest (slow convergence to global equilibrium). This ordering guarantees the hierarchical structure: response completes first, with the information field rapidly following the causal field; then diffusion completes, homogenizing information within the network; finally the steady state is reached, achieving global equilibrium.

Numerical verification. Numerical values of the three characteristic times: \[\begin{aligned} \tau_{\text{resp}} &= 1/\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = 1/0.2335 = 4.28, \\ \tau_{\text{diff}} &= 1/(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1) = 1/(0.0945 \times 0.369) = 1/0.0349 = 28.7, \\ \tau_{\text{ss}} &= 1/\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = 1/0.0231 = 43.3. \end{aligned}\]

Time ordering: \(4.28 < 28.7 < 43.3\) — indeed the hierarchical structure response > diffusion > steady-state.

§2 Unified Equation

Theorem 5.3.2.01 (Unified Dynamical Equation for the Information Field). The three levels combine into:

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]).\]

Steady-state solution: \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = (D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I)^{-1} \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\).

Proof. The unified equation is obtained by linear superposition of the three-layer framework. The diffusion term \(-D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\) describes internal network propagation, and the response term \(-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}})\) describes causal field driving. The two are added to give the total evolution equation. The steady-state condition \(\partial \rho / \partial t = 0\) yields \((D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I)\rho^* = \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho^{\text{eq}}\), whose solution is \(\rho^* = (D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I)^{-1} \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho^{\text{eq}}\). ∎

Detailed derivation. Derivation steps for the unified equation:

  1. Linear superposition. The total evolution rate of the information field is the superposition of the diffusion and response terms. This is the standard assumption of linear response theory — the effects of two independent processes can be linearly superposed. The physical basis for linear superposition is: diffusion and response act on different degrees of freedom (diffusion acts between nodes within the network, response acts on the coupling between the information field as a whole and the causal field), and the two do not interfere.

  2. Steady-state solution. The steady-state condition \(\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}} / \partial t = 0\) gives: \[D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}.\] Rearranged as \((D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I) \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\). Since \(D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I\) is a positive definite matrix (\(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) is positive semidefinite, \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} > 0\)), the inverse exists and is unique, so the steady-state solution exists and is unique.

  3. Explicit form of the steady-state solution. In the eigenbasis of \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\), \((D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I)^{-1} = \sum_k (D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}})^{-1} v_k v_k^T\). Therefore: \[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \sum_k \frac{\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}}{D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}} (v_k^T \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}) v_k.\]

  4. Dominant mode. When \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \gg D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1\) (response-dominated), \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* \approx \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\) — the information field closely follows the causal field. When \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \ll D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1\) (diffusion-dominated), \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* \approx \sum_k \mu_k^{-1} (v_k^T \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}) v_k\) — the information field is primarily controlled by network diffusion. At our position, \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234\), \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 \approx 0.035\), \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} / (D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1) \approx 6.7\) — response-dominated, the information field closely follows the causal field.

Numerical verification. Verify the existence and uniqueness of the steady-state solution. The eigenvalues of the matrix \(M = D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} I\) are \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\). The smallest eigenvalue is \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \cdot 0 + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234 > 0\) — \(M\) is positive definite and invertible.

Comparative discussion. The form of the unified equation has a formal correspondence with the Fokker-Planck equation (describing the evolution of probability densities in stochastic processes). The Fokker-Planck equation \(\partial_t P = \mathcal{L} P\) contains drift and diffusion terms. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the response term \(-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}})\) corresponds to the drift term (tending toward equilibrium), and the diffusion term \(-D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\) corresponds to the diffusion term (network propagation). Key difference: the coefficients of the Fokker-Planck equation are determined by the microscopic dynamics of the stochastic process, whereas the coefficients of the unified equation are determined by coding weights and algebraic connectivity.

Cross-volume references. The derivation of \(D_{\mathcal{I}}\) and \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) in the diffusion term is found in 5.1 §1.1–§1.2. The derivation of \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) in the response term is found in 5.2 §1.2.

2.2 Structure of the Coupling Terms

Coupling term structure of the unified equation:

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t} = \underbrace{-D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}_{\text{internal diffusion}} + \underbrace{-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}_{\text{self-attenuation}} + \underbrace{\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]}_{\text{causal driving}}.\]

Physical meaning of the three terms:

  1. Internal diffusion \(-D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\): information propagates in the network — driven by the Graph Laplacian
  2. Self-attenuation \(-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\): the information field tends toward zero — dissipative effect
  3. Causal driving \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]\): the causal field injects information — source effect

Self-attenuation and causal driving constitute a “leak-source” balance — the steady state of the information field is determined by the balance of the two.

Detailed derivation. The structure of the coupling terms can be understood from the perspective of a “leak-source” model. Rewriting \(-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\) as \(-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}})\) more clearly reveals the “leak-source” structure: \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\) is the deviation of the information field from equilibrium, and \(-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) is the relaxation rate. When \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} > \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\) (information surplus), the system leaks information at rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\); when \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} < \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\) (information deficit), the system absorbs information at rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\).

The internal diffusion term \(-D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\) does not change the total information (\(\text{Tr}(L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho) = 0\)); it only changes the distribution of information among nodes. Therefore, the change in total information of the information field is determined solely by the “leak-source” term:

\[\frac{d}{dt} \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}) = -\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}).\]

Numerical verification. At the steady state \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\), the numerical values of the three terms: \[\begin{aligned} \text{Internal diffusion} &= -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = -0.107 \times 0 = 0, \\ \text{Self-attenuation} &= -\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = -0.234 \times 0.0345 = -0.00807, \\ \text{Causal driving} &= \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}} = 0.234 \times 0.0345 = 0.00807. \end{aligned}\]

Self-attenuation and causal driving exactly cancel — steady-state balance.

2.3 Solutions of the Equation

The evolution solution of the information field is:

\[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* + \sum_{k} c_k e^{-\Lambda_k t} v_k,\]

where \(\Lambda_k = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) is the attenuation rate and \(v_k\) are the Laplacian eigenvectors. The slowest attenuation rate is \(\Lambda_1 = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107 \times 0.370 + 0.234 \approx 0.274\).

Structure of the solution:

  • Steady-state term \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\): long-time limit — the information field reaches equilibrium
  • Transient terms \(\sum c_k e^{-\Lambda_k t} v_k\): decay with time — the process of the information field tending toward equilibrium

The slowest attenuation rate \(\Lambda_1 \approx 0.274\) corresponds to a convergence time of \(\tau_{\text{conv}} = 1/\Lambda_1 \approx 3.6\) — the information field converges to the steady state within approximately 3.6 time units.

Detailed derivation. Formal solution derivation of the equation. The unified equation \(\partial_t \rho = -D L \rho - \gamma (\rho - \rho^{\text{eq}})\) can be written as \(\partial_t (\rho - \rho^*) = -(D L + \gamma I)(\rho - \rho^*)\), where \(\rho^*\) is the steady-state solution. Let \(\delta\rho = \rho - \rho^*\), then \(\partial_t \delta\rho = -(D L + \gamma I) \delta\rho\). In the eigenbasis of \(L\):

\[\delta\rho(t) = \sum_k c_k e^{-(D \mu_k + \gamma) t} v_k.\]

The coefficients \(c_k = \langle v_k, \delta\rho(0) \rangle\) are determined by the initial conditions. Adding the steady-state term yields the complete solution.

The convergence time \(\tau_{\text{conv}} = 1/\Lambda_1\) is determined by the slowest attenuation mode. \(\Lambda_1 = D \mu_1 + \gamma \approx 0.107 \times 0.369 + 0.234 \approx 0.274\), \(\tau_{\text{conv}} \approx 3.65\). This time scale is shorter than the characteristic time \(\tau_I \approx 9.34\) of the information field — because the convergence time is jointly determined by the fastest modes of response and diffusion, not by the slowest diffusion mode.

Numerical verification. At \(t = \tau_{\text{conv}} \approx 3.65\), the deviation from steady state attenuates to \(1/e \approx 0.368\). At \(t = 3\tau_{\text{conv}} \approx 11.0\), it attenuates to \(e^{-3} \approx 0.050\) — 95% convergence.

Cross-volume references. The attenuation rate \(\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = \mu_1 / N_{\text{info}} \approx 0.023\) (Theorem 5.1.2.01) and \(\Lambda_1 \approx 0.274\) are related as follows: \(\Lambda_1\) is the attenuation rate of the unified equation (including the response term), while \(\Gamma_{\text{geo}}\) is the pure diffusion attenuation rate (only the diffusion term). \(\Lambda_1 \gg \Gamma_{\text{geo}}\) indicates that response accelerates convergence.

Detailed derivation. Complete structure of the attenuation rate spectrum. The eigenvalue spectrum of \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) is \(\{0, \mu_1, \mu_1, \mu_3, \ldots, \mu_{15}\}\), where \(\mu_1 = 0.369\) (doubly degenerate, corresponding to the complex conjugate pair), and \(\mu_k \geq \mu_1\) (\(\forall k \geq 3\)). Hence the spectrum of \(\Lambda_k = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) is \(\{\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}, \Lambda_1, \Lambda_1, \Lambda_3, \ldots\}\), where \(\Lambda_1 = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) is the smallest non-zero attenuation rate.

The attenuation rate of mode \(k=0\) (\(\mu_0 = 0\)) is \(\Lambda_0 = \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.234\) — this mode corresponds to the network-wide uniform state, and its attenuation rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) is larger than \(\Lambda_1\). This means the attenuation of the uniform state is faster than that of non-uniform modes — because the response term \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) acts equally on all modes, while the diffusion term \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k\) contributes additional attenuation to non-uniform modes.

Numerical verification. Verify \(\Lambda_0 > \Lambda_1\): \[\Lambda_0 = D_{\mathcal{I}} \times 0 + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = 0.234, \quad \Lambda_1 = 0.107 \times 0.370 + 0.234 = 0.274.\]

\(\Lambda_1 = 0.274 > \Lambda_0 = 0.234\) — non-uniform modes attenuate faster (due to the additional contribution from the diffusion term). This is consistent with intuition: non-uniformity is more rapidly eliminated by diffusion.

§3 Physical Meaning

3.1 Dual Driving of the Information Field

The information field is influenced by two driving sources:

  1. Internal diffusion (\(D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}}\)): information propagates in the network
  2. External response (\(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\)): the information field follows changes in the causal field

A steady state is reached when the two are balanced.

Competition of dual driving:

  • Internal diffusion homogenizes information (tending toward the network uniform state)
  • External response makes information follow the causal field (tending toward causal equilibrium)
  • The balance of the two yields the steady state of the information field — neither completely uniform nor completely following the causal field

Detailed derivation. The competition of dual driving can be understood using a “potential landscape.” Define the effective potential \(V(\rho) = \frac{1}{2} D_{\mathcal{I}} \rho^T L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho + \frac{1}{2} \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} |\rho - \rho^{\text{eq}}|^2\), then the unified equation can be written in gradient flow form: \(\partial_t \rho = -\nabla V(\rho)\). The first term \(\frac{1}{2} D \rho^T L \rho\) attains its minimum at \(\rho \propto (1,1,\ldots,1)^T\) (uniform distribution); the second term \(\frac{1}{2} \gamma |\rho - \rho^{\text{eq}}|^2\) attains its minimum at \(\rho = \rho^{\text{eq}}\). The minima of the two terms are generally different, so the global minimum of \(V(\rho)\) (i.e., the steady state \(\rho^*\)) is a compromise between the two.

At \(\rho^*\), \(\nabla V(\rho^*) = D L \rho^* + \gamma (\rho^* - \rho^{\text{eq}}) = 0\), i.e., \(D L \rho^* = -\gamma (\rho^* - \rho^{\text{eq}})\) — the diffusion “push” balances the response “pull.”

Numerical verification. At the steady state \(\rho^*\), the balance between diffusion push and response pull: \[D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho^* = 0, \quad \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho^* - \rho^{\text{eq}}) = 0.\]

Both terms are simultaneously zero — this is a special property of the Birkhoff Fixed Point \(\sigma^*\): \(\rho^*\) is both a uniform distribution (\(L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho^* = 0\)) and equal to the causal equilibrium distribution (\(\rho^* = \rho^{\text{eq}}\)).

3.2 Information Conservation

At steady state, the total information of the information field is conserved:

\[\frac{d}{dt}\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}) = 0 \implies \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*) = \text{const}.\]

Information is neither created nor destroyed — it is only redistributed within the network.

3.3 Proof of Information Conservation

From the unified equation:

\[\frac{d}{dt}\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}) = -D_{\mathcal{I}} \text{Tr}(L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}) - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}).\]

First term: \(\text{Tr}(L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}) = \text{Tr}((I-T)\rho) = \text{Tr}(\rho) - \text{Tr}(T\rho) = 0\) (since \(T\) is doubly stochastic, \(\text{Tr}(T\rho) = \text{Tr}(\rho)\)).

Second term: at steady state \(\rho = \rho^*\), \(\rho^* = \rho^{\text{eq}}\), so \(\text{Tr}(\rho^* - \rho^{\text{eq}}) = 0\).

Therefore \(d\text{Tr}(\rho)/dt = 0\) — information is conserved. ∎

Detailed derivation. The proof of information conservation can be generalized to non-steady-state situations. At any time \(t\):

\[\frac{d}{dt}\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}) = -\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}).\]

If \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} \neq \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\), then \(\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}})\) can change — the total information of the information field can increase or decrease. But information conservation means: at steady state (\(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}\)), the total information is constant. In non-steady-state, the total information relaxes toward the steady-state value:

\[\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t)) = \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*) + (\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0)) - \text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*)) e^{-\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} t}.\]

The total information tends toward the steady-state value at rate \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) — this is the dynamical process of “leak-source” balance.

Numerical verification. Verify that \(\text{Tr}(T\rho) = \text{Tr}(\rho)\) holds for any \(\rho\). \(T\) is a doubly stochastic matrix, with \(\sum_i T_{ij} = 1\) (column sums equal 1). Therefore: \[\text{Tr}(T\rho) = \sum_i (T\rho)_i = \sum_i \sum_j T_{ij} \rho_j = \sum_j \rho_j \sum_i T_{ij} = \sum_j \rho_j \times 1 = \text{Tr}(\rho).\]

Cross-volume references. The double stochasticity of \(T\) comes from the construction of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix (Theorem 3.3.1.01).

3.4 Relationship between Information Conservation and Attenuation

Information conservation (\(\text{Tr}(\rho) = \text{const}\)) and information attenuation (\(\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023\)) are not contradictory:

  • Information conservation: total information is unchanged — information is redistributed within the network
  • Information attenuation: specific modes attenuate — information flows from higher-order modes to lower-order modes

Information attenuation is “inter-mode transfer” rather than “total quantity reduction” — total information is conserved, but the distribution tends toward uniformity.

Detailed derivation. Mathematical relationship between information conservation and attenuation. Expand \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}\) in the eigenmodes of \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\): \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} = \sum_k c_k v_k\). The total information \(\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}) = c_0\) (only \(v_0\) (uniform mode) contributes to the trace). Information conservation means \(c_0 = \text{const}\). Information attenuation means \(c_k(t) = c_k(0) e^{-\Lambda_k t}\) (\(k \geq 1\)). The two are not contradictory — \(c_0\) is unchanged, while \(c_k\) (\(k \geq 1\)) attenuate.

Physical picture of information attenuation: initially information is concentrated in certain specific modes (e.g., some node has high information density); as time evolves, these modes attenuate and information flows toward the uniform mode. Total information is unchanged, but the “structure” of information is smoothed out — this is the mathematical expression of “forgetting.”

Numerical verification. Mode distribution at \(t = 0\) and \(t = \infty\): \[\begin{aligned} t = 0&: \quad \rho_{\mathcal{I}} = c_0 v_0 + \sum_{k \geq 1} c_k v_k, \\ t = \infty&: \quad \rho_{\mathcal{I}} = c_0 v_0 = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*. \end{aligned}\]

Total information is always \(c_0\), but information in non-uniform modes completely vanishes as \(t \to \infty\).

Comparative discussion. The relationship between information conservation and attenuation is analogous to “unitary evolution vs decoherence” in quantum mechanics. Unitary evolution of a quantum system preserves total probability (\(\text{Tr}(\rho) = 1\)), but decoherence causes off-diagonal elements to attenuate — information flows from “quantum correlations” to “classical probabilities.” In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, information conservation preserves total information, but attenuation causes information to flow from “non-uniform modes” to the “uniform mode” — information changes from “structured” to “structureless.” Both are “information redistribution” rather than “information destruction.”

§4 Three-Field Joint Dynamics

4.1 Three-Field Joint Equations

Extending the information field unified equation to three fields:

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{M}}}{\partial t} = -i[H_{\mathcal{M}}, \rho_{\mathcal{M}}] + \mathcal{D}_T^{(\mathcal{M})}[\rho_{\mathcal{M}}] + \gamma_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{C}}(\rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}] - \rho_{\mathcal{M}}),\]

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{C}}}{\partial t} = -i[H_{\mathcal{C}}, \rho_{\mathcal{C}}] + \mathcal{D}_T^{(\mathcal{C})}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}] + \gamma_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}(\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{M}}] - \rho_{\mathcal{C}}) + \gamma_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}(\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}] - \rho_{\mathcal{C}}),\]

\[\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]).\]

Coupling structure of the three-field equations:

  • ℳ equation: self-evolution + ℳ→𝒞 coupling
  • 𝒞 equation: self-evolution + 𝒞→ℳ coupling + 𝒞→ℐ coupling
  • ℐ equation: self-evolution + ℐ→𝒞 coupling

Note that ℳ and ℐ have no direct coupling — they are relayed through 𝒞.

Detailed derivation. Physical meaning of the terms in the three-field joint equations:

  1. Self-evolution terms: \(-i[H, \rho]\) is the unitary evolution of an isolated sector (for the matter field and causal field). \(-D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}\) is the diffusive evolution of the information field (non-unitary, because the information field is classical-probabilistic).

  2. Dissipative terms: \(\mathcal{D}_T[\rho]\) is the intra-sector dissipation (driven by the intra-sector components of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix \(T\)). For ℳ, \(\mathcal{D}_T^{(\mathcal{M})}\) comes from the identity component \(P_I\) of \(T\) (weight \(\theta_I \approx 0.782\)). For 𝒞, \(\mathcal{D}_T^{(\mathcal{C})}\) comes from the cyclic permutation component \(P_\tau\) of \(T\) (weight \(\theta_\tau \approx 0.209\)).

  3. Coupling terms: \(\gamma_{ij}(\rho_i^{\text{eq}}[\rho_j] - \rho_i)\) is the inter-sector coupling. \(\gamma_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{C}}\) comes from ℳ-𝒞 coupling (cyclic permutation \(P_\tau\)), and \(\gamma_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}\) comes from 𝒞-ℐ coupling (\(Z_2\) transposition \(P_{\sigma_2}\)).

Numerical verification. Hierarchy of three-field coupling strengths: \[\gamma_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{C}} : \gamma_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} : \gamma_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}/\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}} : \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}/\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}} : \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}/\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}} = 1.92 : 0.52 : 0.23.\]

ℳ→𝒞 coupling is strongest (\(\gamma_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{C}} \approx 1.92\)), and 𝒞→ℐ coupling is weakest (\(\gamma_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.23\)). This explains why the information field evolves slowest — its coupling to the causal field is weakest, and the causal field’s driving of it is minimal.

4.2 Origin of the Coupling Terms

The three-field coupling terms come from the three components of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix \(T\):

Coupling Origin Weight Strength
ℳ↔︎𝒞 P_τ (cyclic permutation) θ_τ ≈ 0.209 Moderate
𝒞↔︎ℐ P_σ₂ (transposition) θ_σ₂ ≈ 0.009 Extremely weak
ℳ↔︎ℐ No direct coupling 0 None

ℳ-𝒞 coupling (cyclic permutation) is the strongest inter-sector coupling among the three fields, and 𝒞-ℐ coupling (transposition) is the weakest. This explains why the information field evolves slowest — its direct coupling to the matter field is zero.

Detailed derivation. Algebraic structure of the origin of coupling terms. The three components of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix \(T = \theta_I P_I + \theta_\tau P_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} P_{\sigma_2}\) correspond to three types of permutation operations:

  1. \(P_I\) (identity permutation): weight \(\theta_I \approx 0.782\). This is intra-sector mixing — does not produce inter-sector coupling. The weight of \(P_I\) is largest, indicating that intra-sector dynamics dominate.

  2. \(P_\tau\) (cyclic permutation \(\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{M}\)): weight \(\theta_\tau \approx 0.209\). This is three-field cyclic coupling — produces ℳ↔︎𝒞, 𝒞↔︎ℐ, and ℐ↔︎ℳ couplings. But because the weight of ℐ is extremely small, the ℐ↔︎ℳ coupling is effectively negligible.

  3. \(P_{\sigma_2}\) (\(Z_2\) transposition): weight \(\theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 0.009\). This is the manifestation of Terminal Duality — transposition between the two direct summands of Cl(7). The weight of \(P_{\sigma_2}\) is extremely small (0.009), indicating that the direct physical impact of Terminal Duality is very weak — it is mainly manifested in the internal structure of the information field (\(N_{\text{info}} = 16\)) rather than in coupling strength.

Numerical verification. Quantitative comparison of coupling strengths: \[\theta_I : \theta_\tau : \theta_{\sigma_2} = 0.782 : 0.209 : 0.009 \approx 87 : 23 : 1.\]

Intra-sector mixing (\(\theta_I\)) is approximately 3.6 times the inter-sector coupling (\(\theta_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2}\)) — intra-sector dynamics are far stronger than inter-sector coupling. This is the foundation for the approximate independence of the three fields.

Cross-volume references. The construction of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix \(T\) is found in Theorem 3.3.1.01. The determination of the three component weights \((\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})\) of \(T\) is found in Theorem 6.5.1.01.

4.3 Simplification under the Adiabatic Approximation

Under the adiabatic approximation (\(\xi \gg 1\)), the three-field equations simplify to:

\[\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(t) \approx \rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(t)],\]

\[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(t) \approx \rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t)],\]

\[\frac{d\rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{dt} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}]]).\]

The matter field and causal field are “integrated out,” leaving only the slow dynamics of the information field — this is the information field unified equation of Theorem 5.3.2.01.

Detailed derivation. The simplification process of the adiabatic approximation (Tikhonov’s theorem of singular perturbation theory):

  1. In the three-field equations, \(\epsilon_1 = \tau_M/\tau_I \approx 0.12\) and \(\epsilon_2 = \tau_C/\tau_I \approx 0.23\) are small parameters.

  2. Letting \(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2 \to 0\), the equations for ℳ and 𝒞 degenerate to algebraic equations: \(\rho_{\mathcal{M}} = \rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]\), \(\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = \rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}]\).

  3. Substituting \(\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = \rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}]\) into the equation for \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}\) yields the effective dynamics of \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}\): \[\frac{d\rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{dt} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}]]).\]

  4. Near the Birkhoff Fixed Point \(\sigma^*\), \(\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}] \approx \rho_{\mathcal{C}}^*\) (constant), so \(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}]] \approx \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*\) (constant). This is the unified equation of Theorem 5.3.2.01.

Numerical verification. Precision of the adiabatic approximation: \(\epsilon_1 = 0.12, \epsilon_2 = 0.23\). On the time scale of ℐ, the dynamical error of ℳ and 𝒞 is \(O(\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2) \approx 35\%\). For qualitative structure (such as the existence of the steady state), 35% error is acceptable. For precise quantitative predictions, first-order corrections in \(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2\) are needed.

Cross-volume references. Detailed discussion of the adiabatic approximation is found in 5.2 §3. Standard references on singular perturbation theory (Tikhonov’s theorem) are found in the references of 5.2.

4.4 Self-Consistency of the Unified Equation

Self-consistency of the unified equation requires:

  1. Steady-state existence: \(\rho^*\) satisfies \((D L + \gamma I)\rho^* = \gamma \rho^{\text{eq}}\) — has a unique solution
  2. Steady-state stability: all attenuation rates \(\Lambda_k > 0\) — global convergence
  3. Information conservation: \(\text{Tr}(\rho^*) = \text{const}\) — trace conservation

All three are guaranteed by algebraic structure — the unified equation is self-consistent.

Detailed derivation. Three verifications of self-consistency:

  1. Steady-state existence: The eigenvalues of \(M = D L + \gamma I\) are \(\lambda_M^{(k)} = D \mu_k + \gamma\). The smallest eigenvalue is \(\lambda_M^{(0)} = \gamma > 0\), so \(M\) is invertible, and \(\rho^* = M^{-1} \gamma \rho^{\text{eq}}\) exists and is unique.

  2. Steady-state stability: All \(\Lambda_k = D \mu_k + \gamma > 0\) (\(\mu_k \geq 0\), \(\gamma > 0\)), so \(\delta\rho(t) \to 0\) (\(t \to \infty\)), global convergence.

  3. Information conservation: \(\text{Tr}(\rho^*) = \gamma \text{Tr}(M^{-1} \rho^{\text{eq}})\). At the Birkhoff Fixed Point, \(\rho^* = \rho^{\text{eq}}\), \(\text{Tr}(\rho^*) = \text{Tr}(\rho^{\text{eq}})\) — conserved.

Numerical verification. Numerical verification of self-consistency: \[\begin{aligned} M &= D L + \gamma I, \quad \det(M) = \prod_k (D \mu_k + \gamma) > 0, \\ \Lambda_{\min} &= \gamma = 0.234 > 0, \\ \text{Tr}(\rho^*) &= 0.552 \quad (\text{at } \rho^* = \rho^{\text{eq}}). \end{aligned}\]

§5 Landscape Dependence of the Unified Equation

5.1 Variation of Parameters with Landscape Position

The three parameters \(D_{\mathcal{I}}, \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}, \mu_1\) of the unified equation all vary with landscape position:

γ_ℐ 1 ≈ 0.234 → 0
μ₁ ≈ 1.732 ≈ 0.370 → 0

Detailed derivation. Algebraic origins of the landscape dependence of the three parameters:

  1. \(D_{\mathcal{I}} = C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}\): \(D_{\mathcal{I}}\) decreases as \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*\) decreases. At the symmetric point, \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 1/3\), \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.577 C\); at the extreme point, \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* \to 0\), \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0\). \(D_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0\) means information field diffusion stops — information cannot propagate in the network.

  2. \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*/\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*}\): \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}\) decreases as \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*\) decreases and \(\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*\) increases. At the symmetric point, \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^* = 1/3\), \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = 1\); at the extreme point, \(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* \to 0\), \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0\). \(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0\) means the information field no longer responds to the causal field — the information field and causal field decouple.

  3. \(\mu_1 = |1 - \lambda_2|\): \(\mu_1\) decreases with the degree to which the fixed point deviates from the symmetric point. At the symmetric point, \(\lambda_2 = e^{2\pi i/3}\), \(\mu_1 = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732\); at the extreme point, \(\lambda_2 \to 1\), \(\mu_1 \to 0\). \(\mu_1 \to 0\) means the information network is disconnected.

Numerical verification. Behavior of the three parameters at the extreme point \((S \to 968)\): \[D_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0, \quad \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0, \quad \mu_1 \to 0.\]

All three parameters tend to zero — all dynamics of the unified equation freeze. This is the Conjugate Spectral Geometry counterpart of “heat death.”

Hence \(\tau_{\text{conv}} = 1/\Lambda_1\) is smallest at the symmetric point (approximately 0.76) and tends to infinity at the extreme point.

Physical meaning of the convergence time tending to infinity: at the extreme point, all dynamical processes of the information field freeze — diffusion stops (\(D_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0\)), response stops (\(\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \to 0\)), and the network is disconnected (\(\mu_1 \to 0\)). The information field cannot reach a steady state — this is a “nonequilibrium frozen state.”

5.3 Unified Equation and Algebraic Action

The steady state \(\rho^*\) of the unified equation coincides with the minimum of the Algebraic Action — both are determined by \(\sigma^*\). This guarantees the unity of the dynamical steady state and the geometric extremum.

Detailed derivation. Unity of the unified equation and the Algebraic Action. The steady state \(\rho^*\) of the unified equation is determined by \((D L + \gamma I)\rho^* = \gamma \rho^{\text{eq}}\). At the Birkhoff Fixed Point \(\sigma^*\), \(\rho^* = \rho^{\text{eq}} = \Lambda \sigma^*\). Hence the position of \(\rho^*\) is completely determined by \(\sigma^*\).

On the other hand, the Algebraic Action \(S(\sigma)\) attains its minimum at \(\sigma^*\). Thus the dynamical steady state (\(\rho^*\)) and the geometric extremum (\(\sigma^*\)) are attained at the same point — this is the manifestation of “dynamics-geometry unity.”

This unity is not accidental but an inevitable consequence of the Birkhoff Fixed Point Theorem: \(T\sigma^* = \sigma^*\) simultaneously guarantees that \(S(\sigma)\) attains its minimum at \(\sigma^*\) (geometric extremum) and that \(\partial \rho / \partial t = 0\) holds at \(\rho^* = \Lambda \sigma^*\) (dynamical steady state).

Numerical verification. Verify the relationship between \(\rho^*\) and \(\sigma^*\): \[\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = \Lambda \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 3 \times 0.011484 = 0.03445.\]

At the steady state \(\rho^* = \rho^{\text{eq}}\), \(\partial \rho / \partial t = 0\) — dynamical steady state. Simultaneously \(S(\sigma^*) = 137\) is the minimum — geometric extremum. The two are unified at \(\sigma^*\).
