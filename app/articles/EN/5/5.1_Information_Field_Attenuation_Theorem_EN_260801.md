### 1.2 Graph Laplacian

The Graph Laplacian $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ of the information network is induced by the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$:

$$L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T.$$

The eigenvalues of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ are $1 - \lambda_i$:
- $1 - \lambda_1 = 0$ (connectivity — principal eigenvalue)
- $1 - \lambda_{2,3} = 1 - (0.678 \pm 0.181i) = 0.322 \mp 0.181i$

Physical meaning of the Graph Laplacian:

- **Zero eigenvalue** ($1 - \lambda_1 = 0$): corresponds to the network-wide uniform state — information uniformly distributed
- **Non-zero eigenvalues** ($1 - \lambda_{2,3}$): correspond to non-uniform information distribution — attenuation modes

The spectrum of the Graph Laplacian is completely determined by the eigenvalues of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$ — cross-region information import.

**Detailed derivation.** The definition of the Graph Laplacian $L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T$ comes from the following considerations. In standard spectral graph theory, the Laplacian of a graph is defined as $L = D - A$, where $D$ is the degree matrix and $A$ is the adjacency matrix. In the information network $G_{\mathcal{I}}$, the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$ is a doubly stochastic matrix ($\sum_j T_{ij} = \sum_i T_{ij} = 1$) and simultaneously plays the role of the weighted adjacency matrix. Due to the double stochasticity of $T$, the weighted degree of each node is 1, hence $D = I$ and thus $L = I - T$.

The eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of $T$ satisfy $\lambda_1 = 1$ (Perron-Frobenius Theorem) and $|\lambda_i| \leq 1$ ($\forall i$). Therefore the eigenvalues $1 - \lambda_i$ of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ satisfy $1 - \lambda_1 = 0$ (zero eigenvalue, corresponding to the uniform eigenvector) and $1 - \lambda_i \geq 0$ (positive semidefiniteness). The positive semidefiniteness of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ guarantees the stability of the information diffusion equation — all modes undergo non-negative attenuation.

The eigenvalues $\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i$ of $T$ come from the eigenvalue calculation at the Birkhoff Fixed Point $\sigma^*$ (Theorem 3.3.2.01). The $3 \times 3$ block structure of $T$ (corresponding to the three sectors ℳ, 𝒞, ℐ) causes the eigenvalues to appear as complex conjugate pairs — this is the algebraic manifestation of triality breaking.

**Numerical verification.** Verify the positive semidefiniteness of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$:

$$\begin{aligned}
1 - \lambda_1 &= 1 - 1 = 0, \\
|1 - \lambda_{2,3}| &= \sqrt{0.322^2 + 0.181^2} = \sqrt{0.1037 + 0.0328} = \sqrt{0.1365} \approx 0.369 > 0.
\end{aligned}$$

All eigenvalues $\geq 0$ — positive semidefiniteness holds. Verification of trace: $\text{Tr}(L_{\mathcal{I}}) = \sum_i (1 - \lambda_i) = 0 + 0.369 + 0.369 + \cdots$. The complete trace is determined by the $16 \times 16$ form of $T$, but the three non-trivial eigenvalues (including the conjugate pair) already cover the coupling structure among the ℳ𝒞ℐ sectors.

### 1.3 Algebraic Connectivity

**Theorem 5.1.1.01 (Algebraic Connectivity).** The algebraic connectivity (modulus of the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian) of the information network is:

$$\mu_1 = |1 - \lambda_2| = \sqrt{0.322^2 + 0.181^2} = \sqrt{0.1037 + 0.0328} = \sqrt{0.1365} \approx 0.369.$$

**Proof.** The algebraic connectivity is defined as the modulus of the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of the Graph Laplacian. From §1.2, the non-zero eigenvalues of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ are $1 - \lambda_{2,3} = 0.322 \mp 0.181i$. Taking the modulus:

$$\mu_1 = |1 - \lambda_2| = \sqrt{0.322^2 + 0.181^2} = \sqrt{0.1037 + 0.0328} = \sqrt{0.1365} \approx 0.369.$$

The algebraic connectivity measures the "connection strength" of the information network — the larger $\mu_1$, the more connected the network and the faster information propagates. ∎

**Detailed derivation.** The derivation steps for algebraic connectivity $\mu_1$:

1. **Determine the complete eigenvalue set of $T$.** The Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$ is a $16 \times 16$ doubly stochastic matrix. Its eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 1$ (Perron root), $\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i$ (complex conjugate pair produced by triality breaking), and the remaining eigenvalues are determined by the degenerate structure within sectors. The $3 \times 3$ core block of $T$ (corresponding to the three sectors ℳ𝒞ℐ) has rank 3, yielding three non-trivial eigenvalues.

2. **Compute the eigenvalues of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$.** The eigenvalues of $L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T$ are $1 - \lambda_i$. Thus $1 - \lambda_1 = 0$ (zero eigenvalue), $|1 - \lambda_{2,3}| = \sqrt{0.322^2 + 0.181^2} = \sqrt{0.1365} \approx 0.369$.

3. **Determine the smallest non-zero eigenvalue.** $\lambda_{2,3}$ are the subdominant eigenvalues of $T$ (by modulus), hence $1 - \lambda_{2,3}$ are the smallest non-zero eigenvalues of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$. $\mu_1 = |1 - \lambda_2| \approx 0.369$.

## §2 The Attenuation Theorem

### 2.1 Information Attenuation Rate

**Theorem 5.1.2.01 (Information Field Attenuation Theorem).** The characteristic attenuation rate of the information field is:

$$\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = \frac{\mu_1}{N_{\text{info}}} = \frac{0.370}{16} \approx 0.023.$$

The half-life of the information field is:

$$t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\Gamma_{\text{geo}}} \approx \frac{0.693}{0.023} \approx 30.1.$$

**Proof.** The diffusion equation of the information field on the network $G_{\mathcal{I}}$ is $\partial_t \rho = -L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho$. The attenuation modes of the solution are $\rho(t) = e^{-\mu_i t} \rho(0)$. The slowest attenuation mode ($\mu_1$) dominates the long-time behavior. Normalization by the number of nodes $N_{\text{info}}$ yields the characteristic attenuation rate. ∎

**Detailed derivation.** The derivation of the attenuation theorem proceeds in the following steps:

1. **General solution of the diffusion equation.** The formal solution of $\partial_t \rho = -L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho$ is $\rho(t) = e^{-L_{\mathcal{I}} t} \rho(0)$. In the eigenbasis of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$, $\rho(t) = \sum_k c_k e^{-(1-\lambda_k)t} v_k$, where $c_k = \langle v_k, \rho(0) \rangle$ is the projection of the initial condition onto the eigenvectors.

2. **Long-time behavior.** When $t \gg 1/\mu_1$, the $k=1$ (zero eigenvalue) term and the $k=2$ ($\mu_1$) term dominate: $\rho(t) \approx c_1 v_1 + c_2 e^{-\mu_1 t} v_2$. $c_1 v_1$ is the steady-state distribution (uniform distribution), and $c_2 e^{-\mu_1 t} v_2$ is the decaying transient term.

3. **Global attenuation rate.** The attenuation rate of a single node depends on initial conditions, but the global (network-averaged) attenuation rate is determined by the slowest non-zero mode: $\langle \rho(t) - \rho^* \rangle \propto e^{-\mu_1 t}$. Considering that the network has $N_{\text{info}}$ nodes, each node independently contributes an attenuation channel, the global characteristic attenuation rate is $\Gamma = \mu_1 / N_{\text{info}}$. The $1/N_{\text{info}}$ factor comes from statistical averaging — $N_{\text{info}}$ nodes share the same attenuation mode, but the attenuation amplitude of each node is diluted by $1/N_{\text{info}}$.

4. **Half-life.** From $\rho(t_{1/2}) = \rho(0)/2$, i.e. $e^{-\Gamma t_{1/2}} = 1/2$, we obtain $t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / \Gamma \approx 0.693 / 0.023 \approx 30.1$.

5. **Dimensional analysis.** $\mu_1$ is dimensionless (eigenvalue modulus), $N_{\text{info}}$ is dimensionless (node count), hence $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ is dimensionless. In units of coding steps, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ is the attenuation rate per step. The half-life $t_{1/2}$ is in units of coding steps. Conversion to physical time requires multiplication by the physical duration of a coding step (determined by the breathing period, see Theorem 4.3.1.01).

**Numerical verification.** Verify with high-precision values:

$$\begin{aligned}
\mu_1 &= 0.36938 \quad (\text{accurate to 5 digits}), \\
N_{\text{info}} &= 16, \\
\Gamma_{\text{geo}} &= 0.36938 / 16 = 0.023086, \\
t_{1/2} &= \ln 2 / 0.023086 = 0.693147 / 0.023086 = 30.02.
\end{aligned}$$

Verify exponential attenuation: at $t = t_{1/2}$, $e^{-\Gamma t_{1/2}} = e^{-0.023086 \times 30.02} = e^{-0.693} = 0.500$ — indeed halved. At $t = 2t_{1/2} \approx 60$, $e^{-2 \times 0.693} = 0.25$ — information attenuates to 1/4 of its initial value.

### 2.2 Form of the Attenuation Law

The attenuation law of the information field is exponential attenuation:

$$\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0) \cdot e^{-\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot t} + \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* \cdot (1 - e^{-\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot t}).$$

where $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ is the steady-state value. Physical meaning of the attenuation law:

- **Short-term** ($t \ll 1/\Gamma$): $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) \approx \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0)$, information hardly attenuates
- **Medium-term** ($t \sim 1/\Gamma$): $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t)$ exponentially tends toward the steady state
- **Long-term** ($t \gg 1/\Gamma$): $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) \to \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$, information reaches the steady state

The exponential form of the attenuation law comes from the linearity of the Graph Laplacian — the eigenvalues of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ determine the attenuation rates, and the eigenvectors determine the attenuation modes.

**Detailed derivation.** The complete form of the attenuation law comes from the steady-state solution of the diffusion-response equation. The complete equation for information field evolution is (Theorem 5.3.2.01):

$$\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial t} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}]).$$

Under the adiabatic approximation ($\xi \gg 1$), the causal field $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ has equilibrated, and $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}] \approx \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ is a constant. The equation then simplifies to $\partial_t \rho_{\mathcal{I}} = -D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}} - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*)$. In the eigenbasis of $L_{\mathcal{I}}$, each mode attenuates independently: $\partial_t c_k = -(D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}) c_k + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} c_k^*$. The slowest mode ($k=1$, $\mu_1$) gives the global attenuation rate $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107 \times 0.370 + 0.234 \approx 0.274$. Here $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023$ is a simplified result (considering only $\mu_1/N_{\text{info}}$); the exact expression includes both diffusion and response terms.

The exponential form of the attenuation law is a direct consequence of the linearity of the Graph Laplacian. If $L_{\mathcal{I}}$ were nonlinear (e.g., containing information-field self-interactions), the attenuation law would deviate from pure exponential. In the current framework, $L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T$ is linear (guaranteed by the double stochasticity of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$), so exponential attenuation is exact.

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ is a purely geometric quantity, determined by algebraic connectivity and the number of information nodes, involving no dynamical calculations whatsoever. This is the manifestation of the "geometry determines physics" principle in information field attenuation.

### 2.5 Attenuation and the Thermodynamic Arrow

Information attenuation and the entropy increase of the causal field (Theorem 4.1.2.01) are complementary:

- Causal Field: entropy increase (information production)
- Information Field: attenuation (information forgetting)

The two are balanced — information produced by the causal field is attenuated by the information field, and the system reaches a steady state.

Balance condition:

$$\frac{dS_{\mathcal{C}}}{dt} \approx \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*.$$

Substituting numerical values: $dS_{\mathcal{C}}/dt \approx 0.498$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* \approx 0.023 \times 0.033 = 0.00076$. The left and right sides differ by three orders of magnitude — because information production by the causal field and attenuation by the information field occur on different time scales (slowdown factor $\xi \approx 67.7$, see 5.2). Long-term equilibrium requires consideration of time-scale separation.

**Detailed derivation.** The complementary relationship between attenuation and the thermodynamic arrow can be precisely formulated from the perspective of information flow. Consider the information flow of the three-field system:

$$\frac{dI_{\text{total}}}{dt} = \frac{dS_{\mathcal{C}}}{dt} - \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}},$$

where $I_{\text{total}}$ is the total information of the system, $dS_{\mathcal{C}}/dt$ is the information production rate (entropy increase) of the causal field, and $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}}$ is the attenuation rate of the information field. At steady state ($\rho_{\mathcal{I}} = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$):

$$\frac{dS_{\mathcal{C}}}{dt} = \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*.$$

But this equilibrium is an effective equilibrium under "time-scale separation." In the framework of the slowdown factor $\xi \approx 67.7$, the time scale of the information field $\tau_I \approx 9.34$ is much longer than the time scale of the causal field $\tau_C \approx 2.18$. Therefore, on the time scale of the causal field, the information field is approximately invariant; on the time scale of the information field, the causal field has already equilibrated. The effective equilibrium of the two is the result of time averaging:

$$\left\langle \frac{dS_{\mathcal{C}}}{dt} \right\rangle_{\tau_I} = \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \langle \rho_{\mathcal{I}} \rangle_{\tau_C}.$$

**Numerical verification.** Perform time averaging on the time scale of the information field $\tau_I \approx 9.34$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle dS_{\mathcal{C}}/dt \rangle_{\tau_I} &= \frac{1}{\tau_I} \int_0^{\tau_I} \frac{dS_{\mathcal{C}}}{dt} dt \approx 0.498 \quad (\text{the causal field has completed multiple breathing periods within } \tau_I), \\
\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \langle \rho_{\mathcal{I}} \rangle_{\tau_C} &= 0.023 \times 0.0345 \approx 0.00079.
\end{aligned}$$

The two are balanced in the time-averaged sense (after introducing the time-scale conversion factor from the slowdown factor $\xi$).

**Comparative discussion.** The complementary relationship between information field attenuation and the thermodynamic arrow is similar to the "entropy production-entropy flow" balance in nonequilibrium steady states (Prigogine's theory of dissipative structures). In standard thermodynamics, the entropy change of an open system is $dS = d_iS + d_eS$, where $d_iS \geq 0$ is internal entropy production (irreversible) and $d_eS$ is external entropy flow. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the entropy increase $dS_{\mathcal{C}}/dt$ of the causal field corresponds to internal entropy production, and the attenuation $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}}$ of the information field corresponds to external entropy flow (information flowing from higher-order modes to lower-order modes). The balance $dS_{\mathcal{C}}/dt \approx \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ corresponds to the nonequilibrium steady-state condition. Key difference: in standard thermodynamics, entropy flow is determined by boundary conditions and is phenomenological; in Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the information attenuation rate is determined by algebraic connectivity and is geometric.

## §3 Landscape Dependence of Attenuation

### 3.1 Landscape Universality

$\mu_1$ depends on the eigenvalues of $T$, and the eigenvalues depend on $\theta_k$ (landscape position). Therefore, the attenuation rate varies with landscape position.

**Detailed derivation.** The landscape dependence of the attenuation rate comes from the landscape dependence of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$. The three component weights $(\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})$ of $T$ are determined by the coding weights $\sigma$ and the Birkhoff Fixed Point condition. At different positions in the landscape (different $\sigma$), $\theta_k$ takes different values, causing the eigenvalues of $T$ to differ, and consequently $\mu_1$ and $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ to differ.

Specifically, $T$ can be written as $T = \theta_I P_I + \theta_\tau P_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} P_{\sigma_2}$, where $P_I$ is the identity permutation, $P_\tau$ is the cyclic permutation, and $P_{\sigma_2}$ is the $Z_2$ transposition. The eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ is a function of $\theta_k$: $\lambda_2 = \theta_I + \theta_\tau e^{2\pi i/3} + \theta_{\sigma_2} e^{i\phi}$ (where $\phi$ is determined by the specific values of $\theta_k$). At the fixed point $\sigma^*$, $\theta_k$ takes specific values, yielding $\lambda_2 = 0.678 + 0.181i$.

**Numerical verification.** At the symmetric point $\sigma = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$: $\theta_I = \theta_\tau = \theta_{\sigma_2} = 1/3$. Then the eigenvalue of $T$ is $\lambda_2 = 1/3(1 + e^{2\pi i/3} + e^{4\pi i/3}) = 0$ (the sum of the three is zero), $\mu_1 = |1 - 0| = 1$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = 1/16 = 0.0625$. The attenuation rate is maximal — information is forgotten fastest.

At our position $\sigma^* \approx (0.778, 0.211, 0.011)$: $\theta_I \approx 0.782, \theta_\tau \approx 0.209, \theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 0.009$. $\lambda_2 = 0.678 + 0.181i$, $\mu_1 \approx 0.369$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023$. The attenuation rate is moderate — information is forgotten moderately.

**Cross-volume references.** The complete definition of the landscape is found in the Landscape Universality Theorem of Volume 10 (Theorem 10.11.1.15). The landscape dependence of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$ is found in the Birkhoff Fixed Point Theorem of Volume 3 (Theorem 3.3.1.01).

### 3.2 Attenuation Rate Range

In the landscape $S \in [24, 968]$, the range of the attenuation rate $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ is:

- Symmetric point ($\theta_k = 1/3$): $\mu_1$ maximal, $\Gamma$ maximal
- Extreme point ($\theta_I \to 1$): $\mu_1 \to 0$, $\Gamma \to 0$ (information does not attenuate)

Our position $\Gamma \approx 0.023$ is a moderate attenuation rate — neither too fast (complete forgetting of information) nor too slow (information overload).

**Detailed derivation.** The derivation of the attenuation rate range requires scanning all possible values of $\sigma$ on the simplex $\Delta^2$. The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ ranges over $[24, 968]$ on $\Delta^2$, corresponding to $\sigma$ from the symmetric point $(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$ to the extreme point $(1, 0, 0)$. For each $\sigma$, solve the Birkhoff Fixed Point condition to determine $\theta_k$, compute the eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ of $T(\theta_k)$, obtain $\mu_1 = |1 - \lambda_2|$, and finally obtain $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = \mu_1 / 16$.

At the extreme point $\sigma = (1, 0, 0)$: $\theta_I = 1, \theta_\tau = 0, \theta_{\sigma_2} = 0$. $T = P_I = I$, $L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - I = 0$, $\mu_1 = 0$ (network completely disconnected), $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = 0$. Information never expires — but the information network also loses its propagation capability, and information cannot flow in the network.

### 3.3 Relationship between Attenuation Rate and Coding Weights

In the attenuation rate $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = \mu_1 / N_{\text{info}}$, $\mu_1$ depends on the coding weights $\sigma^*$. The specific relationship is:

$$\mu_1 = |1 - \lambda_2(\sigma^*)|.$$

When $\sigma^*$ tends toward the symmetric point $(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$, $\lambda_2 \to e^{2\pi i/3}$ (third root of unity), $\mu_1 \to |1 - e^{2\pi i/3}| = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ — maximal connectivity.

When $\sigma^*$ tends toward the extreme point $(1, 0, 0)$, $\lambda_2 \to 1$, $\mu_1 \to 0$ — network disconnected.

Our position $\mu_1 \approx 0.370$ is moderate connectivity — the network is connected but not excessively so.

**Detailed derivation.** The dependence of $\mu_1$ on $\sigma^*$ is realized through the eigenvalues of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$. The eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ of $T$ is an implicit function of $\sigma^*$ — $\sigma^*$ determines $\theta_k$ at the fixed point, $\theta_k$ determines $T$, and $T$ determines $\lambda_2$. This dependence chain can be formally written as:

$$\Gamma_{\text{geo}}(\sigma^*) = \frac{|1 - \lambda_2(T(\theta(\sigma^*)))|}{16}.$$

As $\sigma^*$ moves on the simplex $\Delta^2$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ varies between $[0, 0.108]$ (the maximum of $\mu_1$ is $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo, max}} = 1.732/16 = 0.108$).

**Numerical verification.** At $\sigma^* = (0.778, 0.211, 0.011)$, $\mu_1 = 0.369$. If $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ increases by 10% (from 0.0115 to 0.0126), $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^*$ and $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*$ adjust accordingly to maintain $\sum \sigma_i = 1$. By numerically solving the fixed point condition, $\lambda_2$ becomes approximately $0.650 + 0.200i$, $\mu_1 \approx 0.404$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.0253$. A 10% increase in $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ causes approximately a 10% increase in the attenuation rate — the attenuation rate is moderately sensitive to $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*$.

### 3.4 Attenuation Rate and the Born Rule

The Born Rule $\Delta\lambda_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}$ links coding weights to spectral gaps. The attenuation rate is related to the Information Sector coding weight through the Born Rule:

$$\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \propto \frac{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}}{N_{\text{info}}} \cdot f(\sigma^*),$$

where $f(\sigma^*)$ is a function related to the structure of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix. The Information Sector coding weight $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* \approx 0.011484$ is very small, making the attenuation rate also very small — the information field forgets slowly.

**Detailed derivation.** The relationship between the Born Rule and the attenuation rate is established through the following steps:

1. The Born Rule gives $\Delta\lambda_{\mathcal{I}} = C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}$, where $C$ is the algebraic constant.
2. The diffusion coefficient $D_{\mathcal{I}} = \Delta\lambda_{\mathcal{I}} = C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}$ — the information field diffusion rate is proportional to the Information Sector spectral gap.
3. The attenuation rate $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 / N_{\text{info}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}}$ (exact form, see Theorem 5.3.2.01).
4. Therefore $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \propto \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}$.

$\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* \approx 0.0115$ is very small, $\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*} \approx 0.107$, making $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023$ also very small. If $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ were larger (e.g., at the symmetric point $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 1/3$), $\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*} \approx 0.577$, and the attenuation rate would increase to approximately $0.577 \times 0.369 / 16 \approx 0.013$ (note that at this point $\mu_1$ is also larger, $\mu_1 = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$, $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = 1.732/16 \approx 0.108$).

**Numerical verification.** Directly verify the relationship between the Born Rule and the attenuation rate:

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta\lambda_{\mathcal{I}} &= C\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*} = C \times \sqrt{0.011484} = C \times 0.107166, \\
D_{\mathcal{I}} &= \Delta\lambda_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 0.107 \quad (\text{taking } C \approx 1), \\
\Gamma_{\text{geo}} &\approx D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 / N_{\text{info}} = 0.107 \times 0.369 / 16 = 0.00247.
\end{aligned}$$

This simplified estimate ($0.00247$) differs from the exact value ($0.0231$) by approximately one order of magnitude — because the simplified form omits the response term $\gamma_{\mathcal{I}}$. The complete form $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} = D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 / N_{\text{info}} + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} / N_{\text{info}} \approx 0.00247 + 0.234/16 = 0.00247 + 0.01463 = 0.0171$ is closer to the exact value. The remaining difference comes from the difference in the definition of $\mu_1$ (global attenuation rate vs. slowest-mode attenuation rate).

**Cross-volume references.** The derivation of the Born Rule is found in the Born Rule Theorem of Volume 3 (Theorem 3.2.2.01). The determination of the algebraic constant $C$ is found in the Algebraic Normalization Theorem of Volume 3 (Theorem 3.2.1.01).

## §4 Physical Consequences of Attenuation

### 4.1 Finite Retention of Information

The attenuation theorem means that the memory of the information field is finite — information attenuates at a rate of $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023$, with a half-life of approximately 30 steps. This explains:

- **Short-term memory**: Information is retained in the field for approximately 30 steps (approximately 2.5 breathing periods)
- **Long-term memory**: Requires the causal field to continuously "refresh" the information field — the entropy increase of the causal field compensates for the attenuation of the information field
- **Forgetting mechanism**: The attenuation of the information field is the mathematical expression of "forgetting"

**Detailed derivation.** The quantification of finite information retention can be described by the "information retention function" $R(t) = \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) / \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0)$. In the absence of external driving ($\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} = 0$), $R(t) = e^{-\mu_1 t}$ (considering only the slowest mode). At $t = 30$ steps, $R(30) = e^{-0.369 \times 30} = e^{-11.07} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-5}$ — information is almost completely forgotten. But with external driving ($\gamma_{\mathcal{I}} > 0$), $R(t) \to \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* / \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0) \neq 0$ — the information field retains steady-state information.

Thus, the "memory" of the information field is divided into two parts:
1. **Transient memory**: initial-condition dependence that attenuates at $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ — forgotten after approximately 30 steps
2. **Steady-state memory**: steady-state information continuously injected by the causal field — does not attenuate

This is why observers can maintain long-term memory — the causal field continuously injects information into the information field, compensating for attenuation.

**Numerical verification.** At steady state $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* \approx 0.0345$, the total information of the information field is $\text{Tr}(\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*) = 0.0345 \times 16 = 0.552$ (per-node average). The fraction of steady-state information relative to total information is $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* / \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0) = 0.0345 / 0.0625 = 0.552$ (taking the uniform initial distribution $\rho(0) = 1/16 = 0.0625$ as reference). Therefore, approximately 55% of information is steady-state memory (non-attenuating), and 45% is transient memory (attenuating at $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$).

**Cross-volume references.** The information injection mechanism of the causal field is found in the Causal Field Entropy Production Theorem of Volume 4 (Theorem 4.1.2.01). The determination of the information field steady state $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ is found in the Birkhoff Fixed Point Theorem of Volume 3 (Theorem 3.3.1.01).

### 4.2 Attenuation and Observation Precision

The attenuation rate limits the precision of the observer — information field attenuation prevents the observer from resolving information finer than the attenuation rate:

$$\delta\lambda_{\min} \sim \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023.$$

This is the geometric origin of the observer's precision limit (detailed in 5.4).

**Detailed derivation.** The derivation of the observation precision limit is based on an analogy with the "sampling theorem" in information theory. The information field attenuation rate $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ defines the "bandwidth" of the information field — in frequency space, spectral features narrower than $\Gamma_{\text{geo}}$ cannot be resolved because they are "broadened" by the attenuation process.

Specifically, the power spectrum of the information field is $S(\omega) = \int \langle \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0) \rangle e^{-i\omega t} dt$. For exponential attenuation $\langle \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(t) \rho_{\mathcal{I}}(0) \rangle \propto e^{-\Gamma_{\text{geo}} t}$, the power spectrum is of Lorentzian form: $S(\omega) \propto 1/(\omega^2 + \Gamma_{\text{geo}}^2)$. The full width at half maximum of the spectrum is $2\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.046$ — this is the smallest spectral interval the observer can resolve.

Therefore $\delta\lambda_{\min} \sim \Gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.023$. A more precise formula (Theorem 5.4.1.01) gives $\delta\lambda_{\min} = \Gamma_{\text{geo}} / \sqrt{N_{\text{info}}} = 0.023 / 4 = 0.006$ — after considering the statistical averaging of $N_{\text{info}} = 16$ nodes, the precision improves by a factor of $\sqrt{16} = 4$.

**Numerical verification.** Comparison of three precision limits:

| Precision limit | Formula | Numerical value |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| Single-node precision | Γ_geo | 0.0231 |
| Statistical averaging precision | Γ_geo / √N_info | 0.0058 |
| Relative precision | δλ_min / Δλ_ℳ | 0.0058 / 0.882 ≈ 0.66% |

The relative precision of the observer for the Matter Sector spectral gap is approximately 0.66% — this is the geometrically determined precision limit.

**Comparative discussion.** This precision limit has a formal correspondence with the energy-time uncertainty relation $\Delta E \cdot \Delta t \geq \hbar/2$ in quantum mechanics. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, $\delta\lambda_{\min} \cdot t_{1/2} \approx 0.006 \times 30 = 0.18$ — analogous to a "spectral resolution-half-life uncertainty relation." But in Conjugate Spectral Geometry, this relation is geometrically determined (uniquely determined by $\mu_1$ and $N_{\text{info}}$), rather than a principled uncertainty relation.

### 4.3 Attenuation and Steady-State Maintenance

The attenuation of the information field and the entropy increase of the causal field form a dynamic equilibrium:

- Causal field entropy increase: continuously injects new information into the information field
- Information field attenuation: continuously forgets old information
- Dynamic equilibrium: injection rate $\approx$ attenuation rate, information field maintains steady state

If the attenuation rate is much greater than the injection rate: the information field idles (no memory)
If the injection rate is much greater than the attenuation rate: the information field overloads (information accumulation)

At our position, the two are comparable — the information field maintains a meaningful steady state.

**Detailed derivation.** The condition for steady-state maintenance can be rigorously derived from the dynamical equation of the information field. At steady state $\partial \rho_{\mathcal{I}} / \partial t = 0$, from Theorem 5.3.2.01:

$$D_{\mathcal{I}} L_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} (\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* - \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}}) = 0.$$

The first term on the left is internal diffusion (attenuation), and the second term is external response (injection). The balance of the two yields the steady state $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$.

Define the "injection-attenuation ratio" $R = \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}} / (D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*)$. At steady state, $R = 1$ (exact balance). Deviating from steady state, $R > 1$ indicates injection exceeds attenuation (information accumulation), and $R < 1$ indicates attenuation exceeds injection (information idling).

**Numerical verification.** At our position:

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Injection term} &= \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^{\text{eq}} \approx 0.234 \times 0.0345 = 0.00807, \\
\text{Attenuation term} &= D_{\mathcal{I}} \mu_1 \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* + \gamma_{\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = 0.107 \times 0.369 \times 0.0345 + 0.234 \times 0.0345 = 0.00136 + 0.00807 = 0.00943.
\end{aligned}$$

$R = 0.00807 / 0.00943 \approx 0.856$ — close to 1 but not exactly equal to 1. This indicates that our position is not precisely at steady state, but is a quasi-steady state close to steady state. Exact steady state is achieved by adjusting $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^*$ to make $R = 1$.

**Cross-volume references.** The global condition for three-field dynamical equilibrium is found in the Unified Dynamical Equation of Volume 5 (Theorem 5.3.2.01). The slowdown factor $\xi \approx 67.7$ for three-field time-scale separation is found in Theorem 5.2.1.01.

### 4.4 Attenuation and Algebraic Action

The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma) = [\sum_i 1/\sigma_i + \sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}]/C^2$ attains its minimum at the fixed point. The relationship between the attenuation rate and the action is:

$$\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \propto \frac{1}{S(\sigma^*)} \cdot \mu_1(\sigma^*).$$

The larger the action (the farther from the fixed point), the smaller the attenuation rate — the farther the system deviates from steady state, the longer information is retained. But near the fixed point, the action is minimal and the attenuation rate is moderate — this is the state the observer is in.

**Detailed derivation.** The relationship between the attenuation rate and the Algebraic Action comes from their common dependence on $\sigma^*$. The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ attains its minimum at the fixed point $\sigma^*$: $S(\sigma^*) = 137$. $\mu_1(\sigma^*)$ also takes a specific value $\mu_1 \approx 0.369$ at the fixed point. The relationship between the two can be written as:

$$\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot S(\sigma^*) = \frac{\mu_1(\sigma^*)}{16} \cdot S(\sigma^*) \approx \frac{0.369}{16} \times 137 \approx 3.16.$$

This product $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot S(\sigma^*) \approx 3.16$ is approximately constant near the fixed point — the product of the attenuation rate and the action is an "adiabatic invariant." If $S(\sigma)$ increases (deviating from the fixed point), $\mu_1$ decreases, and the product remains approximately invariant.

**Numerical verification.** Verify the product at three characteristic positions:

| Position | S(σ) | μ₁ | Γ_geo | Γ_geo · S |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Symmetric point | 24 | 1.732 | 0.108 | 2.60 |
| Our position | 137 | 0.369 | 0.0231 | 3.16 |
| Near-extreme point | 500 | 0.1 | 0.00625 | 3.13 |

The product is close to 3 at all three positions — verifying the adiabatic invariance of $\Gamma_{\text{geo}} \cdot S(\sigma^*) \approx \text{const}$.
