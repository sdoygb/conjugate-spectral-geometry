## §1 Self-Consistency Filtering

### 1.1 The Filtering Mechanism

The iteration of δ is not arbitrary. At each step, the candidate structures produced must satisfy:

1. **Internal self-consistency**: the relations of the new generator $e_{n+1}$ with the existing generators must be self-consistent ($e_{n+1}^2 = -1$, anticommutativity).
2. **Iterative stability**: the structure is not destroyed by subsequent δ steps — it can be incorporated by the next δ without generating contradictions.

Non-self-consistent structures are eliminated by δ itself. This is not an external filter — it is an intrinsic property of the δ iteration.

### 1.2 Formal Definition of Self-Consistency Filtering

To transform "self-consistency filtering" from an intuitive notion into an operational definition, we give a formal formulation below.

**Definition 4 (Candidate Structure).** Suppose δ has iterated to the $n$-th step, producing the candidate structure $S_n = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$, where each $e_i$ satisfies $e_i^2 = -1$. $S_n$ is a **candidate structure**.

**Definition 5 (Self-Consistency).** A candidate structure $S_n$ is **self-consistent** if it satisfies:

1. **Generator axiom**: for all $i \leq n$, $e_i^2 = -1$.
2. **Anticommutativity axiom**: for all $i \neq j$, $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$.
3. **Associativity**: multiplication on $\text{Cl}(n)$ satisfies the associative law.
4. **Closure**: $S_n$ is not destroyed by the next step of δ (the addition of $e_{n+1}$) — i.e., there exists an $e_{n+1}$ such that $S_{n+1} = (e_1, \dots, e_n, e_{n+1})$ also satisfies the above axioms.

Conditions 1–3 are the defining axioms of a Clifford algebra; condition 4 is "iterative stability" — the structure must be capable of being incorporated by subsequent δ steps without contradiction.

**Definition 6 (Filtering Operator).** The filtering operator $\Sigma$ acts on the set of candidate structures and retains the self-consistent ones:
$$\Sigma(\{S_n\}) = \{S_n \mid S_n \text{ is self-consistent}\}.$$

$\Sigma$ is not externally imposed — it is an intrinsic property of the δ iteration. δ automatically eliminates non-self-consistent candidate structures, because non-self-consistent structures produce contradictions in subsequent iterations and cannot close.

### 1.3 The Survivor Hierarchy

The survivors form a nested hierarchy:
$$\mathcal{S}_0 \subset \mathcal{S}_1 \subset \mathcal{S}_2 \subset \cdots \subset \mathcal{S}_7$$

where $\mathcal{S}_n$ is the set of structures that remain self-consistent after $n$ steps of δ iteration. $\mathcal{S}_7$ is the final survivor set after Seven-Layer Truncation.

**Properties of the survivor hierarchy.**

- $\mathcal{S}_0 = \{\text{Cl}(0)\} = \{\mathbb{R}\}$ — there is only one starting point.
- $\mathcal{S}_1 = \{\text{Cl}(1)\} = \{\mathbb{C}\}$ — there is only one way to add $e_1$.
- $\mathcal{S}_2 = \{\text{Cl}(2)\} = \{\mathbb{H}\}$ — there is only one way to add $e_2$ (up to isomorphism).
- $\mathcal{S}_n$ has only one element up to isomorphism — $\text{Cl}(n)$ — because the generator relations of Clifford algebras rigidly determine the algebraic structure.

This means the filtering operator $\Sigma$ "sieves out" all non-self-consistent candidates at each step — and the self-consistent candidate is unique up to isomorphism. Hence, the Coding Orbit is **unique** — there are no "branches" or "choices".

### 1.4 The Semantic Layer and the Mathematical Layer

| Semantic Layer | Mathematical Layer |
|:--|:--|

**Definition 7 (Berry Connection).** On the parameter space $T^8$ of the Coding Orbit, the Berry connection $\mathcal{A}$ is defined as:
$$\mathcal{A} = \langle \psi(\theta) | d\psi(\theta) \rangle,$$
where $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ is the parameter-dependent coding state.

**Construction of the coding state.** The coding state $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ is the realization of an irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra $\text{Cl}(n)$ under the parameters $\theta = (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n)$. Concretely:

- For each $\text{Cl}(n)$, choose an irreducible representation $\rho_n: \text{Cl}(n) \to \text{Mat}(d_n, \mathbb{R})$ (or $\text{Mat}(d_n, \mathbb{C})$).
- The parameter $\theta_n$ controls the "introduction angle" of the generator $e_n$: $e_n(\theta_n) = \cos\theta_n \cdot e_n^{(0)} + \sin\theta_n \cdot e_n^{(1)}$, where $e_n^{(0)}, e_n^{(1)}$ are two orthogonal "introduction directions".
- The coding state $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ is the ground state of $\rho_n$ under the parameters $\theta$.

**Berry curvature.** The Berry curvature $F$ is the exterior derivative of the Berry connection:
$$F = d\mathcal{A} + \mathcal{A} \wedge \mathcal{A}.$$

The Berry curvature $F$ is a 2-form on the parameter space, describing the "geometric response" of the coding state under changes of parameters.

## §4 The Completeness Criterion

**Theorem 0.4.4.01 (Completeness Criterion).** Let $\Gamma$ be a closed circuit on the Coding Orbit. $\Gamma$ corresponds to a Consummate Theorem (a completed structure) if and only if
$$\oint_\Gamma \mathcal{A} = 2\pi n, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

where $\mathcal{A}$ is the Berry connection on the Coding Orbit.

**Proof (complete: necessity and sufficiency).**

**Necessity.** Suppose $\Gamma$ corresponds to a completed structure (Consummate Theorem). Then that structure is self-consistent under the δ iteration — it is not destroyed by subsequent steps.

Self-consistency means the Berry phase of $\Gamma$ is a topological invariant — invariant under continuous deformations of the parameter space. This is because:

1. A self-consistent structure can be "smoothly" embedded in the δ iteration — no jumps or ruptures are needed.
2. The Berry phase is invariant under smooth deformations (the geometric nature of the Berry phase).
3. Therefore, the Berry phase of $\Gamma$ depends only on the topological class of $\Gamma$, not on the specific path.

The only possible topologically quantized values of the Berry phase are $2\pi n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$), because the Berry phase modulo $2\pi$ is canonical — $\gamma$ and $\gamma + 2\pi$ give the same physical state in $e^{i\gamma}$. Hence the topologically quantized values of the Berry phase are $2\pi n$.

Thus $\oint_\Gamma \mathcal{A} = 2\pi n$.

**Sufficiency.** Suppose $\oint_\Gamma \mathcal{A} = 2\pi n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$). Then the Berry phase of $\Gamma$ is an integer multiple of $2\pi$ — this means the circuit is closed in the quantum sense ($e^{i \cdot 2\pi n} = 1$).

A closed circuit corresponds to a self-consistent structure — that structure can be incorporated into the δ iteration without contradiction. This is because:

1. A closed Berry phase means the coding state returns to itself after the circuit (with an overall phase of $2\pi n$, which is trivial).
2. Returning to itself means the structure has no "unclosed remainder" — all parts are self-consistently connected.
3. Therefore, the structure corresponding to $\Gamma$ is self-consistent — it is consummate.

Thus $\Gamma$ corresponds to a Consummate Theorem. ∎

### 4.1 Operational Meaning of the Criterion

The Completeness Criterion is not merely a mathematical theorem — it is a **filtering tool** for derivation chains:

1. Construct a candidate circuit $\Gamma$ on the Coding Orbit.
2. Compute $\oint_\Gamma \mathcal{A}$.
3. If $= 2\pi n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$), then the theorem corresponding to $\Gamma$ is consummate — the derivation chain is complete.
4. If $\neq 2\pi n$, then the theorem corresponding to $\Gamma$ is not consummate — the derivation chain has a gap and requires supplementation.

**4.1.1 Equivalence of the Completeness Criterion and $S_\text{total} = 0$.** The Completeness Criterion $\oint_\Gamma \mathcal{A} = 2\pi n$ and the vanishing total action ($S_\text{total} = 0$) are equivalent statements:

- When $n = 1$, $\oint_{\Gamma_8} \mathcal{A} = 2\pi$, corresponding to the $\delta^8$ maximal consummate circuit. Then $S_{\text{total}} \equiv 2\pi \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$.
- When $n = 0$, $\oint_\Gamma \mathcal{A} = 0$, corresponding to the trivial circuit (no coding structure).
- When $n > 1$, corresponding to multiple consummate circuits (integer multiples of $\delta^8$).

Therefore, the Completeness Criterion can be equivalently stated as: **a closed circuit $\Gamma$ on the Coding Orbit corresponds to a Consummate Theorem if and only if the total action on that circuit is zero (mod $2\pi$).** This equivalence unifies the semantic concept of 0.1 (mutual conjugate balance = $S_\text{total} = 0$) with the topological computation of 0.3 (Berry phase = $2\pi$). The Completeness Criterion is the topological realization of $S_\text{total} = 0$ on the Coding Orbit.

The concrete operational procedure for using the Completeness Criterion as a derivation-chain filtering tool is as follows:

2. **Ensures self-consistency**: the Completeness Criterion automatically filters out non-self-consistent derivations — non-self-consistent derivation chains do not close, and their Berry phase is not $2\pi n$.
3. **Hierarchical structure**: completeness has a hierarchy — larger circuits contain smaller circuits, but smaller circuits are not necessarily consummate. This reflects the hierarchical structure of the physical world (elementary particles → atoms → molecules → ...).
4. **Uniqueness**: up to isomorphism, the consummate derivation chain is unique — because the Coding Orbit has only one self-consistent candidate at each step. This means the derivation path from δ to physical constants is unique.
