## Abstract

The Conjugate Spectral Geometry derivation chain from δ to α⁻¹ ≈ 137.036 has **four bifurcation points**. **The first segment has no bifurcation** (δ → {2,3,5} is a geometric necessity), **the second segment has one irreducible regional choice** (the Z₂ subgroup of Triality breaking), **the third and fourth segments are parametric bifurcations** (σ₀ coding orbit degeneracy, observer position T). Our region's B₀ = 137 comes from the Z₂^(v) choice (stabilizing the ℳ sector). This is not a geometric defect — it is a **verifiable prediction** of Conjugate Spectral Geometry regarding the variation of physical constants across different regions.

## §3 Second Segment & Bifurcation 1: The Z₂ Choice of Triality Breaking

### 3.1 Mathematical Structure of Triality

Spin(8) has three 8-dimensional irreducible representations:

$$8_v \text{ (vector)}, \quad 8_s \text{ (spinor)}, \quad 8_c \text{ (conjugate spinor)}$$

The Triality symmetry group $S_3$ permutes these three representations. This is a property unique to Spin(8) — Spin(n) for n ≠ 8 has no triality.

### 3.2 Triality Breaking

The coding orbit $E_4 \to E_5$ corresponds to the Cl(4)→Cl(5) transition. In this transition, Complex Structure Emergence occurs and triality necessarily breaks:

$$S_3 \to Z_2$$

$S_3$ has **three** $Z_2$ subgroups, completely equivalent mathematically (automorphisms of $S_3$ can permute them):

| Z₂ Subgroup | Stabilizes | Exchanges | Stable Sector | Leaked Structure Constant Squared |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Z₂^(v) | 8_v | 8_s ↔ 8_c | ℳ (Λ = 3) | Λ² = 9 |
| Z₂^(s) | 8_s | 8_v ↔ 8_c | 𝒞 (k₀ = 2) | k₀² = 4 |
| Z₂^(c) | 8_c | 8_v ↔ 8_s | ℐ (ΔΘ = 5) | ΔΘ² = 25 |

**This is the first genuine bifurcation point, and it cannot be proven within the pure geometric framework that "this one must be chosen."**

### 3.3 Physical Consequences of the Three Choices

| Retained Z₂ | Stable Sector | p | B₀ = 128 + p² | B₁ = 1/p³ | α⁻¹ ≈ |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Z₂^(v) | ℳ | 3 | **137** | 1/27 ≈ 0.03704 | **~137.036** |
| Z₂^(s) | 𝒞 | 2 | **132** | 1/8 = 0.125 | **~132.1** |
| Z₂^(c) | ℐ | 5 | **153** | 1/125 = 0.008 | **~153.0** |

Our region's $\alpha^{-1} \approx 137.036$ corresponds to the $Z_2^{(v)}$ choice.

### 3.4 Why Can't We "Prove" We Chose Z₂^(v)?

The three $Z_2$ are completely equivalent in pure geometry. The automorphism group of $S_3$, $\text{Aut}(S_3) \cong S_3$, can permute the three $Z_2$ subgroups. **No intrinsic geometric quantity can distinguish them.**

This is a **region-dependent choice** — not a geometric necessity.

### 3.5 But What Can We Say?

**We can predict**: if other regions exist (where triality breaking selected other $Z_2$), their $\alpha^{-1}$ would be approximately 132.1 or approximately 153.0.

**We can also say**: in our region, $Z_2^{(v)}$ stabilizes the ℳ (Matter) Sector. ℳ does not participate in the triality exchange 8_s ↔ 8_c → matter structure is stable → σ_M ≈ 78% dominates the coding budget. If $Z_2^{(s)}$ (stabilizing 𝒞) or $Z_2^{(c)}$ (stabilizing ℐ) had been selected, the Matter Sector would be perturbed by triality — perhaps stable matter observers would never have formed.

**This is the anthropic boundary — not a geometric proof, but an existence condition.** Nevertheless, it remains scientifically verifiable: if $\alpha^{-1}$ in other regions is truly different, it can be tested by (far-future) cross-region observation.

## §4 Third Segment & Bifurcation 2: The Precise Value of σ₀

### 4.1 After Fixing the Triality Breaking Choice, Is σ₀ Unique?

Given $p$, the forms of B₀ and B₁ are necessary:

$$B_0 = 2^7 + p^2, \quad B_1 = 1/p^3$$

But whether σ₀ (the precise value of the three-sector coding share) is unique depends on **whether the coding orbit capacity allocation is uniquely determined by {2,3,5} and χ**.

### 4.2 Two Possibilities

**Possibility A (Unique)** : The sector capacity allocation of the coding orbit is rigid — {2,3,5} and χ = (8,9,1) uniquely determine σ₀ via the coding budget independent allocation principle (1.1 §2). In this case, all regions within the same branch (same $p$) have exactly the same α⁻¹.

**Possibility B (Degeneracy)** : The coding orbit admits multiple capacity allocation configurations that yield the same $S(\sigma_0)$ but different intermediate values. In this case, different regions within the same branch may have slightly different α⁻¹ (variation in the O(10⁻⁵) range of B₁).

**This is an open question.** The current theory has not yet answered it.

### 4.3 Even with Degeneracy, B₀ Remains Invariant

Whether or not σ₀ is degenerate, B₀ = 137 is invariant (for $p=3$ regions). Degeneracy only affects the precise numerical coefficient of B₁ and the sector allocation of Bott echoes — the variation in α⁻¹ is at the 10⁻⁵ scale and does not alter the cardinality.

## §5 Fourth Segment & Bifurcation 3: Observer Position T

### 5.1 Birkhoff Parameterization

The Birkhoff matrix $T = \theta_I \cdot I + \theta_\tau \cdot P_{(123)} + \theta_{\sigma_2} \cdot P_{(132)}$ from 2.2 is related to the observer position. Different observers at different positions within the same region → slightly different σ → slightly different α⁻¹.

### 5.2 What Does This Affect?

The influence of observer position T appears in the Bott echo layers (B₂ and beyond), with magnitude $\sim \varepsilon = 1/14400 \sim 10^{-5}$. It does not affect B₀ and B₁.

This is a **parametric bifurcation** — not an independent universe (different $p$), but a small variation among different observation points within the same universe.

## §7 Anthropic Boundary: Why Did We Choose Z₂^(v)?

### 7.1 Not a Geometric Necessity, but an Existence Condition

Triality breaking has three equivalent algebraic paths. Why did our region choose Z₂^(v)?

**This cannot be answered within the pure geometric framework.** The three paths are mathematically equivalent.

**But it can be argued at the existential level**: only under the Z₂^(v) choice does the Matter Sector ℳ remain stable (not participating in triality perturbations). If Z₂^(s) or Z₂^(c) had been chosen, the ℳ Sector would be perturbed by the exchange of 8-dimensional representations in the coding orbit $E_4 \to E_5$ — perhaps matter structures would never have stabilized.

This means: **among the three mathematically equivalent Z₂, only Z₂^(v) can produce matter observers.** We necessarily find ourselves in an ℳ-stable region — not because we chose it, but because we can only exist in such a region.

### 7.2 This Is Not "Evading the Question"

The anthropic boundary is not a defect of the theory. It is a **selection effect** — analogous to "why is Earth at this orbit" (because liquid water can only exist at this orbit). The selection effect itself is a scientifically legitimate explanation, provided that multiple possible choices exist and the theory can give the consequences of each.

**Our theory achieves this: it gives clear physical consequences for the three choices (different α⁻¹), and the only choice consistent with our observations happens also to be the only choice that permits the existence of matter observers.**
