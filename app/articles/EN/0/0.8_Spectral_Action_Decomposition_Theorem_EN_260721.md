## Chapter 1　Spectral Action and Heat Kernel Expansion

### 1.1　Prerequisites: Spectral Triple (from 0.7, proof not repeated)

By Theorem 0.7.1 (Spectral Triple Construction Theorem), an even Spectral Triple of KO dimension = 8 is rigorously constructed from Cl(8):

$$
A = \mathrm{Cl}^0(8), \quad \mathcal{H} = \Delta_8 \oplus \bar{\Delta}_8, \quad D = \delta|_{\mathrm{Cl}(8)}
$$

where $\Delta_8$ is the irreducible real spinor representation of Cl(8) (16-dimensional Majorana-Weyl spinor), and $\mathrm{Cl}^0(8) \simeq \mathbb{R}(128)$ is the even subalgebra of Cl(8).

### 1.2　Spectral Action

The spectral action is defined as (Chamseddine-Connes 1997):

$$
\boxed{S(D) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(f\left(\frac{D^2}{\Lambda^2}\right)\right)}
$$

where:
- $f: \mathbb{R}^+ \to \mathbb{R}^+$ is an even smooth cutoff function ($f(0) = 1$, decaying rapidly as $x \to \infty$)
- $\Lambda$ is the cutoff scale (energy dimension), determined by the upper bound of Seven-Layer Truncation E₇ (Theorem 0.3.3.01)
- $D^2$ is a positive-definite self-adjoint operator with discrete spectrum (Laplace-type operator on the Coding Orbit space)

**Key distinction**: In NCG, $f$ and $\Lambda$ are external inputs. In CSG, both are determined by the Coding Orbit layer structure (§5).

#### 1.2.1 Expansion Constraint from Total Action Being Zero

In CSG, the spectral action is not a free quantity——it must satisfy the Total Action is Zero constraint derived from the δ axiom (0.1 §2.3, 0.7 §2.1.1):

$$
\boxed{S(D) = S_{\text{total}} = 0}
$$

This imposes a rigid constraint on the Seeley-deWitt expansion:

$$
S(D) = \sum_{k=0,2,4,6,8} f_k \Lambda^{8-k} a_k = 0
$$

i.e., the weighted sum of the five non-zero coefficients $a_0$ through $a_8$ must be exactly zero. This constraint is not approximate——it is an exact corollary of δ's non-surjectivity. The value of any single coefficient cannot be determined independently; it must jointly satisfy the zero-sum condition with the other four coefficients. This makes $a_0$ (the cosmological constant term) a **residual absorption term** after three-Sector cancellation, not an independent parameter.

### 1.3　Heat Kernel Expansion

For the positive-definite elliptic operator $D^2$, the heat kernel $\exp(-t D^2)$ has an asymptotic expansion as $t \to 0^+$:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t D^2}) \sim \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} t^{(k-n)/2} \, a_k(D^2), \quad (t \to 0^+)
$$

where $n = 8$ (dimension of the Coding Orbit space), and $a_k(D^2)$ are the Seeley-deWitt coefficients. $a_k \neq 0$ only when $k$ is even (for Dirac-type operators).

Through Laplace transformation, the spectral action can be written as a linear combination of $a_k$:

$$
\boxed{S(D) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} f_k \, \Lambda^{8-k} \, a_k(D^2)}
$$

where $f_k$ are the moments of the cutoff function $f$:

$$
\boxed{f_k = \int_0^{\infty} f(u) \, u^{(8-k)/2 - 1} \, du = \int_0^{\infty} f(u) \, u^{3 - k/2} \, du}
$$

$f_k = 0$ for odd $k$ ($f$ is even), and for $k > 8$, $a_k$ vanishes on compact boundaryless manifolds (the manifold is 8-dimensional, and Seeley-deWitt coefficients of order higher than 8 vanish on boundaryless manifolds).

**Hence the expansion naturally truncates at $k=8$**——this corresponds precisely to the Bott periodicity closure $\delta^8 = 2\pi$.

## Chapter 2　Seeley-deWitt Coefficients: General Form

### 2.1　Coefficients for Dirac-Type Operators

For a Dirac-type operator $D^2 = -\nabla^2 + E$ (where $E$ is the intrinsic curvature term) on an $n = 8$ dimensional manifold, the general form of the Seeley-deWitt coefficients $a_k$ is (Gilkey 1995; Vassilevich 2003):

$$
a_k(D^2) = \frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n/2}} \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \operatorname{tr}\left(\mathcal{A}_k(x)\right) \sqrt{g} \, d^8 x
$$

where $\mathcal{A}_k$ is a local invariant polynomial of order $k$, composed of the curvature tensor $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, gauge field strength $F_{\mu\nu}$, intrinsic curvature $E$, and their covariant derivatives.

For $n=8$, the prefactor is $(4\pi)^{-4} = 1/(256\pi^4)$.

### 2.2　Explicit Expressions for $k=0,2,4,6,8$

The standard forms of the coefficients on an 8-dimensional Dirac spinor bundle are listed below (trace $\operatorname{tr}$ taken over spinor space).

**$a_0$ (Volume Term)**:

$$
a_0(D^2) = \frac{1}{256\pi^4} \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \operatorname{tr}(I) \, \sqrt{g} \, d^8 x
$$

where $\operatorname{tr}(I) = \dim(\Delta_8) = 16$ (Majorana-Weyl spinor dimension).

**$a_2$ (Ricci Term)**:

$$
a_2(D^2) = \frac{1}{256\pi^4} \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \operatorname{tr}\left(\frac{R}{6} + E\right) \sqrt{g} \, d^8 x
$$

where $R$ is the Ricci scalar curvature, and $E$ is the intrinsic curvature term of $D^2$.

**$a_4$ (Curvature Squared Term)**:

$$
a_4(D^2) = \frac{1}{256\pi^4} \frac{1}{360} \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \operatorname{tr}\Big(
12R_{;\mu\mu} + 5R^2 - 2R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu} + 2R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} 
+ 60RE + 180E^2 + 30\Omega_{\mu\nu}\Omega^{\mu\nu}
\Big) \sqrt{g} \, d^8 x
$$

where $\Omega_{\mu\nu} = [D_\mu, D_\nu]$ is the curvature 2-form (containing gauge field strength $F_{\mu\nu}$ and gravitational curvature).

**$a_6$ (Higher Curvature + Higgs Term)**:

$$
a_6(D^2) = \frac{1}{256\pi^4} \frac{1}{6!} \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \operatorname{tr}\Big(
\cdots + 360E^3 + 360E_{;\mu}E_{;\mu} + 60E_{;\mu\mu}R + 120E\Omega_{\mu\nu}\Omega^{\mu\nu} + \cdots
\Big) \sqrt{g} \, d^8 x
$$

The full expression contains 35 terms (Gilkey 1995, §4.8). For CSG, the most critical sub-terms are $E^3$ (corresponding to Higgs self-coupling) and $E_{;\mu}E_{;\mu}$ (corresponding to Higgs kinetic term).

**$a_8$ (Topological Term)**:

$$
a_8(D^2) = \frac{1}{256\pi^4} \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \operatorname{tr}\Big(
\frac{1}{8!} \varepsilon^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_8} R_{\mu_1\mu_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge R_{\mu_7\mu_8}
+ \text{Chern-Simons 7-form}
\Big) \sqrt{g} \, d^8 x
$$

$a_8$ is a linear combination of the Euler characteristic class and the secondary Chern-Simons term. In CSG it corresponds to the topological charge of the $\mathcal{I}$ Sector.

### 2.3　Grouping $a_k$ by Curvature Source

Observing the curvature-dependence structure of $a_0$–$a_8$, they naturally fall into three groups:

| Coefficient | Dominant Curvature Source | Physical Meaning | Natural Attribution |
|:---|:---|:---|:---:|
| $a_0$ | $\int \sqrt{g}$ | Volume/Cosmological constant | Vacuum (global normalization) |
| $a_2$ | $\int R \sqrt{g}$ | Gravity (Ricci scalar curvature) | $\mathcal{C}$ Sector |
| $a_4$ | $\int (R^2 + F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$ | Gauge fields + gravity corrections | $\mathcal{M}$ Sector + $\mathcal{C}$ Sector |
| $a_6$ | $\int ((D\Phi)^2 + V(\Phi))$ | Higgs kinetic + potential | $\mathcal{M}$ Sector |
| $a_8$ | $\int (\text{Pfaff}(R) + \text{CS}_7)$ | Euler characteristic + Chern-Simons | $\mathcal{I}$ Sector |

**Core observation**: This grouping is not artificial, but a natural stratification of Seeley-deWitt coefficients by curvature tensor order. The heat kernel expansion of the Dirac operator on an 8-dimensional manifold **necessarily** splits the action into volume term ($a_0$), gravity term ($a_2$), gauge term ($a_4$), Higgs term ($a_6$), and topological term ($a_8$). CSG's three Sectors $\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}$ are precisely the physical naming of these mathematical structures.

#### 2.3.1 Sector Actions under the Zero-Sum Constraint

Under the global constraint $S_{\text{total}} = 0$, the spectral actions of the three Sectors are defined as:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
S_\mathcal{M} &= f_4 \Lambda^4 a_4^{\text{gauge}} + f_6 \Lambda^2 a_6^{\text{Higgs}} \\

The 8-dimensional volume of the Coding Orbit space $\mathcal{M}^8$ is not a free parameter. By the Seven-Layer Truncation theorem (Theorem 0.3.3.01), the "effective volume" of the Coding Orbit space is determined by the product of the eigenvalues of each layer:

This estimate has $O(10\%)$ precision; the exact volume requires a complete seven-layer spectral density calculation (open problem O3).
