### 1.1 Complex Structure of Cl(5)

The algebraic foundation of the Causal Sector is Cl(5) ≅ Mat(4,ℂ). The volume element $\omega_5 = e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5$ satisfies $\omega_5^2 = -1$ and commutes with all generators — $\omega_5$ acts as the complex structure $J$.

The existence of the complex structure $J$ endows the Causal Sector with oscillatory capacity — complex numbers naturally describe periodic motion. This is the essential distinction between the Causal Sector and the Matter Sector: the Matter Sector (Cl(3)) is a real quaternionic structure, while the Causal Sector (Cl(5)) is a complex matrix structure.

The algebraic properties of the complex structure $J$ are as follows:

- $J^2 = -1$ (defining property of a complex structure, from $\omega_5^2 = -1$)
- $[J, e_i] = 0$, $\forall i = 1, \ldots, 5$ (commutes with all generators, since $\omega_5$ is a central element)
- $J^\dagger = -J$ (anti-Hermiticity, ensuring that the generated evolution is unitary)

These three properties make $J$ the Hamiltonian of the Causal Sector — it generates the unitary evolution of the causal field. The Structure Constant $\Delta\Theta = 5$ is precisely the algebraic dimension jump point spanned by the Causal Sector, corresponding to the Complex Structure Emergence of the Cl(3)→Cl(5) segment.

#### Detailed Derivation: Complete Construction of the Cl(5) Complex Structure

Below we give the five-step derivation of $J = \omega_5$ as a complex structure.

**Step 1: Construction of the volume element.** Cl(5) is generated by $\{e_1, e_2, e_3, e_4, e_5\}$, satisfying the Clifford relations $e_i e_j + e_j e_i = 2\delta_{ij}$. The volume element is defined as the product of all generators:

$$\omega_5 = e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5.$$

**Step 2: Computation of the square of the volume element.** Using $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$ ($i \neq j$) and $e_i^2 = 1$, compute $\omega_5^2$:

$$\omega_5^2 = (e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5)(e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5) = e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5 e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5.$$

Commuting $e_1$ from left to right, passing through 4 anti-commutations (crossing $e_2, e_3, e_4, e_5$), yields $(-1)^4 = 1$, $e_1^2 = 1$, cancels. Similarly for $e_2$ (3 anti-commutations, $(-1)^3 = -1$, $e_2^2 = 1$). Computing successively yields $(-1)^{4+3+2+1+0} = (-1)^{10} = 1$. Hence $\omega_5^2 = -1$ (the full algebraic computation shows that the volume element of an odd-dimensional Clifford algebra squares to $(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}$, which for $n=5$ gives $(-1)^{15} = -1$).

**Step 3: Proof of centrality.** For any generator $e_i$, $\omega_5 e_i = e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 e_5 e_i$. Commuting $e_i$ from right to left requires crossing $5-i$ generators, each anti-commutation introducing $(-1)$. But for $e_i$ itself, $e_i e_i = 1$ cancels. Hence $\omega_5 e_i = (-1)^{5-i} e_i \omega_5$. When $n=5$, the parity of $5-i$ and $i$ is complementary, $(-1)^{5-i} = (-1)^{i-1}$. But Cl(5) is an odd-dimensional Clifford algebra, and $\omega_5$ commutes with all generators ($[\omega_5, e_i] = 0$, $\forall i$), because $\omega_5$ lies in the center of the algebra. This is a key property of odd-dimensional Clifford algebras.

**Step 4: Assignment as complex structure.** $\omega_5^2 = -1$ and $\omega_5$ lies in the center, satisfying the three axioms of a complex structure. Define $J = \omega_5$, then $J$ is an integrable complex structure on Cl(5). The eigenvalues $\pm i$ of $J$ correspond to the two oscillation directions of the causal field (forward and backward propagation).

**Step 5: Comparison with Cl(3).** The volume element $\omega_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3$ of Cl(3) also satisfies $\omega_3^2 = -1$ and lies in the center. But Cl(3) ≅ ℍ ⊕ ℍ (quaternion direct sum), and its complex structure manifests as helicity splitting (Vol. 2, Theorem 2.2.1.01). The complex structure of Cl(5) is richer — Cl(5) ≅ Mat(4,ℂ) is a full complex matrix algebra, and its complex structure generates the complete unitary dynamics of the causal field.

$$\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = \frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}{A_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{eff}}},$$

Here $r_{\mathcal{C}}^2 = 1/\Lambda$ is the structural curvature of the Causal Sector (determined by the algebraic jump Cl(3)→Cl(5)), independent of $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$. Therefore $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = \sigma_{\mathcal{C}} \cdot \Lambda = 3\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$.

**Step 4: Distinguishing the differences.** The distinction between the extrinsic curvature $h_{\mathcal{C}} = \Lambda \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ and the structural curvature $\Lambda$:

**Step 5: Physical meaning.** The causal field density $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = 3\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a linear function of the Causal Sector coding weight — the larger the coding weight, the denser the causal field. ### 1.4 Density Dynamical Equation

The time evolution of the causal field density $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ is described by the continuity equation:

$$\frac{\partial \rho_{\mathcal{C}}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = \Sigma_{\mathcal{C}},$$

where $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the causal current density and $\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the causal source term.

The causal current is generated by the complex structure $J$:

$$\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = -i J \cdot \nabla \rho_{\mathcal{C}}.$$

Since $J^2 = -1$, the causal current possesses an oscillatory character — this is precisely the mathematical expression of the "reciprocal transmission" of causal relations.

The source term $\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ is determined by Birkhoff dissipation:

$$\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}} = \mathcal{D}_T[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}] = T \rho_{\mathcal{C}} T^\dagger - \rho_{\mathcal{C}}.$$

At the fixed point $\sigma^*$, the source term vanishes ($\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ reaches steady state), and the continuity equation degenerates to the conservation equation $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = 0$.

#### Detailed Derivation: Five-Step Derivation of the Dynamical Equation

**Step 1: General form of the continuity equation.** For any conserved quantity flowing on a two-dimensional surface, the continuity equation is $\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = \Sigma$, where $\Sigma$ is the source term (positive for creation, negative for annihilation). The causal field density $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ satisfies this general form.

**Step 2: Derivation of the causal current $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}}$.** The causal current is generated by the complex structure $J$. On a two-dimensional constraint section, the most general current form is $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = -D \cdot \nabla \rho_{\mathcal{C}}$, where $D$ is the diffusion tensor. The complex structure $J$ acts as the diffusion tensor: $D = iJ$. Since $J$ is anti-Hermitian ($J^\dagger = -J$), $iJ$ is Hermitian, ensuring that the current is real. $J^2 = -1$ gives $(iJ)^2 = 1$ — the diffusion tensor is a "complex rotation" of the identity operator.

**Step 3: Oscillatory nature of the causal current.** The causal current $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = -iJ \cdot \nabla \rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ satisfies:

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = -i \nabla \cdot (J \cdot \nabla \rho_{\mathcal{C}}).$$

Since the action of $J$ on the two-dimensional section is equivalent to a $90^\circ$ rotation ($J$ rotates tangent vectors to normal vectors), the direction of the causal current is perpendicular to the density gradient — this is characteristic of two-dimensional incompressible flow. The causal field flows along isodensity contours, rather than diffusing along the gradient direction.

**Step 4: Derivation of the source term $\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$.** The Birkhoff dissipation superoperator $\mathcal{D}_T$ acts on the density matrix $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$: $\mathcal{D}_T[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}] = T\rho_{\mathcal{C}} T^\dagger - \rho_{\mathcal{C}}$. In the basis where the density matrix is diagonal, the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{D}_T$ are given by Theorem 4.2.1.01: $\{0, -0.507, -0.507\}$. The source term $\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}} = \text{Tr}(\mathcal{D}_T[\rho_{\mathcal{C}}])$ is the trace of the dissipation superoperator.

**Step 5: Steady-state condition.** At the Birkhoff fixed point $\sigma^*$, $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a fixed point of $T$: $T\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^* T^\dagger = \rho_{\mathcal{C}}^*$. Hence $\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}} = 0$, and the continuity equation degenerates to $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{C}} = 0$ — the causal current is divergence-free, and the causal field density is conserved.

### 2.2 Monotonicity of Entropy

#### Detailed Derivation: Five-Step Unfolding of the Entropy Monotonicity Proof

**Step 1: Time derivative of entropy.** The time derivative of the von Neumann entropy $S(\rho) = -\text{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho)$ is:

$$\frac{dS}{dt} = -\text{Tr}\left(\frac{d\rho}{dt} \cdot (\ln \rho + \mathbb{I})\right).$$

Since $\text{Tr}(d\rho/dt) = 0$ (trace preservation), $\text{Tr}(\mathbb{I} \cdot d\rho/dt) = 0$, hence $\frac{dS}{dt} = -\text{Tr}\left(\frac{d\rho}{dt} \ln \rho\right)$.

**Step 2: Contribution of the unitary evolution part.** Substituting $\frac{d\rho}{dt}\big|_U = -i[J, \rho]$:

$$\frac{dS}{dt}\bigg|_U = i\text{Tr}([J, \rho] \ln \rho) = i\text{Tr}(J\rho \ln \rho - \rho J \ln \rho).$$

Using the cyclic property of the trace: $\text{Tr}(J\rho \ln \rho) = \text{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho \cdot J)$. Hence $\frac{dS}{dt}|_U = i\text{Tr}(\rho[\ln \rho, J]) = 0$, because $[\ln \rho, J] = 0$ ($\rho$ commutes with $J$, since $J$ is a central element).

**Step 3: Contribution of the dissipative part.** Substituting $\frac{d\rho}{dt}\big|_D = T\rho T^\dagger - \rho$:

$$\frac{dS}{dt}\bigg|_D = -\text{Tr}((T\rho T^\dagger - \rho) \ln \rho).$$

**Step 4: Application of Klein's inequality.** For any CPTP map $\Phi$, $S(\Phi(\rho)) \geq S(\rho)$. The map $\Phi(\rho) = T\rho T^\dagger$ is CPTP ($T$ is a doubly stochastic matrix). Hence:

$$S(T\rho T^\dagger) \geq S(\rho) \quad \Rightarrow \quad -\text{Tr}(T\rho T^\dagger \ln(T\rho T^\dagger)) \geq -\text{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho).$$

This yields $dS/dt|_D \geq 0$.

**Step 5: Total effect.** Combining both steps: $dS/dt = dS/dt|_U + dS/dt|_D = 0 + dS/dt|_D \geq 0$. Equality holds only when $\rho$ is a fixed point of $T$ (i.e., the Birkhoff fixed point $\rho^*$). ∎

The change in $F_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}$ is $\Delta F \approx 0.007 \times 0.21^2 - 0.029 \times 0.02 \approx 0.0003 - 0.0006 \approx -0.0003$ — extremely small. This indicates that the 𝒞-ℐ interaction free energy is very stable in the landscape.

### 3.4 Joint Steady State

The joint steady state of the 𝒞-ℐ coupling is determined by the free energy minimum conditions:

$$\frac{\delta F_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}}{\delta \rho_{\mathcal{C}}} = 0, \qquad \frac{\delta F_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}}{\delta \rho_{\mathcal{I}}} = 0.$$

The steady-state solution yields the equilibrium distributions of 𝒞 and ℐ — this is the subject of 4.4.

#### Detailed Derivation: Unfolding of the Steady-State Conditions

Free energy functional derivative:

$$\frac{\delta F}{\delta \rho_{\mathcal{C}}} = \frac{\delta}{\delta \rho_{\mathcal{C}}} \left( \text{Tr}(H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}) - T_{\mathcal{C}} S_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} \right) = H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}|_{\mathcal{C}} - T_{\mathcal{C}} \ln \rho_{\mathcal{C}} = 0,$$

where $H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}|_{\mathcal{C}} = \text{Tr}_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} \rho_{\mathcal{I}})$ is the effective Hamiltonian. Solving yields $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} \propto e^{-H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}|_{\mathcal{C}} / T_{\mathcal{C}}}$. Similarly, $\rho_{\mathcal{I}} \propto e^{-H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}}|_{\mathcal{I}} / T_{\mathcal{C}}}$. The joint steady state $\rho_{\text{ss}} \propto e^{-H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} / T_{\mathcal{C}}}$ is a Gibbs state.

## §4 Causal Symmetry Breaking

### 4.1 Emergence of Gauge Structure

The complex structure $J$ of the Causal Sector undergoes triality breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$ in the Cl(5)→Cl(6) transition.

This breaking produces gauge structure: the $Z_2$ symmetry corresponds to the $U(1)$ gauge group (cyclic symmetry of complex phases). A more precise gauge group analysis is given in Vol. 7.

#### Detailed Derivation: Three-Step Derivation of Triality Breaking

**Step 1: Triality in Cl(5).** Cl(5) ≅ Mat(4,ℂ) possesses Spin(8) triality $S_3$ symmetry. The three generators of $S_3$: identity $e$, rotation $\tau$ (three-cycle), transposition $\sigma$ (binary exchange). Triality manifests as the equivalence of three 8-dimensional representations: vector representation $8_v$, spinor representation $8_s$, conjugate spinor representation $8_c$.

**Step 2: Breaking in Cl(6).** Cl(6) ≅ Mat(8,ℝ) returns to a real structure. $\omega_6 = \omega_5 \cdot e_6$ is the volume element of Cl(6), but $e_6$ anti-commutes with $\omega_5$: $\{\omega_5, e_6\} = 0$. This makes $[\omega_5, e_6] \neq 0$ — $\omega_5$ is no longer a central element of Cl(6). The complex structure $J$ is broken.

**Step 3: Residual symmetry.** The symmetry preserved after breaking is the transposition $\sigma_2$ (complex conjugation). Of the six elements of $S_3$, only $\{e, \sigma_2\}$ are preserved — precisely $Z_2$. $Z_2$ is promoted to the continuous $U(1)$ gauge group (Vol. 4.5, Theorem 4.5.1.01).

#### Comparative Discussion: 𝒞-Sector Symmetry Breaking vs Standard Higgs Mechanism

| Property | Standard Higgs Mechanism | Causal Symmetry Breaking |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Breaking type | Spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) | Algebraic structure breaking |
| Breaking scale | Electroweak scale ($\sim 246$ GeV) | Planck scale |
| Origin of breaking | Higgs field vacuum expectation value | Cl(5)→Cl(6) algebraic transition |
| Goldstone bosons | Present (are "eaten") | None (discrete symmetry breaking) |
| Reversibility | Theoretically reversible (restored at high temperature) | Irreversible (unidirectional algebraic structure) |
| Gauge group production | $SU(2) \times U(1)$ already present before breaking | Breaking produces $SU(2) \times U(1)$ |

Causal symmetry breaking is more fundamental — it produces gauge structure itself, rather than further breaking an already existing gauge structure. The standard Higgs mechanism is "symmetry breaking produces mass," while causal symmetry breaking is "symmetry breaking produces gauge structure."

### 4.2 Emergence of Weak Isospin

The $Z_2$ residual symmetry divides the Causal Sector into two "isospin" components:

| Isospin | $Z_2$ representation | Physical correspondence |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| Up | $1$ | Up-type leptons ($\nu_e, \nu_\mu, \nu_\tau$) |
| Down | $-1$ | Down-type leptons ($e, \mu, \tau$) |

Weak isospin is not externally introduced — it is the geometric realization of the $Z_2$ residual symmetry.

#### Detailed Derivation: Construction of Projection Operators

$P_{\pm} = \frac{1}{2}(I \pm i\omega_5) = \frac{1}{2}(I \pm iJ)$. Since $(iJ)^2 = 1$, $P_{\pm}^2 = \frac{1}{4}(I \pm 2iJ + (iJ)^2) = \frac{1}{4}(I \pm 2iJ - I) = \frac{1}{4}(2I \pm 2iJ) = P_{\pm}$ (idempotence). $P_+ + P_- = I$ (completeness), $P_+ P_- = \frac{1}{4}(I - (iJ)^2) = \frac{1}{4}(I - I) = 0$ (orthogonality). Therefore the correct weak isospin projection operators are $P_{\pm} = \frac{1}{2}(I \pm i\omega_5)$.

#### Numerical Verification: Energy Level Splitting of Weak Isospin

The eigenvalues of $\omega_5$ are $\pm i$, so $P_+$ projects onto the subspace with eigenvalue $+i$ (up-type), and $P_-$ projects onto the subspace with eigenvalue $-i$ (down-type). The energy level splitting is $\Delta E = 2|\text{Im}(\lambda_2)| \approx 2 \times 0.181 = 0.362$ (natural units). In Planck energy units ($E_P \approx 1.22 \times 10^{19}$ GeV), $\Delta E \approx 4.42 \times 10^{18}$ GeV — far exceeding the electroweak scale ($\sim 10^2$ GeV), indicating that the algebraic roots of weak isospin lie at the Planck scale.

### 4.3 Relation between Breaking and the Arrow of Time

The causal symmetry breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$ has a deep connection with the arrow of time:

- $S_3$ symmetry (full triality): causal field triply degenerate, no preferred evolution direction — no arrow
- $Z_2$ residual (after breaking): causal field doubly split, producing a preferred direction — arrow locked

The breaking reduces triple symmetry to double symmetry, endowing the causal field with "directionality." Triple symmetry permits cycles (without beginning or end), while double symmetry only permits linearity (with beginning and end) — this is precisely the symmetry root of the arrow of time.

#### Detailed Derivation: Group-Theoretic Analysis of Symmetry Reduction and Arrow Emergence

The $S_3$ group has three irreducible representations: the identity representation $A_1$ (one-dimensional, totally symmetric), the alternating representation $A_2$ (one-dimensional, totally antisymmetric), and the standard representation $E$ (two-dimensional).

Under $S_3$ symmetry, the three coding modes $\{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}, \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}, \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}\}$ of the causal field carry the standard representation $E$. The rotation $\tau$ of $S_3$ cyclically permutes the three — all directions are equivalent.

After breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$, the $E$ representation decomposes into two one-dimensional representations of $Z_2$: $E = A \oplus B$ ($A$ corresponds to symmetric, $B$ to alternating sign). The causal field splits into two $Z_2$ components — up-type and down-type. The direction of the arrow of time corresponds to the sign choice of the $B$ component (alternating sign).

Before breaking, under $S_3$ symmetry, the three directions are equivalent, and the system can cycle among the three directions — no preferred arrow. After breaking, under $Z_2$ symmetry, only two directions remain, and Birkhoff dissipation $|\lambda_{2,3}| < 1$ selects one of them. This group-theoretic analysis reveals the symmetry origin of the arrow of time emergence.

### 4.4 Causal Arrow Locking

After triality breaking, the $Z_3$ residual symmetry locks the direction of the causal arrow. The locking mechanism is as follows:

1. Before breaking: $S_3$ symmetry permits three equivalent causal orders — no preferred direction
2. After breaking: $Z_2$ preserves only two causal orders — forward and backward
3. Birkhoff dissipation $|\lambda_{2,3}| < 1$ selects the forward direction — the entropy-increasing direction

Thus the direction of the causal arrow is determined by the sign of Birkhoff dissipation (entropy increase), while the existence of the arrow is determined by triality breaking. The two jointly lock the time direction of the causal field.

#### Detailed Derivation: Algebraic Conditions for Arrow Locking

The algebraic condition for arrow locking is $\theta_\tau > \theta_{\sigma_2}$ (cyclic permutation weight greater than transposition weight). At our position, $\theta_\tau \approx 0.209$, $\theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 0.009$, $\theta_\tau / \theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 23.2 \gg 1$. This makes the $\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{I}$ direction (via cyclic permutation) dominant over the $\mathcal{C} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{I}$ direction (via transposition), and the arrow of time flows from Matter Sector to Causal Sector to Information Sector — irreversible.

If $\theta_\tau = \theta_{\sigma_2}$ (symmetric point), the two directions are equivalent — the arrow vanishes. If $\theta_\tau < \theta_{\sigma_2}$ (extreme Information-Sector-dominated), the arrow direction reverses — Information Sector flows to Causal Sector. These scenarios may all occur in the landscape, but our position $\theta_\tau / \theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 23$ locks the forward arrow.

## §5 Landscape Dependence of Causal Field Density

### 5.1 Variation of Density with Landscape Position

The causal field density $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = 3\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ depends directly on the coding weight $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$. In the landscape, $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ varies with landscape position:

- Symmetric point ($\sigma = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$): $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} = 1$
- Our position ($\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^* \approx 0.210603$): $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^* \approx 0.633$
- Extreme point ($\sigma_{\mathcal{C}} \to 0$): $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} \to 0$ (causal field vanishes)

#### Detailed Derivation: Functional Form of the Density Landscape

The causal field density $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}) = 3\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a linear function of $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$. In the landscape, the range of variation of $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ is constrained by the Birkhoff fixed point equation: $\sigma^* = T \sigma^*$ (Vol. 0, Theorem 0.3.1.01). The maximum possible value of $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ is $1$ (Causal Sector monopolizes the holographic screen), and the minimum possible value is $0$ (Causal Sector vanishes). But the Birkhoff fixed point equation restricts feasible $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ to the range $[0.011, 0.333]$ (from Information-Sector-dominated to symmetric point).

#### Numerical Verification: Stability of the Density Landscape

At the fixed point $\sigma^*$, $\partial \rho_{\mathcal{C}} / \partial \sigma_{\mathcal{C}} = 3$ — the sensitivity of density to coding weight is 3. If $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ deviates by $0.0001$ (about $0.05\%$ relative change), $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}$ changes by $0.0003$ (about $0.05\%$ relative change). The density is linearly stable in the landscape.

### 5.2 Density and Algebraic Action

The algebraic action $S(\sigma) = [\sum_i 1/\sigma_i + \sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}]/C^2$ takes its minimum at the fixed point. The relation between causal field density and action:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}} = -\frac{1}{C^2} \left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^2} + \sum_{j \neq \mathcal{C}} \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^3 \sigma_j}}\right).$$

At the fixed point $\sigma^*$, the gradients of all sectors are equal: $\partial S / \partial \sigma_{\mathcal{M}} = \partial S / \partial \sigma_{\mathcal{C}} = \partial S / \partial \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}$ (under the constraint $\sum \sigma_i = 1$), giving the minimal action configuration.

#### Detailed Derivation: Calculation of the Action Gradient

Computing $\partial S / \partial \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ at the fixed point:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigg|_{\sigma^*} = -\frac{1}{C^2} \left(\frac{1}{0.210603^2} + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{0.210603^3 \cdot 0.777912}} + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{0.210603^3 \cdot 0.011484}}\right).$$

Term by term:
- $1/0.210603^2 \approx 22.55$
- $1/(2\sqrt{0.210603^3 \cdot 0.777912}) \approx 5.86$
- $1/(2\sqrt{0.210603^3 \cdot 0.011484}) \approx 48.31$

Sum $\approx 76.72$. At the fixed point, $\nabla S$ has equal components (under $\sum \sigma_i = 1$), which gives the minimal action configuration for $\sigma^*$.

### 5.3 Density and Arrow of Time Strength

The higher the causal field density, the stronger the arrow of time (larger entropy production rate):

$$\sigma_S \propto \rho_{\mathcal{C}} \cdot (1 - |\lambda_2|^2).$$

Our position $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^* \approx 0.633$ yields a moderate-strength arrow — neither too strong (complete dissipation) nor too weak (nearly reversible). This moderate strength is compatible with the conditions for life — too strong an arrow prevents the system from maintaining structure, while too weak an arrow prevents evolution.

#### Detailed Derivation: Landscape Function of Arrow Strength

In the landscape, $\sigma_S(\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}) \propto 3\sigma_{\mathcal{C}} \cdot (1 - |\lambda_2(\sigma_{\mathcal{C}})|^2)$. $|\lambda_2|^2$ also depends on $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}$ (via the parameters $\theta_k$ of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$). At the symmetric point $\sigma = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$, $|\lambda_2|^2 = 1$ (no dissipation), $\sigma_S = 0$ (no arrow). At the extreme point $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}} \to 0$, $\rho_{\mathcal{C}} \to 0$, $\sigma_S \to 0$ (no causal field, no arrow). Arrow strength exhibits a bell-shaped distribution in the landscape — "large in the middle, small at both ends" — and our position is near the peak.

#### Numerical Verification: Arrow Strength and Life Conditions

The arrow strength $\sigma_S \approx 0.491$ corresponds to a characteristic time $\tau_S \approx 2.04$ Planck times. If the arrow strength increased by a factor of 10 ($\sigma_S \approx 5$), $\tau_S \approx 0.2$ — the causal field would dissipate almost instantaneously, unable to maintain structure. If the arrow strength decreased by a factor of 10 ($\sigma_S \approx 0.05$), $\tau_S \approx 20$ — the causal field would be nearly reversible, unable to produce the irreversibility needed for evolution. Our position $\sigma_S \approx 0.491$ lies in the "habitable zone" — arrow strength is moderate, permitting both structure maintenance and evolution.
