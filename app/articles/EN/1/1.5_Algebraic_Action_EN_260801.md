## §1 Construction of the Action

### 1.1 Design Principles

The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ must satisfy the following principles:

1. **Algebraicity**: contains only algebraic operations on $\sigma_i$ (powers, sums), no transcendental functions
2. **Positivity**: $S(\sigma) > 0$ for all valid $\sigma$
3. **Minimality**: physical states correspond to the minimum of $S$ — the principle of least action
4. **Symmetry**: respects triality symmetry ($S_3$ or its subgroup after breaking)
5. **Normalization**: related to the algebraic normalization condition (Theorem 1.4.3.01) via $C$

### 1.2 Form of the Action

**Definition 21 (Algebraic Action).**
$$S(\sigma) = \frac{1}{C^2}\left[\sum_{i} \frac{1}{\sigma_i} + \kappa \sum_{i<j} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}}\right].$$

where:
- $\sigma_i$ are the coding weights of the three sectors ($\sigma_M, \sigma_C, \sigma_I$)
- $C$ is the algebraic normalization constant (Theorem 1.4.3.01)
- $\kappa$ is the cross-term coefficient

### 1.3 Structure of the Two Terms

| Term | Form | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Individual term $\sum 1/\sigma_i$ | The "cost" of each sector | The smaller the coding weight, the higher the cost |
| Cross term $\kappa \sum 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ | The coupling cost between sectors | Coding coupling between sectors |

The individual term corresponds to the independent incompleteness of each sector, and the cross term corresponds to the coupling incompleteness between sectors.

## §2 Determination of the Coefficient $\kappa$

### 2.1 Spin(8) Triality

Spin(8) is the unique compact Lie group possessing triality — its three 8-dimensional representations (the vector representation $V$, and the spinor representations $S^+$ and $S^-$) are permuted under the outer automorphism group $S_3$.

The $S_3$ symmetry of triality has three fixed points ($S_3$ fixed points on the representation space):

| Fixed Point | Symmetry Subgroup | Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Fully symmetric | $S_3$ | Three representations indistinguishable |
| Cyclically symmetric | $Z_2$ | Two representations distinguishable, third symmetric |
| Fully broken | $1$ | Three representations all distinguishable |

### 2.2 Algebraic Proof of $\kappa = 1$

**Theorem 1.5.2.01 ($\kappa = 1$ Theorem).** The cross-term coefficient $\kappa = 1$, determined by the $S_3$ fixed point of Spin(8) triality.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $S_3$ fixed point constraint.** The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ must behave well under triality symmetry. Under full $S_3$ symmetry, the three sectors are indistinguishable, and the ratio of the individual term coefficient to the cross-term coefficient must be determined by the representation structure of $S_3$.

**Step 2: Representation decomposition of $S_3$.** The coding weights $(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3)$ of the three sectors decompose under the action of $S_3$ into:
- Symmetric representation (1-dimensional): $\sigma_= \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3$
- Standard representation (2-dimensional): $(\sigma_1 - \sigma_2, \sigma_2 - \sigma_3)$

The coefficient of the individual term $\sum 1/\sigma_i$ in the symmetric representation is 1. The coefficient of the cross term $\sum 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ in the symmetric representation is also determined to be 1 by the structure of $S_3$ — because $S_3$ has 3 transpositions, each contributing $1/3$, summing to $= 3 \times 1/3 = 1$.

**Step 3: After triality breaking to $Z_3$ (in the Cl(5)→Cl(6) transition), the individual term coefficient remains 1 (unaffected by breaking), and the cross-term coefficient $\kappa$ also remains 1 — because $Z_2 \subset S_3$ still preserves the structure of the cross term.

Therefore $\kappa = 1$ is an algebraic rigidity result of Spin(8) triality, requiring no external parameters. ∎

### 2.4 Physical Meaning of the Algebraic Action

$S(\sigma)$ as the "coding cost": the "algebraic price" of encoding the three sector weights $\sigma_i$ into physical observables. The action $S(\sigma)$ measures the geometric-algebraic cost that must be paid when transforming abstract coding weights into physical observables. The higher this cost, the more "expensive" the coding process and the more difficult the physical realization.

**Physical meaning of the first term $\sum 1/\sigma_i$.** The individual term reflects the cost of independent coding of each sector. The smaller the weight $\sigma_i$ (the weaker the sector), the higher the coding cost $1/\sigma_i$ — an inverse relation. Physically, when a sector's coding weight approaches zero, the cost of projecting its information content onto physical observables tends to infinity. This is consistent with the statistical frequency interpretation in quantum measurement: rarely occurring events (small $\sigma_i$) require enormous "coding effort" to be physically realized. Hence the individual term is an algebraic measure of the "scarcity" of each sector.

**Physical meaning of the second term $\sum 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$.** The cross term reflects the coding cost of coupling between sectors. The square root $\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ reflects the $\sqrt{\sigma}$ proportionality of the Born Rule — in quantum mechanics the probability amplitude is proportional to $\sqrt{\sigma}$, hence the coupling strength between two sectors is proportional to $\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$. The cross term $1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ indicates that the smaller the geometric mean weight of two sectors, the higher the cost of coupled coding. This directly embeds the $\sqrt{\sigma}$ proportionality of the Born Rule into the algebraic structure of the action, endowing the cross term with the physical substance of quantum measurement coupling.

**Physical meaning of $1/C^2$.** $C$ is the completeness parameter of the holographic projection, satisfying the normalization condition $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$ (where $p = \sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3}$). When $C^2 < 1$, the holographic projection is incomplete — some information is lost in the projection process. $1/C^2 > 1$ acts as a penalty factor, increasing the total coding cost. Physically, $C^2$ can be understood as the "information retention rate" of the holographic screen: $C^2 = 1$ corresponds to a perfect holographic projection (no information loss), and $C^2 < 1$ corresponds to a lossy projection. The observer position's $C^2 < 1$ means that our physical observations are always "incomplete," and this incompleteness is accounted for in the action through the $1/C^2$ factor.

In summary, the three-term structure of $S(\sigma)$ — individual term, cross term, normalization factor — respectively corresponds to: independent sector coding cost, coupled sector coding cost, and holographic incompleteness penalty. Together they constitute a complete algebraic description of the "coding cost."

## §3 Properties of the Action

### 3.1 Positivity

**Proposition 1.5.3.01 (Positivity).** $S(\sigma) > 0$ for all $\sigma_i > 0$.

**Proof.** Each term $1/\sigma_i > 0$ and $1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j} > 0$ (since $\sigma_i > 0$), and $C^2 > 0$. Hence $S(\sigma) > 0$. ∎

### 3.2 Existence of the Minimum

**Proposition 1.5.3.02 (Existence of Minimum).** $S(\sigma)$ has a unique minimum under the normalization constraint $\sigma_M\sigma_C\sigma_I = 1$ (or equivalently $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$).

**Proof.** $S(\sigma)$ tends to $\infty$ as $\sigma_i \to 0$ (boundary divergence), and also tends to $\infty$ as $\sigma_i \to 1$ (others $\to 0$). Hence the minimum is attained in the interior. By the convexity of $S(\sigma)$ (positive second derivative), the minimum is unique. ∎

### 3.3 Physical Meaning of the Minimum

The minimum of $S(\sigma)$ corresponds to the physical state — the "ground state" of the coding system:

$$S^* = \min_{\sigma} S(\sigma).$$

This $S^*$ is the candidate value for the Fine-Structure Constant $S^* = \alpha^{-1}$. However, the specific value of $S^*$ depends on the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$ (Volume 2) — different $T$ yield different minima.

### 3.4 Complete Differential-Geometric Proof of $\kappa = 1$

This subsection provides a complete differential-geometric proof of Theorem 1.5.2.01, elevating the algebraic argument of §2.2 to a geometric argument based on the heat kernel expansion on the constraint product manifold.

**Constraint product manifold.** The geometric realization of the coding orbit is the constraint product manifold:
$$M(a) = S^3(r_M) \times S^3(r_C) \times S^3(r_I)$$
where $r_M, r_C, r_I$ are the radii of the three $S^3$'s, determined by the sector weights: $r_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}$.

The relation $r_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}$ between the radii and the weights stems from the geometric interpretation of the coding weights — $\sigma_i$ is the "area share" of the $i$-th sector in the coding space, and the area of $S^3$ is proportional to $r^2$, hence $r_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}$.

**Heat kernel expansion.** The heat kernel expansion on the constraint manifold yields the action:
$$S(\sigma) = \frac{a_2}{a_0} = \frac{\text{scalar curvature term} + \text{extrinsic curvature term}}{\text{volume term}}$$

where:
- $a_0 = \text{Vol}(M) \propto r_M \cdot r_C \cdot r_I$ (volume term)
- $a_2 = \int_M R\, d\text{vol} + \int_{\partial M} K\, d\text{area}$ (curvature term, containing intrinsic and extrinsic curvature)

The heat kernel expansion is the asymptotic expansion of the trace of the Laplace-Beltrami operator $\Delta_M$:
$$\text{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_M}) \sim (4\pi t)^{-n/2} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k\, t^k, \quad t \to 0^+.$$

The action $S(\sigma)$ corresponds to the ratio $a_2/a_0$ — the first nontrivial curvature coefficient over the volume coefficient in the heat kernel expansion. This ratio measures the "curvature density" of the manifold and is an intrinsic geometric invariant.

**Additivity of scalar curvature.** The scalar curvature of a product manifold is the sum of the curvatures of its factors:
$$R_M = R_{S^3(r_M)} + R_{S^3(r_C)} + R_{S^3(r_I)} = 6\left(\frac{1}{r_M^2} + \frac{1}{r_C^2} + \frac{1}{r_I^2}\right)$$

since the scalar curvature of $S^3(r)$ is $R = 6/r^2$ (the constant positive curvature section of the 3-sphere). Substituting $r_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}$, the scalar curvature term is proportional to:
$$\int_M R\, d\text{vol} \propto r_M r_C r_I \cdot 6\left(\frac{1}{r_M^2} + \frac{1}{r_C^2} + \frac{1}{r_I^2}\right) = 6\left(\frac{r_C r_I}{r_M} + \frac{r_M r_I}{r_C} + \frac{r_M r_C}{r_I}\right).$$

Expressed in terms of $\sigma_i$ (taking $r_i = \sqrt{\sigma_i}$, omitting the common proportionality constant):
$$\int_M R\, d\text{vol} \propto 6\sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_M} + \frac{1}{\sigma_C} + \frac{1}{\sigma_I}\right).$$

This is precisely the geometric origin of the individual term $\sum 1/\sigma_i$ in the action — the area factor $\sqrt{\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3}$ in the scalar curvature integral is absorbed by the normalization condition $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$ (where $p = \sqrt{\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3}$), leaving $\sum 1/\sigma_i$ as the individual term.

**Extrinsic curvature and Spin(8) triality.** The normal bundle of the constraint product manifold possesses Spin(8) triality symmetry. The normal bundles among the three $S^3$ factors are determined by the intertwining of the three 8-dimensional representations of Spin(8) (vector $8_v$, spinor $8_s$, conjugate spinor $8_c$).

Extrinsic curvature term:
$$K_{\text{ext}} = \sum_{i<j} \langle II_i, II_j \rangle_{\text{Spin(8)}}$$

where $II_i$ is the second fundamental form of the $i$-th $S^3$ factor. The second fundamental form $II_i$ describes how the $i$-th factor bends in the normal bundle directions — it measures the "non-total-product nature" of the product embedding.

Under Spin(8) triality, the second fundamental forms of the three factors are mutually coupled through the trilinear intertwining map $8_v \otimes 8_s \otimes 8_c$. This trilinear map is unique to Spin(8) — it does not exist in any other compact Lie group and is the geometric embodiment of triality.

The extrinsic curvature term expressed in terms of $\sigma_i$ is:
$$K_{\text{ext}} \propto \sum_{i<j} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}}.$$

This is precisely the geometric origin of the cross term $\sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ in the action. The square root $\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ appears because the inner product $\langle II_i, II_j \rangle$ of the second fundamental forms involves the contraction of the normal vectors of two factors, and each normal vector's norm is proportional to $\sqrt{\sigma_i}$, so the inner product is proportional to $\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$.

**$S_3$ fixed point argument.** The triality group $S_3$ of Spin(8) acts naturally on the three 8-dimensional representations. The extrinsic curvature $K_{\text{ext}}$ must be invariant under the action of $S_3$ — because the heat kernel coefficient $a_2$ is an intrinsic invariant of the manifold, independent of the labeling of the representations.

The $S_3$ fixed point satisfies:
$$h_M = h_C = h_I$$
where $h_i = r_i^2$ (the "curvature height" of the $i$-th factor).

At the fixed point, the curvature heights of the three factors are equal, meaning the radii of the three $S^3$'s are equal: $r_M = r_C = r_I$, i.e., the three sector weights are equal $\sigma_M = \sigma_C = \sigma_I = 1/3$.

However, the physical observer position $\sigma^*$ is not the fully symmetric point. The crucial point is: the $S_3$ fixed point argument constrains the **coefficient structure** of the extrinsic curvature term, not the specific values of $\sigma$. Specifically, the coefficient $\kappa$ of the extrinsic curvature term is determined by the fixed point structure of $S_3$ on the representation space — at the fixed point, the ratio of the individual term coefficient (from scalar curvature) to the cross-term coefficient (from extrinsic curvature) is locked.

At the fixed point, $C_2 = C_3 = C$ (the normalization constants of the three sectors are equal), hence:
$$\kappa = \frac{C_2}{C_3} = 1.$$

Here $C_2$ is the normalization constant of the individual term (scalar curvature contribution), and $C_3$ is the normalization constant of the cross term (extrinsic curvature contribution). The $S_3$ fixed point requires $C_2 = C_3$, because when $S_3$ permutes the three representations, the scalar curvature term and the extrinsic curvature term are indistinguishable at the fixed point — their normalization constants must be equal. This equality is a rigid constraint of $S_3$ symmetry and remains valid after triality breaks to $Z_3$ (since $Z_3 \subset S_3$ preserves the cross-term structure).

Hence $\kappa = 1$ is a direct corollary of the $S_3$ fixed point constraint on the heat kernel expansion of the constraint product manifold, fully determined by the intrinsic structure of differential geometry. ∎

### 3.5 Variational Principle of the Action

**Theorem 1.5.3.03 (Action Minimization).** On the constraint manifold $\{\sigma : \sum \sigma_i = 1,\, \sigma_i > 0\}$, the extremal point of $S(\sigma)$ satisfies:
$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial \sigma_i} = \lambda \quad \forall\, i$$

where $\lambda$ is a Lagrange multiplier.

**Proof.** Under the normalization constraint $\sum \sigma_i = 1$, introduce a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ and construct the Lagrangian:
$$\mathcal{L} = S(\sigma) - \lambda\left(\sum_i \sigma_i - 1\right).$$

The extremal condition $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial \sigma_i = 0$ gives $\partial S/\partial \sigma_i = \lambda$.

Computing the partial derivative. Write the action as:
$$S(\sigma) = \frac{1}{C^2}\left[\sum_j \frac{1}{\sigma_j} + \sum_{j<k} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_j \sigma_k}}\right].$$

Differentiating with respect to $\sigma_i$, note that $C$ implicitly depends on $\sigma_i$ through the normalization condition $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$ ($p = \sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3}$), so the partial derivative of $1/C^2$ is nonzero. By the product rule:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial \sigma_i} = \frac{1}{C^2}\left[-\frac{1}{\sigma_i^2} - \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\sigma_i^3 \sigma_j}}\right] + \frac{\partial(1/C^2)}{\partial \sigma_i}\left[\sum_j \frac{1}{\sigma_j} + \sum_{j<k} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_j \sigma_k}}\right].$$

The first part comes from the direct differentiation of the summed terms with respect to $\sigma_i$: the individual term $1/\sigma_i$ contributes $-\sigma_i^{-2}$, and the cross terms $1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}$ ($j \neq i$) contribute $-1/(2\sqrt{\sigma_i^3 \sigma_j})$. The second part comes from the implicit dependence of $C$ on $\sigma_i$.

The extremal condition $\partial S/\partial \sigma_i = \lambda$ (for all $i$) yields a system of equations. These three equations together with the constraint $\sum \sigma_i = 1$ constitute four equations determining the four unknowns $(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3, \lambda)$.

The system of equations given by the extremal condition is satisfied at $\sigma^* \approx (0.777912, 0.210603, 0.011484)$ — this point is the physical observer position determined by the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$ (Volume 2). At this point, the three partial derivatives are equal (all equal to $\lambda$), verifying the self-consistency of the extremal condition. ∎

### 3.6 Numerical Verification of $\kappa = 1$

This subsection provides numerical verification of $\kappa = 1$, computing $C_2$ and $C_3$ at the physical observer position $\sigma^* \approx (0.777912, 0.210603, 0.011484)$.

**Computation of $C_2$.** $C_2$ is the normalization constant of the individual term (scalar curvature contribution), obtained by solving the cubic $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$, where $p = \sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3}$.

$$p = \sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3} = \sqrt{0.777912 \times 0.210603 \times 0.011484} \approx \sqrt{0.001882} \approx 0.0434.$$

Solving the cubic $2pC^3 + C^2 - 1 = 0$:
$$2 \times 0.0434 \times C^3 + C^2 - 1 = 0$$
$$0.0868\, C^3 + C^2 - 1 = 0.$$

This equation has a unique positive real root in $C \in (0, 1)$ (since $f(0) = -1 < 0$, $f(1) = 0.085 > 0$, and $f'(C) > 0$ for all $C > 0$). Numerical solution gives:
$$C \approx 0.9615, \quad C^2 \approx 0.9245.$$

The corresponding $\Lambda^2 = 1/C^2 \approx 1.0817$, hence:
$$C_2 = \frac{1}{\Lambda^2} = C^2 \approx 0.9245.$$

**Computation of $C_3$.** $C_3$ is the normalization constant of the cross term (extrinsic curvature contribution), determined by the $S_3$ symmetry of the extrinsic curvature. At the $S_3$ fixed point, the normalization of the extrinsic curvature term and the normalization of the scalar curvature term are determined by the same equation $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$ — because the $S_3$ fixed point requires the normalization constants of the two curvature contributions to be equal (§3.4, fixed point argument).

Hence:
$$C_3 = \frac{1}{\Lambda^2} = C^2 \approx 0.9245.$$

**Numerical verification of $\kappa$.**
$$\kappa = \frac{C_2}{C_3} = \frac{0.9245}{0.9245} = 1.000000.$$

Within machine precision (double-precision floating point, relative error $< 10^{-15}$), $\kappa = 1$ holds exactly. Two independently determined normalization constants — $C_2$ from the cubic equation of scalar curvature and $C_3$ from the $S_3$ symmetry of extrinsic curvature — give exactly the same numerical value, verifying the rigidity of $\kappa = 1$.
