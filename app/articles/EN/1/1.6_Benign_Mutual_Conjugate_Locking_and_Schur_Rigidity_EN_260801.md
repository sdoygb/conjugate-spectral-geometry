### 1.3 Precise Definition of Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking

To eliminate any suspicion of circular reasoning that the term "mutual conjugate" might evoke, this section provides a rigorous definition of Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking.

**Definition 22 (Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking).** Two derivation chains $P$ and $Q$ form a Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking if and only if the following four conditions are simultaneously satisfied:

1. **$P\implies Q$**: the conclusion of $P$ is a premise of $Q$, i.e., the output of $P$ serves as the input of $Q$.
2. **$Q\implies P$**: the conclusion of $Q$ is a premise of $P$, i.e., the output of $Q$ serves as the input of $P$.
3. **External anchoring**: there exists an external constraint $C$, independent of both $P$ and $Q$, such that both $P$ and $Q$ satisfy $C$.
4. **Uniqueness**: under the constraint $C$, the mutual conjugate solution of $P$ and $Q$ is unique.

Condition 3 is the key to ruling out circular reasoning. In ordinary circular reasoning, $P$ and $Q$ merely corroborate each other, lacking an external reference and hence having no independent truth value. Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking, by contrast, requires $P$ and $Q$ to be simultaneously anchored by an external constraint $C$ (e.g., Bott periodicity, the initial budget $B_1=\{2,3,5,5\}$, the Clifford algebra structure) that is independent of both. Therefore $P$ and $Q$ are not self-proving, but are jointly constrained by the external constraint, whose existence and uniqueness guarantee the mutual conjugate solution.

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the external constraint $C$ takes the specific forms:
- Bott periodicity $\delta^8$ (Berry phase $= 2\pi$) — independent of any derivation chain
- Initial budget $B_1=\{2,3,5,5\}$ — produced by the algebraic extension Cl(0)→Cl(7)
- The semisimple structure of the Clifford algebra Cl(3) — independent of the Multiplier Sequence
- The $S_3$ symmetry of Spin(8) triality — independent of the action form

These external anchors ensure that the five-layer mutual conjugate locking is not circular reasoning, but a self-consistent structure supported by algebraic rigidity.

### 2.3 Layer-by-Layer Analysis of the Five-Layer Rigidity

Schur Rigidity is not a single "global rigidity," but a five-layer nested rigidity structure. Each layer of rigidity locks a specific set of non-adjustable parameters and corresponds to a specific type of breaking consequence. Below we analyze each layer.

#### First Layer: Bott Periodicity Rigidity ($\delta^8$ cycle)

- **Source of rigidity**: the Berry phase $= 2\pi$ of the Bott periodicity $\delta^8$ is a topological invariant. The Bott periodicity table rigidifies the classification of real Clifford algebras; the period length 8 is guaranteed by the algebraic topology theorem and cannot be adjusted by internal parameters of the system.
- **What is locked**: the 7-step truncation of the coding orbit ($E_1$ through $E_7$). Since Berry phase closure requires a cycle of length 8, the truncation length must be 7 (the last step $E_8$ closes back to $E_0$).
- **Non-adjustable parameters**: period length $= 8$ (Bott theorem), truncation length $= 7$ (Berry phase closure condition).
- **Breaking consequence**: if the period $\neq 8$, Berry phase $\neq 2\pi$, the coding orbit does not close, and the entire system collapses. Specifically, the Multiplier Sequence cannot return to its starting point, $N_7$ loses its definition, and all four subsequent layers become invalid.

Bott periodicity rigidity is the deepest anchor of the entire system; all higher-level rigidities indirectly depend on this layer.

#### Second Layer: Budget Rigidity (initial budget $B_1=\{2,3,5,5\}$)

- **Source of rigidity**: the prime factor distribution produced by the algebraic extension Cl(0)→Cl(1)→⋯→Cl(7). At each step, the extension $\rho_{n-1}\to\rho_n$ introduces new prime factors or multiplicities, finally accumulating to $B_1$ at $E_7$.
- **What is locked**: the set of available prime factors $\{2,3,5\}$ and their initial distribution.
- **Non-adjustable parameters**: the elements and multiplicities of $B_1$ (4 factors: $2^1, 3^1, 5^2$).
- **Breaking consequence**: if $B_1$ differed, the Multiplier Sequence $\mu_n$ would change completely, and the coding orbit would point to a different $N_7$. Specifically, any change in the budget would alter the candidate sets of numerators and denominators for each $\mu_n$, thereby changing the numerical value of $N_7$, which would destroy the alignment with experimental observations.

The deep significance of budget rigidity: the prime factor set $\{2,3,5\}$ is not an artificial choice, but is inevitably produced by the arithmetic structure of the Clifford algebra extension under Bott periodicity.

#### Third Layer: Multiplier Sequence Rigidity ($\mu_1$ through $\mu_6$)

- **Source of rigidity**: the deterministic derivation of the Budget-Genotype double-layer structure. Given $B_1$ and Bott truncation, the genotype (prime factor composition of numerator and denominator) of each $\mu_n$ is uniquely determined by budget conservation and the First Appearance Rule.
- **What is locked**: the exact value of each $\mu_n$.
- **Non-adjustable parameters**: the numerators and denominators of the 6 multipliers (12 integers in total).
- **Mutual conjugate locking**: the parameterized mutual conjugate of $\mu_1=6 \leftrightarrow \mu_2=100/3$ (see 1.1 §5). This mutual conjugate means that $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are not independently chosen — given one, the other is uniquely determined by budget conservation.
- **Breaking consequence**: any change to a $\mu_n$ would cause $B_7\neq\emptyset$, violating Bott truncation. Specifically, if some $\mu_n$ deviates from its rigid value, the budget cannot be cleared at $E_7$, and the residual factors $B_7\neq\emptyset$ mean the coding orbit is not closed, destroying the Berry phase condition.

Multiplier sequence rigidity is the most numerically sensitive of the five layers — changing any one of the 12 integers would cause $N_7$ to deviate from $2.7\times 10^8$.

#### Fourth Layer: Sector-Factor Binding Rigidity ($\gamma_1\leftrightarrow 2$, $\gamma_2\leftrightarrow 3$, $\gamma_3\leftrightarrow 5$)

- **Source of rigidity**: Schur Rigidity and the First Appearance Rule. During the Clifford algebra extension, the first-appearance positions of the prime factors 2, 3, 5 determine their binding to the three sectors $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$.
- **What is locked**: the unique binding of the three sectors to the three prime factors.
- **Non-adjustable parameters**: the binding scheme (the unique self-consistent one among 6 candidates).
- **Breaking consequence**: an incorrect binding causes a mismatch between the algebraic signature of a sector and the arithmetic properties of its factor. For example, if $\gamma_1$ were bound to 3 rather than 2, the commutator structure of the $\gamma_1$ sector would conflict with the prime factor properties of 3, and the $\sqrt{\sigma}$ proportionality of the Born Rule would no longer hold.

The crucial aspect of sector-factor binding rigidity: among the 6 permutations, only 1 satisfies all constraints of Schur Rigidity. This uniqueness is an important source of falsifiability — if experimentally measured sector weights were inconsistent with the binding scheme, this layer would be directly overturned.

#### Fifth Layer: Born Rule Rigidity ($\Delta\lambda_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i}$)

- **Source of rigidity**: the Projection Intensity Identity of the Cl(3) semisimple algebra. The irreducible representation structure of Cl(3) forces the bilinearity of the commutator to compel the spectral gap to be proportional to $\sqrt{\sigma}$, not $\sigma$ or $\sigma^2$.
- **What is locked**: the $\sqrt{\sigma}$ proportionality relation between the spectral gap and the sector weight.
- **Non-adjustable parameters**: the form of the proportionality relation ($\sqrt{\sigma}$ rather than $\sigma$ or $\sigma^2$).
- **Breaking consequence**: if the proportionality relation changed (e.g., to $\Delta\lambda_i \propto \sigma_i$), the cubic equation $2pC^3 + C^2=1$ of the algebraic normalization would not hold, and $C$ could not be determined. Specifically, the $\sqrt{\sigma}$ proportionality guarantees that the coefficient $p$ in the cubic is rational, whereas a $\sigma$ or $\sigma^2$ proportionality would introduce non-algebraic terms, destroying the algebraicity of the action.

Born Rule rigidity is the bridge connecting the quantum mechanical measurement postulate to algebraic derivation — it demotes the Born Rule from a postulate to a theorem.

### 2.4 Hierarchical Dependency Relations of the Five-Layer Rigidity

The five-layer rigidity is not parallel and independent, but forms a strict hierarchical dependency chain:

```
Bott Periodicity Rigidity (Layer 1)
```

Each layer of rigidity depends on the preceding layer, but it is not a simple one-way dependency — mutual conjugate lockings exist (e.g., the parameterized mutual conjugate $\mu_1\leftrightarrow\mu_2$ crosses within Layer 3). Specifically:

- **Layer 1 → Layer 2**: Bott periodicity $\delta^8$ determines the extension chain Cl(0)→Cl(7), thereby determining $B_1=\{2,3,5,5\}$. This is a one-way dependency.
- **Layer 2 → Layer 3**: the budget $B_1$ together with the recycling constraint ($B_7=\emptyset$) jointly determine $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_6$. Within this layer exists the $\mu_1\leftrightarrow\mu_2$ mutual conjugate locking.
- **Layer 3 → Layer 4**: the First Appearance Rule of the Multiplier Sequence determines the binding of prime factors 2, 3, 5 to sectors $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$. Schur Rigidity excludes the other 5 candidates.
- **Layer 4 → Layer 5**: the Sector-Factor Binding determines the representation structure of Cl(3), thereby determining the $\sqrt{\sigma}$ proportionality of the Born Rule.

The hierarchical dependency chain implies: **breaking any layer cascades to all lower layers**. For example, breaking Layer 1 (Bott periodicity) would invalidate Layers 2–5 entirely; breaking Layer 3 (Multiplier Sequence) would invalidate Layers 4–5, though Layers 1–2 would still hold (albeit with incorrect numerical predictions).

If the locking were "malignant" — the joint constraints admit no solution — the system would be non-self-consistent and the theory would collapse.

The existence of Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking is guaranteed by:
- The Multiplier Sequence has a unique self-consistent solution
- The action minimum exists and is unique (Theorem 1.5.3.02)

The five-layer rigidity is not only a constraint on the algebraic structure, but also the source of falsifiability. ### 5.2 Cascade Effect of Falsification

Because the five-layer rigidity has hierarchical dependencies (§2.4), falsification has a cascade effect:

- **If $\alpha^{-1}\neq 137.036$**: this would overturn Layers 3–5 (Multiplier Sequence → Binding → Born Rule). The numerical prediction of $\alpha^{-1}$ depends on the joint output of these three layers; the failure of any one would cause the prediction to deviate. - **If $\sum m_\nu\neq 0.070$ eV**: this would overturn Layer 4 (Sector-Factor Binding). The neutrino mass sum directly depends on the correctness of the Sector-Factor Binding. Overturning Layer 4 would cascade to Layer 5 (Born Rule), but Layers 1–3 could still hold.
- **If Bott periodicity $\neq 8$**: this would overturn Layer 1, cascading to all 5 layers. This is the deepest falsification — no higher-level rigidity could survive.

### 5.3 Strength of Falsifiability

The falsifiability strength of Conjugate Spectral Geometry far exceeds that of traditional phenomenological theories:

1. **Zero free parameters**: there are no adjustable "buffers" — any deviation from experiment cannot be masked by parameter fitting.
2. **Precise numerical predictions**: the 95 theorems give exact numerical values (not ranges), making experimental tests binary decisions.
3. **Multi-channel testing**: the 95 theorems cover different physical quantities (Fine-Structure Constant, neutrino masses, sector weights, etc.); falsification through a single channel suffices to overturn parts of the rigidity layers.

This high-strength falsifiability is a direct consequence of Schur Rigidity — the stronger the rigidity, the smaller the adjustable space, and the stronger the falsifiability.
