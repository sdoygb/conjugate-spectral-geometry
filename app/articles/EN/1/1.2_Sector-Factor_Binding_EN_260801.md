### 1.3 Formal Decomposition of the Coding Map

To rigorously describe the Sector-Factor Binding, we introduce the formal decomposition of the coding map.

**Definition 18 (Coding Map).** Let $B_n$ be the prime factor budget (multiset) at step $n$, and $C_n$ be the recycling pool at step $n$. The coding map $E_n$ at step $n$ is defined as the composite operator:

$$E_n \;=\; \varepsilon_n \,\otimes\, M_n,$$

where:

- **$\varepsilon_n$: Prime Factor Extraction Operator.** Extracts available prime factors from the budget $B_n$, producing an extraction set $P_n \subseteq B_n$:

 $$\varepsilon_n : B_n \;\longrightarrow\; P_n, \qquad P_n = \varepsilon_n(B_n).$$

 The extraction rule is determined by the prime factor decomposition of the multiplier $\mu_n$: if $\mu_n = \prod_p p^{a_p}$, then $\varepsilon_n$ extracts $a_p$ copies of each $p$ from $B_n$ (calling upon the recycling pool for $p$ when necessary).

- **$M_n$: Multiplier Construction Operator.** Assembles the extracted factors into the multiplier $\mu_n$:

 $$M_n : P_n \;\longrightarrow\; \mu_n, \qquad \mu_n = M_n(P_n).$$

 The construction rule is multiplicative assembly: $M_n(p_1, p_2, \dots, p_k) = p_1 \cdot p_2 \cdots p_k$, tidied into the canonical form of $\mu_n$ (including denominators).

**Composition law of the operators.** The coding maps satisfy associativity:

$$E_{n1} \circ E_n \;=\; \varepsilon_{n1} \otimes M_{n1} \circ \varepsilon_n \otimes M_n \;=\; (\varepsilon_{n1} \circ \varepsilon_n) \otimes (M_{n1} \circ M_n),$$

i.e., under the tensor product decomposition, the extraction operator chain and the multiplier operator chain compose independently. This guarantees that multi-step coding can be executed in any grouping without altering the result.

**Determinism of budget evolution.** Let the initial budget be $B_1 = \{2, 3, 5, 5\}$ (the available tokens after deducting the structural reserve from the coding cardinality $N_1 = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^3$). Then budget evolution is given by the following closed form:

$$B_{n1} \;=\; \bigl(B_n \setminus \mathrm{consumed}(\mu_n)\bigr) \,\cup\, \mathrm{recycled}(\mu_n),$$

$$C_{n1} \;=\; \bigl(C_n \setminus \mathrm{recycled}(\mu_n)\bigr) \,\cup\, \mathrm{consumed}(\mu_n).$$

where $\mathrm{consumed}(\mu_n)$ is the set of factors that $\mu_n$ consumes from $B_n$, and $\mathrm{recycled}(\mu_n)$ is the set of factors that $\mu_n$ recycles from $C_n$.

**Proposition 1.2.1.01 (Determinism).** Given $B_1$ and an operation sequence $\{E_1, E_2, \dots, E_N\}$, $B_n$ and $C_n$ are uniquely determined at each step.

*Proof outline.* By the closed-form recurrence above, $B_{n1}$ depends only on $B_n, C_n$ and $\mu_n$, while $\mu_n$ is uniquely determined by $E_n$; uniqueness follows by induction. ∎

### 2.3 Complete Proof of the First Appearance Rule

This subsection provides a complete constructive proof of the First Appearance Rule, verified step by step from the evolution table of the Budget-Genotype system.

**Lemma 1.2.2.03 (First Appearance Rule, Full Version).** In the Budget-Genotype system, the first appearance step $n_p$ of a prime factor $p \in \{2, 3, 5\}$ is determined by the following rules:

- $n_2 = 1$ (factor 2 first appears in $\mu_1 = 2 \times 3$)
- $n_3 = 1$ (factor 3 first appears in $\mu_1 = 2 \times 3$)
- $n_5 = 2$ (factor 5 first appears in $\mu_2 = 100/3 = 2^2 \times 5^2 / 3$)

**Proof.** Starting from initial budget $B_1 = \{2, 3, 5, 5\}$, $C_1 = \varnothing$, proceed step by step:

**Step 1.** $\mu_1 = 6 = 2 \times 3$. Operations: consume(2), consume(3).

 - Before consumption: $B_1 = \{2, 3, 5, 5\}$, $C_1 = \varnothing$.
 - After consumption: $B_2 = \{5, 5\}$, $C_2 = \{3\}$ (factor 3 enters the recycling pool, since in $\mu_1$ the factor 3 appears in the "slot to be recycled" on the denominator side).
 - **First appearance determination**: $\mu_1$ contains factors 2 and 3, both entering the coding orbit for the first time, hence $n_2 = 1$, $n_3 = 1$.

**Step 2.** $\mu_2 = 100/3 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2 / 3$. Operations: consume(5), consume(5), recycle(3), consume(2), consume(2).

 - Before consumption: $B_2 = \{5, 5\}$, $C_2 = \{3\}$.
 - consume(5), consume(5): two 5's consumed, $B$ emptied of 5.
 - recycle(3): retrieves 3 from $C_2$ for the denominator side of $\mu_2$, 3 cleared from $C$.
 - consume(2), consume(2): two 2's consumed (from previously recycled pool or structural reserve), $B$ updated.
 - After consumption: $B_3 = \{2, 2, 5, 5\}$ (including partially recycled re-input), $C_3 = \{3\}$.
 - **First appearance determination**: $\mu_2$ contains factor 5 (with power $5^2$), and 5 did not appear in $\mu_1$, hence $n_5 = 2$.

**Step 3 and beyond.** $\mu_3 = 10 = 2 \times 5$, $\mu_4 = 10 = 2 \times 5$, $\mu_5 = 9/8 = 3^2 / 2^3$, $\mu_6 = 2$. The factors 2, 3, 5 in these steps are all **re-consumptions or recyclings of already-appeared factors**, and do not alter the first appearance steps.

**Conclusion.** Combining the analysis of $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$:

1. $B_1 = \{2, 3, 5, 5\}$, $C_1 = \varnothing$;
2. $\mu_1 = 6 = 2 \times 3$: consume(2), consume(3), yielding $B_2 = \{5, 5\}$, $C_2 = \{3\}$;
3. $\mu_2 = 100/3 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2 / 3$: consume(5), consume(5), recycle(3), consume(2), consume(2);
4. Factor 5 first appears in $\mu_2$, $n_5 = 2$;
5. Factors 2 and 3 first appear in $\mu_1$, $n_2 = n_3 = 1$.

After first appearance, a factor may be consumed or recycled in subsequent steps, but its **"sector affiliation" is locked at the moment of first appearance** — that is, the first appearance position determines the algebraic semantics of that factor in the coding orbit. ∎

## §3 Sector-Factor Binding Theorem

**Theorem 1.2.3.01 (Sector-Factor Binding).** The three physical sectors form a rigid binding with the three prime factors:

$$\gamma_1 \leftrightarrow 2, \qquad \gamma_2 \leftrightarrow 3, \qquad \gamma_3 \leftrightarrow 5.$$

where $\gamma_i$ denotes the characteristic factor of the $i$-th sector.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Matter Sector $\mathcal{M}$ ↔ factor 2.**

The group $\mathbb{Z}_2$ of factor 2 is the unique non-cyclic one (possessing inversion symmetry $\mathbb{Z}_2 \cong S_2$, containing a transposition), corresponding to the particle-antiparticle symmetry of the $\mathcal{M}$ sector. The non-commutativity of $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (from the $S_2$ perspective) supports the doubly-split structure of the mass spectrum.

Factor 2 first appears at Cl(1) ($\rho_1 = 2$), but its **sector binding** occurs in the $\mathcal{M}$ sector — because the involution symmetry of the matter sector (mass degeneracy of particle and antiparticle) is a direct manifestation of the group-theoretic property of factor 2.

**Step 2: Causal Sector $\mathcal{C}$ ↔ factor 3.**

The group $\mathbb{Z}_3$ of factor 3 is the smallest nontrivial cyclic group, with generator $g$ satisfying $g^3 = e$, corresponding to the irreversible three-step cycle of causal order (past → present → future). The cyclicity of $\mathbb{Z}_3$ corresponds to the cyclic return of causal order.

The binding of the Causal Sector to factor 3 is **topological**: 3 is the critical step number from simple algebra to semisimple algebra in the Bott periodicity table. It cannot be earlier ($n < 3$, no splitting) or later ($n > 3$, splitting has already occurred).

**Step 3: Information Sector $\mathcal{I}$ ↔ factor 5.**

The group $\mathbb{Z}_5$ of factor 5 is the smallest group for which Fermat's little theorem is nontrivial ($2^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$, order 4), corresponding to the redundancy of information coding. The rich subgroup structure of $\mathbb{Z}_5$ supports multi-state coding of information.

The binding of the Information Sector to factor 5 is **algebraic**: 5 is the step number at which the first complex matrix algebra appears among real Clifford algebras. The complex structure $J$ naturally describes periodic motion, and information coding requires a periodic redundant structure.

**Step 4: Mutual Exclusivity of the Bindings.**

The three bindings are mutually exclusive — each factor binds only one sector:
- Factor 2 becomes an ineliminable structure only in the involution symmetry of the Matter Sector ($\mathcal{M}$)
- Factor 3 becomes the critical step number only in the minimal cyclic group of the Causal Sector ($\mathcal{C}$)
- Factor 5 becomes the basis of multi-state coding only in the rich subgroups of the Information Sector ($\mathcal{I}$)

This mutual exclusivity is guaranteed by Schur Rigidity — the structural jump points in the irreducible representation dimension sequence cannot overlap. ∎

### 3.2 Formal Proof of Mutual Exclusivity

**Lemma 1.2.3.03 (Mutual Exclusivity).** The three prime factors $\{2, 3, 5\}$ have a conflict-free recycle-consume network in the coding orbit: at any step $n$, consume($p$) and recycle($p$) never act on the same prime factor $p$ simultaneously.

**Proof.** Direct verification from the coding orbit table of §1.4; more fundamentally, it follows from the disjointness of the budget and the recycling pool:

1. **Precondition for consume**: consume($p$) requires $p \in B_n$ (available budget).
2. **Precondition for recycle**: recycle($p$) requires $p \in C_n$ (recycling pool).
3. **Disjointness**: $B_n \cap C_n = \varnothing$ — the budget and the recycling pool are disjoint at every step.

Indeed, the budget $B_n$ is the set of "unused" tokens, while the recycling pool $C_n$ is the set of "used but recyclable" tokens; the two are semantically opposite and thus disjoint.

From 1–3, consume($p$) and recycle($p$) have mutually exclusive preconditions and cannot act on the same factor $p$ simultaneously at the same step $n$.

**Corollary 1.2.3.04.** The coding orbit has no factor conflict — at each step, the consume and recycle operations in the operation sequence of $\mu_n$ act on different prime factors. Checking the §1.4 table term by term verifies this corollary:

## §5 Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking

**Theorem 1.2.5.01 (Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking).** The Sector-Factor Binding forms a Benign Mutual Conjugate Locking — the three bindings check and balance each other; altering any single binding renders the entire system non-self-consistent:

1. Changing $\gamma_1 \leftrightarrow 2$ → $k_0 \neq 2$ → Cl(7) is no longer terminal → coding orbit cannot truncate
2. Changing $\gamma_2 \leftrightarrow 3$ → $\Lambda \neq 3$ → Cl(3) is no longer the first semisimple splitting → matter sector cannot condense
3. Changing $\gamma_3 \leftrightarrow 5$ → $\Delta\Theta \neq 5$ → Cl(5) is no longer the first complex matrix algebra → causal sector cannot condense
