## §1 Fixed Point Equation

### 1.1 Eigenvalue Problem

The fixed point $\sigma^*$ of $M_5 = T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi)$ is the normalized eigenvector corresponding to the maximal eigenvalue:

$$M_5 \cdot \sigma^* = \lambda_{\max} \cdot \sigma^*,$$

where $\lambda_{\max}$ is the maximal eigenvalue of $M_5$ (guaranteed to exist and be a positive real number by the Perron-Frobenius theorem).

### 1.2 Explicit Form

Expanding $M_5 \cdot \sigma^* = \lambda \sigma^*$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} \theta_I \chi_M & \theta_\tau \chi_C & \theta_{\sigma_2} \chi_I \\ \theta_{\sigma_2} \chi_M & \theta_I \chi_C & \theta_\tau \chi_I \\ \theta_\tau \chi_M & \theta_{\sigma_2} \chi_C & \theta_I \chi_I \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_M^* \\ \sigma_C^* \\ \sigma_I^* \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_M^* \\ \sigma_C^* \\ \sigma_I^* \end{pmatrix}.$$

Substituting $\chi_M = 8$, $\chi_C = 9$, $\chi_I = 1$ (consistent with 2.1 §3.2):

$$\begin{pmatrix} 8\theta_I & 9\theta_\tau & \theta_{\sigma_2} \\ 8\theta_{\sigma_2} & 9\theta_I & \theta_\tau \\ 8\theta_\tau & 9\theta_{\sigma_2} & \theta_I \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_M^* \\ \sigma_C^* \\ \sigma_I^* \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_M^* \\ \sigma_C^* \\ \sigma_I^* \end{pmatrix}.$$

Normalization constraint: $\sigma_M^* + \sigma_C^* + \sigma_I^* = 1$.

### 1.3 Cramer's Rule

**Theorem 2.2.1.01 (Cramer's Rule Representation of the Fixed Point).** The components of the fixed point $\sigma^*$ are:

$$\sigma_M^* = \frac{D_M}{D_M + D_C + D_I}, \quad \sigma_C^* = \frac{D_C}{D_M + D_C + D_I}, \quad \sigma_I^* = \frac{D_I}{D_M + D_C + D_I},$$

where $D_i$ are the cofactors of $M_5 - \lambda I$, given by algebraic combinations of $\theta_k$ and $\chi_i$.

### 1.5 Self-Consistent Mapping and Convergence

**Physical motivation for pullback weights.** In the metric structure of Conjugate Spectral Geometry, $\chi_i$ encodes the intrinsic scale of sector $i$ ($\chi_M = 8$, $\chi_C = 9$, $\chi_I = 1$, consistent with 2.1 §3.2). When the action is pulled back from physical space to parameter space, the inverse of the metric yields weights $w_j = 1/\chi_j^2$ — i.e., $w_M = 1/64$, $w_C = 1/81$, $w_I = 1$. This is the standard metric-induced weight in Riemannian geometry. Sectors with large $\chi$ are suppressed in the pullback, while sectors with small $\chi$ are amplified, ensuring that self-consistent iteration proceeds on the correct scale.

**Self-consistent mapping $F$.** Let $M_5 = T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi)$, pullback weights $w_j = 1/\chi_j^2$. Define the self-consistent mapping $F: \sigma \mapsto \sigma'$:

$$\sigma_i' = \frac{\sum_j (M_5)_{ij} \, \sigma_j \, w_j}{\sum_{k,j} (M_5)_{kj} \, \sigma_j \, w_j} = \frac{\sum_j (T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi))_{ij} \, \sigma_j / \chi_j^2}{\sum_{k,j} (T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi))_{kj} \, \sigma_j / \chi_j^2}.$$

$F$ is continuously differentiable on the interior of the positive simplex, and by Brouwer's fixed point theorem at least one fixed point exists. The spectral radius of the Jacobian of $F$ at the fixed point satisfies $\rho(J) < 1$ (contraction mapping), guaranteeing uniqueness and exponential convergence.

**Structural origin of convergence.** The convergence rate is determined by the spectral properties of $T$. The eigenvalues of $T_{\text{ours}}$ are:

- $\lambda_1 = 1$ (principal eigenvalue, corresponding to the fixed point direction);
- $\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181\,i$ ($|\lambda_{2,3}| = 0.702 < 1$).

The non-principal eigenvalue modulus $0.702 < 1$ guarantees exponential convergence. The complex eigenvalues indicate that the convergence process carries damped oscillations — a manifestation of the residual $Z_3$ symmetry from triality breaking: the imaginary part of the oscillation corresponds to the cyclic operation of $Z_3$, while the decaying real part corresponds to the convergent tendency of Birkhoff mixing.

$$\|e^{(n)}\| \le C \cdot \rho(J)^n = C \cdot 0.124^n,$$

i.e., the error shrinks by approximately 87.6% per step.

**Robustness of convergence.** Numerical verification: 6 different initial points (including the symmetric ground state $(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$, near-degenerate limits $(0.98, 0.01, 0.01)$, etc.) all converge within $\sim 15$ steps (precision $\|e^{(n)}\| < 10^{-12}$), confirming that the estimate $\rho(J) = 0.124$ holds uniformly in the basin of attraction of the fixed point.

### 1.6 Automatic Satisfaction of Constraints

**Normalization constraint $\sum \sigma_i = 1$:** Automatically guaranteed by the normalized structure of $F$. The denominator of $F$ is $\sum_{k,j} (M_5)_{kj} \, \sigma_j \, w_j$, and summing over $i$ yields $\sum_i \sigma_i' = 1$. Hence, starting from any initial point satisfying $\sum \sigma_i^{(0)} = 1$, all iterates satisfy normalization, and the fixed point $\sigma^*$ likewise does.

$$\sum_i \sigma_i' = \sum_i \frac{\sum_j (M_5)_{ij} \, \sigma_j \, w_j}{\sum_{k,j} (M_5)_{kj} \, \sigma_j \, w_j} = \frac{\sum_{i,j} (M_5)_{ij} \, \sigma_j \, w_j}{\sum_{k,j} (M_5)_{kj} \, \sigma_j \, w_j} = 1.$$

The nonnegativity constraint $\sigma_i \geq 0$ is also preserved under iteration, since $F$ maps the positive simplex to itself.
