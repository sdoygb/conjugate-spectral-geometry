## §1 Origin of Cross-Sector Coupling

### 1.1 Statement of the Problem

Volume 1 established the algebraic action $S(\sigma)$ (Theorem 1.3.7.01–26), whose minimum $S^*$ yields physical constants. However, the specific value of $S^*$ depends on the distribution of the coding weights $\sigma = (\sigma_M, \sigma_C, \sigma_I)$ — different $\sigma$ yield different $S^*$.

The distribution of $\sigma$ is determined by the coding process, but the coding process is not a simple linear mapping — cross-sector coupling causes the coding weights of different sectors to influence one another. This article defines the precise mathematical form of this cross-sector coupling.

### 1.2 Cl(5)→Cl(6) Transition and Triality Breaking

The Cl(5)→Cl(6) transition is the point where triality breaking occurs in the coding orbit:

| Stage | Coding Layer | Triality State |
|:---:|:---:|:---|
| Full S₃ | E₁–E₃ | Three representations indistinguishable |
| **Breaking** | **E₄–E₅** | **S₃ → Z₂** |
| Z₃ Preserved | E₆ | Cyclic symmetry |
| Fully Broken | E₇ | No symmetry |

Triality breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$ means: before the Cl(5)→Cl(6) transition, the representations of the three sectors are interchangeable under $S_3$; after the transition, the cyclic subgroup $Z_3 = \{I, (1\,2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$ symmetry is preserved, while the three transpositions are eliminated. The cyclic structure of the coding orbit ($E_1 \to E_2 \to \cdots \to E_8$) naturally preserves $Z_3$ cyclic symmetry — this is precisely the algebraic root of why the Birkhoff mixing matrix $T$ takes circulant matrix form.

This breaking is not artificially introduced — it is the inevitable consequence of the algebraic structure change from Cl(5) = Mat(4,$\mathbb{C}$) to Cl(6) = Mat(8,$\mathbb{R}$). The complex structure $J = \omega_5$ is no longer preserved in Cl(6) ($\omega_6^2 = -1$ but $\omega_6$ does not commute with all generators), leading to a partial loss of triality. The preserved $Z_3$ corresponds to the cyclic structure of the coding orbit — cyclic permutations of the three sectors along the coding orbit preserve cyclic symmetry, while transposition operations (causal inversion) are suppressed under $Z_3$ breaking.

## §2 Birkhoff Doubly Stochastic Matrix

### 2.1 Definition

**Definition 10 (Birkhoff Doubly Stochastic Matrix).** A $3 \times 3$ matrix $T$ is doubly stochastic if:

1. All entries are nonnegative: $T_{ij} \geq 0$
2. Each row sums to 1: $\sum_j T_{ij} = 1$
3. Each column sums to 1: $\sum_i T_{ij} = 1$

The set of $3 \times 3$ doubly stochastic matrices constitutes the **Birkhoff polytope** $\mathcal{B}_3$ — a 4-dimensional convex polytope ($3 \times 3 - 2 \times 2 - 1 = 4$ degrees of freedom).


### 2.2 Three-Parameter Decomposition

**Theorem 2.1.2.01 (Three-Parameter Decomposition of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix).** The Birkhoff mixing matrix $T$ in the coding framework decomposes as a convex combination of three permutation matrices:

$$T = \theta_I \cdot I + \theta_\tau \cdot P_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} \cdot P_{\sigma_2},$$

where:
- $I$ is the identity permutation matrix
- $P_\tau$ is the permutation matrix of the 3-cycle $(1\,3\,2)$
- $P_{\sigma_2}$ is the permutation matrix of the 3-cycle $(1\,2\,3)$
- $\theta_I + \theta_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} = 1$ (double stochasticity)

$P_\tau = (1\,3\,2)$ acts as $(a, b, c) \mapsto (b, c, a)$, $P_{\sigma_2} = (1\,2\,3)$ acts as $(a, b, c) \mapsto (c, a, b)$. $T$ therefore takes **circulant matrix** form:

$$T = \begin{pmatrix} \theta_I & \theta_\tau & \theta_{\sigma_2} \\ \theta_{\sigma_2} & \theta_I & \theta_\tau \\ \theta_\tau & \theta_{\sigma_2} & \theta_I \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Proof.** By the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem, any doubly stochastic matrix is a convex combination of permutation matrices. $S_3$ has 6 elements (3 transpositions, 2 3-cycles, 1 identity), but triality breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$ preserves the cyclic subgroup $Z_3 = \{I, (1\,3\,2), (1\,2\,3)\}$, eliminating all transpositions:

| Z₃ Element | Permutation | Coefficient | Action |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|
| Identity | I | θ_I | (a,b,c) ↦ (a,b,c) |
| 3-cycle (1 3 2) | P_τ | θ_τ | (a,b,c) ↦ (b,c,a) |
| 3-cycle (1 2 3) | P_σ₂ | θ_σ₂ | (a,b,c) ↦ (c,a,b) |

The 3 transpositions $(1\,2)$, $(1\,3)$, $(2\,3)$ are suppressed under $Z_3$ breaking — cyclic symmetry forbids inversion operations. Hence $T$ is determined by 3 parameters $(\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})$, with the normalization constraint $\sum\theta = 1$, giving 2 effective degrees of freedom. The circulant matrix form is a direct algebraic consequence of $Z_3$ symmetry. ∎

### 2.3 2-Dimensional Face of the Birkhoff Polytope

The 3 parameters $(\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})$ of $T$ satisfy $\theta_I + \theta_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} = 1$ and $\theta_k \geq 0$ — this defines a 2-dimensional simplex (triangle). But the full Birkhoff polytope $\mathcal{B}_3$ is 4-dimensional — our 3-parameter decomposition covers only a 2-dimensional face of $\mathcal{B}_3$.

This 2-dimensional face is precisely the degrees of freedom permitted by triality breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$ — the $Z_3$ cyclic symmetry constrains the 4-dimensional polytope to a 2-dimensional face.

**Dimensional analysis of the feasible region.** The feasible region of the Birkhoff polytope $\mathcal{B}(3)$ in this problem is 2-dimensional. Specifically, a $3 \times 3$ doubly stochastic matrix has 6 permutation matrix vertices (the 6 elements of $S_3$), but is subject to 4 independent constraints (3 row sums $=1$ minus 1 redundancy — the sum of three rows equals the sum of three columns, so the row and column constraints each have one linear dependence; the actual number of independent constraints is $2 \times 3 - 2 = 4$), hence the degrees of freedom $= 9 - 5 = 4$ (5 = 4 independent constraints + 1 normalization). But triality breaking $S_3 \to Z_2$ preserves the cyclic subgroup $Z_3 = \{I, (1\,3\,2), (1\,2\,3)\}$, eliminating 3 transpositions, reducing the effective degrees of freedom to 2.

**Physical reason for dimensional reduction.** The reduction from 4 to 2 dimensions is not an arbitrary truncation but a necessary consequence of $Z_3$ symmetry. Under full $S_3$ symmetry, any of the 6 permutation matrix vertices could independently appear in the Birkhoff decomposition, yielding a 4-dimensional polytope. After $Z_3$ breaking, the 3 transpositions are completely suppressed, and only the 3 cyclic elements of $Z_3$ remain:

| Z₃ Preserved Elements | Permutation | Physical Sector |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Identity | I | Matter self-preservation |
| 3-cycle (1 3 2) | P_τ | Causal cycle |
| 3-cycle (1 2 3) | P_σ₂ | Information cycle |

The suppressed 3 transpositions $\{(1\,2), (1\,3), (2\,3)\}$ do not belong to the preserved subgroup under $Z_3$ and therefore do not participate in the Birkhoff decomposition. The 3 $Z_3$ elements plus the normalization constraint give exactly 2 degrees of freedom.
- Vertex $I$ ($\theta_I = 1$): pure identity, no cross-sector coupling, no mixing — physically corresponds to a completely non-interacting limit, inconsistent with observation.
- Vertex $P_\tau$ ($\theta_\tau = 1$): pure 3-cycle $(1\,3\,2)$, produces period-3 cycles (§4.4), non-convergent.
- Vertex $P_{\sigma_2}$ ($\theta_{\sigma_2} = 1$): pure 3-cycle $(1\,2\,3)$, likewise produces period-3 cycles.
- 3 transpositions $P_{(1\,2)}$, $P_{(1\,3)}$, $P_{(2\,3)}$: completely suppressed under $Z_3$ breaking, do not participate in the Birkhoff decomposition.

Thus, the physically feasible region is strictly restricted to the 2-dimensional face spanned by $I P_\tau P_{\sigma_2}$, with the three vertices corresponding to the three extreme sector-dominance scenarios. The analytic formula from Cramer's rule yields a unique solution within this face, guaranteeing unique determinacy of the Birkhoff weights.

## §3 M₅ Matrix

### 3.1 Definition

**Definition 11 ($M_5$ Cross-Sector Coupling Matrix).**
$$M_5 = T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi),$$

where:
- $T$ is the Birkhoff doubly stochastic matrix (Theorem 2.1.2.01)
- $\text{diag}(\chi) = \text{diag}(\chi_M, \chi_C, \chi_I)$ is the diagonal matrix of coding cumulative stretches

$M_5$ combines the coding cumulative stretches $\chi$ with the cross-sector mixing $T$ to produce the final sector coding weights $\sigma$:

$$\sigma = M_5 \cdot \sigma^{(0)} = T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi) \cdot \sigma^{(0)},$$

where $\sigma^{(0)}$ is the initial coding weight.

### 3.2 χ Values

The coding cumulative stretches $\chi_i$ are determined by the multiplier sequence and sector-factor binding (Theorem 1.1.3.01):

| Sector | Cumulative Stretch χ_i | Source |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| ℳ | χ_M = 8 | Matter component of μ₁ × μ₂ |
| 𝒞 | χ_C = 9 | Cumulative stretch of the causal sector (including triality-breaking complex structure contribution) |
| ℐ | χ_I = 1 | Information sector has no net stretch (recycling balance) |

**Detailed derivation of χ values.** The sector stretch factors $\chi$ are determined by the multiplier sequence and sector-factor binding (Theorem 1.1.3.01). Specifically:

- **χ_M = 8**: From the product of the first two multipliers μ₁ × μ₂ restricted to the matter sector. During coding layers E₁–E₃, the matter sector carries the full semisimple splitting burden, accumulating a stretch of 8.
- **χ_C = 9**: From the causal sector's accumulation across layers E₄–E₅. The triality breaking induces an additional complex structure factor, raising the stretch from the naive 8 to 9.
- **χ_I = 1**: The information sector operates on a recycling baseline — information is neither stretched nor compressed (conservation of coding measure). The recycling baseline invariance theorem (Theorem 1.1.4.01) guarantees χ_I = 1.

### 3.3 Fixed Point Equation

The fixed point $\sigma^*$ of $M_5$ satisfies:

$$M_5 \cdot \sigma^* = \lambda_{\max} \cdot \sigma^*,$$

where $\lambda_{\max}$ is the maximal eigenvalue of $M_5$ (guaranteed to exist and be a positive real number by the Perron-Frobenius theorem).

### 3.4 Cramer's Rule Analytic Formula for Birkhoff Weights

**Theorem 2.1.3.01 (Cramer's Rule for Birkhoff Weights).** The Birkhoff weights $(\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})$ are analytically determined by Cramer's rule as rational functions of the coding cumulative stretches $\chi$ and the fixed point $\sigma^*$.

## §4 Intrinsic Encoding of the Chern-Simons Three Terms

### 4.2 Intrinsic Encoding of Berry Phase

The three sectors ℳ, 𝒞, ℐ correspond to the three contributions to the Berry phase in the Bott period $n=8$ loop. The Birkhoff weights $(\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})$ encode the relative weights of the three Chern-Simons integrals $I_1, I_2, I_3$.

### 4.3 Convergence Analysis

The convergence of the Birkhoff mixing iteration is guaranteed by the spectral radius of the non-principal eigenvalues. For $T_{\text{ours}} = (0.782, 0.209, 0.009)$:

- $\lambda_1 = 1$ (principal eigenvalue, fixed-point direction);
- $\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181\,i$ ($|\lambda_{2,3}| = 0.702 < 1$).

The non-principal eigenvalue modulus $0.702 < 1$ guarantees exponential convergence. The complex eigenvalues indicate that the convergence process carries damped oscillations — a manifestation of the residual $Z_3$ symmetry from triality breaking.

## §5 Variation Range of T

### 5.1 2-Dimensional Birkhoff Polytope

$T$ varies on a 2-dimensional simplex: $\theta_I + \theta_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} = 1$, $\theta_k \geq 0$. Each point on this simplex corresponds to a different cross-sector coupling configuration.

### 5.2 Preview of the Landscape

Different $T$ values produce different $S = \alpha^{-1}$ values via the fixed point $\sigma^*(T)$ and the algebraic action $S(\sigma^*)$. The set of all possible $S$ values is the **landscape** $\mathcal{L}$ (detailed in 2.3).

### 5.3 Unistochastic Connection to CKM/PMNS Mixing Matrices

The Birkhoff mixing matrix $T$ has a natural connection to unistochastic matrices: $T_{ij} = |U_{ij}|^2$ for some unitary matrix $U$. This links the cross-sector coupling structure to the CKM and PMNS mixing matrices in the Standard Model.

### 5.4 Physical Meaning of T

$T$ encodes how the three sectors mix during the coding process:
- $\theta_I$ (identity): self-coupling within each sector
- $\theta_\tau$ (3-cycle $(1\,3\,2)$): causal cycle ℳ→𝒞→ℐ→ℳ
- $\theta_{\sigma_2}$ (3-cycle $(1\,2\,3)$): information cycle ℳ→ℐ→𝒞→ℳ
