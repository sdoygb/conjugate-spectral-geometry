## Chapter 3 Representation-Theoretic Classification of the 230 Space Groups

> **[Tool Layer Declaration]** The group representation theory used in this chapter (Frobenius reciprocity theorem, character formulas, induced representations) is standard mathematical tooling, not derived from the Sole Axiom δ (Zero Motion) of Geometric Theory. It is introduced as an analytical language to classify crystallographic symmetries on the geometric sector parameter space. The rigorous connection between group representation theory and the Geometric Theory framework is an open research direction (see Theorem 1.4.3.01).

### §3.1 Irreducible Representations of Point Groups

**Proposition 10.19.3.01 (Point Group Representation Compatibility)**: The information field spherical harmonic modes on the geometric sector parameter space $\Sigma \cong S^2$ are compatible with the irreducible representations of the 32 three-dimensional crystallographic point groups.

**Argument**: The information field modes on the coding orbit $\Sigma \cong S^2$ are expanded in spherical harmonics $Y_l^m$. Spherical harmonics transform under $SO(3)$ rotations according to irreducible representations $D^{(l)}$ (dimension $2l+1$). The 32 crystallographic point groups are discrete subgroups of $SO(3)$. By Frobenius reciprocity, the $SO(3)$ representation $D^{(l)}$ decomposes under the point group $G$ as a direct sum of irreducible representations of $G$:
$$D^{(l)}|_G = \bigoplus_\alpha n_\alpha^{(l)} D^{(\alpha)}$$
where $n_\alpha^{(l)}$ is the multiplicity of the irreducible representation $D^{(\alpha)}$, determined by the character formula:
$$n_\alpha^{(l)} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi^{(l)}(g)^* \chi^{(\alpha)}(g)$$
The decompositions of low-order spherical harmonics ($l=0,1,2$) naturally cover the 32 point groups: $l=0$ ($D^{(0)}$, 1-dim) → totally symmetric representation $A_{1g}$; $l=1$ ($D^{(1)}$, 3-dim) → decomposes as $A_{2u} \oplus E_u$ (cubic group) or $A_{2u} \oplus E_{1u}$ (hexagonal group); $l=2$ ($D^{(2)}$, 5-dim) → decomposes as $E_g \oplus T_{2g}$ (cubic group) or $E_{1g} \oplus E_{2g}$ (hexagonal group).

### §3.2 Projective Representations of Space Groups

**Proposition 10.19.3.02 (Projective Representations of Space Groups)**: The 230 space groups, as discrete subgroups of the Euclidean group $E(3)$, have their projective representations jointly determined by point group representations and Bloch's theorem for the translation group.

**Argument**: An element of the space group $S$ is $\{R|\mathbf{t}\}$, where $R \in G$ (point group) and $\mathbf{t}$ is a translation. By the Seitz representation, the space group acts on Hilbert space as:
$$\hat{P}(R|\mathbf{t})\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \psi(R^{-1}\mathbf{r} - R^{-1}\mathbf{t})$$
By Bloch's theorem, the irreducible representations of the translation subgroup $T$ are $\exp(i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{t})$, so the space group representation can be reduced to representations of the "little group" of the point group at $\mathbf{k}$. The complete classification of the 230 space groups is accomplished by induced representations from the little group representations.

### §3.3 Geometric Constraint on Coordination Number

**Proposition 10.19.3.03 (Geometric Constraint on Coordination Number)**: The coordination number (CN) is constrained by the irreducible representation dimensions of spherical harmonics on the coding orbit $S^2$ under point group actions. The irreducible decomposition of low-order modes ($l \leq 2$) provides an upper-bound estimate of the number of independent geometric directions.

The coordination number describes the nearest-neighbor interaction count of a central atom and is a crystallographic geometric parameter. On $S^2$, low-order spherical harmonic modes correspond to fundamental geometric directions: $l=0$ ($D^{(0)}$, 1-dim): 1 totally symmetric direction; $l=1$ ($D^{(1)}$, 3-dim): 3 independent spatial directions; $l=2$ ($D^{(2)}$, 5-dim): 5 independent quadrupole directions. Crystallographic point group symmetry further reduces the number of independent directions. This framework provides a qualitative constraint relationship between the number of geometric modes and the coordination number, not a quantitative equation.

**Proposition 10.19.4.01 (Representation-Theoretic Selection Rules for Orbital Activity)**: Under the point group $G$, atomic orbitals are classified by irreducible representations $D^{(\alpha)}$. The orbital degeneracy $N^{\mathrm{orb}}$ equals the dimension $d_\alpha$ of the irreducible representation.

**Argument**: The $d$ orbitals ($l=2$) decompose under the $O_h$ point group as $e_g$ (2-dim) and $t_{2g}$ (3-dim). **Proposition 10.19.5.01 (Representation-Theoretic Classification of Phonon Modes)**: Optical phonon modes at the Brillouin zone center are classified by irreducible representations of the point group; active modes are determined by character projection.

**Argument**: Phonon modes correspond to normal modes of lattice vibrations, transforming under the point group according to irreducible representations. By the character projection operator:
$$\hat{P}^{(\alpha)} = \frac{d_\alpha}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi^{(\alpha)}(g)^* \hat{P}(g)$$

### §6.2 Chern Classes and Topological Invariants

**Construction 6.2 (First Chern Class)**: If the construction of the complex line bundle $L$ is completed, its first Chern class $c_1(L) \in H^2(\Sigma; \mathbb{Z})$ is determined by the winding number of the complex percolation function level set:
$$c_1(L) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_\Sigma F = \frac{1}{2\pi} \oint_{\Phi_{\mathbb{C}}=\Phi_F} dA = n \in \mathbb{Z}$$
where $F = dA$ is the curvature 2-form and $n$ is the number of times the level set winds around the North pole of $S^2$.

**Key Statement**: The specific integer value of $c_1(L)$ is determined by the homotopy class of the clutching function $[\Sigma, U(1)] \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and is **not necessarily 2**. **Topological Invariants**: The number of connected components $N$ of the Fermi surface, the genus $g$ of each component, and the Chern number $c_1$, if well-defined, are topological invariants preserved under perturbations.

### §6.4 Homotopy Analysis of Connectivity
