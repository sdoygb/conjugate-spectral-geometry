## Chapter 2 Technical Skeleton of the Deng Work

### §2.1 Hard Spheres → Boltzmann (2408.07818)

**Physical Setting**: $N$ hard spheres of radius $\varepsilon$ undergoing elastic collisions on the $d$-dimensional torus $\mathbb{T}^d$. Initial positions and velocities are randomly distributed (close to equilibrium).

**Boltzmann-Grad Limit**:
$$N \to \infty, \quad \varepsilon \to 0, \quad N\varepsilon^{d-1} \to \beta > 0$$

Physical meaning: the number of particles goes to infinity, each particle shrinks to zero, but the mean free path remains a finite constant $1/\beta$. The collision frequency does not degenerate in the limit.

**Core Difficulty — Recollisions**: After particles $i$ and $j$ collide, they are pushed back by other particles and collide again. This destroys Boltzmann's Stosszahlansatz (molecular chaos hypothesis) — two particles are not statistically independent when they collide. For 140 years, all attempts at rigorously deriving the Boltzmann equation stalled here.

**Core Breakthrough — Collision Graph Classification Theorem**: All $N$-body collision histories are represented as graphs (vertices = particles, edges = collisions). Graphs are divided into two classes:

| Graph Type | Graph-Theoretic Property | Limit Behavior | Physical Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Tree collision graphs** | Acyclic (trees, forests) | Dominant (non-vanishing) | Particles scatter and never meet again |
| **Ring collision graphs** | Contain cycles (ring/recollision) | Measure → 0 (negligible) | Particles collide, then return and collide again |

**Core of the Theorem**: In the Boltzmann-Grad limit, the measure of ring collisions $\sim N\varepsilon^{d-1}$ (for $d \ge 3$) or $\sim \varepsilon \log N$ (for $d=2$) → 0. This is not trivial — at finite $N$ ring collisions do exist, but they are infinitely diluted in the limit. **The arrow of time and entropy increase emerge naturally from the process of ring structures being overwhelmed by tree structures.**

**Direct Corollary**: Many-body correlation functions factorize in the limit:
$$f_N(t, x_1, v_1, \ldots, x_N, v_N) \to \prod_{i=1}^{N} f_1(t, x_i, v_i)$$

This is the rigorously proved "molecular chaos" — Boltzmann's Stosszahlansatz holds in the Boltzmann-Grad limit.

### §2.2 Boltzmann → Fluid Equations (2503.01800)

Starting from the Boltzmann equation:

$$\partial_t f + v \cdot \nabla_x f = Q(f, f)$$

where $Q$ is the hard-sphere collision operator. Performing a Hilbert expansion near equilibrium (Maxwell distribution):

$$f = f_{\text{Maxwell}}(1 + \varepsilon h_1 + \varepsilon^2 h_2 + \cdots)$$

Substituting the expansion into the Boltzmann equation and matching order by order:
- Zeroth order: $f_{\text{Maxwell}}$ makes the collision term $Q=0$ (equilibrium)
- First order: yields the compressible Euler equations (inviscid, no heat conduction)
- Second order: yields the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (including viscosity and heat conduction)

The viscosity coefficient at second order comes from the projection of the linearized collision operator onto the non-hydrodynamic subspace — i.e., how collision relaxation "smoothes out" velocity-space anisotropy.

### §2.3 Arrow of Time and Ring Collapse

The profound significance of the Deng work lies not only in technical proof, but in providing a **dynamical origin of the arrow of time**:

- Microscopic dynamics (Newton's equations) are strictly time-reversible
- Ring collisions have measure → 0 in the limit; tree collisions dominate

This is philosophically consistent with our construction of causal time ($\tau = \sqrt{\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}} \cdot \eta$, Theorem 7.8.3.01): the arrow of time is not an appended axiom, but emerges from structural asymmetry.

### §4.2 Combinatorial Definition of Cumulants

**Definition 4.3 (k-Point Joint Density)**:
$$\rho^{(k)}_\tau(i_1, \ldots, i_k) = \mathbb{E}_{T_{ss}^\tau}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k} \delta_{X_j, i_j}\right]$$

where $X_j$ is the node-position random variable of the $j$-th information trajectory.

**Definition 4.4 (k-th Order Information Field Cumulant)**: The $k$-th order cumulant is defined via the standard combinatorial formula:

$$\boxed{C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau) = \sum_{\pi \in \Pi_k} (-1)^{|\pi|-1} (|\pi|-1)! \prod_{B \in \pi} \rho^{(|B|)}_\tau(\{i_j\}_{j \in B})}$$

Equivalent generating function form:

$$C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau) = \left. \frac{\partial^k}{\partial t_1 \cdots \partial t_k} \log \mathbb{E}_{T_{ss}^\tau}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k} t_j \delta_{X_j, i_j}\right)\right] \right|_{t_1 = \cdots = t_k = 0}$$

**Physical Meaning**:
- $C_1(i) = \rho_\tau(i)$: first-order cumulant = marginal distribution
- $C_2(i, j) = \rho^{(2)}_\tau(i, j) - \rho_\tau(i)\rho_\tau(j)$: covariance
- $C_3(i, j, k)$: the residual after subtracting all pairwise correlations and triple independent products
- $C_k$ measures the **residual of information among $k$ nodes that cannot be explained by lower-order correlations**

**Key Property** (Lemma 5.6.3.02): Cumulants can be expanded as a weighted sum of connected paths on the graph $G_{\mathcal{I}}$ linking $k$ nodes. A connected path $\gamma$ is a subgraph of $G_{\mathcal{I}}$ whose vertex set is exactly $\{i_1, \ldots, i_k\}$ and which is connected:

$$C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau) = \sum_{\gamma: \text{connected k-path}} w(\gamma) \cdot \prod_{e \in \gamma} T_{ss}(e)^\tau$$

This expansion is the central bridge for the Doeblin coupling argument in Article 5.7 — the decay of cumulants is controlled by the decay of edge weights of connected paths under causal advancement.

## Chapter 5 Doeblin Coupling and Cumulant Upper Bound (See Article 5.7 for Details)

### §5.1 η-ξ Separated Coupling Construction

The core of Article 5.7 is the construction of a Doeblin-type coupling on $G_{\mathcal{I}}$. For two initial states $x_0, y_0 \in V$, a joint process $(X_\tau, Y_\tau)_{\tau \ge 0}$ is constructed.

**Preliminary Properties** (all from existing theorems, independent of A.1):

**Property 5.1 (η-Direction Mixing)**: For any $f: V \to \mathbb{R}$,
$$\|T_{ss}^\tau f - \bar{f}\|_\infty \leq \|f\|_\infty \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}$$
where $\bar{f} = (1/N_{\text{info}})\sum_i f(i)$, $\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} = 391.05 \text{ rad}^{-2}$. (Source: Theorem 7.13.3.05, Birkhoff mixing property.)

**Property 5.2 (ξ-Direction Contraction)**: The discrete difference along the ξ direction $\nabla_\xi f(i) = f(i_+) - f(i_-)$ satisfies:
$$\|\nabla_\xi (T_{ss}^\tau f)\|_\infty \leq e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \tau} \cdot \|\nabla_\xi f\|_\infty$$
where $\Lambda_\xi \approx -2.51$. (Source: Theorem 7.13.4.01, hard-direction Lyapunov contraction.)

**Coupling Construction (Definition 5.7.2.01)**: At each step $\tau \to \tau+1$:

**(a) η Direction — Mixing-Driven**: With probability $1 - e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}}$, the two chains independently sample from $T_{ss}$, maximizing η-direction diffusion; the coordinate difference decays at rate $\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}$.

**(b) ξ Direction — Contraction-Driven**: Apply reflective coupling: with probability $1 - e^{-|\Lambda_\xi|}$, ensure $|\Delta\xi_{\tau+1}| \leq e^{-|\Lambda_\xi|} \cdot |\Delta\xi_\tau|$.

**(c) Gluing Rule**: If $X_{\tau^*} = Y_{\tau^*}$, thereafter $X_\tau = Y_\tau$ (standard Doeblin gluing).

The coupling mechanisms in the η and ξ directions operate independently — guaranteed by the decomposability of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix (the block structure in the proof of Theorem 7.13.3.05).

### §5.2 Coupling Failure Probability

**Lemma 5.7.3.01 (Single-Pair Coupling Failure Probability)**:
$$\mathbb{P}(X_\tau \neq Y_\tau) \leq \frac{|\eta(x_0) - \eta(y_0)|}{\Delta\eta_{\max}} \cdot e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} + \frac{|\xi(x_0) - \xi(y_0)|}{\Delta\xi_{\max}} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \tau}$$

The two terms are respectively the η-direction mixing failure term and the ξ-direction contraction failure term. When $\tau$ is sufficiently large, $\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \gg |\Lambda_\xi|$ ($391.05 \gg 2.51$), the mixing term decays before the contraction term.

**Lemma 5.7.3.02 (Multi-Pair Coupling Failure Probability)**: For $k$ independent coupling processes,
$$\mathbb{P}(\text{at least one pair unglued}) \leq k \cdot \left(e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} + e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \tau}\right)$$

### §5.3 Main Theorem and Its Proof

**Theorem 5.7.4.03 (Main Inequality)**: For the $k$-th order cumulant ($k \ge 3$) on the information field graph $G_{\mathcal{I}}$, under steady-state causal circulation $T_{ss}$:

$$\boxed{|C_k(i_1, \ldots, i_k; \tau)| \leq k! \cdot N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2} \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot d_\xi}}$$

where $d_\xi = \max_{1 \le a < b \le k} |\xi(i_a) - \xi(i_b)|$.

**Proof Outline**:

**Step 1 (Coupling → Independence)**: Coupling gluing $\iff$ the statistical behavior of two chains is indistinguishable. If the chains corresponding to all $k$ nodes glue to the same reference chain, their joint distribution factorizes into the product of $k$ independent copies. The cumulant $C_k$ (the residual after subtracting factorized contributions) is contributed by configurations where **at least one pair is unglued**.

**Step 2 (Cumulant Decay under Partial Coupling)**: Suppose among the $k$ nodes, $m$ of them ($2 \le m \le k$) form a connected correlation subgraph. According to the connected-graph expansion of Lemma 5.6.3.02, each connected edge corresponds to a "return event" — i.e., two chains that should be independent approach each other again in the ξ direction. The upper bound on the return probability is given by the coupling failure probability.

**Step 3 (Worst Case)**: Maintaining $k$-body correlations requires at least $(k-2)$ independent return events (otherwise the correlation can be expressed through lower-order cumulants — this is an intrinsic property of the cumulant definition: after subtracting all partition contributions, the residual comes from irreducible connected structures). The failure probability of each return is $\sim e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}$ (mixing incomplete) + $e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot \bar{\Delta}\xi}$ (contraction incomplete).

**Step 4 (Product Upper Bound)**: The joint failure probability of $(k-2)$ independent returns is the product:
$$\mathbb{P}_{\text{fail}} \leq \prod_{j=1}^{k-2} \left(e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} + e^{-|\Lambda_\xi| \cdot \bar{\Delta}\xi}\right) \leq e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau} \cdot \left(1 + e^{-(|\Lambda_\xi|\bar{\Delta}\xi - \lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\tau)}\right)^{k-2}$$

Since $\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} / |\Lambda_\xi| \approx 155.8 \gg 1$, $|\Lambda_\xi|\bar{\Delta}\xi \ll \lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\tau$ holds at $\tau \sim \tau_{\text{mix}}$, the contraction term is far smaller than the mixing term, and the bracket factor $\approx 1$.

**Step 5 (Combinatorial Factor)**: The combinatorial weight $k!$ comes from permutations of $k$ nodes, and $N_{\text{info}}^{-(k-1)/2}$ comes from the normalization standardization of individual node contributions under the uniform distribution. The $d_\xi$ factor comes from the contraction coupling failure correction for the farthest separation distance in the ξ direction. ∎

Equivalently, after curvature scaling: $\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} = \mu_{Cl} \cdot \kappa_{S^2} = 0.2982 \times 1311.6 \approx 391.05 \text{ rad}^{-2}$, where $\kappa_{S^2} = \pi^2 / \overline{\Delta}\eta^2 \approx 1311.6$ is the curvature scaling factor of the η direction on $S^2$ (derivation in the scale analysis of Theorem 7.13.3.05). This conversion ensures $\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}$ is identical across all scales — from $N_{\text{info}}=16$ to $N_{\text{info}} \approx 10^{22}$.

**Proof Outline**:

Intra-fiber mixing (random geometric graph on $S^2$, spectral gap $\mu_F$) is far faster than inter-fiber mixing (Clifford graph, spectral gap $\mu_{Cl} = 0.2982$) — intuitive analogy: a 16-story building, people on each floor mix within their floor ($\mu_F$, fast), then communicate across floors ($\mu_{Cl}$, slow). The slowest mixing rate of the entire building is determined by the stairwell between floors (the slowest cross-floor channel), not by the corridors within each floor (no matter how fast, they cannot help cross-floor mixing).

Formalization: the fiber-averaging projection $\Pi: \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{info}}} \to \mathbb{R}^{16}$ averages within each fiber. The projected effective dynamics:
$$T_{\text{eff}} = \Pi T_{\mathcal{I}} \Pi^\dagger = T_{Cl} \otimes I_{N_f}$$

The spectrum of $T_{\text{eff}}$ = spectrum of $T_{Cl}$ (each eigenvalue degenerate $N_f$ times). The spectral gap of the full graph $T_{\mathcal{I}}$ is given by the spectral gap of $T_{\text{eff}}$ (intra-fiber modes decay faster and do not limit the full-graph convergence rate).

**This theorem does not require A.1.** It holds in a purely algebraic manner at finite $N_{\text{info}}$ — requiring only the Clifford graph and the fibration structure, not the convergence of the graph Laplacian to a differential operator.
