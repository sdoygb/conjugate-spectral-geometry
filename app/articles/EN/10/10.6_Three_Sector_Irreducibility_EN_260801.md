## §2 Rigorous Definitions and Preliminary Lemmas

### §2.1 Threefold Decomposition of Hilbert Space

**Definition 2.1** (Total Hilbert Space). $$\mathcal{H} = L^2(M(a), \mathcal{S})$$

where the inner product is induced by the Riemannian metric $g$ on $M(a)$ and the Hermitian structure on the spinor bundle.

---

**Definition 2.2** (Projection Operators of the Threefold Decomposition). From the sector coupling dynamics of the tri-dual geometric spaces, the tangent bundle admits a **metric-orthogonal** decomposition:

$$T\Sigma = \mathcal{M} \oplus \mathcal{C} \oplus \mathcal{I}$$

where $\mathcal{M}$ is the Material realm (generated by the $\theta_M$ coordinate), $\mathcal{C}$ is the Causal realm (generated by the $\theta_C$ coordinate), and $\mathcal{I}$ is the Information realm (generated by the $\theta_I$ coordinate).

$$\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{I}}$$

which in turn induces an orthogonal direct sum decomposition of the Hilbert space:

$$\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \oplus \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}} \oplus \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$$

where $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}} = L^2(M(a), \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{X}})$, $\mathcal{X} \in \{\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I}\}$.

Define orthogonal projection operators $P_{\mathcal{M}}, P_{\mathcal{C}}, P_{\mathcal{I}} : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{H}$, satisfying:

- $P_{\mathcal{X}}^2 = P_{\mathcal{X}} = P_{\mathcal{X}}^*$ (self-adjoint idempotent);
- $P_{\mathcal{X}} P_{\mathcal{Y}} = 0$ for $\mathcal{X} \neq \mathcal{Y}$ (orthogonality);
- $P_{\mathcal{M}} + P_{\mathcal{C}} + P_{\mathcal{I}} = I_{\mathcal{H}}$ (completeness).

The threefold decomposition $T\Sigma = \mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{C}\oplus\mathcal{I}$ is metric-orthogonal.

### §2.2 Sector Restriction Operators and Cross Operators

**Definition 2.3** (Sector Diagonal Operators and Cross Operators). Let $D$ be the 9-dimensional Dirac operator constructed in Theorem 1.1.5.01-20, with domain $\text{dom}(D) = H^1(M(a), \mathcal{S})$ (first-order Sobolev space). Define:

**Sector diagonal operators**:

$$D_{\mathcal{X}} := P_{\mathcal{X}} D P_{\mathcal{X}} : \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}} \to \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}},\quad \mathcal{X} \in \{\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I}\}$$

**Sector cross operators**:

$$D_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}} := P_{\mathcal{X}} D P_{\mathcal{Y}} : \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{Y}} \to \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}},\quad \mathcal{X} \neq \mathcal{Y}$$

**Block matrix representation of the Dirac operator**: For any $\psi = (\psi_{\mathcal{M}}, \psi_{\mathcal{C}}, \psi_{\mathcal{I}}) \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \oplus \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}} \oplus \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$, we have:

$$D\psi = \begin{pmatrix} D_{\mathcal{M}} & D_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{C}} & D_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{I}} \\ D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} & D_{\mathcal{C}} & D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{I}} \\ D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}} & D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{C}} & D_{\mathcal{I}} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \psi_{\mathcal{M}} \\ \psi_{\mathcal{C}} \\ \psi_{\mathcal{I}} \end{pmatrix}$$

---

**Lemma 10.6.2.01 (Adjoint Relation of Cross Operators).** $D_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}^* = D_{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{X}}$, and $D_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}$ is a densely defined closed operator.

*Proof.* $D$ is a self-adjoint operator. For $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{Y}}, \phi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}}$:

$$\langle D_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}} \psi, \phi \rangle = \langle P_{\mathcal{X}} D P_{\mathcal{Y}} \psi, \phi \rangle = \langle D \psi, \phi \rangle = \langle \psi, D \phi \rangle = \langle \psi, P_{\mathcal{Y}} D P_{\mathcal{X}} \phi \rangle = \langle \psi, D_{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{X}} \phi \rangle$$

Hence $D_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}^* = D_{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{X}}$. The closedness of $D$ is inherited by its sector restrictions. ∎

### §2.3 Orthogonal Decomposition and Norm Identity

**Lemma 10.6.2.02 (Norm Orthogonal Decomposition).** For any $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ (i.e., $\psi_{\mathcal{M}} = \psi, \psi_{\mathcal{C}} = \psi_{\mathcal{I}} = 0$), we have:

$$\|D\psi\|^2 = \|D_{\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 + \|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 + \|D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2$$

where the three terms belong respectively to the mutually orthogonal subspaces $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}, \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}, \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$.

*Proof.* From Definition 2.3:

$$D\psi = D_{\mathcal{M}}\psi + D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi + D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}\psi$$

where $D_{\mathcal{M}}\psi = P_{\mathcal{M}} D \psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$, $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi = P_{\mathcal{C}} D \psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}$, $D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}\psi = P_{\mathcal{I}} D \psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$.

By Definition 2.2, $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \perp \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}} \perp \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$. Hence the three terms are mutually orthogonal, and the squared norm equals the sum of the individual squared norms. ∎

### §3.2 Spectral Gap Direction Spectral Domination Inequality

**Lemma 10.6.3.02 (Spectral Gap Direction Spectral Domination Inequality).** Let $\epsilon > 0$ satisfy $\epsilon \ll \lambda_2$ (Dirac spectral direction gap, prerequisite Theorem G-5), and let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ be the spectral gap direction subspace of Definition 2.4. Then for any $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ (normalized $\|\psi\| = 1$), we have:

$$\langle \psi, D^2 \psi \rangle = \langle \psi, H^{\text{con}} \psi \rangle + O(\epsilon)$$

where $\langle \psi, H^{\text{con}} \psi \rangle$ is defined by identifying the angular fluctuations of $\psi$ with the $(\xi, \eta)$ coordinates, and $O(\epsilon)$ is a constant independent of $\psi$.

*Proof.*

**Step 1: Normal freezing.** In a neighborhood $U_\Sigma \subset M(a)$ of the sector parameter space $\Sigma \cong S^2$, adopt local coordinates $x = (y, z)$, where $y \in \Sigma$ are cross-sectional coordinates and $z \in N\Sigma$ are normal coordinates ($\dim N\Sigma = 7$). The Dirac operator $D^2$ separates in these coordinates as:

$$D^2 = -\Delta_\Sigma + D_{N\Sigma}^2 + \text{cross terms}$$

where $-\Delta_\Sigma$ is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on $\Sigma$ (determined by the induced metric $g_\Sigma$), and $D_{N\Sigma}$ is the normal Dirac operator.

The spectral lower bound of the normal operator $D_{N\Sigma}^2$ is $\lambda_2$ (Dirac spectral direction gap, prerequisite Theorem M-3). By Definition 2.4, $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ satisfies $\langle \psi, D^2 \psi \rangle \leq \epsilon$. Hence:

$$\langle \psi, D_{N\Sigma}^2 \psi \rangle \leq \epsilon$$

By the spectral theorem, $\|D_{N\Sigma} \psi\|^2 \leq \epsilon$, i.e., $\psi$ is approximately constant in the normal direction (frozen). The remainder $\|D_{N\Sigma} \psi\|^2$ is $O(\epsilon)$. This freezing property is the mathematically rigorous version of the Dirac spectral freezing theorem (Theorem 1.1.5.01).

**Step 2: Induced metric and Hessian.** The induced metric $g_\Sigma$ on the constrained manifold $\Sigma$ is determined by the Born Normalization Condition (Theorem 1.2.3.01) $\theta_M + \theta_C + \theta_I = \pi/2$ and the Hessian of the action $S$. In $(\xi, \eta)$ coordinates, the inverse of $g_\Sigma$ is proportional to $H^{\text{con}}$:

$$g_\Sigma^{ij} = c \cdot H^{\text{con}}_{ij},\quad c > 0 \text{ is a constant}$$

The proportionality constant $c$ is determined by dimensional homogeneity $[g_\Sigma] = [\text{rad}]^{-2}$ and $[H^{\text{con}}] = [\text{rad}]^{-2}$. This proportionality relation is a direct corollary of the geometric structure of the sector parameter space (Theorem 1.1.5.01); its rigorous geometric-spectral correspondence (Spectral Expansion/Born Rule (Theorem 1.1.5.01) mapping) is established in Theorem 1.1.5.01-20.

The principal symbol of the Laplace–Beltrami operator $-\Delta_\Sigma$ is $g_\Sigma^{ij} \xi_i \xi_j$. Hence its spectral gap direction spectrum (low-frequency limit) is controlled by the eigenvalues of $H^{\text{con}}$.

**Step 3: Lichnerowicz formula.** On the 2-dimensional $\Sigma \cong S^2$, the Lichnerowicz formula gives:

$$D_\Sigma^2 = \nabla^*\nabla + \frac{1}{4} R_\Sigma$$

where $R_\Sigma$ is the scalar curvature (constant, Gaussian curvature). Hence the spectral gap direction spectrum of $D_\Sigma^2$ is isomorphic to that of $-\Delta_\Sigma$ up to a constant shift of $R_\Sigma/4$. This constant shift is absorbed into $O(\epsilon)$ (since the spectral gap direction energy $\ll \lambda_2$, prerequisite Theorem G-5).

**Step 4: Quadratic form control.** Combining Steps 1–3, for $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$:

$$\langle \psi, D^2 \psi \rangle = \langle \psi, -\Delta_\Sigma \psi \rangle + \langle \psi, D_{N\Sigma}^2 \psi \rangle + \text{cross terms}$$

$$= \langle \psi, H^{\text{con}} \psi \rangle + O(\epsilon)$$

where $O(\epsilon)$ comes from the normal freezing remainder (Step 1) together with the Lichnerowicz curvature term and higher-order coordinate terms (Step 3). Since the spectral gap direction energy $\ll \lambda_2$, the curvature term is of the same order as the normal remainder, uniformly denoted as $O(\epsilon)$. ∎

**Remark 3.3** (Distinction from "Unitary Equivalence"). This lemma does **not** claim that $D_\Sigma^2|_{\text{soft}}$ is unitarily equivalent to $H^{\text{con}} \otimes I_2$ (such an assertion would be too strong; no unitary map has been constructed). It merely establishes a **domination inequality for the energy quadratic form**: the expectation of $D^2$ on the spectral gap direction subspace is controlled by the Hessian quadratic form, with error $O(\epsilon)$. This inequality is sufficient to support the subsequent min-max lower bound estimates.

### §3.3 Min-Max Lower Bound for Cross Operators

**Lemma 10.6.3.03 (Schur Complement Lower Bound for Cross Operators).** Let $Q_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) = \langle \psi, D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi \rangle$ be the quadratic form of $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$, with domain $\text{dom}(Q_{\mathcal{C}}) = \{\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} : D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}\}$. Then for any $\epsilon > 0$:

$$\inf_{\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \setminus \{0\}} \frac{Q_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|^2} \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon)$$

Similarly:

$$\inf_{\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \setminus \{0\}} \frac{Q_{\mathcal{I}}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|^2} \geq \frac{H_{MI}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon)$$

where $Q_{\mathcal{I}}(\psi) = \langle \psi, D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}} \psi \rangle$.

*Proof.*

**Step 1: Finite-rank approximation.** By Definition 2.4, $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ is a finite-dimensional subspace. Let $N = \dim \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} < \infty$. On this subspace, the matrix representation of $D^2$ is an $N \times N$ Hermitian matrix.

**Step 2: Hessian–Dirac quadratic form correspondence.** By Lemma 10.6.3.02, for $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$:

$$\langle \psi, D^2 \psi \rangle = \langle \psi, H^{\text{con}} \psi \rangle + O(\epsilon)$$

Expanding $\psi$ in $(\xi, \eta)$ coordinates (by spectral gap direction freezing, $\eta \approx 0$, prerequisite Theorem M-4), we have:

$$\langle \psi, H^{\text{con}} \psi \rangle = \lambda_1 |\langle v_1, \psi \rangle|^2 + \lambda_2 |\langle v_2, \psi \rangle|^2$$

Since $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ (Material realm), its projection onto the spectral gap direction basis $v_1$ is nonzero (by Lemma 10.6.3.01, the $\theta_M$ component $v_{1M} \neq 0$). Hence $|\langle v_1, \psi \rangle|^2 > 0$.

**Step 3: Schur complement analysis.** In the spectral decomposition of the finite-dimensional matrix $H^{\text{emb}}$, the spectral gap direction subspace corresponds to the eigenvalue $\lambda_1$. Partition $H^{\text{emb}}$ by sectors $(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$:

$$H^{\text{emb}} = \begin{pmatrix} H_{MM} & H_{MC} & H_{MI} \\ H_{MC} & H_{CC} & H_{CI} \\ H_{MI} & H_{CI} & H_{II} \end{pmatrix}$$

The effective operator after projection onto the $\mathcal{M}$ sector (Schur complement) is:

$$S_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{M}} = H_{MM} - \begin{pmatrix} H_{MC} & H_{MI} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} H_{CC} & H_{CI} \\ H_{CI} & H_{II} \end{pmatrix}^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} H_{MC} \\ H_{MI} \end{pmatrix}$$

The eigenvalues $\mu_{CI}$ of the $\mathcal{C},\mathcal{I}$ submatrix $\begin{pmatrix} H_{CC} & H_{CI} \\ H_{CI} & H_{II} \end{pmatrix}$ satisfy $\mu_{CI} < \lambda_2$ (by the spectral structure of the Hessian, prerequisite Theorems G-4, G-5). Replacing $\mu_{CI}$ by $\lambda_2$ as the denominator, since $\lambda_2 > \mu_{CI}$, the cross terms subtracted in the Schur complement are compressed, yielding a **strictly positive lower bound** (conservative estimate):

$$|S_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{M}}| \geq H_{MM} - \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2 - H_{CC}} - \frac{H_{MI}^2}{\lambda_2 - H_{II}}$$

(here $\lambda_2$ serves as a conservative upper bound estimate for the "environment" energy of the $\mathcal{C},\mathcal{I}$ sectors, prerequisite Theorem M-3). Although this estimate compresses the cross-coupling strength, it preserves the strict positivity of the Schur complement, sufficient to support the theorem's conclusion.

The cross term $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$ corresponds to the $-\frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2 - H_{CC}}$ term in the Schur complement (the negative sign indicates that coupling lowers the effective energy). Its absolute value gives the cross-coupling strength:

$$|S_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}| = \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2 - H_{CC}}$$

**Step 4: Min-max principle.** By the Courant–Fischer–Weyl min-max principle, the minimum spectral value of $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ equals the infimum of the Rayleigh quotient on this subspace:

$$\lambda_{\min} = \inf_{\psi \neq 0} \frac{Q_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|^2}$$

By Steps 2–3, this infimum is controlled by the Schur complement of $H^{\text{emb}}$:

$$\lambda_{\min} \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2 - H_{CC}} - O(\epsilon)$$

Since $\lambda_2 = 58760.77 \gg H_{CC} = 367.7347$, we have $\lambda_2 - H_{CC} \approx \lambda_2$. Hence:

$$\lambda_{\min} \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon)$$

As $\epsilon \to 0$ (spectral gap direction limit), $O(\epsilon) \to 0$, yielding a strict lower bound. ∎

**Remark 3.4** (Distinction from "Operator Norm Inequality"). This lemma employs the **min-max principle** (Rayleigh quotient infimum) in place of the "operator norm lower bound" used in the original proof. The min-max principle converts the spectral problem of an infinite-dimensional operator into a matrix eigenvalue problem on a finite-dimensional subspace, directly corresponding to the Schur complement of the Birkhoff Hessian second-order structure at the fixed point. The error in the original proof lay in conflating the operator norm (supremum over all normalized vectors) with the norm of a specific eigenstate. The min-max principle avoids this confusion, because the infimum is by definition the minimum spectral value.

## §4 Sector Indecomposability Theorem

### §4.1 Rigorous Statement of the Theorem

**Theorem 10.6.4.01 (Sector Indecomposability Theorem).** Let $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{H}, D, J, \gamma)$ be the 9-dimensional real ℳ𝒞ℐ three-sector of Theorem 1.1.5.01-20, let $P_{\mathcal{M}}, P_{\mathcal{C}}, P_{\mathcal{I}}$ be the orthogonal projections of Definition 2.2, and let $D_{\mathcal{X}\mathcal{Y}}$ be the cross operators of Definition 2.3. Let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ be the spectral gap direction subspace of Definition 2.4. Then:

**(A)** For any nonzero $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$, $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi \neq 0$ and $D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}} \psi \neq 0$;

**(B)** The min-max lower bounds of the cross operators on the spectral gap direction subspace are:

$$\inf_{\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \setminus \{0\}} \frac{\|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi\|^2}{\|\psi\|^2} \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon) > 0$$

$$\inf_{\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}} \setminus \{0\}} \frac{\|D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}} \psi\|^2}{\|\psi\|^2} \geq \frac{H_{MI}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon) > 0$$

**(C)** Define the sector residual fraction:

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) := \frac{\|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi\|^2}{\|D_{\mathcal{M}} \psi\|^2 + \|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi\|^2 + \|D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}} \psi\|^2},\quad \psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$$

Then:

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2 / \lambda_2 - O(\epsilon)}{\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}^{\max} + H_{MC}^2 / \lambda_2 + H_{MI}^2 / \lambda_2 - O(\epsilon)}$$

where $\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}^{\max} = \sup \sigma(D_{\mathcal{M}}^2|_{\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}})$ is the maximum spectral value of the material sector operator on the spectral gap direction subspace. The lower bound for $\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\psi)$ is obtained analogously.

### §4.2 Proof: Cross Operators Have No Zero Modes

**Step 1: Nonzero components of the spectral gap direction eigenvector.** By Lemma 10.6.3.01, the spectral gap direction eigenvector $v_1$ of $H^{\text{emb}}$ is nonzero in all three components $\theta_M, \theta_C, \theta_I$. Under the spectral gap direction spectral domination (Lemma 10.6.3.02), the spectral gap direction subspace $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ is isomorphic (as finite-dimensional vector spaces) to the one-dimensional space spanned by $v_1$ (two-dimensional on the spinor degrees of freedom).

**Step 2: The spectral gap direction subspace is not a sector subspace.** Since all three components of $v_1$ are nonzero, $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ is not a subspace of any single $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}}$. Specifically:

$$\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \not\subset \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\quad \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \not\subset \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}},\quad \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \not\subset \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$$

The intersection of $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ with each $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}}$ is a finite-dimensional nonzero subspace.

**Step 3: Non-vanishing of cross operators.** Let $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ be nonzero. Then $\psi$ is the nonzero image of $P_{\mathcal{M}}$ projected onto $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$. Since $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ is not a subspace of $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$, the restriction of $P_{\mathcal{M}}$ to $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ is not the identity map. Hence the preimage $\tilde{\psi}$ of $\psi$ in $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ (satisfying $P_{\mathcal{M}} \tilde{\psi} = \psi$) must possess nonzero $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}$ components.

By the Hessian–Dirac quadratic form correspondence (Lemma 10.6.3.02), the action of $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}$ corresponds to the $\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{C}$ matrix element $H_{MC}$ of $H^{\text{emb}}$. Since $H_{MC} \neq 0$, $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi \neq 0$. Similarly $D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}} \psi \neq 0$.

**Step 4: Elliptic regularity and unique continuation.** Suppose $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ satisfies $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}} \psi = 0$. By Definition 2.3, $P_{\mathcal{C}} D \psi = 0$, i.e., the $\mathcal{C}$-component of $D\psi$ is zero. By Lemma 10.6.3.02, in a neighborhood $U$ of the Birkhoff fixed point σ*, the spectral gap direction subspace of $D^2$ is isomorphic to $H^{\text{con}}$, and $H_{MC} \neq 0$ implies that $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}|_U$ has no zero modes.

$D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$, as a sector restriction of the Dirac operator $D$ (a first-order differential operator system), inherits the ellipticity of $D$ in its principal symbol. By the Calderón-type unique continuation property for first-order elliptic systems, if $\psi$ vanishes on $U$, then $\psi$ vanishes on all of $M(a)$. Hence the kernel of $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ is $\{0\}$. ∎

### §4.3 Proof: Lower Bound Estimates

**Step 1: Operator spectra on the spectral gap direction subspace.** By Lemma 10.6.3.03, the minimum spectral value of $D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ satisfies:

$$\lambda_{\min}(D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}|_{\text{soft}}) \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon)$$

Similarly:

$$\lambda_{\min}(D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}^* D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}|_{\text{soft}}) \geq \frac{H_{MI}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon)$$

**Step 2: Spectral upper bound of the material sector operator.** The maximum spectral value of $D_{\mathcal{M}}^2$ on the spectral gap direction subspace is controlled by the Hessian–Dirac spectral domination (Lemma 10.6.3.02):

$$\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}^{\max} = \sup \sigma(D_{\mathcal{M}}^2|_{\mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)}}) \leq H_{MM} + O(\epsilon) = 31.3012 + O(\epsilon)\ \text{rad}^{-2}$$

(exactly equal to $H_{MM}$ at the Birkhoff fixed point σ*; in its neighborhood the upper bound is preserved by continuity).

**Step 3: Lower bound of the residual fraction.** For normalized $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$, by Lemma 10.6.3.02:

$$\|D\psi\|^2 = \|D_{\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 + \|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 + \|D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2$$

From Steps 1 and 2:

$$\|D_{\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 \leq \lambda_{\mathcal{M}}^{\max},\quad \|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon),\quad \|D_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2 \geq \frac{H_{MI}^2}{\lambda_2} - O(\epsilon)$$

Hence:

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) = \frac{\|D_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}}\psi\|^2}{\|D\psi\|^2} \geq \frac{H_{MC}^2 / \lambda_2 - O(\epsilon)}{\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}^{\max} + H_{MC}^2 / \lambda_2 + H_{MI}^2 / \lambda_2 - O(\epsilon)}$$

Substituting numerical values:

$$\frac{H_{MC}^2}{\lambda_2} = \frac{(3.4145)^2}{58760.77} = 1.984 \times 10^{-4}$$

$$\frac{H_{MI}^2}{\lambda_2} = \frac{(69.3547)^2}{58760.77} = 8.186 \times 10^{-2}$$

$$\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}^{\max} \approx 31.3012$$

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) \geq \frac{1.984 \times 10^{-4}}{31.3012 + 1.984 \times 10^{-4} + 8.186 \times 10^{-2}} \approx 6.32 \times 10^{-6}$$

Similarly:

$$\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\psi) \geq \frac{8.186 \times 10^{-2}}{31.3012 + 1.984 \times 10^{-4} + 8.186 \times 10^{-2}} \approx 2.61 \times 10^{-3}$$

∎

## §5 Quantitative Estimates of Anthropic Residuals

### §5.1 Causal Residual Lower Bound

**Theorem 10.6.5.01 (Quantitative Lower Bound of the Causal Residual).** For any nonzero anthropic state $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ (normalized $\|\psi\|=1$), in the spectral gap direction limit $\epsilon \to 0$:

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) \geq 6.32 \times 10^{-6}$$

This lower bound is rigorously determined by the purely geometric constants $H_{MC}, H_{MI}, H_{MM}, \lambda_2$ — i.e., the algebraic output of the Hessian of the Algebraic Action S(σ) (Theorem 1.4.3.01) at the Birkhoff fixed point σ* $p_0$ — with parameters fixed by local properties.

*Proof.* From Theorem 7.12.1.01(C), substituting numerical values:

$$H_{MC} = 3.4145,\ H_{MI} = 69.3547,\ H_{MM} = 31.3012,\ \lambda_2 = 58760.77$$

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(\psi) \geq \frac{(3.4145)^2 / 58760.77}{31.3012 + (3.4145)^2 / 58760.77 + (69.3547)^2 / 58760.77} = 6.32 \times 10^{-6}$$

∎

### §5.2 Information Residual Lower Bound

**Theorem 10.6.5.02 (Quantitative Lower Bound of the Information Residual).** For any nonzero anthropic state $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{soft}}^{(\epsilon)} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}$ (normalized $\|\psi\|=1$), in the spectral gap direction limit $\epsilon \to 0$:

The information residual is approximately $413$ times larger than the causal residual, determined by the numerical structure $H_{MI} \gg H_{MC}$.

*Proof.* As in Theorem 7.12.1.03, substituting numerical values:

$$\delta_{\mathcal{I}}(\psi) \geq \frac{(69.3547)^2 / 58760.77}{31.3012 + (3.4145)^2 / 58760.77 + (69.3547)^2 / 58760.77} = 2.61 \times 10^{-3}$$

∎
