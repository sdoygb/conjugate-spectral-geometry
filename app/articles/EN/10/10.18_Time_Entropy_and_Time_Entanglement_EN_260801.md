## 1. Introduction

Mainstream physics has in recent years begun to explore the concept of "time entanglement" — the possibility of nonlocal correlations between quantum systems at two different instants of time, analogous to the quantum entanglement of spatially separated particles. Such studies typically employ pseudo-density matrices (PDM) and temporal Bell inequalities for theoretical analysis, but lack a clear geometric picture.

Conjugate Spectral Geometry reduces time to the unidirectional advance of the causal field $C^3$ ($\partial S/\partial\eta < 0$), and reduces information storage to the coding orbit encoding of the information realm $I^3$. On this basis, time entanglement is naturally interpreted as: systems at different causal times sharing the same information field coordinate $\xi$, thereby producing nonlocal correlations on the coding orbit. This article presents the complete mathematical formulation of this picture.

## 4. Geometric Criterion for Time Entanglement

### 4.1 Core Criterion (Geometric Model Theorem)

**Theorem 10.18.1.01 (Time Entanglement Criterion)**: Let the states of a system at causal times $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ be described by coding orbits $\Sigma_{\tau_1}$ and $\Sigma_{\tau_2}$ respectively, with coordinates $(\xi_1, \eta_1)$ and $(\xi_2, \eta_2)$. According to the cross-sector coupling structure of Theorem 2.4.6.01, the I-sector as an information phase channel does not map to independent particles (Consummate Reproduction). Time entanglement is the geometric manifestation of I-sector phase correlations on the coding orbit, producing no new particle excitations.

By Theorem 1.3.4.01, the information field direction $\xi$ is the Dirac spectral direction $\lambda_2$ direction; sections with different $\xi$ are exponentially suppressed in their information field fluctuations $\exp[-\lambda_2(\Delta\xi)^2/2]$ and decoupled. Therefore, nonzero time entanglement exists between systems at two instants if and only if:

$$\xi_1 = \xi_2.$$

[Geometric model theorem: a deduction based on the Dirac spectral direction freezing property of Theorem 1.3.4.01, not directly derived from Sole Axiom δ (Zero Motion).]

### 4.2 Entanglement Entropy Formula (Geometric Model Formula)

When $\xi_1 = \xi_2$, the $\eta$-direction distance between the two coding orbits is $\Delta\eta = |\eta_1 - \eta_2|$. In the strong anisotropy limit of the spectral gap direction to Dirac spectral direction ($\lambda_2/\lambda_1 \approx 150$), the off-diagonal coupling terms are suppressed at the $\lambda_2$ scale, and $(\xi, \eta)$ approximately serve as the principal axes of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix. By the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix structure of Theorem 1.3.4.01, the two-point information overlap kernel on the joint section is $\exp[-(1/2)(\lambda_1(\Delta\eta)^2 + \lambda_2(\Delta\xi)^2)]$.

Taking the logarithm of the effective degrees of freedom and introducing the coding orbit baseline regularization (model regularization constant $1$), we obtain:

**Theorem 10.18.1.02 (Time Entanglement Entropy Formula)**:
$$S_{\text{ent}}(\tau_1, \tau_2) = \frac{1}{2}\log\left(1 + \frac{1}{\lambda_1 (\Delta\eta)^2 + \lambda_2 (\Delta\xi)^2}\right)$$

[Geometric model formula: a deduction based on the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix structure of Theorem 1.3.4.01; the $1$ regularization and the coefficient $1/2$ are both model constructions of this article.]

**Dimensional and Regularization Notes**: In the formula, $\Delta\eta$ and $\Delta\xi$ are both in units of $\text{rad}$, $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are in units of $\text{rad}^{-2}$, and the product is dimensionless. The constant term $1$ originates from the baseline entanglement entropy regularization of the coding orbit (a model regularization constant), ensuring that the entropy tends to zero as $\Delta\xi^2\Delta\eta^2 \to \infty$, i.e., zero entanglement entropy when completely causally independent.

**Self-Consistency Analysis**:

- When $\Delta\xi = 0$ and $\Delta\eta \to 0$ (same information field coordinate and coincident causal times), the denominator tends to zero, $S_{\text{ent}} \to \infty$, signifying complete entanglement.
- When $\Delta\xi \neq 0$ (different information field coordinates), even if $\Delta\eta = 0$, the denominator is dominated by $\lambda_2(\Delta\xi)^2$, and $S_{\text{ent}}$ is a finite value, decaying to zero as $\Delta\xi$ increases.
- When $\Delta\eta \to \infty$ (causal times fully separated), $S_{\text{ent}} \to 0$, degenerating to classical causal independence.

**Numerical Example**: Take $\Delta\eta = 1.0\ \text{rad}$, $\Delta\xi = 1/\sqrt{\lambda_2} = 0.00413\ \text{rad}$ (the minimum resolvable scale in the Dirac spectral direction), $\lambda_1 = 392.21$, $\lambda_2 = 58760.77$, then:

$$\lambda_1 (\Delta\eta)^2 + \lambda_2 (\Delta\xi)^2 = 392.21 + 58760.77 \times (0.00413)^2 = 392.21 + 1.000 = 393.21,$$

$$S_{\text{ent}} = \frac{1}{2}\log\left(1 + \frac{1}{393.21}\right) \approx \frac{1}{2} \times 2.54 \times 10^{-3} \approx 1.27 \times 10^{-3}.$$

This indicates that time entanglement is generally extremely weak, unless $\Delta\eta$ is very small (i.e., the two causal times nearly coincide).
