## §4 Attack Point III: "Wightman Axioms" — Unsuitable for Gauge Fields

### §4.1 Core Assumptions of the Wightman Axioms

The Wightman axiom system is an axiomatization attempt for quantum field theory, with core requirements including:

1. Field operators are operator-valued Schwartz distributions on $\mathbb{R}^4$
2. Existence of a unique Poincaré-invariant vacuum state
3. Spectral condition: the joint spectrum of energy-momentum operators is contained in the forward light cone
4. Locality: field operators at spacelike separation commute (or anticommute)

The Osterwalder-Schrader axioms are their Euclidean version, requiring reflection positivity.

### §4.2 Known Limitations

The Wightman axioms have known technical difficulties in the gauge field case:

1. **They were designed for scalar fields** — gauge fields require BRST cohomology to handle gauge invariance, for which the Wightman axioms provide no natural framework. Physical gauge conditions (such as the Lorenz gauge $\partial^\mu A_\mu = 0$) and ghost fields have no elegant formulation in the Wightman framework
2. **Osterwalder-Schrader reflection positivity** in the non-Abelian gauge field case — the proof of reflection positivity after lattice truncation is non-trivial; the Gribov copy problem complicates gauge fixing at the non-perturbative level
3. **The axioms assume fields are defined on $\mathbb{R}^4$** — returning to Attack Point II
4. **In the four-dimensional non-Abelian case, no theory has been proven to satisfy the full Wightman/OS axioms** — even for free fields, only scalar and spinor fields have complete constructions. Yang-Mills theory is highly successful at the perturbative level, but its non-perturbative definition remains uncaptured by the Wightman framework

### §4.3 Conjugate Spectral Geometry's Quantization Framework

Conjugate Spectral Geometry uses **deformation quantization**, bypassing the Wightman axioms entirely:

- What is quantized is the Poisson algebra structure of $S$ (Poisson bracket → star product commutator), yielding a discrete spectrum (Theorem 7.8.9.11)
- The dynamics is **interspectral dissipative relaxation** — the gradient flow is a non-Hamiltonian flow (Axiom 2), and in the classical limit the star product commutator recovers the symplectic flow (Theorem)
- No unique Poincaré-invariant vacuum state required — the ground state on $\Sigma$ is $(30^\circ,30^\circ,30^\circ)$ ($S_{\min}=24$), and the physical vacuum is the potential minimum point $P_0$ on the Sector Parameter Space, without assuming Poincaré invariance
- No locality axiom required — the causal structure emerges from spectral causality on $\Sigma$. The fast-slow mode separation ($\tau_{\text{slow}} = 7.28$ days, $\tau_{\text{fast}} = 1.15$ hours) naturally produces the causal cone

### §4.4 What the Wightman Axioms Resemble

The Wightman axioms resemble a **mold tailored for a specific type of theory**. If your theory happens to fit — say $\phi^4$ scalar field theory — fine. Not fitting does not necessarily indicate a problem with the theory; it may indicate that the mold itself needs to be expanded.

The history of gauge field quantization corroborates this: physicists never truly used the Wightman axioms to quantize gauge fields. They used path integral BRST renormalization — a framework that is mathematically not fully rigorous but physically extremely successful. Requiring Yang-Mills theory to satisfy the Wightman axioms is like requiring fluid mechanics to be formulated using the first-order axioms of molecular dynamics — in principle possible, but you miss the correct degrees of freedom at the emergent level.

**Attack Strength Assessment**: This attack point is a methodological judgment — it points out that the Millennium Problem selected an axiom system that has historically been bypassed and is unsuitable for gauge fields as its yardstick. It involves no theorems, but is independent of the Conjugate Spectral Geometry axiom system (the limitations of the Wightman axioms have been widely discussed in the mathematical physics community).
