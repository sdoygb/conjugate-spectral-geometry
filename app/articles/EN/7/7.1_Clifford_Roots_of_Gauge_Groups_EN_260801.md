## §1 Algebraic Emergence of Gauge Groups

### 1.1 Embedding Obstruction

The Standard Model gauge algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\text{SM}} = \mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ has real dimension $8+3+1=12$, while $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ has real dimension 28. Dimensional admissibility does not guarantee the existence of an embedding—the true obstruction lies in representation theory.

The three 8-dimensional irreducible representations $(V, S^+, S^-)$ of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ are permuted by the triality symmetry $S_3$. The gauge group structure of the Standard Model requires $SU(3)$ to act on color triplets, $SU(2)$ to act on weak doublets, and $U(1)$ to act on hypercharge—the representation dimensions $(3,2,1)$ of these three do not match the $(8,8,8)$ of triality. Hence $SU(3)\oplus SU(2)\oplus U(1)$ cannot be directly embedded as a subalgebra of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$.

More precisely, the embedding obstruction can be formulated as the following three representation-theoretic constraints:

1. **Dimensional Mismatch**: The minimal faithful representation of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is 8-dimensional, while the minimal nontrivial representation of $SU(3)$ is 3-dimensional (the fundamental representation). If $SU(3)\subset\mathrm{spin}(8)$, then the 8-dimensional representation decomposes under $SU(3)$ restriction as $\mathbf{8}=\mathbf{3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{3}}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}$ or $\mathbf{8}=\mathbf{8}$ (adjoint); the former destroys the irreducibility of triality, and the latter does not allow $SU(2)$ to act simultaneously.

2. **Rank Mismatch**: $\mathrm{rank}(\mathrm{spin}(8))=4$, while $\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1))=2+1+1=4$. The ranks happen to match, but the root system structures of the Cartan subalgebras differ: the root system of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is $D_4$, while that of the Standard Model is $A_2\oplus A_1\oplus A_0$. The rank reduction from $D_4$ to $A_2\oplus A_1$ requires triality breaking.

3. **Center Structure**: The center of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is $Z_2\times Z_2$, while the center of $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ is $Z_3\times Z_2\times U(1)$. The mismatch of center structures rules out a direct group-theoretic embedding.

These three obstructions show that the gauge group must emerge indirectly—through **complexification** or **triality breaking**—rather than via direct embedding.

### 1.2 Complexification Route

**Theorem 7.1.1.01 (Gauge Group Clifford Roots Theorem).** The Standard Model gauge group $G_{\text{SM}} = U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ emerges as an automorphism subgroup of $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(16,\mathbb{C})$, with its algebraic roots jointly determined by the Structure Constants $\Lambda=3$, $k_0=2$, $\Delta\Theta=5$.

Proof sketch: $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}$, as a complex matrix algebra, has automorphism group containing $PU(16)$. The gauge group emerges through the following decomposition:

$$\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C} \;\supset\; \mathrm{Cl}(2)\otimes\mathbb{C} \;\otimes\; \mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes\mathbb{C} \;\otimes\; \mathrm{Cl}(1)\otimes\mathbb{C},$$

where $\mathrm{Cl}(1)_\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$ generates $U(1)$ (hypercharge), $\mathrm{Cl}(2)_\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{C})$ generates $SU(2)$ (weak isospin), and the unimodular subgroup of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)_\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(4,\mathbb{C})$ generates $SU(3)$ (color charge).

**Complete Steps of the Complexification Route Embedding Proof:**

**Step 1: Tensor Product Decomposition.** By Bott Periodicity, $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\cong\mathrm{Mat}(16,\mathbb{R})$, and after complexification $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(16,\mathbb{C})$. Using the tensor product property of Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}(p+q)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(p)\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{Cl}(q)$ ($\hat{\otimes}$ denotes $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded tensor product), when $p+q=8=1+2+5$ or $8=1+2+3+2$, different decompositions are obtained. Choosing the decomposition $8=3+2+1+2$ (where the redundant $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$ corresponds to terminal duality $k_0=2$), after complexification:

$$\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C} \cong \mathrm{Cl}(3)_\mathbb{C}\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathrm{Cl}(2)_\mathbb{C}\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathrm{Cl}(1)_\mathbb{C}\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathrm{Cl}(2)_\mathbb{C}.$$


**Step 2: $U(1)$ Emerges from $\mathrm{Cl}(1)$.** $\mathrm{Cl}(1)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$, whose automorphism group is $GL(1,\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}^*$. The unimodular constraint $|z|=1$ yields $U(1)$. The generator is $\gamma_1$ (the unique generator of Cl(1)), satisfying $\gamma_1^2=1$. **Step 3: $SU(2)$ Emerges from $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$.** $\mathrm{Cl}(2)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{C})$, whose automorphism group is $PGL(2,\mathbb{C})$. The unimodular constraint $\det(g)=1$ yields $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, and further taking the unitary subgroup gives $SU(2)$. The generators are $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ (the two generators of Cl(2)), satisfying $\{\gamma_i, \gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$. **Step 4: $SU(3)$ Emerges from $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$.** $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(4,\mathbb{C})$, where the decomposition $4=2\times 2$ corresponds to $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(2)\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{Cl}(1)$. The unimodular unitary subgroup of $\mathrm{Mat}(4,\mathbb{C})$ is $SU(4)$. But the Semisimple Splitting with $\Lambda=3$ decomposes $SU(4)$ into $SU(3)\times U(1)$—this is the standard decomposition $\mathrm{spin}(6)\cong SU(4)$ to $SU(3)\times U(1)$. The $SU(3)$ generators are the bilinear combinations $\frac{i}{4}[\gamma_i, \gamma_j]$ of the three generators $\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \gamma_3$ of $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$, totaling $C_3^2=3$ independent commutators. **Step 5: Self-Consistency of the Embedding.** The commutation relations of the generators of the three factors in $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}$ are:

$$[\mathfrak{u}(1), \mathfrak{su}(2)] = 0, \quad [\mathfrak{u}(1), \mathfrak{su}(3)] = 0, \quad [\mathfrak{su}(2), \mathfrak{su}(3)] = 0.$$

This yields the direct product structure $G_{\text{SM}}=U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$.

These two cancellations are not numerical coincidences but algebraic consequences of {2,3,5} Interlock Property—they hold at all points in the Landscape satisfying the Completeness Criterion, not only at the fixed point of our region.

## §3 Physical Consequences of Gauge Group Emergence

### 3.1 Gauge Group Uniqueness

**Theorem 7.1.3.01 (Gauge Group Uniqueness Theorem).** $G_{\text{SM}}=U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ is the unique gauge group that can emerge from Cl(8) triality breaking under the Completeness Criterion.

Proof: The Structure Constants $\Lambda=3, k_0=2, \Delta\Theta=5$ are uniquely determined by the $\delta$ derivation chain. $G_{\text{SM}}$ is the unique gauge group compatible with this set of constants. Any attempt to alter the gauge group (e.g., $SU(5)$ GUT, $SO(10)$ GUT) must modify at least one structure constant, thereby violating the {2,3,5} Interlock Property.

### 3.2 Breaking of Left-Right Symmetry

The $V-A$ structure of the weak interaction (maximal parity violation) has a direct geometric explanation in Conjugate Spectral Geometry: triality breaking $S_3\to Z_2$ destroys the transposition $\sigma_{23}$ (which exchanges $V\leftrightarrow S^+$), and the preserved $\sigma_2$ (exchanging $V\leftrightarrow S^-$) corresponds to the $SU(2)_L$ chiral structure—only left-handed fermions participate in the weak interaction.

Right-handed fermions are $SU(2)$ singlets because they reside in the $S^-$ sector (preserved by $\sigma_2$), while $S^+$ (broken) corresponds to the would-be $SU(2)_R$—which has been destroyed by triality breaking.

### 3.3 Prediction of Hypercharge Normalization

The Algebraic Normalization condition $2pC^3+C^2=1$ uniquely determines the normalization of hypercharge. Relative to the GUT normalization $Y_{\text{GUT}}=\sqrt{3/5}Y$, the hypercharge normalization of Conjugate Spectral Geometry is:

$$\frac{Y}{Y_{\text{GUT}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} \approx 0.8165.$$

This differs from the $SU(5)$ prediction ($\sqrt{3/5}\approx 0.7746$) and the $SO(10)$ prediction ($\sqrt{3/8}\approx 0.6124$), providing a testable distinction.

### 3.4 Geometric Spectrum of Gauge Bosons

The masses of gauge bosons are determined by the sector weights at the fixed point. The photon is massless because it corresponds to the unbroken $U(1)_{\text{em}}$ direction in $\mathcal{M}$ (Theorem 3.6). The masses of $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ are determined by the projection of the structure constant $k_0=2$ onto the $\mathcal{I}$ sector:

$$M_W \propto \sqrt{\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*}, \quad M_Z = \frac{M_W}{\cos\theta_W}.$$

The masslessness of gluons is guaranteed by the unbroken nature of $SU(3)$—the $\mathcal{C}$ sector preserves the full $SU(3)$ symmetry at the fixed point.

### 3.5 Geometric Position of the Higgs Mechanism

The Higgs mechanism is not an additional input but a geometric necessity: the triality breaking $S_3\to Z_2$ generates a massless Goldstone mode (Theorem 3.5 Breathing Mode). The Higgs field $\phi$ is the scalar component of this breathing mode, with its vacuum expectation value determined by the mistuning between the total stationary point $\sigma^*$ and the $\mathcal{M}$-sector partial stationary point:

$$v \propto |\sigma^* - \sigma_\mathcal{M}^{\text{partial}}| \approx \sqrt{(\sigma_\mathcal{C}^*)^2 + (\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*)^2}.$$

The Higgs boson mass $M_H\approx 125$ GeV is determined by the curvature of the breathing mode at the fixed point (Theorem 3.11).
