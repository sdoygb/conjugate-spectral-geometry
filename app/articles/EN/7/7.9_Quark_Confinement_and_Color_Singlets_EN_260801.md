## §1 Algebraic Proof of Color Confinement

### 1.1 Sector Completeness Constraint

The three-sector weights $\sigma=(\sigma_\mathcal{M}, \sigma_\mathcal{C}, \sigma_\mathcal{I})$ satisfy the completeness constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$. This constraint is a direct corollary of Birkhoff double stochasticity $\sum\theta_k=1$ — the normalization of the three-sector weights guarantees that the action $S(\sigma)$ is finite on the Landscape.

**Algebraic Foundation of Sector Completeness:**

The double stochasticity of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T=\theta_I P_I\theta_\tau P_\tau\theta_{\sigma_2}P_{\sigma_2}$ requires:

$$\sum_k\theta_k = 1, \quad \sum_k\theta_k P_k = T, \quad T\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}, \quad T^T\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}.$$

The solution of the fixed-point equation $\sigma^*=T\cdot\mathrm{diag}(\chi)\cdot\sigma^*$ satisfies $\sum\sigma_i^*=1$ — this is the projection of Birkhoff double stochasticity at the fixed point. Sector completeness $\sum\sigma_i=1$ is therefore an algebraically rigid constraint and cannot be relaxed.

The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)=[\sum 1/\sigma_i + \sum 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}]/C^2$ diverges as $\sigma_i\to 0$ — $1/\sigma_i\to\infty$. Hence any sector allocation that makes some $\sigma_i=0$ causes the action to diverge and is physically unrealizable. Sector completeness requires not only $\sum\sigma_i=1$ but also $\sigma_i>0$ for all $i$ — this is the non-negativity requirement of the Born Rule $\Delta\lambda_i\propto\sqrt{\sigma_i}$.

### 1.2 Destructive Nature of Free Color Charges

Free color charges (such as free quarks) carry non-zero $\mathcal{C}$ Sector weight — color charge is a quantum number of the Causal Sector. If free color charges were to exist, their $\mathcal{C}$ Sector weight $\sigma_\mathcal{C}^{\text{free}}$ would have to be "extracted" from the fixed point $\sigma^*$, rendering the remaining sector allocation incomplete.

**Detailed Analysis of How Free Color Charges Destroy Sector Completeness:**

Let the Causal Sector weight carried by a free color charge be $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$. The existence of a free color charge would require:

$$\sigma_\mathcal{C}^{\text{total}} = \sigma_\mathcal{C}^*\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C},$$

but the Birkhoff double stochasticity constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$ requires:

$$\sigma_\mathcal{M}^*\sigma_\mathcal{C}^{\text{total}}\sigma_\mathcal{I}^* = 1\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C} > 1.$$

This violates the completeness constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$. To restore completeness, weight must be "borrowed" from other sectors:

$$\sigma_\mathcal{M}^{\text{new}} = \sigma_\mathcal{M}^* - \alpha\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}, \quad \sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}} = \sigma_\mathcal{I}^* - \beta\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}, \quad \alpha\beta=1.$$

But the Born Rule $\Delta\lambda_i\propto\sqrt{\sigma_i}$ requires $\sigma_i>0$ — if $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$ is sufficiently large, $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}}=\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*-\beta\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}<0$ (since $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\approx 0.011484$ is extremely small), violating the Born Rule.

Even if $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$ is sufficiently small that $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}}>0$, the Algebraic Action under the new sector allocation would deviate from the fixed point $\sigma^*$ — configurations deviating from the fixed point increase the action $S(\sigma)$, corresponding to physically unstable configurations. The Jacobian spectral radius $\rho=0.124<1$ guarantees that the fixed point is an attractor — any configuration deviating from $\sigma^*$ will rapidly return to the fixed point, and the "free color charge" is pulled back by the algebraic constraint.

### 1.3 Confinement Theorem

Therefore, free color charges are algebraically forbidden in Conjugate Spectral Geometry — confinement is an algebraic constraint, not a dynamical effect.

Proof: Free color charges carry $\mathcal{C}$ Sector quantum numbers. If a free color charge were to exist, its quantum number contribution $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}>0$ would cause the total $\mathcal{C}$ Sector weight to exceed $\sigma_\mathcal{C}^*$. The completeness constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$ requires subtracting an equal amount of weight from other sectors, but the extremely small value $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\approx 0.011484$ means the Information Sector cannot provide sufficient weight — any free color charge with $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}>\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*/\beta$ would make $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}}<0$, violating the Born Rule.

## §3 Physical Picture of Confinement

### 3.1 Algebraic Derivation of the Confinement Potential

The confinement potential $V(r)$ is, geometrically, the energy cost of deviating from the fixed point:

$$V(r) = S(\sigma(r)) - S(\sigma^*),$$

where $\sigma(r)$ is the sector weight distribution at separation $r$. For large $r$, $V(r) \propto r$ — the linear confinement potential emerges as the leading-order deviation of the action from its minimum.

### 3.2 Correspondence with Asymptotic Freedom

At short distances ($r\to 0$, equivalent to high energies $\mu\to\infty$), the Birkhoff constraints are completely released, and the effective coupling tends to zero — asymptotic freedom. At long distances ($r\to\infty$, equivalent to $\mu\to\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$), the Birkhoff constraints are completely locked, and the effective coupling diverges — confinement.

### 3.3 Relation between Color Confinement and Electroweak Symmetry Breaking

Color confinement and electroweak symmetry breaking share the same geometric origin — both are consequences of triality breaking $S_3\to Z_2$. Confinement corresponds to the complete closure of color charges in the $\mathcal{C}$ Sector, and electroweak symmetry breaking corresponds to the Higgs mechanism bridging the $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{I}$ Sectors.
