## §1 Algebraic Derivation of Three Generations

### 1.1 Semisimple Splitting and Root System

The Structure Constant $\Lambda=3$ arises from the Semisimple Splitting of Cl(3) — the Cartan subalgebra of Cl(3) has dimension 1, but its semisimple part (the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ subalgebra) is spanned by the bilinear generators $T_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma_i, \gamma_j]$. The number of independent bilinear generators is:

$$C_\Lambda^2 = \frac{\Lambda(\Lambda-1)}{2} = \frac{3\times 2}{2} = 3.$$

> **Terminological Note**: Here $C_\Lambda^2$ is the number of independent bilinear combinations $T_{ij}$ generated by the $\Lambda$ generators of Cl(3) (i.e., the number of generators of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$), and is not the root lattice dimension in standard root system theory. The standard $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ root system $\Delta=\{\pm\alpha\}$ has a 1-dimensional root lattice, while $C_3^2=3$ counts the number of Lie algebra generators. To avoid confusion, this paper does not use the term "root lattice dimension."

**Detailed Derivation of Semisimple Splitting:**

Cl(3) is generated by three generators $\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \gamma_3$, satisfying the Clifford relation $\{\gamma_i, \gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$. The complete algebraic structure of Cl(3) is:

$$\mathrm{Cl}(3) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(2)\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{Cl}(1) \cong \mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{C} \cong \mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{C}).$$

But the **real** structure of Cl(3) contains a semisimple part $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and a central part $\mathbb{R}$. The semisimple part is formed by the bilinear generators $T_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma_i, \gamma_j]$, totaling $C_3^2=3$ independent elements. These 3 elements satisfy the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Lie algebra:

$$[T_{12}, T_{23}] = T_{31}, \quad [T_{23}, T_{31}] = T_{12}, \quad [T_{31}, T_{12}] = T_{23}.$$

The root system $\Delta=\{\pm\alpha\}$ consists of the two roots of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, where $\alpha$ is the positive root of the Cartan subalgebra (taking $T_{12}$). The three bilinear generators $T_{12}, T_{23}, T_{31}$ correspond to three independent weight directions — the zero-weight direction (the direction where the eigenvalue of $T_{12}$ is zero) and two non-zero weights ($\pm\alpha$).

$$N_{\text{gen}} = C_\Lambda^2 = \frac{\Lambda(\Lambda-1)}{2} = 3.$$

The three weights correspond to three irreducible representations — i.e., three fermion generations. The algebraic structure forces exactly three generations, neither more nor less.

**Step 1: From $\delta$ to $\Lambda=3$.** Zero Motion $\delta$ produces the Clifford algebra chain $\mathrm{Cl}(0)\to\mathrm{Cl}(1)\to\cdots\to\mathrm{Cl}(8)$ through Bott periodicity truncation. **Step 2: From $\Lambda=3$ to the number of bilinear generators.** The root system $\Delta$ of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ contains 2 roots, but the number of independent bilinear generators is $C_\Lambda^2=\frac{\Lambda(\Lambda-1)}{2}=3$. **Step 3: From the number of bilinear generators to the number of weights.** The 3 bilinear generators $T_{12}, T_{23}, T_{31}$ correspond to 3 weight directions — two non-zero weights ($\pm\alpha$) and one zero weight.
