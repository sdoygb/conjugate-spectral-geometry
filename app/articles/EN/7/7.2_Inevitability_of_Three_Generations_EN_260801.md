## §1 Algebraic Derivation of Three Generations

### 1.1 Semisimple Splitting and Root System

The Structure Constant $\Lambda=3$ originates from the Semisimple Splitting of Cl(3) — the Cartan subalgebra of Cl(3) is 1-dimensional, but its semisimple part (the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ subalgebra) is spanned by the bilinear generators $T_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma_i, \gamma_j]$. The number of independent bilinear generators is:

$$C_\Lambda^2 = \frac{\Lambda(\Lambda-1)}{2} = \frac{3\times 2}{2} = 3.$$

> **Terminological note**: Here $C_\Lambda^2$ denotes the number of independent bilinear combinations $T_{ij}$ generated by the $\Lambda$ generators of Cl(3) (i.e., the number of generators of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$), not the root lattice dimension in standard root system theory. The standard $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ root system $\Delta=\{\pm\alpha\}$ has a 1-dimensional root lattice, while $C_3^2=3$ counts the number of Lie algebra generators. To avoid confusion, the term "root lattice dimension" is not used in this article.

**Detailed derivation of the Semisimple Splitting:**

Cl(3) is generated by three generators $\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \gamma_3$ satisfying the Clifford relations $\{\gamma_i, \gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$. The complete algebraic structure of Cl(3) is:

$$\mathrm{Cl}(3) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(2)\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{Cl}(1) \cong \mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{C} \cong \mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{C}).$$

However, the **real** structure of Cl(3) contains a semisimple part $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and a central part $\mathbb{R}$. The semisimple part consists of the bilinear generators $T_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma_i, \gamma_j]$, yielding $C_3^2=3$ independent elements. These three elements satisfy the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Lie algebra:

$$[T_{12}, T_{23}] = T_{31}, \quad [T_{23}, T_{31}] = T_{12}, \quad [T_{31}, T_{12}] = T_{23}.$$

The root system $\Delta=\{\pm\alpha\}$ consists of the two roots of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, where $\alpha$ is the positive root of the Cartan subalgebra (taken as $T_{12}$). The three bilinear generators $T_{12}, T_{23}, T_{31}$ correspond to three independent weight directions — the zero weight (the direction where the eigenvalue of $T_{12}$ is 0) and two non-zero weights ($\pm\alpha$).
