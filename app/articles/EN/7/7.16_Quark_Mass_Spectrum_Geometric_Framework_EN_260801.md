## Abstract

This article establishes a geometric framework for the quark mass spectrum. The quark sector differs fundamentally from the lepton sector — confinement implies that quarks are not asymptotic states, color constraints render the $C_n$ hierarchy formula inapplicable, and there is no framework-endogenous anchor point analogous to the electron mass. The current framework is divided into four tiers: (I) **Structure Tier** — the constraint structure of three-generation coding weight configurations ({2,3,5} Interlock Property, Birkhoff Mixing Matrix Spectral Rigidity, Color Singlet), theorem-level; (II) **Scaling Tier** — the mass operator M̂ (3.1 §5) directly applicable to $u, d, s$, theorem-level but with $\sigma_M$ obtained by back-inference from external mass input; (III) **Anchoring Tier** — the geometric origin of first-generation quark $\sigma_M$, open; (IV) **Splitting Tier** — the $\sigma_M$ differences between up-type and down-type quarks and the $\eta_{\text{QCD}}$ correction factor for heavy quarks, partially directional. Each tier's theorem propositions, external input status are stated.

**Honest Statement:** The quark mass spectrum has not yet achieved theorem-level closure comparable to the lepton mass spectrum. The current framework provides the correct constraint structure (theorem-level), but numerical predictions still rely on external PDG mass input as anchor points. Once the Anchoring Tier is closed, the quark mass spectrum will become a pure prediction of the framework.

### 2.1 Sole Axiom δ and the ℳ𝒞ℐ Three-Sector

All derivations in this article are locked to the Sole Axiom δ (Zero Motion). **Coding Weight Sum Constraint (derived from Sole Axiom δ, Born Normalization Theorem 1.4.3.01):**

$$\sigma_M + \sigma_C + \sigma_I = 90^\circ \tag{2.1}$$

The sum of three-sector coding weights is a right angle — this is the angular formulation of Born normalization on the product sphere class. Verification: for the $u$ quark, $7.88^\circ + 9.62^\circ + 72.50^\circ = 90.00^\circ$; the same holds exactly for $c, t$.

$$\text{Parameter Space} = \mathcal{M} \oplus \mathcal{C} \oplus \mathcal{I}, \quad \sigma_M + \sigma_C + \sigma_I = 90^\circ \tag{2.3}$$

## 5 Anchoring Tier: Origin of First-Generation $\sigma_M$

### 5.1 Endogenous Anchoring in the Lepton Sector

The lepton sector possesses a framework-endogenous anchoring chain:

$$S_e = 137.036 \xrightarrow{\text{Consummate Reproduction}} \sigma_M^{(e)} = 57.93^\circ \xrightarrow{\hat{M}} m_e = 510.999\ \text{keV}$$

$S_e$ is directly locked by the extremum of the Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ and the Information Sector Dirac Spectral Equation, determined by local region properties. $m_e$ is a pure output of the framework.

### 5.2 Absence of a Corresponding Anchor in the Quark Sector

The quark sector has no endogenous anchoring chain analogous to $S_e \to \sigma_M$. Possible reasons:

- **Confinement**: Quarks are not asymptotic states; their $S$ values must be defined on the joint sector parameter space rather than the single-particle sector parameter space.
- **Color**: SU(3) color degrees of freedom mean that a single quark's $S$ is not independently observable.
- **Mass scale separation**: There exists a gap, bridged by the confinement scale, between quark masses ($\sim \text{MeV}$) and hadron masses ($\sim \text{GeV}$).

### 5.3 Current Scheme: External Mass Input

Current anchoring scheme: the $\sigma_M$ of the $u$ and $d$ quarks are obtained by back-inference from PDG running masses via the mass operator M̂ (Table 4.1). This is regarded as **external input** — not a framework-endogenous prediction.

## 7 Heavy Quark Tier: The $\eta_{\text{QCD}}$ Correction Factor

For $c, b, t$ quarks, $m \gg \kappa_c K = 839.759\ \text{MeV}$ ($K = 839.759$ keV, $\kappa_c = 10^3$). For example, $m_t \approx 172.6\ \text{GeV}$, $m_t/(\kappa_c K) \approx 206$, which cannot be covered by $\sin^3\sigma_M \leq 1$ alone. The mass operator requires the $\eta_{\text{QCD}}$ correction factor.

### 7.2 Constructive Definition of the Correction Factor

$$m_q = \kappa_c \cdot K \cdot \sin^3\sigma_M^{(q)} \cdot \eta_{\text{QCD}}^{(q)} \tag{7.1}$$

where $\eta_{\text{QCD}}^{(q)} \geq 1$, $\eta_{\text{QCD}} \approx 1$ for light quarks, and $\eta_{\text{QCD}} > 1$ for heavy quarks.
