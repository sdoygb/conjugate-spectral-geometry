## §1 Algebraic Emergence of the Gauge Group

### 1.1 Embedding Obstruction

The real dimension of the Standard Model gauge algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\text{SM}} = \mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ is $8+3+1=12$, while the real dimension of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is 28. Admissibility of dimension does not guarantee the existence of an embedding — the genuine obstruction lies in representation theory.

The three 8-dimensional irreducible representations $(V, S^+, S^-)$ of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ are permuted by the triality symmetry $S_3$. The gauge group structure of the Standard Model requires $SU(3)$ acting on color triplets, $SU(2)$ acting on weak doublets, and $U(1)$ acting on hypercharge. The representation dimensions $(3,2,1)$ of the three do not match the $(8,8,8)$ of triality. Hence $SU(3)\oplus SU(2)\oplus U(1)$ cannot be directly embedded as a subalgebra into $\mathrm{spin}(8)$.

More precisely, the embedding obstruction can be formulated as the following three representation-theoretic constraints:

1. **Dimension Mismatch**: The smallest faithful representation of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is 8-dimensional, while the smallest non-trivial representation of $SU(3)$ is 3-dimensional (fundamental representation). If $SU(3)\subset\mathrm{spin}(8)$, then the 8-dimensional representation, upon restriction to $SU(3)$, decomposes as $\mathbf{8}=\mathbf{3}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{3}}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}$ or $\mathbf{8}=\mathbf{8}$ (adjoint); the former destroys the irreducibility of triality, and the latter precludes $SU(2)$ from acting simultaneously.

2. **Rank Mismatch**: $\mathrm{rank}(\mathrm{spin}(8))=4$, while $\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1))=2+1+1=4$. The ranks happen to match, but the root systems of the Cartan subalgebras differ: the root system of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is $D_4$, while that of the Standard Model is $A_2\oplus A_1\oplus A_0$. The rank reduction from $D_4$ to $A_2\oplus A_1$ requires triality breaking.

3. **Center Structure**: The center of $\mathrm{spin}(8)$ is $Z_2\times Z_2$, while the center of $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ is $Z_3\times Z_2\times U(1)$. The mismatch of center structures rules out a direct group-theoretic embedding.

These three obstructions demonstrate that the gauge group must emerge indirectly — either through **complexification** or **triality breaking** — rather than through direct embedding.

### 1.2 Complexification Route

**Theorem 7.1.1.01 (Clifford Origin of the Gauge Group Theorem).** The Standard Model gauge group $G_{\text{SM}} = U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ emerges as an automorphism subgroup of $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(16,\mathbb{C})$, with its algebraic origin jointly determined by the Structure Constants $\Lambda=3$, $k_0=2$, $\Delta\Theta=5$.

Proof sketch: As a complex matrix algebra, $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}$ has an automorphism group containing $PU(16)$. The gauge group emerges through the following decomposition:

$$\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C} \;\supset\; \mathrm{Cl}(2)\otimes\mathbb{C} \;\otimes\; \mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes\mathbb{C} \;\otimes\; \mathrm{Cl}(1)\otimes\mathbb{C},$$

where $\mathrm{Cl}(1)_\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$ generates $U(1)$ (Hypercharge), $\mathrm{Cl}(2)_\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{C})$ generates $SU(2)$ (Weak Isospin), and the unimodular subgroup within $\mathrm{Cl}(3)_\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(4,\mathbb{C})$ generates $SU(3)$ (color charge).

**Complete steps of the complexification route embedding proof:**

**Step 1: Tensor Product Decomposition.** By Bott Periodicity, $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\cong\mathrm{Mat}(16,\mathbb{R})$, and after complexification $\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(16,\mathbb{C})$. Exploiting the tensor product property of Clifford algebras $\mathrm{Cl}(p+q)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(p)\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{Cl}(q)$ (where $\hat{\otimes}$ denotes the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded tensor product), when $p+q=8=1+2+5$ or $8=1+2+3+2$, different decompositions are possible. Choosing the decomposition $8=3+2+1+2$ (where the redundant $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$ corresponds to the Terminal Duality $k_0=2$), after complexification:

$$\mathrm{Cl}(8)\otimes\mathbb{C} \cong \mathrm{Cl}(3)_\mathbb{C}\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathrm{Cl}(2)_\mathbb{C}\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathrm{Cl}(1)_\mathbb{C}\;\hat{\otimes}\;\mathrm{Cl}(2)_\mathbb{C}.$$


**Step 2: $U(1)$ Emerges from $\mathrm{Cl}(1)$.** $\mathrm{Cl}(1)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}$, whose automorphism group is $GL(1,\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}^*$. **Step 3: $SU(2)$ Emerges from $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$.** $\mathrm{Cl}(2)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(2,\mathbb{C})$, whose automorphism group is $PGL(2,\mathbb{C})$. The unimodular constraint $\det(g)=1$ yields $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, and taking the unitary subgroup yields $SU(2)$. The generators are $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ (the two generators of Cl(2)), satisfying $\{\gamma_i, \gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$. **Step 4: $SU(3)$ Emerges from $\mathrm{Cl}(3)$.** $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathrm{Mat}(4,\mathbb{C})$, where the decomposition $4=2\times 2$ corresponds to $\mathrm{Cl}(3)\cong\mathrm{Cl}(2)\hat{\otimes}\mathrm{Cl}(1)$. The unimodular unitary subgroup of $\mathrm{Mat}(4,\mathbb{C})$ is $SU(4)$. However, the Semisimple Splitting $\Lambda=3$ decomposes $SU(4)$ into $SU(3)\times U(1)$ — this is the standard decomposition of $\mathrm{spin}(6)\cong SU(4)$ into $SU(3)\times U(1)$. $$[\mathfrak{u}(1), \mathfrak{su}(2)] = 0, \quad [\mathfrak{u}(1), \mathfrak{su}(3)] = 0, \quad [\mathfrak{su}(2), \mathfrak{su}(3)] = 0.$$
