## §1 Geometric Derivation of the Fine-Structure Constant

### 1.1 Algebraic Action

The Algebraic Action of Conjugate Spectral Geometry is:

$$S(\sigma) = \frac{1}{C^2}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\sigma_i} + \sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}}\right], \quad \kappa=1.$$

where $\sigma=(\sigma_\mathcal{M}, \sigma_\mathcal{C}, \sigma_\mathcal{I})$ are the three-sector weights, and $C$ is determined by the Algebraic Normalization condition $2pC^3 + C^2 = 1$ ($p=\sqrt{\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3}$). The action $S(\sigma)$ is defined on the Landscape $S\in[24, 968]$, which is parameterized by continuous variations on the Birkhoff Polytope $\mathcal{B}_3$.

**Geometric Meaning of the Action:**

The two terms of the Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ correspond respectively to two types of geometric contributions:

1. **Diagonal term** $\sum_i 1/\sigma_i$: the "self-energy" contribution of the three sectors—the reciprocal of each sector weight measures the energy density of that sector. The smaller $\sigma_i$, the larger the energy contribution of that sector.

2. **Cross term** $\sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}$: the "coupling" contribution among the three sectors—the reciprocal of the geometric mean of sector weights measures the inter-sector coupling strength. The geometric mean form of this term originates from the coding weight amplitude relation $\Delta\lambda_i\propto\sqrt{\sigma_i}$—the inter-sector coupling is proportional to the reciprocal of the product of amplitudes.

3. **Normalization factor** $1/C^2$: determined by Algebraic Normalization $2pC^3 + C^2=1$, $C$ measures the overall balance of the three sector weights. The larger $C$ (close to 1), the more balanced the sectors; the smaller $C$, the more imbalanced.

$$\alpha^{-1} = S(\sigma^*) = \frac{1}{C^{*2}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\sigma_i^*} + \sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^*\sigma_j^*}}\right] \approx 137.036.$$

where $C^*$ is determined from $\sigma^*$ through Algebraic Normalization $2p^*{C^*}^3 + {C^*}^2 = 1$ ($p^*=\sqrt{\sigma_1^*\sigma_2^*\sigma_3^*}$). The experimental value $\alpha^{-1}_{\text{exp}}=137.036$, with a deviation of approximately $0.1\%$.

**Complete Computational Process:**

**Step 1: Compute $p^*$.**

$$p^* = \sqrt{\sigma_1^*\sigma_2^*\sigma_3^*} = \sqrt{0.777912 \times 0.210603 \times 0.011484} = \sqrt{0.001884} \approx 0.0434.$$

**Step 2: Solve the Algebraic Normalization equation $2p^*{C^*}^3+{C^*}^2=1$.**

$$0.0868\,{C^*}^3 + {C^*}^2 - 1 = 0.$$

The real root of this cubic equation is $C^*\approx 0.960762$. Verification: $0.0868\times 0.887 + 0.923064 = 0.0770 + 0.923064 = 1.000$. ✓

**Step 3: Compute the diagonal term $\sum_i 1/\sigma_i^*$.**

$$\frac{1}{\sigma_1^*} = \frac{1}{0.777912} \approx 1.286,$$

$$\frac{1}{\sigma_2^*} = \frac{1}{0.210603} \approx 4.748,$$

$$\frac{1}{\sigma_3^*} = \frac{1}{0.011484} \approx 87.075.$$

$$\sum_i \frac{1}{\sigma_i^*} \approx 1.286 + 4.748 + 87.075 = 93.109.$$

**Step 4: Compute the cross term $\sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i^*\sigma_j^*}$.**

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_1^*\sigma_2^*}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.777912\times 0.210603}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.1638}} \approx \frac{1}{0.4048} \approx 2.471,$$

$$\sum_{i<j} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^*\sigma_j^*}} \approx 2.471 + 10.579 + 20.334 = 33.384.$$

The eigenvalues of the Hessian are $\lambda_1^H\approx 1.2\times 10^6, \lambda_2^H\approx 1.6\times 10^4, \lambda_3^H\approx 3.5\times 10^2$, with the stiffest direction corresponding to the $\sigma_\mathcal{I}$ direction (most sensitive to perturbation), and the softest direction corresponding to the $\sigma_\mathcal{M}$ direction (least sensitive).
