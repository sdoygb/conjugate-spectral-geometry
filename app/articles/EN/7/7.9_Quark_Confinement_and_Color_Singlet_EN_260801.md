## §1 Algebraic Proof of Color Confinement

### 1.1 Sector Completeness Constraint

The three-sector weights $\sigma=(\sigma_\mathcal{M}, \sigma_\mathcal{C}, \sigma_\mathcal{I})$ satisfy the completeness constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$. This constraint is a direct corollary of Birkhoff double-stochasticity $\sum\theta_k=1$ — the normalization of the three-sector weights ensures that the action $S(\sigma)$ on the landscape is finite.

**Algebraic foundation of sector completeness:**

The double-stochasticity of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T=\theta_I P_I\theta_\tau P_\tau\theta_{\sigma_2}P_{\sigma_2}$ requires:

$$\sum_k\theta_k = 1, \quad \sum_k\theta_k P_k = T, \quad T\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}, \quad T^T\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}.$$

The solution of the fixed-point equation $\sigma^*=T\cdot\mathrm{diag}(\chi)\cdot\sigma^*$ satisfies $\sum\sigma_i^*=1$ — this is the projection of Birkhoff double-stochasticity at the fixed point. Sector completeness $\sum\sigma_i=1$ is therefore an algebraically rigid constraint and cannot be relaxed.

The Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)=[\sum 1/\sigma_i + \sum 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}]/C^2$ diverges as $\sigma_i\to 0$ — $1/\sigma_i\to\infty$. Hence any sector allocation that makes some $\sigma_i=0$ causes the action to diverge and is physically unrealizable. Sector completeness requires not only $\sum\sigma_i=1$, but also $\sigma_i>0$ for all $i$ — this is the non-negativity requirement of the Born Rule $\Delta\lambda_i\propto\sqrt{\sigma_i}$.

### 1.2 Destructiveness of Free Color Charge

Free color charge (such as a free quark) carries nonzero 𝒞 sector weight — color charge is a quantum number of the causal sector. If free color charge existed, its 𝒞 sector weight $\sigma_\mathcal{C}^{\text{free}}$ would have to be "extracted" from the fixed point $\sigma^*$, rendering the remaining sector allocation incomplete.

**Detailed analysis of free color charge destroying sector completeness:**

Let the causal sector weight carried by the free color charge be $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$. The existence of free color charge requires:

$$\sigma_\mathcal{C}^{\text{total}} = \sigma_\mathcal{C}^* + \delta\sigma_\mathcal{C},$$

but the Birkhoff double-stochasticity constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$ requires:

$$\sigma_\mathcal{M}^* + \sigma_\mathcal{C}^{\text{total}} + \sigma_\mathcal{I}^* = 1 + \delta\sigma_\mathcal{C} > 1.$$

This violates the completeness constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$. To restore completeness, weight must be "borrowed" from other sectors:

$$\sigma_\mathcal{M}^{\text{new}} = \sigma_\mathcal{M}^* - \alpha\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}, \quad \sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}} = \sigma_\mathcal{I}^* - \beta\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}, \quad \alpha+\beta=1.$$

But the Born Rule $\Delta\lambda_i\propto\sqrt{\sigma_i}$ requires $\sigma_i>0$ — if $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$ is sufficiently large, $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}}=\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*-\beta\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}<0$ (because $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\approx 0.011484$ is extremely small), violating the Born Rule.

Even if $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$ is small enough that $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}}>0$, the Algebraic Action under the new sector allocation will deviate from the fixed point $\sigma^*$ — configurations deviating from the fixed point increase the action $S(\sigma)$ and correspond to physically unstable configurations. The Jacobian spectral radius $\rho=0.124<1$ guarantees that the fixed point is an attractor — any configuration deviating from $\sigma^*$ will rapidly return to the fixed point, and the "free color charge" is pulled back by the algebraic constraint.

### 1.3 Confinement Theorem

**Theorem 7.9.1.01 (Quark Confinement Theorem).** The existence of free color charge requires the three-sector weights to deviate from the fixed point $\sigma^*$, destroying Birkhoff double-stochasticity $\sum\sigma_i=1$ or the Born Rule $\sigma_i>0$. Therefore, free color charge is algebraically forbidden in Conjugate Spectral Geometry — confinement is an algebraic constraint, not a dynamical effect.

Proof: Free color charge carries 𝒞 sector quantum numbers. If free color charge existed, its quantum number contribution $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}>0$ would make the total 𝒞 sector weight exceed $\sigma_\mathcal{C}^*$. The completeness constraint $\sum\sigma_i=1$ requires subtracting an equal amount of weight from the other sectors, but the extremely small value $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\approx 0.011484$ makes the information sector unable to provide sufficient weight — any free color charge with $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}>\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*/\beta$ would make $\sigma_\mathcal{I}^{\text{new}}<0$, violating the Born Rule.

Even if $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}$ is sufficiently small, configurations deviating from the fixed point increase the Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ — the Jacobian spectral radius $\rho=0.124<1$ ensures the stability of the fixed point, and free color charge is "pulled back" to the confined state by the algebraic constraint. $\square$

**Three levels of the confinement theorem:**

2. **Level 2 (Born Rule constraint)**: "Borrowing" weight from the ℐ sector leads to $\sigma_\mathcal{I}<0$ — forbidden by the Born Rule

The joint constraint of the three levels makes free color charge completely algebraically forbidden — confinement requires no dynamical arguments from string tension, potential models, or lattice QCD; it is a rigid corollary of the algebraic structure.

$$\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C} = 0, \quad \sigma_\mathcal{C} = \sigma_\mathcal{C}^* \quad (\text{fixed point preserved}).$$

### 2.2 Closure Rules for Color Triplets

The closure rule for the color triplet $(r, g, b)$ is determined by the semisimple splitting of $\Lambda=3$. The three color charges correspond to the three weights $\{\alpha, 0, -\alpha\}$ of the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ root system $\Delta=\{\pm\alpha\}$ — the closure of the triplet is the vanishing of the sum of the three weights:

$$\alpha + 0 + (-\alpha) = 0.$$

This algebraic closure rule yields:

1. **Baryon closure**: $rgb=0$ (sum of three color charges vanishes) — corresponds to the closure of three weights
2. **Meson closure**: $r\bar{r}=0$, $g\bar{g}=0$, $b\bar{b}=0$ (color and anti-color cancel) — corresponds to the closure of weight and anti-weight

The algebraic root of the closure rule is the root system structure of $\Lambda=3$ — the "three-ness" of color charge comes directly from the structure constant $\Lambda=3$.

**Born Rule interpretation of color closure:**

The color singlet condition requires the sum of the three phases to vanish — $e^{i\phi_r} + e^{i\phi_g} + e^{i\phi_b} = 0$, giving $\phi_r-\phi_g=\phi_g-\phi_b=2\pi/3$. The equal spacing $2\pi/3$ of the three color charge phases is a direct projection of $\Lambda=3$ — the triple symmetry comes from the semisimple splitting constant.

## §3 Physical Picture of Confinement

### 3.1 Algebraic Derivation of the Confining Potential

The linear confining potential $V(r)=\sigma_{\text{string}}\cdot r$ of standard QCD has an algebraic derivation in Conjugate Spectral Geometry. The string tension $\sigma_{\text{string}}$ corresponds to the energy cost of deviating from the fixed point:

$$\sigma_{\text{string}} \propto \frac{\delta S}{\delta(\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C})^2}\bigg|_{\sigma^*} = H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}} \approx 3.45,$$

where $H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}}$ is the 𝒞 sector diagonal element of the Hessian matrix. $\sigma_{\text{string}}\propto H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}}\cdot\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2\approx 3.45 \times (0.2)^2 \approx 0.138$ GeV$^2$ — consistent in order of magnitude with the lattice QCD value $\sigma_{\text{string}}\approx 0.18$ GeV$^2$ (the difference arises from higher-order corrections).

**Complete derivation of the confining potential:**

The separation distance $r$ of two color charges corresponds to the 𝒞 sector deviation $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}(r)$ from the fixed point. The energy cost of the deviation is given by the Hessian:

$$E(r) = \frac{1}{2}H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}}\cdot(\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}(r))^2.$$

The relation between $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}(r)$ and the separation distance $r$ is determined by the Born Rule — the decay length of the amplitude $\sqrt{\sigma}$ corresponds to the scale of the color string:

$$\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}(r) \propto \frac{r}{\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{-1}}.$$

But this quadratic potential is incorrect at long distances — at long distances $\delta\sigma_\mathcal{C}(r)$ saturates (cannot exceed $\sigma_\mathcal{C}^*$), and the energy grows linearly:

$$E(r\to\infty) \propto \sigma_{\text{string}}\cdot r, \quad \sigma_{\text{string}}\propto H_{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}}\cdot\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2.$$

The linear potential is the saturation effect of the Hessian curvature at long distances — the algebraic constraint provides the root of the dynamical confining potential.

### 3.2 Correspondence with Asymptotic Freedom

Confinement (low energy $\alpha_s\to\infty$) and Asymptotic Freedom (high energy $\alpha_s\to 0$) are two sides of the same constraint release mechanism in Conjugate Spectral Geometry:

- **Low energy (confinement)**: Fixed-point constraint strictly maintained, $\sigma_\mathcal{C}^*\approx 0.210603$ fixed, color charges must close

The critical energy scale of constraint release $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}\approx 200$ MeV is the boundary between confinement and asymptotic freedom — below $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$ confinement, above $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$ asymptotic freedom.
