$$S(\sigma) = \frac{1}{C^2}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\sigma_i} + \sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}}\right], \quad \kappa=1.$$

**Geometric Meaning of the Action:**

The two terms of the Algebraic Action $S(\sigma)$ correspond to two types of geometric contributions:

1. **Diagonal terms** $\sum_i 1/\sigma_i$: the "self-energy" contributions of the three sectors — the reciprocal of each sector's weight measures the energy density of that sector. The smaller $\sigma_i$ is, the greater the energy contribution of that sector.

2. **Cross terms** $\sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i\sigma_j}$: the "coupling" contributions among the three sectors — the geometric mean reciprocal of the sector weights measures the coupling strength between sectors. The geometric mean form of this term originates from the Coding weight amplitude relation $\Delta\lambda_i\propto\sqrt{\sigma_i}$ — the inter-sector coupling is proportional to the reciprocal of the product of amplitudes.

3. **Normalization factor** $1/C^2$: determined by the Algebraic Normalization $2pC^3 + C^2=1$, $C$ measures the overall balance of the three-sector weights. The closer $C$ is to 1, the more balanced the sectors; the smaller $C$ is, the more unbalanced the sectors.

$$p^* = \sqrt{\sigma_1^*\sigma_2^*\sigma_3^*} = \sqrt{0.777912 \times 0.210603 \times 0.011484} = \sqrt{0.001884} \approx 0.0434.$$
