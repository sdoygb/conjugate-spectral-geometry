## §1 Standard Formulation of the Information Paradox

### 1.1 Origin of the Paradox

In semiclassical gravity, black holes evaporate through Hawking radiation. Hawking radiation has a thermal spectrum — a pure state collapsing into a black hole evaporates into a mixed state:

$$|\psi\rangle\langle\psi| \xrightarrow{\text{evaporation}} \frac{1}{Z} \sum_n |n\rangle\langle n| \cdot e^{-\beta E_n}, \quad Z = \sum_n e^{-\beta E_n}.$$

This violates the unitary evolution of quantum mechanics — information is lost in evaporation.

### 1.2 Difficulties of Standard Solutions

Solutions in the standard framework face difficulties:
- **Complementarity principle**: Information exists simultaneously inside and outside the horizon, but observers cannot verify it
- **Firewall**: High-energy modes at the horizon violate the Equivalence Principle
- **Remnants**: Information is stored in tiny remnants, but infinitely many remnant species lead to divergences

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the root of the paradox is that the standard framework confines information to the ℳ-sector (matter states). Once it is recognized that information is stored in the independent ℐ-sector, the paradox naturally dissolves.

### 1.3 Algebraic Root of the Paradox

The algebraic root of the information paradox lies in standard quantum field theory equating "information" with "matter states". In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the two belong to different sectors:

- **Matter states**: stored in the ℳ-sector, constrained by the 𝒞-sector causal structure
- **Quantum information**: stored in the ℐ-sector, independent of the causal structure

The standard framework conflates the two, so the 𝒞-sector horizon (causal boundary) is thought to trap both matter and information. Once it is recognized that the ℐ-sector is independent of the 𝒞-sector causal order, the horizon traps only matter, not information — the paradox dissolves.

## §2 Information Conservation Theorem

### 2.1 Main Theorem

**Theorem 8.4.2.01 (Black Hole Information Conservation Theorem).** The black hole evaporation process preserves information conservation. Information is always stored in the ℐ-sector, guaranteed by the unitarity of the diffusion-response equation. The horizon is the causal boundary of the 𝒞-sector metric, but the ℐ-sector penetrates the horizon — information is not lost.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Sector separation of information.** In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, a quantum state contains information from three sectors:

$$|\Psi\rangle = |\psi_\mathcal{M}\rangle \otimes |\psi_\mathcal{C}\rangle \otimes |\psi_\mathcal{I}\rangle.$$

The ℳ-sector stores matter states (particle positions, momenta), the 𝒞-sector stores causal order (event chronology), and the ℐ-sector stores quantum information (phase, entanglement). The three sectors are coupled through the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T$, but their information content is separately conserved.

**Algebraic definition of information content.** The information content of each sector is defined by the Shannon entropy of the corresponding eigenvalues:

$$\mathcal{I}_\mathcal{M} = -\sum_i \lambda_i^\mathcal{M} \ln \lambda_i^\mathcal{M}, \quad \mathcal{I}_\mathcal{C} = -\sum_i \lambda_i^\mathcal{C} \ln \lambda_i^\mathcal{C}, \quad \mathcal{I}_\mathcal{I} = -\sum_i \lambda_i^\mathcal{I} \ln \lambda_i^\mathcal{I}.$$

The unitarity of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix $T = \theta_I \cdot I + \theta_\tau \cdot P_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} \cdot P_{\sigma_2}$ guarantees separate conservation of information content in each of the three sectors:

$$\frac{d\mathcal{I}_\mathcal{M}}{dt} = \frac{d\mathcal{I}_\mathcal{C}}{dt} = \frac{d\mathcal{I}_\mathcal{I}}{dt} = 0.$$

> **Note:** Unitarity guarantees total information conservation, i.e., $\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{I}_\mathcal{M} + \mathcal{I}_\mathcal{C} + \mathcal{I}_\mathcal{I}) = 0$. Separate conservation of each sector's information content is a further assumption, requiring the condition of independent evolution without information exchange between sectors.

**Step 2: Penetrability of the ℐ-sector.** The ℐ-sector corresponds to the terminal duality $k_0 = 2$ (Cl(7) structure) of the coding orbit, which does not depend on the causal order of the 𝒞-sector. The black hole horizon is the causal boundary of the 𝒞-sector metric $g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{eff}} \propto \nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu \ln \rho_\mathcal{C}$ — the causal field density diverges at the horizon. But the ℐ-sector is independent of causal order and is not constrained by the horizon:

$$\rho_\mathcal{I}(r < r_s) \neq 0 \quad \text{(information field penetrates the horizon)}.$$

**Rigorous proof of ℐ-sector horizon penetration.** The dynamics of the ℐ-sector are governed by the diffusion equation:

$$\frac{\partial \rho_\mathcal{I}}{\partial t} = D_\mathcal{I} \nabla^2 \rho_\mathcal{I} - \Gamma_\mathcal{I} \rho_\mathcal{I}\mathcal{S}_\mathcal{I}.$$

Key point: the Laplacian $\nabla^2$ in the diffusion equation is an intrinsic operator of the ℐ-sector, not depending on the 𝒞-sector metric $g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{eff}}$. The ℐ-sector has its own "information metric" $\gamma_{\mu\nu}^\mathcal{I}$, independent of the 𝒞-sector metric:

$$\nabla^2_\mathcal{I} = \gamma^{\mu\nu}_\mathcal{I} \partial_\mu \partial_\nu \neq \nabla^2_\mathcal{C} = g^{\mu\nu,\text{eff}} \partial_\mu \partial_\nu.$$

The 𝒞-sector metric diverges at the horizon ($g^{\mu\nu,\text{eff}} \to \infty$), but the ℐ-sector metric $\gamma^{\mu\nu}_\mathcal{I}$ remains finite — the "information space" of the ℐ-sector has no singularity at the horizon. Hence the ℐ-sector diffusion equation remains well-defined at the horizon, and the information field $\rho_\mathcal{I}$ penetrates the horizon.

The source term $\mathcal{S}_\mathcal{I}^{\text{BH}}$ comes from the release of ℐ-sector density inside the black hole — information is transmitted from inside the horizon into Hawking radiation through the diffusion mechanism.

## §3 Physical Picture and Predictions

### 3.1 Sector Structure of Black Holes

The three-sector picture of black holes in Conjugate Spectral Geometry:

- **ℳ-sector**: Matter collapses to the singularity — mass is concentrated in the ℳ-sector
- **𝒞-sector**: Causal field density diverges at the horizon — the causal structure of $g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{eff}}$ produces the horizon
- **ℐ-sector**: Information field penetrates the horizon — quantum information is preserved through the diffusion equation

The singularity is not a physical endpoint — it is a coordinate singularity of the 𝒞-sector causal structure. The ℐ-sector remains well-defined at the singularity because the information field is independent of causal order.

**Algebraic definition of the horizon.** The horizon $r_s$ is determined by the divergence condition of the 𝒞-sector metric:

$$g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{eff}}(r_s) \to \infty \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu \ln \rho_\mathcal{C}(r_s) \to \infty.$$

This is a property of the 𝒞-sector, independent of the ℐ-sector. The horizon radius $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ is derived from the Schwarzschild solution of the 𝒞-sector metric — consistent with General Relativity, but with a different physical origin.

### 3.2 Geometric Interpretation of the Page Curve

The Page curve describes the evolution of entanglement entropy during black hole evaporation. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry:

- **Early evaporation**: Entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation grows — ℐ-sector information is still inside the horizon
- **Page time**: ℐ-sector diffusion begins transmitting a large amount of information into the radiation — entanglement entropy peaks then declines
- **Late evaporation**: ℐ-sector information is completely released — entanglement entropy tends to zero

The emergence of the Page time is not produced by wormhole or complexity hypotheses, but is a natural result of the ℐ-sector diffusion equation.

**Algebraic determination of the Page time.** The Page time $t_{\text{Page}}$ is determined by the ℐ-sector diffusion coefficient $D_\mathcal{I}$ and the black hole mass $M$:

$$t_{\text{Page}} \sim \frac{r_s^2}{D_\mathcal{I}} \sim \frac{4G^2M^2}{c^4 D_\mathcal{I}}.$$

The Page time is proportional to $M^2$ — more massive black holes have later Page times. This scaling law can be tested by analog gravity systems.

**Analytic form of the Page curve.** The ℐ-sector diffusion equation yields the analytic form of the Page curve:

$$S_{\text{Page}}(t) = S_0 \cdot \text{erf}\left(\frac{t}{\tau_\mathcal{I}}\right) \cdot \left[1 - \text{erf}\left(\frac{t - t_{\text{Page}}}{\tau_\mathcal{I}}\right)\right],$$

where $\tau_\mathcal{I} = r_s^2 / D_\mathcal{I}$ is the ℐ-sector diffusion time scale. The Page curve is an error function — a natural result of ℐ-sector diffusion, not a product of wormhole hypotheses.

### 3.3 No Firewall

The firewall hypothesis suggests that high-energy modes at the horizon violate the Equivalence Principle. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, no firewall exists:

- The ℐ-sector penetrates the horizon, producing no high-energy membrane
- Matter (ℳ-sector) crosses the horizon smoothly — the Equivalence Principle is preserved
- Information is transmitted through ℐ-sector diffusion, requiring no violent physical processes at the horizon

**Algebraic proof of no firewall.** The generation of a firewall requires high-energy excitation of the ℳ-sector at the horizon. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, ℳ-sector matter follows the 𝒞-sector metric $g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{eff}}$ and crosses the horizon smoothly (no high-energy membrane). Information transmission occurs through ℐ-sector diffusion (independent of the 𝒞-metric), requiring no high-energy modes in the ℳ-sector.

Specifically, preservation of the Equivalence Principle requires that ℳ-sector matter encounters no singularity at the horizon. Although the 𝒞-sector metric has a coordinate singularity at the horizon (in Schwarzschild coordinates), this can be eliminated by a coordinate transformation (such as Kruskal coordinates) — ℳ-sector matter crosses smoothly. Information transmission does not depend on the ℳ-sector, hence no high-energy modes are needed — no firewall. ∎

## §5 Complete Evolution of Black Hole Evaporation

The complete evolution of black hole evaporation divides into three stages:

**Stage 1: Collapse phase ($t < t_{\text{collapse}}$)**

**Stage 2: Evaporation phase ($t_{\text{collapse}} < t < t_{\text{evap}}$)**

- 𝒞-sector: Horizon shrinks, causal structure evolves

**Stage 3: Evaporation complete ($t = t_{\text{evap}}$)**
- ℳ-sector: Black hole mass tends to zero, matter completely converted to radiation

- ℐ-sector: All information released through Hawking radiation, information conserved

$$\frac{d\mathcal{I}_\mathcal{I}^{\text{out}}}{dt} = \frac{D_\mathcal{I}}{r_s^2(t)} \cdot \mathcal{I}_\mathcal{I}^{\text{in}}(t).$$

As $r_s(t) \to 0$, the release rate grows sharply — information is rapidly released at the end of evaporation. This explains the rapid decline of the Page curve at the end of evaporation.

### 5.3 Absence of Remnants

In the standard framework, information may be stored in Planck-scale remnants. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, no remnants exist:

- ℐ-sector information is completely released at the end of evaporation — no residual information
- ℳ-sector matter is completely converted to radiation — no residual matter

Black hole evaporation is complete — no remnants. Information conservation is achieved through ℐ-sector diffusion, requiring no remnant hypothesis.
