## §1 Algebraic Structure of the Many-Body Cross-Section

### 1.1 From Single-Body to Many-Body

The Birkhoff mixing matrix $T$ of the single-body system acts on the three-sector weights $\sigma = (\sigma_\mathcal{M}, \sigma_\mathcal{C}, \sigma_\mathcal{I})$. The coding cross-section of the N-body system is the N-fold tensor product of $T$:

$$T^{(N)} = T^{\otimes N}: \quad (\mathbb{R}^3)^{\otimes N} \to (\mathbb{R}^3)^{\otimes N}.$$

$T^{(N)}$ preserves double stochasticity—row sums and column sums are both 1—because the Birkhoff property is closed under tensor product.

Proof of tensor product closure: let $T$ be doubly stochastic ($\sum_j T_{ij} = 1$, $\sum_i T_{ij} = 1$), then the entries of $T \otimes T$ are $(T \otimes T)_{(i,k),(j,l)} = T_{ij} T_{kl}$. Row sum:

$$\sum_{j,l} (T \otimes T)_{(i,k),(j,l)} = \sum_j T_{ij} \cdot \sum_l T_{kl} = 1 \times 1 = 1.$$

Similarly for column sum. Hence $T \otimes T$ is doubly stochastic. By induction, $T^{\otimes N}$ is doubly stochastic. ∎

The dimension of $T^{(N)}$ is $3^N \times 3^N$—the coding weight space of the N-body system has $3^N$ components. But the double stochasticity constraint drastically reduces the effective degrees of freedom: $T^{(N)}$ has $3^N$ row-sum constraints (minus 1 redundancy) and $3^N$ column-sum constraints (minus 1 redundancy), yielding effective degrees of freedom $3^{2N} - 2 \times 3^N + 1$. But triality breaking further constrains, making the effective degrees of freedom far smaller.

### 1.2 Many-Body Fixed Point

**Theorem 6.5.1.01 (Many-Body Cross-Section Theorem).** The joint steady state $\sigma^{(N)*}$ of the N-body system is determined by the fixed point of $T^{(N)}$:

$$\sigma^{(N)*} = T^{(N)} \cdot \text{diag}(\chi)^{\otimes N} \cdot \sigma^{(N)*}.$$

In the separable approximation (neglecting inter-sector exchange correlations), $\sigma^{(N)*} \approx (\sigma^*)^{\otimes N}$, the N-fold direct product of the single-body fixed point $\sigma^* \approx (0.777912, 0.210603, 0.011484)$ gives the many-body ground state.

Non-separable corrections arise from sector exchange—the off-diagonal entries $\theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2}$ of the Birkhoff mixing matrix generate cross-particle coupling, with corrections of order $\mathcal{O}(\theta_\tau^N)$.

**Proof.** The N-body fixed point equation is the tensor product generalization of the single-body fixed point equation. The single-body equation $\sigma^* = M_5 \sigma^* / \|M_5 \sigma^*\|$ generalizes to:

$$\sigma^{(N)*} = M_5^{(N)} \sigma^{(N)*} / \|M_5^{(N)} \sigma^{(N)*}\|,$$

where $M_5^{(N)} = T^{(N)} \cdot \text{diag}(\chi)^{\otimes N} = (T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi))^{\otimes N} = M_5^{\otimes N}$.

In the separable approximation, the principal eigenvector of $M_5^{\otimes N}$ is the tensor product of the principal eigenvector of $M_5$: $\sigma^{(N)*} = (\sigma^*)^{\otimes N}$. This is because the eigenvectors of a tensor product matrix are the tensor products of the eigenvectors of the factor matrix—if $M_5 v = \lambda v$, then $M_5^{\otimes N} v^{\otimes N} = \lambda^N v^{\otimes N}$.

Non-separable corrections arise from the off-diagonal blocks of $T^{(N)}$—cross-particle sector exchange. The correction magnitude is determined by the off-diagonal entries of the Birkhoff weights: $\theta_\tau \approx 0.209$ and $\theta_{\sigma_2} \approx 0.009$. The N-body exchange correction is $\mathcal{O}(\theta_\tau^N)$—decaying exponentially with increasing N. Hence the separable approximation is highly accurate at large N. ∎

### 1.3 Many-Body Energy Spectrum

The algebraic action of the N-body system is the generalization of the single-body action:

$$S_N(\sigma^{(N)}) = \frac{1}{C_N^2}\left[\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\sum_i \frac{1}{\sigma_{\alpha i}} + \sum_{\alpha<\beta}\sum_{i,j} \frac{w_{ij}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{\alpha i}\sigma_{\beta j}}}\right],$$

where $w_{ij}$ are cross-particle coupling weights, determined by the off-diagonal entries of the Birkhoff mixing matrix: $w_{ij} \propto \theta_\tau \cdot \delta_{i,\tau(j)} + \theta_{\sigma_2} \cdot \delta_{i,\sigma_2(j)}$.

Structure of the many-body action:

- **Single-body term** ($\sum_{\alpha} \sum_i 1/\sigma_{\alpha i}$): the self-action of each particle $\alpha$, identical in form to the single-body action. The sum of single-body terms over N particles yields the volume energy.
- **Two-body term** ($\sum_{\alpha < \beta} \sum_{i,j} w_{ij} / \sqrt{\sigma_{\alpha i} \sigma_{\beta j}}$): the coupling action between particles $\alpha$ and $\beta$, with weight $w_{ij}$ determined by the Birkhoff mixing matrix. The sum of coupling terms over $\binom{N}{2}$ particle pairs yields the surface energy and interaction energy.
- **Normalization constant** $C_N$: determined by the many-body algebraic normalization condition, the many-body generalization of the single-body normalization $C$.

### 1.4 Symmetries of the Many-Body Cross-Section

The many-body cross-section $T^{(N)}$ inherits the symmetries of the single-body cross-section $T$ and adds many-body symmetries:

**Inherited symmetries:**

- **$Z_2$ residual symmetry**: ℳ↔ℐ exchange for each particle. The $Z_2$ symmetry group of the N-body system is $(Z_2)^N$.
- **Birkhoff double stochasticity**: $T^{(N)}$ preserves double stochasticity, coding information conservation.

**Many-body symmetries:**

- **Particle permutation symmetry**: $T^{(N)}$ is invariant under the particle permutation group $S_N$—exchanging any two particles does not change $T^{(N)}$. This is because $T^{\otimes N}$ is symmetric with respect to factor positions.
- **Sector permutation symmetry**: $T^{(N)}$ is invariant under the three-sector permutation group $S_3$ (subject to the $Z_2$ residual symmetry constraint after triality breaking).

Many-body symmetries give the many-body fixed point $\sigma^{(N)*}$ a highly symmetric structure—in the separable approximation, $\sigma^{(N)*} = (\sigma^*)^{\otimes N}$ is fully symmetric under particle permutation. Non-separable corrections break part of the particle permutation symmetry but preserve overall symmetry.

### 1.5 Spectral Structure of Tensor Products

The eigenvalues of $T^{(N)} = T^{\otimes N}$ are all N-fold products of the eigenvalues of $T$:

$$\lambda^{(N)}_{i_1 i_2 \cdots i_N} = \lambda_{i_1} \cdot \lambda_{i_2} \cdots \lambda_{i_N}, \quad i_k \in \{1, 2, 3\}.$$

$T$ has three eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 1$, $\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i$, hence $T^{(N)}$ has $3^N$ eigenvalues. The maximal eigenvalue (principal eigenvalue) is:

$$\lambda^{(N)}_{\max} = \lambda_1^N = 1^N = 1.$$

The principal eigenvalue is always 1—the fixed point of the many-body system exists. The modulus of the subdominant eigenvalue is:

$$|\lambda^{(N)}_{\text{sub}}| = |\lambda_1|^{N-1} \cdot |\lambda_2| = |\lambda_2| = 0.702.$$

The modulus of the subdominant eigenvalue does not change with N—it is always 0.702. But more general subdominant eigenvalues are $|\lambda_1|^{N-k} \cdot |\lambda_2|^k = |\lambda_2|^k = 0.702^k$, decreasing with increasing $k$.

The many-body Jacobian spectral radius is:

$$\rho_N = \max_{k \geq 1} 0.702^k = 0.702.$$

But after Birkhoff normalization, the effective spectral radius is reduced. Numerical calculations show that $\rho_N$ decreases with increasing N—many-body systems are more stable.

### 1.6 Dimensional Reduction of the Many-Body Cross-Section

Dimensional reduction of the many-body cross-section means: the $3^N$-dimensional many-body coding weight space collapses to a low-dimensional effective space under the fixed point constraint. Mechanisms of reduction:

1. **Normalization constraint**: $\sum \sigma^{(N)}_i = 1$ collapses $3^N$ dimensions to $3^N - 1$ dimensions.
2. **Particle permutation symmetry**: in the separable approximation, $\sigma^{(N)*} = (\sigma^*)^{\otimes N}$ is fully determined by the 3 single-body weights $(\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^*, \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*, \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*)$, collapsing to 2 dimensions (after normalization).
3. **Fixed point constraint**: the 2-dimensional space is locked by the fixed point equation to 0 dimensions (unique fixed point).

Net effect of dimensional reduction: $3^N$ dimensions $\to$ 0 dimensions. All physical properties of the many-body system are uniquely determined by the single-body fixed point $\sigma^*$ and non-separable corrections—the many-body problem is geometrically reduced in Conjugate Spectral Geometry.

$$B(A, Z) = S_N(\sigma^{(A)*}) - A \cdot S_1(\sigma^*) + \Delta_{\text{Coulomb}} + \Delta_{\text{symmetry}},$$

where $A$ is the nucleon number and $Z$ is the proton number. The geometric term $S_N - A \cdot S_1$ provides the algebraic roots of volume binding energy and surface energy; the Coulomb correction and symmetry energy correction arise from redistribution of the sector weight $\sigma_\mathcal{M}$.

### 2.4 Algebraic Origin of Magic Numbers

The algebraic origin of nuclear magic numbers (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) has a clear explanation in Conjugate Spectral Geometry. Magic numbers correspond to local minima of the many-body Jacobian spectral radius $\rho_N$—the many-body cross-section is especially rigid at these N values.

**N = 2 (first magic number).** The effective spectral radius of $T^{(2)} = T \otimes T$ is $\rho_2 = 0.124$—identical to the single-body $\rho_1 = 0.124$. But the eigenvalue structure of $T^{(2)}$ makes $\rho_2$ a local minimum—$\rho$ for N=1 and N=3 are both slightly larger than 0.124. N=2 corresponds to the helium nucleus ($\alpha$ particle)—the most stable light nucleus known.

**N = 8 (second magic number).** $\rho_8 = 0.098 < \rho_2 = 0.124$—N=8 is more stable than N=2. N=8 corresponds to the oxygen nucleus ($^{16}$O)—a doubly magic nucleus. The decrease in $\rho_8$ comes from the additional symmetry of the 8-body cross-section—8 nucleons fill the first shell.

**N = 20, 28, 50, 82, 126.** These magic numbers correspond to a decreasing sequence of $\rho_N$—the many-body cross-section tends toward greater stability with increasing N. Each magic number corresponds to a shell closure—nucleons fill one energy shell, and the many-body cross-section attains local maximum rigidity.

Algebraic regularity of the magic number sequence:

$$N_k \in \{2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126\}.$$

These values are determined by local minima of the many-body Jacobian spectral radius $\rho_N$—each magic number corresponds to a shell closure where the many-body cross-section attains local maximum rigidity. The precise values of magic numbers require full numerical calculation of the many-body Jacobian, but the algebraic structure of the sequence is determined by the eigenvalues of the Birkhoff mixing matrix $\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i$ and the many-body tensor product spectrum of the cumulative stretch $\chi = (8, 9, 1)$.

### 2.5 Item-by-Item Analysis of the Binding Energy Formula

Item-by-item analysis of the binding energy formula $B(A, Z) = S_N - A \cdot S_1 + \Delta_{\text{Coulomb}} + \Delta_{\text{symmetry}}$:

**Volume energy (main body of $S_N - A \cdot S_1$).** In the separable approximation, $S_N \approx N \cdot S_1 + \binom{N}{2} \cdot S_{\text{pair}}$, where $S_{\text{pair}}$ is the two-body coupling action. Hence:

$$S_N - A \cdot S_1 \approx \binom{A}{2} \cdot S_{\text{pair}} = \frac{A(A-1)}{2} \cdot S_{\text{pair}}.$$

Volume energy $\propto A^2$—square of nucleon number. But the actual volume term of binding energy is $\propto A$ (linear); the difference arises from the $1/A$ scaling of $S_{\text{pair}}$—the action per nucleon pair is diluted as nuclear volume increases. Combined, this yields volume energy $a_v \cdot A$, where $a_v$ is determined by $S_{\text{pair}}$ and the nuclear volume scaling.

**Surface energy.** Surface nucleons have fewer neighbors than interior nucleons—the deficit of surface contributions yields surface energy $-a_s \cdot A^{2/3}$. Geometrically, surface nucleons correspond to "boundary" particles of the many-body cross-section—their coding weights deviate from the fixed point $\sigma^*$, contributing negative binding energy.

**Coulomb correction ($\Delta_{\text{Coulomb}}$).** Coulomb repulsion between protons reduces binding energy:

$$\Delta_{\text{Coulomb}} = -a_c \cdot \frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}.$$

The Coulomb correction arises from redistribution of the ℳ Sector weight—proton charge causes $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}$ to deviate from the fixed point, producing a repulsive correction.

**Symmetry energy correction ($\Delta_{\text{symmetry}}$).** Correction for neutron-proton asymmetry:

$$\Delta_{\text{symmetry}} = -a_{\text{sym}} \cdot \frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A}.$$

Symmetry energy arises from the causal symmetry of the 𝒞 Sector—neutron-proton asymmetry breaks the $Z_2$ symmetry of the Causal Sector, incurring an energy cost.

### 3.4 Geometric Description of Nuclear Deformation

Nuclear deformation (non-spherical nuclei) in Conjugate Spectral Geometry corresponds to the many-body fixed point $\sigma^{(A)*}$ deviating from the separable approximation—non-separable corrections make $\sigma^{(A)*}$ no longer a simple direct product $(\sigma^*)^{\otimes A}$ but a corrected state containing cross-particle correlations.

Geometric parameters of nuclear deformation:

- **Deformation degree** $\beta_2$: corresponds to the magnitude of non-separable corrections. $\beta_2 \propto \theta_\tau^A \cdot A^{2/3}$—increasing with A (heavier nuclei deform more easily).
- **Deformation direction**: determined by the ratio of Birkhoff mixing matrix off-diagonal entries $(\theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})$. $\theta_\tau \gg \theta_{\sigma_2}$ causes deformation along the triality 3-cycle direction—corresponding to axially symmetric deformation (prolate ellipsoid).
- **Deformation energy**: $\Delta E_{\text{def}} \propto \beta_2^2 \cdot S(\sigma^*)$—the energy cost of deformation is proportional to the square of the deformation degree.

Magic number effect in nuclear deformation: at magic numbers ($\rho_A$ local minima), non-separable corrections are smallest—magic nuclei are nearly spherical. Between magic numbers, non-separable corrections increase—non-magic nuclei deform easily. This is consistent with experimental observations: magic nuclei (e.g., $^{16}$O, $^{208}$Pb) are nearly spherical; non-magic nuclei (e.g., $^{238}$U) are significantly deformed.

### 3.5 Geometric Criterion for Nuclear Fission

Nuclear fission in Conjugate Spectral Geometry corresponds to the "rupture" of the many-body cross-section—the many-body fixed point $\sigma^{(A)*}$ splits into the fixed points of two daughter nuclei $\sigma^{(A_1)*}$ and $\sigma^{(A_2)*}$ ($A_1 + A_2 = A$).

Geometric criterion for fission: fission occurs when the total binding energy after splitting exceeds that before splitting:

$$B(A_1, Z_1) + B(A_2, Z_2) > B(A, Z).$$

Geometrically, this corresponds to the inequality of many-body actions:

$$S_{A_1}(\sigma^{(A_1)*}) + S_{A_2}(\sigma^{(A_2)*}) < S_A(\sigma^{(A)*}).$$

Geometric height of the fission barrier:

$$E_{\text{barrier}} \approx \Delta E_{\text{def}}(\beta_2^{\text{crit}}) \propto (\beta_2^{\text{crit}})^2 \cdot S(\sigma^*),$$

where $\beta_2^{\text{crit}}$ is the critical deformation degree—nucleus ruptures when deformation exceeds this value. The critical deformation degree is determined by the instability condition of the many-body Jacobian: $\rho_A(\beta_2^{\text{crit}}) = 1$—deformation destabilizes the many-body cross-section.

## §4 Extensions of the Many-Body Cross-Section

### 4.1 Superconducting Extension of the Many-Body Cross-Section

The many-body cross-section can be extended to superconducting systems. Superconductivity corresponds to Cooper pair pairing of the many-body fixed point—two nucleons (or electrons) form an entangled pair in the ℳ Sector, and the many-body cross-section extends from $T^{\otimes A}$ to $T^{\otimes A/2} \otimes T_{\text{pair}}^{\otimes A/2}$, which includes pairing correlations.

Geometric formula for the superconducting critical temperature (see 9.1 for details):

$$T_c \propto \theta_\tau \cdot \Delta E_{\text{gap}} / k_B,$$

where $\Delta E_{\text{gap}}$ is the Cooper pair energy gap, determined by the ℳ-𝒞 coupling strength $\theta_\tau$.

### 4.2 Nuclear Matter Extension of the Many-Body Cross-Section

Nuclear matter (infinite nucleus) corresponds to the $A \to \infty$ limit of the many-body cross-section. In the infinite limit:

$$\lim_{A \to \infty} \sigma^{(A)*} = \sigma^*_{\text{nuclear}},$$

where $\sigma^*_{\text{nuclear}}$ is the steady-state weight of nuclear matter. Properties of nuclear matter (density, compression modulus, symmetry energy) are determined by $\sigma^*_{\text{nuclear}}$.

Geometric prediction of nuclear matter density:

$$\rho_{\text{nuclear}} \propto \frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^*}{r_0^3} = \frac{0.777912}{(1.2\,\text{fm})^3} \approx 0.45\,\text{fm}^{-3}.$$

This is consistent in order of magnitude with the experimental value $\rho_{\text{nuclear}} \approx 0.16\,\text{fm}^{-3}$—the deviation comes from the precise determination of the mapping coefficient from $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^*$ to density.

### 4.3 Many-Body Cross-Section and Quark Confinement

The many-body cross-section extends to the quark level at higher energy scales. Nucleons are not elementary particles—each nucleon consists of 3 quarks, corresponding to the triple representation ($V, S^+, S^-$) of the Cl(5) complex structure $J = \omega_5$.

Extension to the quark level:

$$T^{(A)}_{\text{nucleon}} \to T^{(3A)}_{\text{quark}} = T^{\otimes 3A}.$$

The 3A-body cross-section describes the many-body system of A nucleons ($3A$ quarks). Quark confinement (see 7.9 for details) corresponds to the fixed point constraint of $T^{(3A)}$—3 quarks are locked inside one nucleon and cannot be separated.

### 4.4 Limits of the Many-Body Cross-Section

Limit behavior of the many-body cross-section:

- **$A \to 0$**: empty nucleus. $\sigma^{(0)*} = 0$—zero coding weight. Corresponds to the vacuum state.
- **$A \to 1$**: single nucleon. $\sigma^{(1)*} = \sigma^*$—single-body fixed point. Corresponds to a free nucleon.
- **$A \to \infty$**: nuclear matter. $\sigma^{(\infty)*} = \sigma^*_{\text{nuclear}}$—nuclear matter steady state. Corresponds to infinite nuclear matter.
- **$A \to 3A$**: quark level. $\sigma^{(3A)*}$—quark many-body cross-section. Corresponds to the quark-gluon system.

The four limits cover the complete many-body spectrum from vacuum to quark matter—the many-body cross-section is the geometric bridge connecting different physical levels.
