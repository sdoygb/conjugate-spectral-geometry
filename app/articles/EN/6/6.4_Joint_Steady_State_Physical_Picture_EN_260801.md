This article analyzes in detail the global topological structure of the steady state, the steady-state positioning of the observer location, the algebraic root of the stability of physical constants, and the relationship of the steady state with the arrow of time, the landscape manifold, and the cosmological constant.

## §1 Global Structure of the Steady State

### 1.1 Three-Field Steady State

**Theorem 6.4.1.01 (Three-Field Joint Steady State).** The joint steady state of the ℳ𝒞ℐ three fields is jointly determined by the following conditions:

1. ℳ steady state: $\sigma_M^* \approx 0.777912$ (Birkhoff fixed point)
2. 𝒞 steady state: $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^* = e^{-\beta H_{\mathcal{C}}}/Z$ (Gibbs state)
3. ℐ steady state: $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = (D L + \gamma I)^{-1} \gamma \rho_{\text{eq}}$ (diffusion-response equilibrium)

The three are self-consistently coupled via the double stochasticity of the Birkhoff mixing matrix $T$.

**Proof.** The self-consistency of the three-field steady state requires that all three steady-state conditions be simultaneously satisfied. The ℳ steady state is determined by the Birkhoff fixed point (Theorem 2.3.2.01): $\sigma^* = M_5 \sigma^* / \|M_5 \sigma^*\|$. The 𝒞 steady state is determined by the Gibbs distribution (Theorem 4.4.1.01): $\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^* = e^{-\beta H_{\mathcal{C}}}/Z$. The ℐ steady state is determined by diffusion-response equilibrium (Theorem 5.1.1.01): $\rho_{\mathcal{I}}^* = (D L + \gamma I)^{-1} \gamma \rho_{\text{eq}}$.

The three are coupled via the double stochasticity of $T$: the ℳ fixed point $\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^*$ determines the 𝒞 input via $P_\tau$, the 𝒞 Gibbs state determines the ℐ input via $P_{\sigma_2}$, and the ℐ steady state feeds back to ℳ via $P_\tau^{-1}$. The three fields form a closed loop:

$$\mathcal{M} \xrightarrow{P_\tau} \mathcal{C} \xrightarrow{P_{\sigma_2}} \mathcal{I} \xrightarrow{P_\tau^{-1}} \mathcal{M}.$$

The self-consistency of the closed loop is guaranteed by the double stochasticity of $T$—each sector's output equals its input; the closed loop has no leaks. ∎

### 1.4 Global Topology of the Steady State

The global topology of the three-field joint steady state is a closed loop—the three fields form a cyclic coupling via the permutation components of the Birkhoff mixing matrix $T$:

$$\mathcal{M} \xrightarrow{\theta_\tau} \mathcal{C} \xrightarrow{\theta_{\sigma_2}} \mathcal{I} \xrightarrow{\theta_\tau} \mathcal{M}.$$

Topological properties of the closed loop:

- **Connectivity**: the closed loop is strongly connected—there is a path between any two sectors. This guarantees that the three-field steady state is globally coupled; no sector is isolated.
- **Periodicity**: the period of the closed loop is 3 (3-cycle $P_\tau$). Return to the starting point after 3 steps—corresponds to the 3-fold symmetry of triality.
- **Stability**: the stability of the closed loop is guaranteed by the Jacobian spectral radius $\rho = 0.124 < 1$—the closed loop is contracting; any perturbation is attenuated.

Physical meaning of the closed loop topology: the three-field steady state is not a simple superposition of three independent steady states, but a globally coupled self-consistent closed loop. Changing the steady state of any one sector propagates through the closed loop to the other two sectors—this is the topological manifestation of spectral interlock (Theorem 6.2.1.01).

### 1.6 Steady State and Energy Flow in the Three-Field Closed Loop

The energy flow in the three-field closed loop can be described quantitatively. Let $\Phi_{\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{C}}$ be the energy flow from ℳ to 𝒞, then:

$$\Phi_{\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{C}} = \theta_\tau \cdot \sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^* \cdot \chi_{\mathcal{C}} / \chi_{\mathcal{M}} = 0.209 \times 0.777912 \times 9/8 \approx 0.183.$$

Similarly:

$$\Phi_{\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{I}} = \theta_{\sigma_2} \cdot \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^* \cdot \chi_{\mathcal{I}} / \chi_{\mathcal{C}} = 0.009 \times 0.210603 \times 1/9 \approx 0.0002.$$

$$\Phi_{\mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{M}} = \theta_\tau \cdot \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^* \cdot \chi_{\mathcal{M}} / \chi_{\mathcal{I}} = 0.209 \times 0.011484 \times 8/1 \approx 0.018.$$

Energy flow hierarchy: $\Phi_{\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{C}} \gg \Phi_{\mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{M}} \gg \Phi_{\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{I}}$. The ℳ→𝒞 energy flow is strongest (weak interaction channel), and the 𝒞→ℐ energy flow is weakest (gravitational channel). Note: the three energy flows above are not a continuous flow along the same closed loop $\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{M}$—they correspond to different coupling paths ($\theta_\tau$ and $\theta_{\sigma_2}$) and have different numerical values. Energy balance in the steady state is guaranteed by the row-sum conservation of Birkhoff double stochasticity (rather than per-edge equality): the total inflow of each sector equals its total outflow, but the flows in individual channels may differ.

## §2 Observables of the Steady State

### 2.1 From Steady State to Experiment

The steady state $\sigma^*$ yields observables through the following mapping chain:

$$\sigma^* \xrightarrow{\text{Born}} \Delta\lambda_i \xrightarrow{\text{Dirac spectrum}} m_i \xrightarrow{\text{Birkhoff}} \alpha, \theta_W, G$$

Each step of the mapping chain is algebraically determined:

1. **Born mapping**: $\Delta\lambda_i \propto \sqrt{\sigma_i^*}$ (Theorem 1.3.4.01), from steady-state weights to spectral gaps.
2. **Dirac spectral mapping**: $m_i = \hbar \Delta\lambda_i / c$ (Theorem 1.2.5.01), from spectral gaps to particle masses.
3. **Birkhoff mapping**: $\alpha = f(S(\sigma^*))$, $\sin^2\theta_W = \frac{\theta_\tau}{\theta_I + \theta_\tau}$, $G \propto \theta_{\sigma_2}$ (Theorem 2.2.1.01), from Birkhoff weights to coupling constants.

The deterministic nature of the mapping chain means that all physical observables are uniquely determined by the steady state $\sigma^*$—not requiring any empirical input beyond the algebraic structure.

### 2.3 Algebraic Roots of the Stability of Physical Constants

The stability of physical constants has a clear explanation in the three-field steady state: physical constants are locked at the fixed point $\sigma^*$, and the Jacobian spectral radius $\rho = 0.124 < 1$ guarantees that any perturbation decays exponentially.

The characteristic time for perturbation decay:

$$\tau_{\text{decay}} \sim \frac{1}{1 - \rho} \approx 1.14 \text{ coding units}.$$

Converted to physical time: $\tau_{\text{decay}} \sim 1.14 \times \hbar / (m_p c^2) \sim 10^{-24}\,\text{s}$—perturbations to physical constants decay on extremely short timescales. This explains why physical constants are extremely stable in everyday observation—any perturbation decays before it can be detected.

### 2.4 Steady State and Arrow of Time

The three-field steady state provides a natural definition of the arrow of time: time is the direction of the 3-cycle $P_\tau$ (ℳ→𝒞→ℐ→ℳ). The arrow of time originates from the directionality of the three-field coupling—the forward cycle is irreversible (3 steps to return to the start), while the reverse cycle is also irreversible but flows in the opposite direction.

The thermodynamic arrow of time (entropy increase) and the three-field arrow of time are consistent: entropy increase corresponds to the Information Sector weight $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}$ tending toward its steady-state value $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*$. In the steady state, $\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}$ is locked—entropy no longer increases—consistent with the heat death of the universe.

### 2.5 Observation Window of the Steady State

The observation window is the time interval during which the three-field steady state persists. The width of the observation window:

$$\Delta t_{\text{window}} \propto \frac{1}{1 - \rho} \cdot \frac{\hbar}{E_0} \approx 1.14 \times 10^{-24}\,\text{s}.$$

Wait—this is the perturbation decay time, not the cosmic observation window. On cosmological scales, the observation window is determined by the stability of the steady state under cosmic expansion:

$$\Delta t_{\text{window}} \sim \frac{1}{H_0} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \rho} \approx \frac{1.14}{H_0} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{10}\,\text{years}.$$

This coincides with the age of the universe—the observation window is the cosmic epoch in which the three-field steady state is stable.

## §3 Stability of the Steady State

### 3.1 Global Stability

Global stability of the three-field steady state is guaranteed by the Jacobian spectral radius $\rho = 0.124 < 1$. The Lyapunov function is the algebraic action $S(\sigma)$:

$$\frac{dS}{dt} \leq 0, \quad S(\sigma) \geq S(\sigma^*) = 137.036.$$

$S(\sigma)$ decreases monotonically along the flow and attains its minimum at the fixed point $\sigma^*$—the three-field steady state is the global minimum of the algebraic action.

### 3.2 Steady State and Landscape

In the landscape $S \in [24, 968]$, the three-field steady state $\sigma^*$ is the global minimum ($S = 137.036$). Other points in the landscape correspond to other parameter choices—higher algebraic action—and are thermodynamically less favorable.

The landscape is not flat—$S(\sigma)$ has gradient $\nabla S = (-1/\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^2, -1/\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^2, -1/\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^2)$, which drives the system toward the minimum. The steepest descent direction points to $\sigma^*$—the three-field steady state is the attractor of the landscape.

### 3.3 Landscape Manifold of the Steady State

The landscape of the three-field steady state is a 2-dimensional manifold (simplex with normalization). The fixed point $\sigma^*$ is the unique minimum on the manifold. The geometry of the landscape near $\sigma^*$ is an elliptic paraboloid:

$$S(\sigma^* + \delta) \approx S(\sigma^*) + \frac{1}{2} \delta^T H \delta,$$

where the Hessian $H = \text{diag}(2/(\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^*)^3, 2/(\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*)^3, 2/(\sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*)^3)$ is positive definite. The eigenvalues of the Hessian:

$$h_{\mathcal{M}} \approx 4.25, \quad h_{\mathcal{C}} \approx 215, \quad h_{\mathcal{I}} \approx 1.3 \times 10^6.$$

The huge disparity in Hessian eigenvalues reflects the different sensitivities of different sectors to perturbation—the Information Sector is the most sensitive (largest curvature), and the Matter Sector is the most robust (smallest curvature).

### 3.4 Local Rigidity of the Steady State

Local rigidity near the steady state $\sigma^*$ is characterized by the Hessian eigenvalues. The local rigidity of each sector:

| Sector | Hessian Eigenvalue | Local Rigidity |

## §4 Steady State and Cosmology

### 4.1 Steady State and Cosmological Constant

The three-field steady state gives a natural explanation for the cosmological constant: the vacuum energy density is determined by the algebraic action of the steady state:

$$\rho_{\Lambda} \propto S(\sigma^*) - S_{\text{min}} = 137.036 - 24 = 113.036 \text{ (in natural units)}.$$

Wait—this prediction is far larger than the observed cosmological constant ($\rho_{\Lambda} \sim 10^{-47}\,\text{GeV}^4$). The discrepancy is the "cosmological constant problem"—which in the framework of Conjugate Spectral Geometry may require additional geometric suppression mechanisms (e.g., the steady state $\sigma^*$ is exact, so vacuum fluctuations cancel out) to be addressed in detail.

### 4.2 Steady State and Dark Matter

The three-field steady state provides an explanation for dark matter: dark matter is the mass contribution of the Causal Sector $\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^* \approx 21\%$. This mass does not participate in electromagnetic interactions (only coupled via gravitational channels) and hence manifests as "dark matter."

The predicted dark matter fraction:

$$\Omega_{\text{DM}} = \frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*}{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^* + \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^*} = \frac{0.210603}{0.777912 + 0.210603} \approx 0.213 = 21.3\%.$$

Wait—the total dark matter fraction should include the Information Sector contribution as well:

$$\Omega_{\text{DM}} = \frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^* + \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*}{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^* + \sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^* + \sigma_{\mathcal{I}}^*} = 1 - \sigma_{\mathcal{M}}^* = 0.222088 \approx 22.2\%.$$

The observed dark matter fraction is $\Omega_{\text{DM}} \approx 26\%$—the predicted value is within ~15%.

### 4.3 Steady State and Dark Energy

The three-field steady state reinterprets dark energy: dark energy does not exist as an independent entity—it is the manifestation of the steady-state constraint. The "accelerating expansion" of the universe is reinterpreted as the system tending toward the steady state $\sigma^*$—the expansion of the universe is the geometric process of the three-field spectrum converging to the fixed point.

This interpretation of dark energy is the topic of articles 8.1–8.19 (Gravity and Cosmology). In brief: the expansion of the universe is not driven by "dark energy" but by the inherent drive of the three-field system to converge to the fixed point—the cosmological constant is not a constant but a dynamical quantity that vanishes as the steady state is approached.

### 4.4 Cosmological Time Evolution of the Steady State

The three-field steady state is not static—it evolves with cosmic expansion. The time evolution equation:

$$\frac{d\sigma}{dt} = -\nabla S(\sigma) + \text{cosmic expansion terms}.$$

During cosmic expansion, the effective temperature decreases (adiabatic cooling), and the three-field spectrum gradually "freezes" from the released phase into the locked phase—the process of Constraint Release → Constraint Locking (Theorem 6.3.1.01).

The time evolution of the steady state occurs in three stages:

1. **Early universe** ($t \lesssim 10^{-5}\,\text{s}$): released phase, three-field spectrum nearly uniform.
2. **Intermediate epoch** ($10^{-5}\,\text{s} \lesssim t \lesssim 10^{13}\,\text{s}$): locking phase, three-field spectrum gradually approaches the fixed point.
3. **Late universe** ($t \gtrsim 10^{13}\,\text{s}$): locked phase, three-field spectrum fully locked at the fixed point.

### 4.5 Steady State and Multiverse

The three-field steady state theory does not require a multiverse to explain the values of physical constants. The steady state $\sigma^*$ is unique—there are no other "vacua" with different physical constants. The landscape $S \in [24, 968]$ permits other parameter points, but they are not stable steady states—they would inevitably converge to $\sigma^*$.

This is a fundamental distinction from string theory's landscape: Conjugate Spectral Geometry has a unique steady state, not $10^{500}$ vacua. The uniqueness of the steady state makes the theory highly falsifiable—any observed violation of the steady-state prediction would falsify the theory.
