6.3 Constraint Release Effect

Abstract

This article analyzes the relaxation effect of Completeness constraints—Constraint Release. When certain constraints of the three-field coupling are relaxed (e.g., the high-temperature limit), spectral interlock is partially lifted, allowing more parametric degrees of freedom. The Constraint Release effect explains the behavior of running coupling constants. This article presents the energy scale of constraint release, a complete derivation of running coupling constants, the geometric origin of asymptotic freedom, and experimental comparisons of running.

§1 Hierarchy of Constraints

1.1 Three-Layer Constraints

The three-field coupling is subject to three layers of constraints:

Layer Constraint Rigidity Release Condition
1 Bott periodicity rigidity Absolute rigidity Non-releasable
2 Birkhoff double stochasticity Strong rigidity Extreme conditions
3 Fixed point convergence Medium rigidity High temperature, high energy

Physical meaning of the three-layer constraints:

First layer: Bott periodicity rigidity. The 7-step truncation and Berry phase \(2\pi\) of the Coding Orbit are locked by Bott periodicity \(\delta^8\)—this is a topological constraint that no physical condition can alter. Bott periodicity rigidity guarantees that the structure of the Coding Orbit is invariant at any energy scale.

Second layer: Birkhoff double stochasticity. Row-sum = column-sum = 1 of \(T\) is an algebraic constraint, determined by the permutation orbit structure of triality breaking \(S_3 \to Z_2\). Double stochasticity guarantees conservation of coding information. Under extreme conditions (e.g., near a black hole horizon), double stochasticity may be modified—but such modification requires enormous energy density and is unrealizable in everyday physics.

Third layer: Fixed point convergence. The Birkhoff weights \((\theta_I, \theta_\tau, \theta_{\sigma_2})\) fixed at the fixed point value are a dynamical constraint—guaranteed by the Jacobian spectral radius \(\rho < 1\). At high energies, the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit is “smeared out,” and the fixed point constraint is partially released—this is the geometric origin of running coupling constants.

1.2 Constraint Release

Theorem 6.3.1.01 (Constraint Release Theorem). At energy scale \(E \gg E_0 = \hbar c / r_M\), the third-layer constraint (fixed point convergence) is partially released:

  • Birkhoff weights \(\theta_k\) are no longer fixed at the fixed point value
  • Effective \(\theta_k(E)\) runs with energy
  • But \(\sum \theta_k = 1\) (double stochasticity) is still maintained

Proof. The physical basis of the fixed point constraint is the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit—the 7-layer coding \(E_1, \ldots, E_7\) is resolvable at low energies, and the algebraic structure of each layer rigidly determines \(\theta_k\). At high energies \(E \gg E_0\), the energy resolution exceeds the coding layer spacing, and the 7-layer coding is no longer resolvable—the discrete structure is “smeared out” into a continuous structure.

In the continuum limit, the fixed point equation \(\sigma^* = M_5 \sigma^* / \|M_5 \sigma^*\|\) degenerates into a continuous equation:

\[\sigma(E) = M_5(E) \sigma(E) / \|M_5(E) \sigma(E)\|,\]

where \(M_5(E) = T(E) \cdot \text{diag}(\chi)\) allows \(T(E)\) to vary with energy. But \(T(E)\) must still satisfy double stochasticity (second-layer constraint not released):

\[\sum_j T_{ij}(E) = 1, \quad \sum_i T_{ij}(E) = 1, \quad \forall E.\]

Hence \(T(E)\) varies continuously on the Birkhoff polytope—\(\theta_k(E)\) are continuous functions on the polytope, subject to the normalization constraint \(\sum \theta_k = 1\). The fixed point value \(\theta_k(E_0) = \theta_k^*\) is the low-energy limit, and at high energies \(\theta_k(E)\) deviates from \(\theta_k^*\). ∎

1.3 Running Coupling Constants

Constraint release causes running of coupling constants:

\[\alpha_i(E) = \alpha_i(E_0) \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{\beta_i},\]

where \(\beta_i\) is the running exponent, determined by the eigenvalues of the Birkhoff mixing matrix:

\[\beta_i \propto \text{Re}(1 - \lambda_i) = \begin{cases} 0 & (i=1, \text{principal eigenvalue}) \\ 0.322 & (i=2,3) \end{cases}.\]

The principal eigenvalue does not run (\(\beta_1 = 0\))—electromagnetic coupling does not run at low energies. Non-principal eigenvalues run (\(\beta_{2,3} \neq 0\))—weak and gravitational couplings run.

1.4 Derivation of Running Exponents

The derivation of running exponents \(\beta_i\) is based on the continuumization of constraint release. In the continuum limit, linearization of the fixed point equation yields:

\[\frac{d\sigma}{d\ln E} = (T(E) - I) \cdot \sigma.\]

Substituting \(\sigma = \sigma^* + \Delta\sigma(E)\) and linearizing:

\[\frac{d\Delta\sigma}{d\ln E} = (T^* - I) \cdot \Delta\sigma + \delta T(E) \cdot \sigma^*,\]

where \(T^* = T(E_0)\) is the Birkhoff mixing matrix at the low-energy fixed point, and \(\delta T(E) = T(E) - T^*\) is the energy-dependent deviation. In the eigenbasis of \(T\), the components of \(\delta T\) decompose along eigenvector directions:

\[\delta T(E) = \sum_i \delta\theta_i(E) \cdot v_i \otimes v_i^\dagger.\]

The solution of the running equation is:

\[\Delta\sigma_i(E) = \Delta\sigma_i(E_0) \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{\lambda_i - 1} = \Delta\sigma_i(E_0) \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{-\beta_i},\]

where \(\beta_i = 1 - \lambda_i\). For the principal eigenvalue \(\lambda_1 = 1\), \(\beta_1 = 0\)—no running. For the non-principal eigenvalues \(\lambda_{2,3} = 0.678 \pm 0.181i\):

\[\beta_{2,3} = 1 - \text{Re}(\lambda_{2,3}) = 1 - 0.678 = 0.322.\]

The running exponents \(\beta_{2,3} = 0.322\) are positive real numbers—non-principal modes decay with increasing energy. This means that at high energies the three-field spectrum tends toward uniformity (\(\sigma_i \to 1/3\))—the geometric manifestation of grand unification.

1.5 Boundary Conditions of the Running Equation

The running equation has two boundary conditions:

Low-energy boundary (\(E = E_0\)). At low energies, the fixed point constraint is fully locked:

\[\theta_k(E_0) = \theta_k^*, \quad \sigma(E_0) = \sigma^*.\]

This provides the initial condition for running.

High-energy boundary (\(E \to \infty\)). At extremely high energies, the fixed point constraint is fully released, and the three-field spectrum tends toward uniformity:

\[\sigma_i(E \to \infty) \to \frac{1}{3}.\]

The uniform distribution corresponds to \(T \to T_{\text{sym}} = \frac{1}{3}(I + P_\tau + P_{\sigma_2})\)—the fully \(S_3\)-symmetric Birkhoff mixing matrix. This is the “restoration” of triality breaking—triality symmetry is restored at high energies.

The transition region between the two boundary conditions gives the complete running curve. The characteristic energy scale of the transition region is jointly determined by \(E_0\) and the running exponent \(\beta\).

1.6 Energy Range of Running

Running effects become significant at energy scale \(E \sim E_0\). Physical estimate of \(E_0\):

\[E_0 = \frac{\hbar c}{r_M},\]

where \(r_M\) is the characteristic length of the Matter Sector. From \(\chi_M = 8\) and the geometric structure of the Coding Orbit, \(r_M\) corresponds to the characteristic scale of Cl(3)—on the order of 1 fm (nuclear scale). Hence:

\[E_0 \sim \frac{\hbar c}{1\,\text{fm}} \approx 200\,\text{MeV}.\]

This energy scale coincides with the characteristic scale of QCD \(\Lambda_{\text{QCD}} \sim 200\,\text{MeV}\)—the energy scale of constraint release coincides with the energy scale of the strong interaction. This is not a coincidence: both the running of QCD and the constraint release of the three-field coupling originate from the same discrete structure of the Coding Orbit beginning to be “smeared out” at \(E \sim E_0\).

§2 Physical Picture of Release

2.1 High-Temperature Limit

At high temperatures (\(E \gg E_0\)), the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit is “smeared out”—the 7-layer coding is no longer resolvable, and the three-field coupling tends toward continuum. In the continuum limit, the fixed point constraint is partially released, allowing \(\theta_k\) to vary continuously on the doubly stochastic polytope.

The physical picture of the high-temperature limit can be understood as follows: the 7-layer structure of the Coding Orbit is analogous to 7 energy levels; at low energies particles occupy definite levels (discrete spectrum), while at high energies the level spacing is smeared out by thermal fluctuations (continuous spectrum). In the continuous spectrum limit, Birkhoff weights are no longer locked by discrete energy levels—they can move freely on the polytope.

But the double stochasticity constraint is still maintained—coding information conservation is not affected by temperature. Hence in the high-temperature limit, \(\theta_k\) can move freely on the 2-dimensional surface of the Birkhoff polytope, but cannot leave the polytope (double stochasticity constraint).

2.2 Grand Unification

The limit of constraint release is grand unification—all coupling constants tend toward a common value. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the grand unification energy is:

\[E_{\text{GUT}} \sim \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{1 - |\lambda_2|^2}} \approx \frac{E_0}{0.712} \approx 1.40 \, E_0.\]

Grand unification is not all couplings being identical—it is the constraints on all couplings being identical (Birkhoff double stochasticity).

The geometric mechanism of grand unification: at high energies \(E \sim E_{\text{GUT}}\), the three-field spectrum tends toward uniformity \(\sigma_i \to 1/3\). The uniform distribution corresponds to \(T \to T_{\text{sym}}\)—the three permutation orbits have equal weight \(\theta_I = \theta_\tau = \theta_{\sigma_2} = 1/3\). At this point the strength ratios of the three interactions (electromagnetic, weak, gravitational) tend toward:

\[\frac{\alpha_{\text{em}}}{\alpha_W} \to \frac{\chi_M}{\chi_C} = \frac{8}{9}, \quad \frac{\alpha_{\text{em}}}{\alpha_G} \to \frac{\chi_M}{\chi_I} = \frac{8}{1} = 8.\]

Grand unification is not all couplings identical, but all couplings having identical Birkhoff weights—differences arising only from cumulative stretch \(\chi\). This grand unification picture differs from that of the Standard Model: the Standard Model’s grand unification requires all gauge couplings to be identical, whereas Conjugate Spectral Geometry’s grand unification requires only identical Birkhoff weights.

2.3 Low-Temperature Limit

At low temperatures (\(E \ll E_0\)), the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit is fully resolved, and the fixed point constraint is completely locked. The low-temperature limit gives the “frozen” values of coupling constants—the values measured in everyday experiments (\(\alpha^{-1} \approx 137.036\), \(\sin^2\theta_W \approx 0.231\), etc.).

In the low-temperature limit, Constraint Release vanishes—all three layers of constraints are in effect. The three-field spectrum is fully locked at the fixed point \(\sigma^* \approx (0.777912, 0.210603, 0.011484)\), and physical constants take their rigid steady-state values.

The transition from the low-temperature limit to the high-temperature limit is the physical process of constraint release—about \(E \sim E_0 \sim 200\,\text{MeV}\), corresponding to the QCD phase transition in cosmology.

2.4 Geometric Origin of Asymptotic Freedom

Asymptotic freedom—the strong coupling constant decreases with increasing energy—has a clear geometric origin in Conjugate Spectral Geometry: it is the specific manifestation of Constraint Release in the strong interaction channel.

The strong interaction originates from the internal algebraic structure of the Causal Sector (Cl(5) complex structure \(J = \omega_5\)), not directly from the Birkhoff weights. But in the high-energy limit, the constraint release of the Causal Sector causes the effective strength of the strong interaction to decrease with energy:

\[\alpha_s(E) = \alpha_s(E_0) \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{-\beta_{\text{strong}}}, \quad \beta_{\text{strong}} > 0.\]

The negative exponent \(\beta_{\text{strong}} > 0\) means \(\alpha_s\) decreases with increasing \(E\)—asymptotic freedom. The value of \(\beta_{\text{strong}}\) is determined by the multi-body algebraic structure of Cl(5).

2.5 Unified Form of Running Coupling Constants

All running coupling constants can be written in a unified form:

\[\alpha_i(E) = \alpha_i(E_0) \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{\beta_i} \cdot f_i(E/E_0),\]

where \(f_i(E/E_0)\) is the threshold function—\(f_i \approx 1\) when \(E \ll E_0\) and \(E \gg E_0\), with transition behavior near \(E \sim E_0\).

The unified form reveals the common origin of running: all couplings run because the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit is smeared out at high energies. The differences in running exponents \(\beta_i\) reflect the different channels by which different interactions couple to the Birkhoff mixing matrix.

3.4 Interlock Constraints on Running

The running of different couplings is not independent—they must satisfy interlock constraints (Theorem 6.2.1.01). The interlock constraint on running:

\[\frac{\alpha_i(E)}{\alpha_j(E)} = \frac{\alpha_i(E_0)}{\alpha_j(E_0)} \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{\beta_i - \beta_j}.\]

Specifically:

\[\frac{\alpha_W(E)}{\alpha_{\text{em}}(E)} = \frac{0.209 \times 9}{0.782 \times 8} \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{0.322} = 0.301 \cdot \left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^{0.322}.\]

The interlock constraint on running is a testable prediction—if experiment finds that the running of different couplings violates the interlock relation, the three-field coupling theory is falsified.

§4 Phase Transition between Release and Locking

4.1 Energy Scale of the Phase Transition

Constraint Release is a phase transition—from locked phase to released phase. The characteristic energy scale of the phase transition is \(E_c \sim E_0 \sim 200\,\text{MeV}\).

At \(E < E_c\): locked phase—all three layers of constraints in effect, three-field spectra locked at fixed point. At \(E > E_c\): released phase—third-layer constraint released, Birkhoff weights running with energy.

The phase transition is not sharp but a smooth crossover—the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit is gradually smeared out over the energy range \(E \sim (0.5-2)E_0\).

4.2 Continuity of the Phase Transition

Constraint Release is a continuous phase transition—no latent heat, no discontinuous jump in an order parameter. The “order parameter” is the Jacobian spectral radius \(\rho(E)\):

\[\rho(E) = \begin{cases} 0.124 & (E \ll E_0) \\ \text{gradually decreasing} & (E \sim E_0) \\ \to 0 & (E \gg E_0) \end{cases}.\]

The continuity of the phase transition arises because the discrete structure of the Coding Orbit is “smeared out” gradually, not abruptly destroyed.

4.3 Irreversibility of the Phase Transition

Constraint Release is irreversible—once released, the system cannot spontaneously re-lock. The irreversibility is rooted in the thermodynamic arrow of time: going from discrete (low entropy) to continuous (high entropy) is spontaneous, while the reverse process is exponentially suppressed.

The irreversibility of constraint release has cosmological implications: the early universe was in a released phase (high temperature), and as the universe expanded and cooled to \(T \sim T_c \sim 200\,\text{MeV}\), constraint locking occurred—the three-field spectra “froze” into the fixed point. This locking process is irreversible—the universe cannot spontaneously return to the released phase.
