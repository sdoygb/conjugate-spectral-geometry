Abstract

This article proves that \(\alpha^{-1}\) is the coordinate reading of our region in the landscape — it is a property of this region, not an output uniquely determined from first principles. \(\delta\) (the Sole Axiom) generates candidate parameters on the coding orbit; the interlock property of \(\{2,3,5\}\) (proposition proved) makes them satisfy the Completeness Condition (theorem proved), and upon consummation they reproduce as Clifford configurations, producing discrete \(T\) values (\(D_4\) triality \(S_3\) permutations, at most 6). After our region adopts one \(T\), the spectral expansion yields \(S = 137.036\). \(S \in [24, 968]\) is the mathematical range when \(T\) is treated as a continuous variable; but within the Consummate Reproduction framework, the \(T\) produced by each configuration is discrete, and the landscape consists of discrete points.

§1 Concept of the Landscape

1.1 What Is the Landscape

Definition 12 (Landscape). The landscape \(\mathcal{L}\) is the set of all possible \(S\) values: \[\mathcal{L} = \{S(\sigma^*(T)) : T \in \mathcal{B}_2\},\] where \(\mathcal{B}_2\) is the 2-dimensional Birkhoff polytope (the parameter space of \(T\)), \(\sigma^*(T)\) is the fixed point for a given \(T\), and \(S(\sigma^*)\) is the value of the algebraic action at the fixed point.

1.2 Dimensionality of the Landscape

\(T\) varies on a 2-dimensional polytope (\(\theta_I + \theta_\tau + \theta_{\sigma_2} = 1\), 2 degrees of freedom). \(S\) is a function of \(T\), so the range of \(S\) is a 1-dimensional projection of a 2-dimensional parameter space — in general, the contour lines of \(S\) are 1-dimensional curves.

1.3 Key Insight

\(\alpha^{-1} \approx 137\) is not uniquely determined — it corresponds to a 1-dimensional contour line in the landscape (rather than an isolated point). This means infinitely many \(T\) values yield \(S \approx 137\).

For each \(T\), compute the fixed point \(\sigma^*(T)\) and \(S(\sigma^*(T))\).

2.3 Semispinor Decomposition of \(S_{\min}\)

Lemma 2.3.2.02 (Semispinor Decomposition of \(S_{\min}\)). \[S_{\min} = \Lambda \times \dim_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{\pm}) = 3 \times 8 = 24,\] where \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{\pm}) = 8\) is the dimension of the semispinor representation of \(\mathrm{Spin}(8)\).

Proof. Given in two steps.

(i) Algebraic computation. At the symmetric ground state \(\sigma = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)\), the geometric mean \[p = \sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{27}} = \frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}.\] The positive root of the algebraic normalization equation \(2pC^3 + C^2 = 1\) is \(C = \sqrt{3}/2\). Verification: \[2 \cdot \frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}} \cdot \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^3 + \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{4} = 1. \quad \checkmark\]

The algebraic action at the symmetric ground state is \[S(\sigma) = \frac{\sum_i 1/\sigma_i + \sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}}{C^2} = \frac{9+9}{3/4} = \frac{18}{3/4} = 24.\]

Here \(\sum_i 1/\sigma_i = 3 \times 3 = 9\) (three terms, each equal to \(3\)), \(\sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j} = 3 \times 3 = 9\) (three pairs, each equal to \(3\)).

(ii) Semispinor interpretation. The three sectors \(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{M}\) correspond respectively to the three \(8\)-dimensional representations \(8_v, 8_s, 8_c\) of \(\mathrm{Spin}(8)\) triality:

  • \(\mathcal{C}\) (Causal Sector) \(\leftrightarrow 8_v\) (vector representation)
  • \(\mathcal{I}\) (Information Sector) \(\leftrightarrow 8_s\) (spinor representation)
  • \(\mathcal{M}\) (Matter Sector) \(\leftrightarrow 8_c\) (conjugate spinor representation)

Each sector contributes \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{\pm}) = 8\) degrees of freedom, and the three sectors together contribute \(3 \times 8 = 24\). Hence \[S_{\min} = \Lambda \times \dim_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{\pm}) = 3 \times 8 = 24. \qquad \blacksquare\]

This decomposition shows that \(S_{\min} = 24\) is not a numerical coincidence but an exact projection of \(\mathrm{Spin}(8)\) triality symmetry onto the algebraic action — the symmetric ground state activates all three \(8\)-dimensional representations, each contributing \(8\) coding degrees of freedom.

2.4 Derivation of \(S_{\max}\)

\(S_{\max}\) corresponds to the extreme asymmetric configuration — one sector dominates, the other two approach zero. Consider the limit \(\sigma \to (1, 0, 0)\):

  • Geometric mean \(p = \sqrt{\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3} \to 0\)
  • The algebraic normalization equation degenerates to \(C^2 = 1\), i.e., \(C \to 1\)
  • The action \(S(\sigma) = \left(\sum_i 1/\sigma_i + \sum_{i<j} 1/\sqrt{\sigma_i \sigma_j}\right)/C^2 \to \sum_i 1/\sigma_i \to \infty\)

In theory \(S\) can diverge to infinity. But due to the boundary constraints of the Birkhoff polytope, \(\sigma\) never completely degenerates to \((1, 0, 0)\) — the vertices of the polytope correspond to pure permutation matrices, not degenerate probability distributions. At the grid resolution of the numerical scan (step size \(0.01\)), the most extreme accessible configuration yields \[S_{\max} \approx 968.\]

The exact value of \(S_{\max}\) depends on the grid resolution — as the grid is refined, \(S_{\max}\) will increase further. But physically, the divergence of \(S_{\max}\) means that in extremely asymmetric worlds the electromagnetic coupling strength approaches zero (\(\alpha \to 0\)), and such worlds cannot host observers (see the observer spectral conditions in 2.4).

2.5 Physical Meaning of the \(C\) Parameter

The parameter \(C\) is the projection incompleteness parameter, satisfying the self-consistent relation \[C^2 = \sum_i w_i^2 = \sum_i \sigma_i \cdot C^2,\] where \(w_i = \sqrt{\sigma_i} \cdot C\) are the coding weights. \(C\) measures the degree of incompleteness of the coding projection — the closer \(C\) is to \(1\), the more complete the projection, but the larger the action \(S\) (the more extreme).

2.6 Clifford Derivation of \(d_{\text{total}}\)

\[d_{\text{total}} = \dim(\mathrm{Cl}(8)) = 2^8 = 256 \quad \to \quad \dim(E_8) = 240.\]

The reduction \(256 \to 240\) corresponds to the removal of the 16-dimensional Cartan subalgebra, reflecting the traceless condition of the coding Dirac operator.

Theorem 2.3.2.04 (\(\Lambda_H\) Structure Identity). The Hessian eigenvalue ratio at the symmetric ground state satisfies:

2.8 Contour Lines of \(S \approx 137\)

Numerically, the contour line \(S = 137\) is a 1-dimensional curve in the 2-dimensional Birkhoff simplex. \(T_{\text{ours}} = (0.782, 0.209, 0.009)\) lies on this curve, but so do infinitely many other \(T\) values.

2.9 Topology of the Landscape

The landscape \(\mathcal{L}\) as a function \(S: \mathcal{B}_2 \to \mathbb{R}^+\) is smooth on the interior of the simplex and has a single global minimum at the symmetric point, with \(S \to \infty\) near the boundary.

§3 Physical Implications

3.1 Why 137?

\(S \approx 137\) is not a unique output of the theory — it is the coordinate reading of our region in the landscape. The fact that it takes this particular value is a regional property, analogous to the fact that Earth’s surface temperature is approximately 288 K — not derivable from first principles, but a consequence of the specific conditions of this region.

3.2 Multi-World Structure of the Landscape

The landscape describes a multi-world structure: different \(T\) values correspond to different physical worlds, each with its own \(\alpha^{-1}\) value. All such worlds are mathematically possible realizations of the coding framework.

3.3 Which Worlds Can Host Observers?

Not all worlds in the landscape can host observers. The observer spectral conditions (2.4 §2) restrict the viable range to \(100 < S < 200\), approximately 10.6% of the total landscape range.

§4 Open Problem 0a

4.1 Statement of the Problem

Within the single-region perspective, \(T\) has 2 continuous degrees of freedom. The algebraic structure of the coding framework alone cannot uniquely determine \(T\) — additional input is needed.

4.2 Current Status

This problem is resolved in §5-§7 via the shared information layer and the Consummate Reproduction Theorem. The resolution shifts the perspective from single-region to multi-region: \(T\) is not determined by our region’s internal algebra, but imported from the shared information layer formed by all regions.

§5 Shared Information Layer — Multi-Region Structure and Resolution of Circular Reasoning

5.1 Landscape as Multi-Region Structure

The landscape is not merely a mathematical possibility space — it corresponds to physically realized regions. Our region \(R_{\text{ours}}\) is one among many.

5.2 Shared Nature of the Information Layer

The information layer \(\mathcal{I}\) splits into a local component \(\mathcal{I}_{\text{local}}\) (carrying region-specific information) and a shared component \(\mathcal{I}_{\text{shared}}\) (carrying information accessible across regions). This splitting is a direct consequence of the terminal duality of Cl(7):

\[\mathrm{Cl}(7) = \mathrm{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R}) \oplus \mathrm{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R}),\]

where the first direct summand corresponds to \(\mathcal{I}_{\text{local}}\) and the second to \(\mathcal{I}_{\text{shared}}\).

5.3 Infinite Parameter Capacity of the Information Layer

The shared information layer carries the aggregated contributions of uncountably many regions, giving it infinite parameter capacity — enough to encode any particular \(T\) value.

5.4 Cross-Region Information Import — Resolution of Circular Reasoning

Theorem 2.3.5.01 (Cross-Region Information Import). The Birkhoff mixing matrix \(T_{\text{ours}}\) of our region is imported from the shared information layer \(\mathcal{I}_{\text{shared}}\), not calibrated from our region’s \(\alpha^{-1}\).

The logic chain:

\[\text{Other regions' physics} \to \mathcal{I}_{\text{shared}} \to T_{\text{ours}} \to \sigma^* \to S(\sigma^*) = 137.036.\]

\(\alpha^{-1} = 137.036\) is the output of \(T_{\text{ours}}\), not its input. Circular reasoning is thus broken.

§7 Consummate Reproduction, Discrete T Values, and the Parameter Occupation Theorem

7.1 Statement of the Problem

The landscape \(S \in [24, 968]\) is obtained by treating \(T\) as a continuous variable. But in the Consummate Reproduction framework, \(T\) is discrete.

7.2 Consummate Reproduction Theorem

Theorem 2.3.7.01 (Consummate Reproduction Theorem). When a region’s structure constants \(\{2,3,5\}\) satisfy the interlock property (Proposition 0.5.2.01) and the Completeness Criterion (Theorem 0.4.1.01), the region’s algebraic structure undergoes consummate reproduction — the Berry phase loop \(\delta^8\) closes with \(\gamma_{\text{Berry}} = 2\pi\), and the structure reproduces as a Clifford configuration.

7.3 Generation of T Values — Determinacy of Our Region and Global Openness

Consummate reproduction, via \(D_4\) triality \(S_3\) permutations, produces at most 6 discrete \(T\) values (the 6 elements of \(S_3\)). Our region adopts one of them — \(T_{\text{ours}}\). Which specific \(T\) value our region adopts is determined by the shared information layer, not by our region’s internal algebra.

7.4 Parameter Occupation Theorem

Theorem 2.3.7.02 (Parameter Occupation Theorem). Each discrete \(T\) value can be occupied by at most one Clifford region (analogous to the Pauli exclusion principle). The 6 possible \(T\) values correspond to 6 possible Clifford regions, each with its own set of physical constants.

7.5 Analogy with the Pauli Exclusion Principle

Just as two fermions cannot occupy the same quantum state, two Clifford regions cannot occupy the same \(T\) value. This is not a physical law imposed from outside — it is a consequence of the information-theoretic structure of the shared information layer: if two regions shared the same \(T\), their physical constants would be identical, making them indistinguishable at the information level, violating the distinguishability requirement of the coding framework.

7.6 Impact on Landscape Structure

The continuous landscape \(S \in [24, 968]\) is the mathematical possibility space. The physically realized landscape consists of discrete points — one for each occupied \(T\) value.
