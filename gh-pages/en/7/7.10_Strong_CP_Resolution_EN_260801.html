Abstract

The standard formulation of the strong CP problem is: the \(\theta\)-term \(\frac{\theta}{32\pi^2}G\tilde{G}\) in the QCD Lagrangian violates CP symmetry, yet the experimental bound \(\bar{\theta}<10^{-10}\) is far smaller than the natural expectation \(\bar{\theta}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)\). The standard solution (Peccei-Quinn mechanism) introduces the axion, but the axion has not been discovered to date.

This paper provides a geometric resolution of the strong CP problem: \(\bar{\theta}=0\) is algebraically locked by the determinant reality of Birkhoff double stochasticity. This locking is algebraic, rigid, and non-dynamical — requiring no axion, no Peccei-Quinn symmetry, and no anthropic explanation.

§1 Problem Statement

1.1 Standard Formulation of the Strong CP Problem

The QCD Lagrangian admits a \(\theta\)-term:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{QCD}} \supset \frac{\theta}{32\pi^2}G_{\mu\nu}^a \tilde{G}^{a,\mu\nu}.\]

This term violates CP symmetry (\(P\) and \(T\) broken, \(C\) conserved), generating a neutron electric dipole moment:

\[d_n \approx \bar{\theta}\times 2.4\times 10^{-16}\text{ e·cm}.\]

The experimental bound \(d_n<1.8\times 10^{-26}\) e·cm yields:

\[\bar{\theta}<7.5\times 10^{-11}.\]

Yet the natural expectation for \(\bar{\theta}\) is \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) — this is the core of the strong CP problem.

1.2 Definition of the Strong CP Parameter

\[\bar{\theta} = \theta_{\text{QCD}} + \arg\det(M_u M_d).\]

Here \(\theta_{\text{QCD}}\) is the QCD vacuum angle, and \(M_u, M_d\) are the up/down-type quark mass matrices. \(\arg\det(M_u M_d)\) is the phase of the quark mass matrix determinant — contributed by weak interaction CP violation.

1.3 Limitations of the Standard Solution

The Peccei-Quinn mechanism introduces a global \(U(1)_{\text{PQ}}\) symmetry, dynamically relaxing \(\bar{\theta}\) to zero via the axion field \(a(x)\):

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{PQ}} \supset \frac{a}{f_a}\frac{1}{32\pi^2}G\tilde{G}.\]

But the axion has not been discovered — all axion search experiments (ADMX, CAST, ABRACADABRA, etc.) yield null results. Conjugate Spectral Geometry offers a more concise solution: \(\bar{\theta}=0\) is locked by algebraic constraints, requiring no new particles or new symmetries.

§2 Algebraic Mechanism for \(\bar{\theta}=0\)

2.1 Deep Roots of Determinant Reality

The determinant reality of the Birkhoff mixing matrix \(T\) is not merely a trivial consequence of \(T\) being a real matrix, but has deeper algebraic roots. \(T\) is the Birkhoff representation after triality symmetry \(S_3 \to Z_2\) breaking — the residual symmetry \(Z_3=\{I, (1\,3\,2), (1\,2\,3)\}\) after breaking guarantees that \(T\) is invariant under the \(\sigma_2\) operation:

\[\sigma_2 \cdot T \cdot \sigma_2^{-1} = T.\]

This invariance means that the eigenvalues of \(T\) appear in pairs under the \(\sigma_2\) operation — real eigenvalues or complex conjugate eigenvalue pairs. But \(T\) is a real matrix, so complex eigenvalues must appear in conjugate pairs — the determinant (product of eigenvalues) is therefore real.

Relation between \(Z_2\) symmetry and determinant reality:

The transposition \(\sigma_2\) of \(Z_2=\{I, \sigma_2\}\) acts on the Birkhoff mixing matrix as:

\[\sigma_2: T \mapsto P_{\sigma_2} T P_{\sigma_2}^{-1}.\]

The \(Z_2\) invariance \(P_{\sigma_2}TP_{\sigma_2}^{-1}=T\) of \(T\) implies that \(T\) commutes with \(P_{\sigma_2}\). Commuting real matrices can be simultaneously diagonalized — the eigenvalues of \(T\) decompose on the eigenspaces of \(P_{\sigma_2}\). The eigenvalues of \(P_{\sigma_2}\) are \(\pm 1\) (transposition matrix), with corresponding eigenspaces of dimensions 1 and 2. The restriction of \(T\) to the 2-dimensional eigenspace is a \(2\times 2\) real matrix — its eigenvalues are either real or complex conjugate pairs. In either case, the determinant (product of eigenvalues) is real.

2.2 Topological Locking of \(\theta_{\text{QCD}}=0\)

But the topological structure of \(SU(3)\) (\(\pi_3(SU(3))=\mathbb{Z}\)) comes from its global structure as a 3-dimensional compact Lie group. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the global topology of \(SU(3)\) is determined by the representation of \(Z_3\) after triality breaking (0 or 1), not an arbitrary element of \(\mathbb{Z}\). The representation of \(Z_2\) has only two values (0 and 1), but the physical value of \(\theta_{\text{QCD}}\) must be continuous (\([0, 2\pi)\)) — \(Z_2\) symmetry locks \(\theta_{\text{QCD}}\) to 0 or \(\pi\). 2.3 Coexistence of the CKM Phase and \(\bar{\theta}=0\)

  • CKM phase is a relative phase — the phase difference between \(U_u\) and \(U_d\) in \(V_{\text{CKM}}=U_u^\dagger U_d\)
  • \(\bar{\theta}\) depends on absolute phases — \(\arg\det(M_u M_d)=\arg\det(U_u D_u U_u^\dagger\cdot U_d D_d U_d^\dagger)\), where \(D_u, D_d\) are the diagonalized real masses

The reality of the Birkhoff determinant locks the sum of absolute phases of \(U_u\) and \(U_d\) to zero — \(\arg\det(U_u)+\arg\det(U_d)=0\). But the relative phase of \(U_u\) and \(U_d\) (the CKM phase) is not constrained by this — \(\delta_{\text{CP}}=\arg\det(U_u^\dagger U_d)\) can be nonzero.

Thus \(\bar{\theta}=0\) and \(\delta_{\text{CP}}\neq 0\) can coexist — the former is locked by absolute phases, the latter is determined by relative phases.

Mathematical relation between CKM phase and \(\bar{\theta}\):

\[\bar{\theta} = \theta_{\text{QCD}} + \arg\det(M_u) + \arg\det(M_d),\]

\[\delta_{\text{CP}} = \arg\det(U_u^\dagger U_d).\]

\(\arg\det(M_u)=\arg\det(U_u D_u U_u^\dagger)=\arg\det(D_u)=0\) (\(D_u\) is a real diagonal matrix), and similarly \(\arg\det(M_d)=0\). Hence \(\bar{\theta}=\theta_{\text{QCD}}=0\).
