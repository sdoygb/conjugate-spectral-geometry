7.16 Geometric Framework for the Quark Mass Spectrum

Number: 7.16 | Date: 260801.1 (English translation) | Series: Standard Model Reconstruction | Language: EN

Abstract

This article establishes a geometric framework for the quark mass spectrum. The quark sector differs fundamentally from the lepton sector — confinement implies that quarks are not asymptotic states, color constraints render the \(C_n\) hierarchy formula inapplicable, and there is no framework-endogenous anchor point analogous to the electron mass. The current framework is divided into four tiers: (I) Structure Tier — the constraint structure of three-generation coding weight configurations ({2,3,5} Interlock Property, Birkhoff Mixing Matrix Spectral Rigidity, Color Singlet), theorem-level; (II) Scaling Tier — the mass operator M̂ (3.1 §5) directly applicable to \(u, d, s\), theorem-level but with \(\sigma_M\) obtained by back-inference from external mass input; (III) Anchoring Tier — the geometric origin of first-generation quark \(\sigma_M\), open; (IV) Splitting Tier — the \(\sigma_M\) differences between up-type and down-type quarks and the \(\eta_{\text{QCD}}\) correction factor for heavy quarks, partially directional. Each tier’s theorem propositions, external input status are stated.

Honest Statement: The quark mass spectrum has not yet achieved theorem-level closure comparable to the lepton mass spectrum. The current framework provides the correct constraint structure (theorem-level), but numerical predictions still rely on external PDG mass input as anchor points. Once the Anchoring Tier is closed, the quark mass spectrum will become a pure prediction of the framework.

1 Introduction: The Challenge of the Quark Mass Spectrum

1.1 Success of the Lepton Sector

3.10 has already given a theorem-level unified description of the charged lepton \((e, \mu, \tau)\) mass spectrum, and No. 7 has given a theorem-level description of the neutrino mass spectrum and PMNS mixing angles. The success of the lepton sector relies on one crucial framework-endogenous anchor point — the electron mass \(m_e = 510.999\ \text{keV}\), jointly determined by \(S_e = 137.036\) and \(K = 839.759\ \text{keV}\), requiring no external experimental input.

From this anchor point, the {2,3,5} Interlock Property and Birkhoff Mixing Matrix Spectral Rigidity (Theorem 1.6.1.01) jointly constrain the \(\sigma_M\) of \(\mu\) and \(\tau\), which in turn yield mass predictions via the mass operator M̂.

2 Prerequisite Framework

2.1 Sole Axiom δ and the ℳ𝒞ℐ Three-Sector

All derivations in this article are locked to the Sole Axiom δ (Zero Motion). Starting from δ, via Clifford algebra construction and Bott periodicity \(\delta^8 = 2\pi\), the algebraic structure framework with Seven-Layer Truncation is established. From the Semisimple Splitting \(\text{Cl}(3) \cong \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}\) (Theorem 1.1.3.01), the parameter space decomposes into the ℳ𝒞ℐ three-sector.

Coding Weight Sum Constraint (derived from Sole Axiom δ, Born Normalization Theorem 1.4.3.01):

\[\sigma_M + \sigma_C + \sigma_I = 90^\circ \tag{2.1}\]

The sum of three-sector coding weights is a right angle — this is the angular formulation of Born normalization on the product sphere class. Verification: for the \(u\) quark, \(7.88^\circ + 9.62^\circ + 72.50^\circ = 90.00^\circ\); the same holds exactly for \(c, t\).

Algebraic Action \(S(\sigma)\) (Theorem 1.6.4.01):

\[S(\sigma_M, \sigma_C, \sigma_I) = \sum_{i \in \{M,C,I\}} \frac{1}{\sin^2\sigma_i} + \sum_{i<j} \frac{1}{\sin\sigma_i \sin\sigma_j} \tag{2.2}\]

ℳ𝒞ℐ Three-Sector Structure (Theorem 1.1.3.01, Cl(3) Semisimple Splitting):

\[\text{Parameter Space} = \mathcal{M} \oplus \mathcal{C} \oplus \mathcal{I}, \quad \sigma_M + \sigma_C + \sigma_I = 90^\circ \tag{2.3}\]

\[\hat{M} \sim K \sin^3\sigma_M \tag{2.4}\]

where \(K = 839.758793\ \text{keV}\) is the universal mass quantum (Consummate Reproduction, theorem proposition). This operator has been verified in the lepton sector (\(e, \mu, \tau\) and \(\nu_1, \nu_2, \nu_3\)) and is universal for the Matter Sector.

Possible geometric origins of \(\kappa_c = 10^3\) include: the dimension of color degrees of freedom (the 8 generators of SU(3) and \(10^3 = (2\times 5)^3\) in the {2,3,5} interlock structure), the bridging of confinement scale and electroweak scale, or the volume factor of the joint sector parameter space relative to the single-particle sector parameter space. The rigorous geometric derivation of \(\kappa_c\) is an open problem.

Birkhoff Mixing Matrix Spectral Rigidity (Theorem 1.6.1.01) asserts that the range of the Algebraic Action \(S\) on the product sphere class has a rigid discrete structure. For fermion families, the allowed \(S\) values correspond to a discrete spectrum \(\{S_n\}\), jointly determined by the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix eigenvalues and the Upper Saturation Steady State \(\sigma_I^{\text{sat}} \approx 72.53^\circ\).

3.1 System of Constraint Equations

The coding weight configurations of three-generation quarks are determined by the following joint constraints:

  1. Coding Weight Sum Constraint (1 per generation): \(\sigma_M^{(i)} + \sigma_C^{(i)} + \sigma_I^{(i)} = 90^\circ\) (3 equations)
  2. {2,3,5} Interlock Constraints (2 independent inter-generational equations): The \(\sigma_C\) and \(\sigma_I\) across three generations are related via the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix effective metric:

\[\frac{S^{(i)}}{S^{(j)}} = \frac{\sin^2\sigma_I^{(i)}}{\sin^2\sigma_I^{(j)}} \cdot \left(\frac{\lambda_2^{\text{eff}} \sin^2\sigma_M^{(i)}\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \sin^2\sigma_C^{(i)}}{\lambda_2^{\text{eff}} \sin^2\sigma_M^{(j)}\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \sin^2\sigma_C^{(j)}}\right)^{1/2} \tag{3.1}\]

  3. Upper Saturation Steady State Priority: The \(\sigma_I\) of the first generation preferentially approaches \(\sigma_I^{\text{sat}} \approx 72.53^\circ\).

In total: 9 unknowns (nine coding weights), 9 constraints (3 Coding Weight Sum Constraints + 2 Interlock + 2 inter-generational \(S\) ratios + 2 Spectral Rigidity), making the system mathematically closed.

5 Anchoring Tier: Origin of First-Generation \(\sigma_M\)

5.1 Endogenous Anchoring in the Lepton Sector

The lepton sector possesses a framework-endogenous anchoring chain:

\[S_e = 137.036 \xrightarrow{\text{Consummate Reproduction}} \sigma_M^{(e)} = 57.93^\circ \xrightarrow{\hat{M}} m_e = 510.999\ \text{keV}\]

\(S_e\) is directly locked by the extremum of the Algebraic Action \(S(\sigma)\) and the Information Sector Dirac Spectral Equation, determined by local region properties. \(m_e\) is a pure output of the framework.

5.2 Absence of a Corresponding Anchor in the Quark Sector

The quark sector has no endogenous anchoring chain analogous to \(S_e \to \sigma_M\). Possible reasons:

  • Confinement: Quarks are not asymptotic states; their \(S\) values must be defined on the joint sector parameter space rather than the single-particle sector parameter space.
  • Color: SU(3) color degrees of freedom mean that a single quark’s \(S\) is not independently observable.
  • Mass scale separation: There exists a gap, bridged by the confinement scale, between quark masses (\(\sim \text{MeV}\)) and hadron masses (\(\sim \text{GeV}\)).

5.3 Current Scheme: External Mass Input

Current anchoring scheme: the \(\sigma_M\) of the \(u\) and \(d\) quarks are obtained by back-inference from PDG running masses via the mass operator M̂ (Table 4.1). This is regarded as external input — not a framework-endogenous prediction.

7.1 Problem: \(m \gg \kappa_c K\)

For \(c, b, t\) quarks, \(m \gg \kappa_c K = 839.759\ \text{MeV}\) (\(K = 839.759\) keV, \(\kappa_c = 10^3\)). For example, \(m_t \approx 172.6\ \text{GeV}\), \(m_t/(\kappa_c K) \approx 206\), which cannot be covered by \(\sin^3\sigma_M \leq 1\) alone. The mass operator requires the \(\eta_{\text{QCD}}\) correction factor.

where \(\eta_{\text{QCD}}^{(q)} \geq 1\), \(\eta_{\text{QCD}} \approx 1\) for light quarks, and \(\eta_{\text{QCD}} > 1\) for heavy quarks.
