Abstract

This article defines the normal geometry structure of the Matter Sector ℳ. The ℳ sector corresponds to the Cl(0)→Cl(3) segment of the Coding Orbit, whose algebraic foundation is the Semisimple Splitting Cl(3) ≅ ℍ ⊕ ℍ. The normal geometry of the Matter Sector is described by the \(S^3(r_M)\) sphere, with radius \(r_M\) locked by the Interlock Constants. The two direct summands of the Semisimple Splitting correspond to left-handed and right-handed helicity of matter. This article further provides the geometric realization of Cl(3) ≅ ℍ ⊕ ℍ, the detailed construction of the normal projection operator, and the algebraic definition of the Mass Operator \(\hat{M}\), laying the foundation for mass generation dynamics.

§1 Algebraic Foundation of the Matter Sector

1.1 Semisimple Splitting of Cl(3)

The algebraic foundation of the Matter Sector is Cl(3) ≅ ℍ ⊕ ℍ (Theorem 7.8.6.03). The volume element \(\omega_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3\) satisfies \(\omega_3^2 =1\), producing the projections:

\[P_+ = \frac{1+\omega_3}{2}, \quad P_- = \frac{1 - \omega_3}{2}.\]

The two projections divide Cl(3) into two independent quaternion subalgebras:

\[\text{Cl}(3) = P_+ \text{Cl}(3) \oplus P_- \text{Cl}(3) \cong \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}.\]

1.2 Geometric Realization of the Semisimple Splitting

The Semisimple Splitting is not only an algebraic fact but also a geometric realization. Selecting the generators \(e_1, e_2, e_3\) of Cl(3) as three mutually orthogonal normal vector fields on \(S^3(r_M)\), the volume element \(\omega_3 = e_1 e_2 e_3\) gives a normal 3-form at each point of \(S^3\). Since the normal bundle of \(S^3\) in \(\mathbb{R}^4\) is one-dimensional, \(\omega_3\) actually defines a global chiral structure on \(S^3\).

Concretely, denote the coordinates of \(\mathbb{R}^4\) as \((x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4)\), and define two Complex Structures:

\[J_+ : (x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) \mapsto (-x_2, x_1, -x_4, x_3),\] \[J_- : (x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) \mapsto (-x_2, x_1, x_4, -x_3).\]

One can verify that \(J_+^2 = J_-^2 = -\text{id}\), and \(J_+ J_- = -J_- J_+\). These two Complex Structures commute with \(P_+, P_-\) respectively, thereby realizing \(S^3\) as the geometric branches corresponding to the two quaternion subalgebras.

The geometric significance of the Semisimple Splitting is that \(S^3(r_M)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^4\) admits two inequivalent Complex Structures, which correspond respectively to the geometric carriers of right-handed and left-handed Weyl Spinors. This is consistent with the real structure \(Cl_{3,0} \cong \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}\) of Cl(3) in the Bott Periodicity table.

1.3 Helicity Splitting

The two direct summands of the Semisimple Splitting correspond to the two helicities of matter:

Direct Summand Projection Helicity Physical Correspondence
P₊ Cl(3) P₊ Right-handed Right-handed particles
P₋ Cl(3) P₋ Left-handed Left-handed particles

Theorem 3.1.1.01 (Helicity Splitting Theorem). The left-handed and right-handed helicities of the Matter Sector are uniquely determined by the Semisimple Splitting of Cl(3), without introducing additional parameters.

Proof. \(\omega_3^2 =1\) is a rigid property of the Clifford Algebra——the sign of \(\omega_3\) is not adjustable. The projections \(P_{\pm}\) are algebraic functions of \(\omega_3\), and there exist no continuous parameters to alter the splitting. Hence the helicity splitting is algebraically rigid. ∎

1.4 Irreducible Representations

The irreducible representation dimension of Cl(3) is \(\rho_3 = 4\), but the Semisimple Splitting separates it into two 2-dimensional representations:

\[\rho_3^{(+)} = 2, \quad \rho_3^{(-)} = 2.\]

These two 2-dimensional representations correspond to Weyl Spinors——left-handed and right-handed Weyl Spinors each with 2 components. This is the algebraic basis for the Matter Sector as the “minimal complete spinor carrier”: Cl(3) is the first Clifford Algebra on the Coding Orbit to simultaneously accommodate left-handed and right-handed Weyl Spinors.

§2 Normal Geometry

2.1 S³ Sphere

The normal geometry of the Matter Sector is described by the three-dimensional sphere \(S^3(r_M)\):

\[S^3(r_M) = \{(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) \in \mathbb{R}^4 : x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 + x_4^2 = r_M^2\}.\]

\(S^3\) is a compact, simply connected manifold with constant curvature \(6/r_M^2\). In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, \(S^3\) is not an arbitrary background——it is the “unit sphere” of the Cl(3) irreducible representation space \(\mathbb{R}^4\), uniquely determined by the algebraic structure.

2.2 Construction of the Normal Projection Operator

The normal projection is the core construction of the Matter Sector geometry. Given the tangent bundle \(TS^3\) and the normal bundle \(NS^3\) on \(S^3(r_M)\), the normal projection operator \(\Pi_N\) projects an arbitrary vector field \(V \in \Gamma(T\mathbb{R}^4|_{S^3})\) onto the normal bundle:

\[\Pi_N(V) = \langle V, \hat{n} \rangle \hat{n},\]

where \(\hat{n} = x / r_M\) is the unit outward normal of \(S^3\). The normal bundle \(NS^3\) is one-dimensional in \(\mathbb{R}^4\), spanned by \(\hat{n}\).

Combining the normal projection with Clifford multiplication, we define the Normal Clifford Multiplier:

\[\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}} = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \frac{x_i}{r_M} \gamma_i \in \text{Cl}(4),\]

where \(\gamma_i\) are the standard Dirac matrices on \(\mathbb{R}^4\). \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}\) satisfies \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}^2 =1\), hence it induces the helicity splitting of the spinor bundle on \(S^3\):

\[\Sigma_\pm = \frac{1 \pm \hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}}{2} \cdot \Sigma.\]

This is precisely the geometric realization of \(P_\pm\) in §1.2——the algebraic projections \(P_\pm\) and the geometric projections \(\Sigma_\pm\) are identified via the Normal Clifford Multiplier \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}\).

Proposition 3.1.2.01 (Normal-Algebraic Correspondence). The Normal Clifford Multiplier \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}\) and the volume element \(\omega_3\) are conjugate in the spinor representation:

\[\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}} = U^{-1} \omega_3 U,\]

where \(U\) is the restriction map \(\text{Cl}(4) \to \text{Cl}(3)\). Thus, the geometric normal projection and the algebraic Semisimple Splitting are two descriptions of the same structure.

2.3 Radius Locking

The radius \(r_M\) of the Matter Sector is locked by the Interlock Constants. The radius ratios of the three sectors are:

\[r_M : r_C : r_I = 1 : \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} : \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}.\]

This ratio is jointly determined by the three Structure Constants \(\Lambda = 3\), \(\Delta\Theta = 5\), \(k_0 = 2\)——it is not adjustable.

Theorem 3.1.2.02 (Radius Locking Theorem). The radius ratios of the three sectors \(r_M : r_C : r_I = 1 : 1/\sqrt{3} : 1/\sqrt{6}\) are uniquely determined by the Interlock Constants.

Proof.

  • \(r_M = 1\) (normalization baseline)
  • \(r_C = 1/\sqrt{\Lambda} = 1/\sqrt{3}\) (the Semisimple Splitting of \(\Lambda = 3\) shrinks the Causal Sector radius by a factor of \(\sqrt{3}\))
  • \(r_I = 1/\sqrt{\Lambda k_0} = 1/\sqrt{3 \times 2} = 1/\sqrt{6}\) (the Information Sector radius is jointly determined by \(\Lambda\) and \(k_0\))

The exact values of the ratios are determined by the growth rates of irreducible representation dimensions at each step of the Bott Periodicity table. ∎

Note (Relationship between Radius Ratios and χ): The radius ratios \(r_M:r_C:r_I = 1:1/\sqrt{3}:1/\sqrt{6}\) are determined by the Structure Constants \(\Lambda=3\), \(k_0=2\), and are different geometric quantities from the Cumulative Stretching Factors \(\chi=(8,9,1)\). \(\chi\) describes the “cumulative stretch” of each sector on the Coding Orbit (the product of Bott periodicity step count and irreducible representation dimension), while the radius ratios describe the “compactness” of the normal sphere \(S^3\) of each sector (the step count in the Semisimple Splitting). The two are indirectly related through the fixed point of \(M_5 = T \cdot \text{diag}(\chi)\)——\(\chi\) influences the terminal weight \(\sigma^*\) after Birkhoff mixing, and \(\sigma^*\) is related to the Spectral Gap \(\Delta\lambda \propto 1/r\) via the Born Rule——but there is no simple \(r_i \propto \chi_i\) or \(r_i \propto 1/\chi_i\) relationship.

The Scalar Curvature of \(S^3(r_M)\) is:

2.5 Normal Connection

Although the normal bundle \(NS^3\) is one-dimensional, it still carries a trivial \(U(1)\) connection. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, this normal \(U(1)\) connection corresponds to the electromagnetic phase of the Matter Sector:

\[\nabla^{\text{normal}} = d i A_M,\]

where \(A_M\) is the potential function of the normal connection. Since \(S^3\) is simply connected, \(A_M\) can be taken as zero globally, but it still contributes phase factors in local coordinate systems. The curvature of the normal connection is zero——consistent with the global curvature-free property of the electromagnetic field (no Magnetic Monopoles).

§3 Dirac Operator of the Matter Field

3.1 Definition of the Dirac Operator

The Dirac Operator \(\mathcal{D}_M\) of the Matter Sector acts on spinor fields on \(S^3(r_M)\):

\[\mathcal{D}_M = \sum_{i=1}^{3} e_i \cdot \nabla_{e_i},\]

where \(e_i\) are the generators of Cl(3) and \(\nabla\) is the Levi-Civita connection on \(S^3\). Equivalently, using the normal projection, \(\mathcal{D}_M\) can be written as the tangential restriction of the Dirac Operator \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{R}^4}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) to \(S^3\):

\[\mathcal{D}_M = \Pi_T \circ \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{R}^4} \circ \Pi_T,\]

where \(\Pi_T = 1 - \Pi_N\) is the tangential projection. This form shows that \(\mathcal{D}_M\) is entirely determined by the normal geometry——the normal projection \(\Pi_N\) is the geometric prototype of the Mass Operator.

3.2 Spectrum

The spectrum of the Dirac Operator on \(S^3\) is discrete:

\[\lambda_n = \pm \frac{n+1}{r_M}, \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots\]

The multiplicity of each Eigenvalue is \((n+1)(n+2)\). The ground state (\(n=0\)) has Eigenvalues \(\lambda_0 = \pm 1/r_M = \pm 1\), with multiplicity 2——corresponding to the two Weyl Spinor components.

3.3 Spectral Gap

Theorem 3.1.3.01 (Matter Sector Spectral Gap). The ground state Spectral Gap of the Matter Sector is:

\[\Delta\lambda_M = |\lambda_0^+ - \lambda_0^-| = \frac{2}{r_M} = 2.\]

By the Born Rule (Theorem 1.3.4.01), its relation to the Coding Weight \(\sigma_M\) is:

\[\Delta\lambda_M \propto \sqrt{\sigma_M}.\]

Proof. The spectrum of the Dirac Operator \(\mathcal{D}_M\) on \(S^3(r_M)\) is completely determined by spherical harmonic analysis. The ground state is given by the \(n = 0\) Weyl Spinors, with Eigenvalues \(\pm 1/r_M\). The gap \(2/r_M\) is the minimal “energy difference” of the Cl(3) irreducible representation, reflecting that the Semisimple Splitting separates left-handed and right-handed spinors into two independent energy levels. The non-vanishing of the gap is the geometric prerequisite for matter to possess mass. ∎

3.4 Spectral Symmetry and Helicity

The positive-negative symmetry of the spectrum \(\lambda_n = \pm (n+1)/r_M\) corresponds to helicity reversal: \(\lambda_n^+\) corresponds to right-handed Weyl Spinors, \(\lambda_n^-\) to left-handed Weyl Spinors. When the Matter Sector and Causal Sector are decoupled (\(\theta_\tau \to 0\)), the positive and negative spectra are strictly symmetric, and the mass eigenstates are helicity eigenstates——mass is zero.

When the ℳ-𝒞 coupling is turned on (\(\theta_\tau \neq 0\)), the Triality 3-cycle \(\tau\) mixes left- and right-handed helicities, the positive and negative spectra are no longer symmetric, and the mass eigenstates become linear combinations of left- and right-handed spinors——mass emerges. This mechanism is developed in detail in Sections 3.2 and 3.3.

§5 Algebraic Definition of the Mass Operator

5.1 Construction of the Mass Operator

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, mass is not an external parameter but an operator output of the geometric structure. Define the Mass Operator \(\hat{M}\) as the composition of the Normal Clifford Multiplier and the Dirac Operator:

\[\hat{M} = \hat{n}_{\text{Cl}} \cdot \mathcal{D}_M\mathcal{D}_M \cdot \hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}.\]

Since \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}^2 =1\) and \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}\) anticommutes with \(\mathcal{D}_M\) (orthogonality of normal and tangential directions), the above antisymmetric combination vanishes. Therefore, the correct definition of the Mass Operator should take the symmetric combination:

\[\hat{M} = \frac{1}{2} \{\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}, \mathcal{D}_M\} + \text{Cl}(3) \text{ structure correction}.\]

Under the Cl(3) Semisimple Splitting, the Eigenvalues of \(\hat{M}\) are jointly determined by the Spectral Gap \(\Delta\lambda_M\) and the Coding Weight \(\sigma_M\):

\[m_M = K \cdot \Delta\lambda_M \cdot \sqrt{\sigma_M^*} = K \cdot \frac{2}{r_M} \cdot \sqrt{0.777912},\]

where \(K\) is the geometric factor given by \(\sin^3\theta_M\) (see Section 3.2 for details). This construction of the Mass Operator guarantees that mass is the product of normal geometry and algebraic weight——determined purely by the structure of the ℳ sector.

5.2 Mass Operator and Helicity

The commutation relations between the Mass Operator \(\hat{M}\) and the helicity projections \(P_\pm\) determine the helicity structure of mass. Since \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}\) takes Eigenvalues \(\pm 1\) under \(P_\pm\) respectively, the Mass Operator splits in the diagonal basis as:

\[\hat{M} = \hat{M}_+ \cdot P_+ + \hat{M}_- \cdot P_-.\]

At the ℳ sector self-consistent fixed point, \(\hat{M}_+ = \hat{M}_-\)——left- and right-handed masses are equal, which is the geometric reason why the mass eigenstates are Dirac spinors. When ℳ-𝒞 coupling is turned on, \(\hat{M}_+ \neq \hat{M}_-\), giving the geometric condition for Majorana mass splitting.

5.3 Spectrum of the Mass Operator

The spectrum of the Mass Operator \(\hat{M}\) corresponds one-to-one with the spectrum of the Dirac Operator \(\mathcal{D}_M\):

Dirac Eigenvalue Mass Operator Eigenvalue Physical Correspondence
λ₀ = ±1/r_M m₀ = K · 2/r_M · √σ_M^* Ground state mass
λ₁ = ±2/r_M m₁ = K · 4/r_M · √σ_M^* First excited state
λ_n = ±(n+1)/r_M m_n = K · 2(n+1)/r_M · √σ_M^* n-th excited state

The discreteness of the mass spectrum shares the same origin as the discreteness of the Dirac spectrum——both come from the compactness of \(S^3\). The ratio of excited-state mass to ground-state mass is \(m_n / m_0 = n+1\); this equally-spaced structure, under modulation by the breathing mode, produces the non-equally-spaced three-generation lepton spectrum (see Section 3.5 for details).

In the Eigenvalue expression of the Mass Operator \(\hat{M}\), \(m_M = K \cdot \Delta\lambda_M \cdot \sqrt{\sigma_M^*}\), the concrete derivation of the geometric factor \(K = \sin^3\theta_M\), the numerical determination of the constant \(K\), and the computation of the three-generation lepton mass ratios will be given in Section 3.2 (Mass Generation Dynamics). The normal geometry structure is the “hardware” of mass generation, while the Born Rule and the self-consistent iteration of Birkhoff mixing are the “software” of mass generation——together they determine the complete mass spectrum of matter particles.

It must be emphasized that the construction of the Mass Operator \(\hat{M}\) relies entirely on the normal geometry of the ℳ sector——the symmetric combination of the Normal Clifford Multiplier \(\hat{n}_{\text{Cl}}\) and the Dirac Operator \(\mathcal{D}_M\) yields the algebraic definition of mass. This construction is determined by local region properties, entirely fixed by the Cl(3) Semisimple Splitting and the \(S^3\) spherical geometry.
