2 Definition of the Breathing Mode

2.1 Definition

Definition 3.11.2.01 (Breathing Mode): The scalar field \(\Phi(x)\) is defined as the relative linear fluctuation of the square root of the determinant of the second-order structure at the Birkhoff Fixed Point σ*:

\[\frac{\sqrt{\det H(\Phi)} - \sqrt{\det H_0}}{\sqrt{\det H_0}} \equiv \frac{\Phi}{S_0}\]

That is:

\[\sqrt{\det H(\Phi)} = \sqrt{\det H_0} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\Phi}{S_0}\right)\]

Geometric meaning: \(\sqrt{\det H}\) is the area element density of the Sector Parameter Space. The breathing mode is a uniform scaling of the area element density — the parameter space is “breathing.”

2.2 Automatic Preservation of Sector Structure Constraint

Φ operates only on the Hessian determinant and does not directly alter the Sector parameters \(\theta_M, \theta_C, \theta_I\). Therefore the Sector structure constraint \(\theta_M + \theta_C + \theta_I = 90^\circ\) is automatically preserved. The breathing mode does not disrupt the integrity of the three-sector structure.

2.3 Hessian Global Scaling Hypothesis

Under the breathing mode, the simplest response of the Hessian is global scaling:

\[H_{ij}(\Phi) = H_{ij}^{(0)} \cdot \left(1+\frac{\Phi}{S_0}\right)\]

Both eigenvalues scale proportionally, preserving the eigendirections. This is the lowest-order isotropic approximation, consistent with the geometric picture of the breathing mode as “uniform dilation.”

Hypothesis level: This is a constructive hypothesis. Derivations below that depend on \(H_{ij} \propto (1+\Phi/S_0)\) (third- and fourth-order potential terms) all take this as a premise.

3 Geometric Lagrangian

3.1 Kinetic Term — Coding Orbit Pixel Gradient

The Cl(7) structure of the Information Sector ℐ yields \(N_{\text{info}} \approx 9 \times 10^{22}\) coding pixels. Differences in fluctuations between neighboring pixels incur a configurational entropy cost. In the continuum limit, with pixel spacing \(a \to 0\), one obtains the 2D kinetic term:

\[\Delta S_{\text{info}} \rightarrow \frac{1}{2} \int (\partial_\mu \Phi)^2 \, d\xi d\eta \quad (\mu = \xi, \eta)\]

\[V^{(2)}(\Phi) = 2\lambda_1 \Phi^2\]

  3. Clifford internal spinor normalization: upon reduction to 4D, \(d_{\text{int}} = 5\) internal generators contribute \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{spinor}} = 2^{5/2} = 4\sqrt{2}\)

\[\boxed{\mathcal{N}_\Sigma = \frac{\int_{S^2} K_G \, dA}{\chi(S^2) \cdot \mathcal{N}_{\text{spinor}}} = \frac{4\pi}{2 \cdot 4\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{8}} = 1.11072073\ldots}\]
