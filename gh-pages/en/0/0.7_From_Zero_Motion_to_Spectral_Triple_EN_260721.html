Abstract

This article rigorously distinguishes “axiom” from “tool”: the δ axiom of Conjugate Spectral Geometry (CSG) remains the sole starting point of the system; the Spectral Triple \((A, \mathcal{H}, D)\) of Noncommutative Geometry (NCG) is not accepted as an axiomatic hypothesis but is constructed from existing CSG conclusions as a theorem. On this basis, NCG tools such as the spectral action principle and the local index theorem are absorbed as intermediate-layer computational frameworks of CSG, the coupling of the three Sectors \(\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}\) obtains a unified variational formulation, and the Completeness Criterion (Berry = \(2\pi\)) is elevated from a “landscape selection criterion” to an inevitable corollary of the index theorem. The number of axioms remains unchanged, and the logical chain is liberated from the conceptual closed loop.

§0. Precise Formulation of the Problem

§0.1 Tension in the Existing CSG Logical Chain

CSG’s derivation chain from the δ axiom to the full spectrum of physical constants has a publicly acknowledged tension at the step “δ → Clifford Algebra”. Recall 0.2 §1.6:

“Suppression-Trace-Emergence is demoted from ‘basis for uniqueness proof’ to ‘philosophical motivation’——they guide the choice of Clifford Algebra but do not constitute mathematical proof.”

Specifically:

  1. The landscape L is the set of all possible algebraic realizations, and Clifford Algebra Cl(n) is one candidate.
  2. The Completeness Criterion (Berry phase = \(2\pi\)) is used to filter candidates.
  3. But the Completeness Criterion itself needs to be defined using KO theory and Chern-Simons forms of Clifford Algebra.

This constitutes a conceptual closed loop:

\[ \text{Use Clifford framework to define completeness} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{Use completeness to filter out Clifford framework} \]

This is not a logical error——under the landscape selection framework, it is self-consistent: completeness is a global topological invariant, independent of the specific algebraic realization. But it lacks an independent external anchor to break the loop.

§0.2 Advantages and Disadvantages of NCG

Alain Connes’ Noncommutative Geometry (NCG) takes the Spectral Triple \((A, \mathcal{H}, D)\) as its axiomatic starting point:

  • A: noncommutative \(C^*\) algebra (generalization of function algebras)
  • \(\mathcal{H}\): Hilbert space (state space)
  • D: Dirac operator (encoding all metric information)

Advantages of NCG:

  1. Spectral action principle: \(S(D) = \operatorname{Tr}(f(D/\Lambda))\), generating gravity + gauge fields + Higgs + fermions in one stroke, without manually setting couplings.
  2. KO dimension theory: the KO dimension of the algebra mod 8 naturally possesses Bott periodicity.
  3. Local index theorem: the NCG version of the Atiyah-Singer index theorem provides a mathematical foundation for the Berry phase.
  4. Maturity: four decades of development, solid mathematical foundations, recognized by mainstream mathematical physics.

Fundamental defect of NCG (as an axiomatic system):

NCG assumes the existence of the \((A, \mathcal{H}, D)\) triple from the outset. This means:

  • The algebraic structure A has already been selected (no explanation of “why this algebra”).
  • The Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\) has already been given (not derived from more fundamental principles).
  • The spectrum of the Dirac operator D has already been presupposed (no explanation of the origin of the spectrum).

From CSG’s perspective, NCG’s axiomatic hypotheses are not fundamental enough. The δ axiom only requires a set Z and a non-trivial self-map δ(Z) ⊊ Z——no need to presuppose algebraic structure, Hilbert space, or Dirac operator. NCG’s starting point is equivalent to beginning from some intermediate station after δ → Clifford, skipping the most crucial emergent process “from nothing to algebra”.

§0.3 Strategy of this Article

Axiom unchanged: δ remains the sole axiom of CSG.

NCG demoted to intermediate-layer tool: The Spectral Triple is not assumed from outside but constructed from existing CSG conclusions——as a theorem, not an axiom. Once constructed, NCG tools such as the spectral action and index theorem can be legitimately used, because they are now mounted on CSG’s derivation chain, not independent hypotheses.

Breaking the logical chain closed loop: If the Completeness Criterion can be derived from the index theorem of the Spectral Triple (without presupposing Clifford Algebra to define the completeness criterion), the conceptual closed loop is broken——Clifford Algebra is obtained through landscape selection + completeness filtering, while completeness itself can be independently verified from a more fundamental geometric structure (the constructed Spectral Triple).

§1.2 Construction

Theorem 0.7.1 (Spectral Triple Construction Theorem): From Cl(8), one can rigorously construct an even Spectral Triple \((A, \mathcal{H}, D)\) of KO dimension = 8:

\[ A = \mathrm{Cl}^0(8), \quad \mathcal{H} = \Delta_8 \oplus \bar{\Delta}_8, \quad D = \delta|_{\mathrm{Cl}(8)} \]

where \(\mathrm{Cl}^0(8)\) is the even subalgebra of Cl(8), \(\Delta_8\) is the irreducible real spinor representation of Cl(8) (16-dimensional Majorana-Weyl spinor), \(\bar{\Delta}_8\) is its conjugate, and \(\delta|_{\mathrm{Cl}(8)}\) is the Bott step action of δ on Cl(8) truncated to 8 dimensions.

Proof sketch:

(i) Legitimacy of the Algebra A. The even subalgebra \(\mathrm{Cl}^0(8)\) of Cl(8) is a real \(C^*\) algebra, of dimension \(2^7 = 128\). Its multiplicative structure is induced by the even part of the Clifford multiplication \(\gamma^\mu \gamma^\nu + \gamma^\nu \gamma^\mu = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}\) of Cl(8). \(\mathrm{Cl}^0(8)\) is isomorphic to a subalgebra of the \(128 \times 128\) real matrix algebra.

(ii) Legitimacy of the Hilbert Space \(\mathcal{H}\). The irreducible real representation \(\Delta_8\) of Cl(8) is generated by the 16-component spinor of the Γ matrices. By Bott Periodicity, the dimension of the irreducible real representation of Cl(8) is \(d_8 = 16\) (Majorana-Weyl). Take \(\mathcal{H} = \Delta_8 \oplus \bar{\Delta}_8 \simeq \mathbb{R}^{32}\), with standard inner product. A acts on \(\mathcal{H}\) through the spinor representation of the even subalgebra.

(iii) Legitimacy of the Dirac Operator D. The action of δ on Cl(8): \(\delta: \mathrm{Cl}(8) \to \mathrm{Cl}(9)\). But Cl(9), through Bott periodicity, is equivalent to a matrix amplification of Cl(1). Truncated to 8 dimensions, define:

\[ D = \sum_{\mu=1}^{8} \gamma^\mu \otimes \partial_\mu \]

where \(\gamma^\mu\) are the representation matrices of the Cl(8) generators on \(\Delta_8\), and \(\partial_\mu\) is the partial derivative operator along the μ-th layer of the Coding Orbit. D is a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent, satisfying all technical requirements of NCG for a Dirac operator.

(iv) Determination of KO Dimension. According to the Bott periodicity table, the KO dimension of the real Clifford Algebra Cl(8) = 8 mod 8 = 0 (trivial). The corresponding Spectral Triple has KO dimension 0 (even, real structure J satisfies \(J^2 = 1\)). But D here is defined on the Coding Orbit, and its spectrum is governed by the Bott step \(\delta^8 = 2\pi\), so the spectral dimension of D (i.e., the dimension perceived by the Fredholm index) is 8.

More precise formulation: The metric dimension of this Spectral Triple (determined by the spectral growth \(\lambda_n \sim n^{1/d}\) of D) is 8, while the KO dimension (determined by the signs of J and γ) is 0 mod 8. This corresponds precisely to the Bott periodicity position of Cl(8): dimension cycles mod 8 in KO theory, and what is physically perceived is 8 dimensions.

§2. Spectral Action and Three-Sector Unification

§2.1 Spectral Action Principle

Once the Spectral Triple \((A, \mathcal{H}, D)\) is established as a theorem, the NCG spectral action principle can be legitimately used (as a computational tool, not an independent axiom):

\[ S(D) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(f\left(\frac{D}{\Lambda}\right)\right) \]

where \(f\) is an even smooth cutoff function and \(\Lambda\) is the cutoff scale. In the CSG framework:

  • \(\Lambda\) is determined by the upper bound of Seven-Layer Truncation E₇ (0.3)
  • The form of \(f\) is constrained by the layer structure of the Coding Orbit: the asymptotic expansion of \(f(x)\) as \(x \to 0\) is determined by the eigenvalues of each layer E\(_k\)

2.1.1 Spectral Constraint from Total Action Being Zero

In CSG, the spectral action \(S(D)\) is not a free quantity——it must satisfy the Total Action is Zero constraint derived from the δ axiom (0.1 §2.3):

\[ \boxed{S_{\text{total}} = S(D) = 0} \]

This means that the expansion of the spectral action is not an approximation with arbitrary truncation, but rather a three-Sector cancellation under the exact zero-sum constraint. In the original NCG framework, \(S(D)\) gives the complete dynamics of the system——but when expanded into three Sectors, the zero-sum property of \(S(D)\) forces the contributions of the three Sectors to cancel each other:

\[ S(D) = S_\mathcal{M} + S_\mathcal{C} + S_\mathcal{I} = 0 \]

This constraint is the core additional condition of CSG compared to NCG: NCG gives the form of the action, CSG gives the value of the action——it must be zero.

§2.2 Expansion and Three-Sector Splitting

Perform a heat kernel expansion (Seeley-deWitt coefficients) of \(S(D)\) around \(\Lambda\):

\[ \operatorname{Tr}\left(f\left(\frac{D}{\Lambda}\right)\right) \sim \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} f_k \, \Lambda^{8-k} \, a_k(D^2) \]

where \(f_k = \int_0^\infty f(u) \, u^{(8-k)/2 - 1} \, du\) are the moments, and \(a_k(D^2)\) are the Seeley-deWitt coefficients.

2.2.1 Sector Splitting under the Zero-Sum Constraint

The three-Sector splitting must satisfy \(S_{\text{total}} = 0\) (0.1 §2.3). In the Seeley-deWitt expansion, this is equivalent to:

\[ \boxed{S_\mathcal{M} + S_\mathcal{C} + S_\mathcal{I} = \sum_{k=0,2,4,6,8} f_k \Lambda^{8-k} a_k = 0} \]

where \(S_\mathcal{M}\) corresponds to \(a_4\) (gauge fields) + \(a_6\) (Higgs), \(S_\mathcal{C}\) corresponds to \(a_2\) (Ricci gravity) + the gravity squared part in \(a_4\), and \(S_\mathcal{I}\) corresponds to \(a_8\) (topological term). \(a_0\) (volume/cosmological constant term), as global normalization, is automatically determined by the zero-sum constraint——it absorbs the residue after three-Sector cancellation.

This zero-sum constraint is a rigid boundary condition of the spectral action expansion: it is not optional, but an inevitable corollary of δ’s non-surjectivity. Any Sector parameter combination violating \(S_{\text{total}} = 0\) is excluded by self-consistency.

§2.3 Variational Formulation of Sector Coupling

\[ D \to D + A + \gamma^5 \otimes \Phi \]

where A is the gauge connection 1-form (Matter Sector), \(\Phi\) is the scalar field (the Higgs part of the Matter Sector), and the “background” part of D carries gravitational degrees of freedom (Causal Sector) and topological degrees of freedom (Information Sector). Variation yields:

2.3.1 S_total = 0 as Global Constraint

The above four variational equations are not independent——they are subject to the global constraint \(S_{\text{total}} = 0\). This means:

  • The equations of motion of the three Sectors are coupled: the gauge field equations of the Matter Sector, the gravitational equations of the Causal Sector, and the topological equations of the Information Sector must jointly satisfy the zero-sum condition.

  • This explains why the coupling constants \(\theta_\mathcal{M}, \theta_\mathcal{C}, \theta_\mathcal{I}\) of the three Sectors are not free parameters, but interlocked quantities locked by the zero-sum constraint (0.5 §2.1).

\[ \operatorname{Index}(D^+) = \int_{\mathcal{M}^8} \hat{A}(\mathcal{M}^8) \, \mathrm{ch}(E) \]

\[ \operatorname{Index}(D^+) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \oint_{S^7} \mathrm{CS}_7(\omega) \]

\[ \frac{1}{2\pi} \oint_{S^7} \mathrm{CS}_7(\omega) = 1 \]

i.e., Berry phase = \(2\pi\).

Theorem 0.7.3 (Index Theorem version of the Completeness Criterion):

\[ \gamma_{\text{Berry}} = 2\pi \cdot \operatorname{Index}(D^+) = 2\pi \]

§4. The Necessity of KO Dimension 8

§4.1 Derived from δ, Not Assumed

In NCG, the KO dimension is an adjustable parameter. But in CSG, KO dimension 8 is a direct corollary of \(\delta^8 = 2\pi\):

  1. δ satisfies \(\delta^8 = 2\pi\) (0.3, Bott Periodicity theorem)
  2. The algebraic realization of δ is Cl(8) (0.2, Clifford algebraization)
  3. The Spectral Triple constructed from this necessarily has KO dimension 8 mod 8 = 0

If the KO dimension were some other value (e.g., KO dimension 0 or 10 as used in Connes’ Standard Model), then δ’s Bott periodicity would not close, the Berry phase would not be \(2\pi\), and the system would not satisfy completeness.

§4.2 Comparison with the Connes-Chamseddine Standard Model

Connes and Chamseddine’s NCG version of the Standard Model uses:

  • Algebra A = \(\mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{H} \oplus M_3(\mathbb{C})\) (manually chosen to match the Standard Model gauge group)

CSG differs in that:

  • A = \(\mathrm{Cl}^0(8)\) (not hand-fitted, but derived)
  • KO dimension = 8 mod 8 = 0 (not 10 mod 8 = 2)
  • The gauge group emerges from the automorphism group of the even subalgebra of Cl(8) (completed in 7.1)

This means that CSG and Connes NCG have substantive differences in their algebraic choices. The gauge group given by CSG’s Cl(8) path may be richer (or more constrained) than \(\mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{H} \oplus M_3(\mathbb{C})\), requiring further Sector analysis to determine. But the core principle remains unchanged: CSG’s algebra is derived from δ, not fitted.
