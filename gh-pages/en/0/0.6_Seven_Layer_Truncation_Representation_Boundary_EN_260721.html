§2 Indecomposability of the Cl(8) Residue

2.1 Algebraic Prerequisites

Let Cl(8) be generated by generators \(\{f_1, \ldots, f_8\}\), satisfying \(f_i^2 =1\), \(f_i f_j = -f_j f_i\) (\(i \neq j\)). As a real vector space, Cl(8) has dimension 256. Notational convention. This article uses the \(f_i\) notation (\(f_i^2 =1\)), while uses the \(e_i\) notation (\(e_i^2 = -1\)). The two conventions are interconverted via \(f_i = i e_i\) (multiplying by the imaginary unit), corresponding to the positive-definite and negative-definite real forms of Clifford algebras. The \(f_i\) notation is chosen to make the projection operator expressions in the \(C(f_8)/A(f_8)\) decomposition (§2.2) more natural——under this convention \(f_8\) itself is an involution (\(f_8^2 =1\)), allowing direct construction of projections \(P_\pm = (1 \pm f_8)/2\). The two conventions are equivalent in the sense of algebraic isomorphism, and the conclusions of this article are independent of notational choice.

2.2 Commuting and Anti-Commuting Subspaces

Definition: \[C(f_8) = \{x \in \text{Cl}(8) \mid x f_8 = f_8 x\}, \quad A(f_8) = \{x \in \text{Cl}(8) \mid x f_8 = -f_8 x\}\]

Proposition (Indecomposability of the Cl(8) Residue). As a vector space, Cl(8) has a direct sum decomposition \(\text{Cl}(8) = C(f_8) \oplus A(f_8)\), where \(C(f_8)\) and \(A(f_8)\) are each 128-dimensional. In the 16-dimensional irreducible real representation of Cl(8), the eigenvalues of \(f_8\) are \(\pm 1\), each of multiplicity 8; \(C(f_8)\) preserves the \(\pm 1\) eigenspace decomposition, isomorphic to \(\text{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R}) \oplus \text{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R})\); \(A(f_8)\) exchanges the two eigenspaces. If the physical state space is restricted to a single eigenspace of \(f_8\) (i.e., the freezing condition at the seventh step of Bott Periodicity), then the matrix elements \(\langle\psi|A(f_8)|\phi\rangle = 0\) for all \(\psi, \phi\) in that physical representation. Hence the 128-dimensional structure of \(A(f_8)\) is unobservable, indecomposable, and irreducible in the \(n \leq 7\) physical world.

Proof.

  1. Centralizer structure: \(C(f_8)\) is generated by \(\{f_1 f_8, f_2 f_8, \ldots, f_7 f_8\}\). Since \((f_i f_8)(f_j f_8) = -f_i f_j = f_j f_i\) (\(i \neq j\)), and \((f_i f_8)^2 = -1\), the set \(\{f_i f_8\}\) generates Cl(7), of dimension \(2^7 = 128\). Thus \(C(f_8) \cong \text{Cl}(7)\), dimension 128.

  2. Anti-commuting subspace dimension: Cl(8) has total dimension 256, so \(A(f_8)\) also has dimension 128. Concretely, \(A(f_8) = \text{Cl}(7)^{\text{odd}} \oplus \text{Cl}(7)^{\text{even}} \cdot f_8\), each 64-dimensional.

  3. Representation-theoretic zero map: In the 16-dimensional real spinor representation, \(f_8\) as a Hermitian matrix has eigenvalues \(\pm 1\), with eigenspaces \(V_+, V_-\) each 8-dimensional. \(C(f_8)\) acts on \(V_+\) and \(V_-\) separately (block diagonal), while \(A(f_8)\) maps \(V_+\) to \(V_-\) (block anti-diagonal). If the physical freezing condition restricts the state space to \(V_+\) (or \(V_-\)), then the matrix elements of \(A(f_8)\) on \(V_+\) are zero maps. Since the \(n \leq 7\) physical representation (Cl(7)-type) is precisely the 8-dimensional real representation on \(V_+\) (or \(V_-\)), \(A(f_8)\) is indecomposable in the \(n \leq 7\) physical world. ∎
