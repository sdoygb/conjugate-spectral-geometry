The Proof Chain

Theorem (\(E_8\) Bridging Theorem). Let Bott periodicity be 8 (Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro). Then the prime factor set \(\{2, 3, 5\}\) is uniquely determined as a topological invariant of dimension 8 by the following proof chain:

\[\text{Bott periodicity} \xrightarrow{\text{Step 1}} \text{Dimension 8} \xrightarrow{\text{Step 2}} E_8 \text{ unique} \xrightarrow{\text{Step 3}} h = 30 \xrightarrow{\text{Step 4}} \{2, 3, 5\}.\]

Proof.

Step 1: Bott periodicity → KO-theoretic 8-periodicity → existence condition for even unimodular lattices.

The connection between the “8” of Bott periodicity and the “8” of the \(E_8\) lattice is not a direct identification, but passes through the rigorous chain of KO theory (real K-theory):

(a) Bott periodicity → 8-periodicity of KO groups.

Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro (1964) proved that real Clifford algebras satisfy Bott periodicity: \(\text{Cl}(n+8) \cong \text{Cl}(n) \otimes \text{Mat}(16, \mathbb{R})\). This periodicity is equivalent to the 8-periodicity of KO groups:

\[KO^{n+8}(X) \cong KO^n(X),\]

i.e., the reduced groups of real K-theory have period 8. The classification of Clifford algebras is given by KO groups: the type of irreducible representation space of \(\text{Cl}(n)\) is determined by \(KO^{-n}(\text{pt})\).

(b) KO 8-periodicity → spinor manifolds → existence condition for even unimodular lattices.

The 8-periodicity of KO theory has a deep connection with the existence condition for even unimodular lattices in lattice theory. Concretely:

  • Even unimodular lattice existence theorem: an even unimodular lattice exists in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) if and only if \(n \equiv 0 \pmod{8}\). This “8” is the same 8 as that of Bott periodicity — the evenness condition \(v \cdot v \in 2\mathbb{Z}\) requires integrality constraints on the quadratic form that coincide with the characteristic class theory of KO theory.
  • Spinor construction of the \(E_8\) lattice: the \(E_8\) root lattice can be constructed as \(E_8 = D_8 \cup (D_8 + \rho)\), where \(D_8\) is the root system of \(\text{Spin}(16)\) and \(\rho\) is a half-spinor weight. The half-spinor weight \(\rho\) comes precisely from the spinor representation of Clifford algebras — this directly links the construction of the \(E_8\) lattice to Clifford algebras.
  • Half-spinor representation of \(\text{Spin}(16)\): the half-spinor representations \(S^{\pm}\) of \(\text{Spin}(16)\) are each 128-dimensional real representations, given by the structure \(\text{Cl}(16) \cong \text{Mat}(16, \mathbb{R}) \otimes \text{Mat}(16, \mathbb{R})\). The \(E_8\) lattice is the \(D_8\) lattice plus a half-spinor weight coset — this construction is a standard application of Clifford spinor theory.

(c) Hence the connecting chain is:

\[\text{Bott periodicity (8)} \xrightarrow{\text{KO theory}} KO^{n+8} \cong KO^n \xrightarrow{\text{spinor manifolds}} \text{even unimodular lattice condition } n \equiv 0 \pmod{8} \xrightarrow{n=8} E_8 \text{ unique}\]

Every step in this chain is a standard mathematical theorem (Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro; Minkowski–Serre; for the spinor construction of \(E_8\) see Conway–Sloane, Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups). There is no semantic jump of “8=8” — the two “8”s are rigorously connected through the 8-periodicity of KO theory and the existence condition for even unimodular lattices.

Step 2: \(E_8\) is the unique even unimodular lattice in dimension 8.

Theorem 0.5.0.02 (Minkowski–Serre). In dimension 8, the \(E_8\) lattice is the unique even unimodular lattice.

This is a classical theorem of lattice theory. Its proof hinges on the following points:

  • Evenness: every vector \(v\) in the lattice \(\Lambda\) satisfies \(v \cdot v \in 2\mathbb{Z}\).
  • Unimodularity: \(\det(\Lambda) = 1\), i.e., the Gram determinant of the lattice is 1.

Dimensional comparison: in dimensions \(< 8\) no even unimodular lattice exists (because evenness requires dimension \(\geq 8\)). In dimension 8 there is exactly one (\(E_8\)). Step 3: The Coxeter number of \(E_8\) is \(h = 30\).

The Coxeter number of \(E_8\) is a known quantity from standard Lie theory, computed from the Cartan matrix:

\[h = \frac{|\Phi(E_8)|}{\text{rank}(E_8)} = \frac{240}{8} = 30.\]

where \(|\Phi(E_8)| = 240\) is the number of roots in the root system of \(E_8\) (a standard result), and \(\text{rank}(E_8) = 8\) is the rank of \(E_8\).

Equivalently, \(h\) is the order of the longest element (Coxeter element) in the Weyl group \(W(E_8)\) of \(E_8\), and is also the sum of the coefficients of the highest root \(\theta\) over all simple roots plus 1. These computations involve no choices or fitting — \(h = 30\) is a rigid invariant of \(E_8\).

Step 4: \(h = 30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5\) yields the prime factor set \(\{2, 3, 5\}\).

\[30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5.\]

The prime factorization of 30 is unique. Therefore \(\{2, 3, 5\}\) is the prime factor set of the Coxeter number of \(E_8\) — a topological invariant of the \(E_8\) lattice, uniquely determined by Bott periodicity. ∎

2.3 Formal Proof of the Semantic Mapping

Note. The semantic mapping proof of this section is auxiliary — it provides a conceptual correspondence between the semantic layer (Suppression-Trace-Emergence) and the algebraic layer. The rigorous foundation of the prime factor set \(\{2, 3, 5\}\) and the individual assignment is provided by the \(E_8\) Bridging Theorem of §0 (a purely topological proof, not dependent on semantics). The above correspondence is not merely an analogy — below we prove that each semantic property has a unique algebraic realization in Clifford algebras, so the mapping is non-replaceable.

Proposition 0.5.2.02 (Inevitability of the Semantic Mapping). Let the three semantic properties of \(\delta\) (Suppression, Trace, Emergence) be as defined in . Then in the Bott periodic table:

  1. The unique algebraic realization of Suppression is the semisimple splitting at \(n = 3\) (\(\Lambda = 3\)).
  2. The unique algebraic realization of Trace is the complex structure emergence at \(n = 5\) (\(\Delta\Theta = 5\)).
  3. The unique algebraic realization of Emergence is the terminal duality at \(n = 7\) (\(k_0 = 2\)).

That is: no other position in the Bott periodic table can satisfy the same semantic property.

Proof.

(1) Suppression ↔︎ Semisimple Splitting (\(\Lambda = 3\)).

The semantics of Suppression is “this is not that” — negation creates a boundary, and the two sides of the boundary do not communicate. The algebraic counterpart is a direct sum decomposition: \(A = A_1 \oplus A_2\) means that elements of \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) mutually “negate” each other (their product is zero).

Formalization: let \(P_{\pm}\) be the projectors of the direct sum decomposition. Then \(P_+P_- = 0\) — an element of one direct summand “annihilates” an element of the other direct summand. This is the precise algebraic expression of “this is not that”: \(P_+\) says “I am this”, \(P_-\) says “I am that”, and \(P_+P_- = 0\) says “this and that do not communicate”.

Uniqueness argument: in the Bott periodic table, direct sum decompositions (semisimple splittings) occur only at odd \(n\) where \(\omega_n^2 = 1\): - \(n = 3\): \(\omega_3^2 = 1\), \(\text{Cl}(3) = \mathbb{H} \oplus \mathbb{H}\) — the first splitting. - \(n = 7\): \(\omega_7^2 = 1\), \(\text{Cl}(7) = \text{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R})^{\oplus 2}\) — the terminal splitting.

\(n = 3\) is the first splitting — for \(n < 3\), all \(\text{Cl}(n)\) are simple algebras, with no direct sum decomposition, hence no algebraic structure of “this is not that”. Suppression, as the first semantic property of \(\delta\) (in Suppression is the first action of \(\delta\)), must correspond to the first splitting — \(n = 3\).

(The splitting at \(n = 7\) is terminal and corresponds to Emergence, not Suppression — see point (3) below.)

(2) Trace ↔︎ Complex Structure (\(\Delta\Theta = 5\)).

The semantics of Trace is “the distinction is remembered” — history is accumulated and not lost. The algebraic counterpart is phase accumulation: the complex structure \(J\) (\(J^2 = -1\)) generates a \(U(1)\) action \(\{e^{i\theta J}\}\), whose orbits are circles \(S^1\), and the phase \(\theta \in [0, 2\pi)\) accumulates the rotational history.

Formalization: let \(J = \omega_5\) be the complex structure. \(J\) defines a \(U(1)\) group action: \[U(1) \curvearrowright \text{Cl}(5), \quad e^{i\theta J} = \cos\theta + J\sin\theta.\] The orbits \(\{e^{i\theta J} \cdot x \mid \theta \in [0, 2\pi)\}\) are \(S^1\). The phase \(\theta\) is “memory” — it records how many rotations have been traversed from the initial state to the current state. \(J^2 = -1\) means two \(90^\circ\) rotations reach “negation” (\(-1\)), but four rotations return to the origin (\(J^4 = 1\)) — precisely the semantics of Trace: history is remembered but does not block action (rotation can continue).

Uniqueness argument: in the Bott periodic table, a central complex structure (\(J = \omega_n\), \(J^2 = -1\), \(J\) commutes with all generators) occurs only at odd \(n\) where \(\omega_n^2 = -1\): - \(n = 1\): \(\omega_1 = e_1\), \(e_1^2 = -1\), but \(e_1\) is a generator and is not central — \(e_1\) commutes with no element other than itself. This does not count as an “emergent” complex structure. - \(n = 5\): \(\omega_5^2 = -1\), \(\omega_5\) commutes with all generators — the first emergent central complex structure.

\(n = 5\) is the position of the first emergent central complex structure — for \(n < 5\) there is no complex structure defined by the volume element, hence no phase accumulation mechanism. Trace, as the second semantic property of \(\delta\) (after Suppression), must correspond to the first phase accumulation — \(n = 5\).

(3) Emergence ↔︎ Terminal Duality (\(k_0 = 2\)).

The semantics of Emergence is “the accumulated pattern closes” — the pattern reaches its final form and is irreversible. The algebraic counterpart must satisfy three conditions:

  • (a) Terminality: located at the terminal of the Coding Orbit (\(n = 7\), the last step; \(n = 8\) is the closure step, not a coding layer).
  • (b) Duality: produces exactly 2 direct summands — the smallest non-trivial choice (1 is trivial; \(\geq 3\) does not occur in Bott periodicity).
  • (c) Ineliminability: the terminal duality cannot be undone by subsequent steps — \(n = 8\) is the Bott return, which “stitches” the duality, but this is the closure step, not an independent coding layer.

Formalization: the two direct summands of \(\text{Cl}(7) = \text{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R}) \oplus \text{Mat}(8, \mathbb{R})\) correspond to two “coding ground states”. These two ground states are the final product of the 7-step accumulation of the Coding Orbit — they are the algebraic expression of “pattern closure”: the structure accumulated through 7 steps of \(\delta\) iteration finally crystallizes as a binary choice.

Uniqueness argument: only two positions in the Bott periodic table produce direct sum splittings (\(n = 3\) and \(n = 7\)). The splitting at \(n = 3\) is already occupied by Suppression (point (1)). \(n = 7\) is the unique position that simultaneously satisfies (a) terminality, (b) duality, and (c) ineliminability: - The splitting at \(n = 3\) is undone by the stitching at \(n = 4\) (\(\text{Cl}(4) = \text{Mat}(2, \mathbb{H})\) is a simple algebra), failing (c). - The splitting at \(n = 7\) cannot be undone by \(n = 8\) as a new coding layer (\(n = 8\) is the closure step), satisfying (c).

Hence \(n = 7\) is the unique algebraic realization of Emergence. \(\square\)
