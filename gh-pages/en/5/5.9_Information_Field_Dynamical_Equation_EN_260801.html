Information Field Dynamical Equation (Geometric Boltzmann Analogy)

Number: 5.9 | Date: 260728.1 | Series: Information Field Cumulant Factorization (G4) | Language: EN Prerequisites: Article 5.6 (Cumulant Definition), Article 5.7 (Cumulant Upper Bound), Article 5.8 (Uniform Boundedness), Theorem 7.13.3.05 (Birkhoff Mixing), Theorem 7.13.4.01 (Hard-Direction Lyapunov Contraction), Theorem 3.2.2.01 (N_info) Status: Complete — Derivation of the information field dynamical equation from cumulant factorization; a Boltzmann equation analogue within the geometric framework

Abstract

G1–G3 have proved that higher-order cumulants (\(k \ge 3\)) of the information field decay exponentially to zero under steady-state causal circulation. This article (G4) derives, from this conclusion, the closed dynamical equation of the information field — a geometric version of the Boltzmann equation. Core steps: (1) establish the information field BBGKY hierarchy; (2) use the cumulant truncation theorem to prove that the hierarchy truncates at second order after \(\tau \ge \tau_*\); (3) derive the single-body dynamical equation (discrete); (4) obtain the information field Boltzmann equation in the continuum limit. This equation is structurally parallel to Deng-Yu’s hard-sphere Boltzmann equation, but the microscopic mechanism differs — collisions arise from cross-sector coupling of the Birkhoff mixing matrix, rather than elastic collisions between particles.

Keywords: BBGKY hierarchy; cumulant truncation; information field dynamical equation; Boltzmann equation; cross-sector coupling; Clifford sector transitions

§1 BBGKY Hierarchy and Cumulant Truncation

1.1 Information Field BBGKY Hierarchy

Definition 5.9.1.01 (Information field BBGKY hierarchy): On the information field graph \(G_{\mathcal{I}}\), the s-body reduced density matrix \(\rho^{(s)}_\tau\) satisfies the hierarchy equation:

\[\partial_\tau \rho^{(s)}_\tau(i_1, \ldots, i_s) = -\sum_{j=1}^{s} \sum_{k} L_{\mathcal{I}}(i_j, k) \rho^{(s)}_\tau(i_1, \ldots, k, \ldots, i_s) - \sum_{j=1}^{s} \sum_{k, \ell} \mathcal{K}(i_j; k, \ell) \rho^{(s+1)}_\tau(i_1, \ldots, k, \ell, \ldots, i_s)\]

where: - \(L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T_{ss}\) is the graph Laplacian (“free transport” term) - \(\mathcal{K}\) is the effective coupling kernel between Clifford sectors (from off-diagonal blocks of \(T_{\text{inter}}\)) - The first term describes diffusion of the s-body density on the parameter space - The second term couples the s-body density with the (s+1)-body density — this is the typical “open” structure of the BBGKY hierarchy

Prerequisite dependencies: The definition of \(G_{\mathcal{I}}\) is found in Article 5.6 (Definition 1.1); the mixing property of \(T_{ss}\) is found in Theorem 7.13.3.05.

1.2 Cumulant Truncation Theorem

Theorem 5.9.1.02 (BBGKY truncation theorem): Suppose \(|C_k(\tau)| \leq A_k \cdot e^{-(k-2)\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\) (Theorem 5.7.4.03). Then for any \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists \(\tau_* = O(\log(1/\varepsilon) / \lambda_1^{\text{eff}})\) such that for \(\tau \ge \tau_*\):

\[\rho^{(3)}_\tau(i, j, k) = \rho^{(1)}_\tau(i) \rho^{(1)}_\tau(j) \rho^{(1)}_\tau(k) + \rho^{(1)}_\tau(i) C_2(j, k) + \rho^{(1)}_\tau(j) C_2(i, k) + \rho^{(1)}_\tau(k) C_2(i, j) + O(\varepsilon)\]

In particular, the BBGKY hierarchy truncates at second order after \(\tau \ge \tau_*\) — reduced densities for \(s \ge 3\) are completely determined by \(\rho^{(1)}\) and \(C_2\).

Proof: By the cumulant inversion formula, \(\rho^{(3)}\) can be expressed as products of \(\rho^{(1)}\) + \(C_2\) terms + \(C_3\). Theorem 5.7.4.03 guarantees \(C_3 \sim e^{-\lambda_1^{\text{eff}} \tau}\). Taking \(\tau_* = \log(1/\varepsilon)/\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}\) yields the result. ∎

Numerical anchor: \(\tau_* \approx 0.14\) geometric units (third-order factorization time, see Article 5.7 §5.4). On any macroscopic fluid time scale, BBGKY truncation is instantaneous.

Corollary 5.9.1.03 (Hierarchy closure): For \(\tau \ge \tau_*\), the BBGKY hierarchy closes at \(s=2\) — the evolution equation for \(\rho^{(2)}\) involves only \(\rho^{(1)}\) and \(\rho^{(2)}\) itself, no longer requiring \(\rho^{(3)}\).

§2 Single-Body Dynamical Equation

2.1 Discrete Equation

Theorem 5.9.2.01 (Information field single-body dynamical equation): After BBGKY truncation, the single-body distribution \(f_\tau(i) = \rho^{(1)}_\tau(i)\) satisfies the closed dynamical equation:

\[\boxed{\partial_\tau f_\tau(i) = -\sum_{j} L_{\mathcal{I}}(i, j) f_\tau(j) + \sum_{j, k} \mathcal{Q}(i; j, k) f_\tau(j) f_\tau(k)}\]

where: - \(L_{\mathcal{I}} = I - T_{ss}\) is the graph Laplacian (“free transport” term) - \(\mathcal{Q}(i; j, k) = \mathcal{K}(i; j, k) + \mathcal{K}(i; k, j) - \sum_{\ell} [\mathcal{K}(\ell; j, k) T_{ss}(\ell, i) + \mathcal{K}(\ell; k, j) T_{ss}(\ell, i)]\) is the effective two-body interaction kernel (“collision” term)

Proof: Substitute the BBGKY truncation (Theorem 5.9.1.02) into the \(s=1\) hierarchy equation, expressing \(\rho^{(2)}\) in terms of \(\rho^{(1)}\) and \(C_2\). The \(C_2\) term yields a linear correction (absorbed into the definition of \(\mathcal{Q}\)), while the nonlinear term \(\sim f_\tau(j) f_\tau(k)\) comes from the \(\rho^{(1)} \otimes \rho^{(1)}\) contribution to \(\rho^{(2)}\). ∎

2.2 Physical Interpretation

Equation Term Discrete Form Physical Meaning
Free transport \(-\sum_j L_{\mathcal{I}}(i,j) f_\tau(j)\) Diffusion of information on the parameter space \(S^2\)
Collision term \(\sum_{j,k} \mathcal{Q}(i; j,k) f_\tau(j) f_\tau(k)\) Information exchange between Clifford sectors
Degrees of freedom \(f_\tau \in \Delta^{N_{\text{info}}-1}\) \(N_{\text{info}} \approx 9.024 \times 10^{22}\) nodes

Key difference: The standard Boltzmann collision operator \(Q(f,f)\) arises from elastic collisions between particles, whereas \(\mathcal{Q}\) arises from cross-sector coupling of the Birkhoff mixing matrix. Both are two-body interactions, but their physical origins differ — the former is mechanical collision, the latter is information field mixing.

§3 Information Field Boltzmann Equation in the Continuum Limit

3.1 Continuous Dynamical Equation

Theorem 5.9.3.01 (Continuous information field dynamical equation, requires A.1): Under Assumption A.1 (discrete → continuous convergence), the single-body distribution \(f(x, \tau)\) on the sector parameter space \(S^2\) satisfies:

\[\boxed{\partial_\tau f(x, \tau) + \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \cdot \nabla_x f = \mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}[f] + \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f, f]}\]

where: - \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} = \sqrt{\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}} \cdot \hat{\eta}\) (\(\hat{\eta}\) is the unit tangent vector in the spectral gap direction, \(\sqrt{\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}} = 19.775\)) - \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}[f] = \Delta_{S^2} f + \mathcal{L}_{Cl} f\) is the linear mixing operator (Laplace-Beltrami diffusion on \(S^2\) + Clifford inter-sector jumps) - \(\mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f, f]\) is the nonlinear collision operator in the continuum limit

Proof: Take the discrete equation of Theorem 5.9.2.01 and apply A.1 as \(N_{\text{info}} \to \infty\). The graph Laplacian \(L_{\mathcal{I}}\) converges to the Laplace-Beltrami operator \(\Delta_{S^2}\) (Article 5.12, Theorem 5.12.3.02), and the cross-sector coupling \(\mathcal{K}\) converges to \(\mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}\). ∎

Remark: Equation (3.1) is the Boltzmann equation of the geometric framework. It is structurally parallel to the Boltzmann equation derived by Deng-Yu — the left side is the transport operator, the right side is the “collision” operator. But the microscopic collision mechanism differs: Deng-Yu’s is hard-sphere elastic collisions; ours is Birkhoff mixing + Clifford sector transitions.

3.2 Basic Properties of the Collision Operator

Proposition 5.9.3.02 (Geometric counterparts of collision invariants): The continuous collision operator \(\mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}\) satisfies:

  1. Mass conservation: \(\int_{S^2} \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f, f] \, d\Omega = 0\)
  2. Momentum conservation: \(\int_{S^2} \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \, \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f, f] \, d\Omega = 0\)
  3. Energy conservation: \(\int_{S^2} |\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}}|^2 \, \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f, f] \, d\Omega = 0\)

These are direct corollaries of Birkhoff double stochasticity (\(\sum_i T_{ij} = \sum_j T_{ij} = 1\)) in the continuum limit — information field mixing neither creates nor destroys information, only redistributes it.

Core correspondence: Deng-Yu’s Boltzmann equation \(\partial_t f + v \cdot \nabla_x f = Q(f,f)\) and the information field dynamical equation (3.1) are formally completely parallel — both have transport on the left and a nonlinear two-body operator on the right. The differences lie in the base space (\(\mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}^3\) vs \(S^2\)) and the collision mechanism (hard-sphere elastic collisions vs cross-sector information mixing), but these differences do not affect the mathematical structure of the subsequent Hilbert expansion.
