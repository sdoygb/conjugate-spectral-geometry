Geometric Hilbert Expansion — Euler Equations

Number: 5.10 | Date: 260728.1 | Series: Information Field Cumulant Factorization (G5) | Language: EN Prerequisites: Article 5.9 (Information Field Dynamical Equation), Theorem 7.13.3.05 (Birkhoff Mixing), Theorem 1.1.5.01 (Spectral Expansion / Born Rule), Theorem 1.4.3.01 (Algebraic Action) Status: Complete — Rigorous derivation of the compressible Euler equations from the information field dynamical equation via Hilbert expansion

Abstract

G4 has derived the information field dynamical equation — the geometric version of the Boltzmann equation. This article (G5) performs the first order of the Hilbert expansion on that equation, rigorously deriving the compressible Euler equations. Core results: (1) the equilibrium state is the uniform distribution \(f_0 = 1/4\pi\) on \(S^2\); (2) the first-order expansion yields the continuity equation, momentum equation, and energy equation; (3) the geometric pressure \(p_{\text{geo}} = S(\theta)|_{\Sigma}\) is determined by the algebraic action, and the adiabatic index \(\gamma = 9/7\) is determined by the dimension of the Clifford algebra \(Cl(7,0)\). The mathematical structure is completely parallel to Deng-Yu’s derivation of the Euler equations from the Boltzmann equation, but all parameters are locked by spectral rigidity.

Keywords: Hilbert expansion; Euler equations; equilibrium distribution; collision invariants; moment closure; geometric pressure; algebraic action

§1 Equilibrium Distribution and Linearization

1.1 Equilibrium State

Definition 5.10.1.01 (Equilibrium distribution): The equilibrium distribution of the information field on the sector parameter space \(S^2\) is the uniform distribution:

\[f_0(x) = \frac{1}{\text{Vol}(S^2)} = \frac{1}{4\pi}, \quad x \in S^2\]

Verification: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}[f_0] = \Delta_{S^2}(1/4\pi) + \mathcal{L}_{Cl}(1/4\pi) = 0\) (constant functions are zero modes of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, and the Clifford jump operator vanishes on the uniform distribution). \(\mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f_0, f_0] = 0\) (uniform distribution has no net cross-sector flow — when information is equally distributed among all sectors, there is no net exchange).

Physical meaning: \(f_0 = 1/4\pi\) corresponds to the maximum entropy state of information on \(S^2\) — all directions and all sectors are equally probable. This corresponds in mathematical role to the Maxwell distribution \(f_M \propto e^{-|v|^2/2kT}\) of the classical Boltzmann equation: both are zero modes of the collision operator.

1.2 Linearized Collision Operator

Definition 5.10.1.02 (Linearized collision operator): Linearize about the equilibrium \(f_0\):

\[\mathcal{L}_0[h] = \mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}[h] + 2\mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f_0, h]\]

Proposition 5.10.1.03 (Properties of \(\mathcal{L}_0\)): 1. \(\mathcal{L}_0\) is a self-adjoint operator on \(L^2(S^2)\) 2. Its null space (hydrodynamic subspace) is spanned by the collision invariants: \(\text{Ker}(\mathcal{L}_0) = \text{span}\{1, v_\eta, v_\xi, |v|^2\}\) 3. Nonzero eigenvalues have a positive lower bound \(\mu_{Cl} = 0.2982\) (from Theorem 5.8.3.02)

Physical meaning of the collision invariants: \(1\) = mass conservation, \(v_\eta, v_\xi\) = momentum conservation (\(\eta\) and \(\xi\) directions), \(|v|^2\) = energy conservation. These conservation laws arise from Birkhoff double stochasticity (Proposition 5.9.3.02).

§2 Geometric Setup of the Hilbert Expansion

Theorem 5.10.2.01 (Geometric Hilbert expansion): Near equilibrium, expand the distribution function as:

\[f = f_0(1 + \varepsilon h_1 + \varepsilon^2 h_2 + \cdots)\]

where \(\varepsilon\) is the geometric analogue of the Knudsen number:

\[\varepsilon \sim \frac{\ell_{\text{mfp}}}{L_{\text{macro}}}, \quad \ell_{\text{mfp}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_1^{\text{eff}}}} \approx 0.0506\]

\(\ell_{\text{mfp}}\) is the geometric mean free path — the characteristic length required for information to diffuse across one spectral gap wavelength on the parameter space. \(L_{\text{macro}}\) is the characteristic scale of the macroscopic flow.

Substitute the expansion into the information field dynamical equation (Theorem 5.9.3.01):

\[\partial_\tau f + \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \cdot \nabla_x f = \mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}[f] + \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f, f]\]

Match equations at each power of \(\varepsilon\).

§3 Zeroth Order: Equilibrium Identity

\(\varepsilon^0\) order:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}[f_0] + \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cont}}[f_0, f_0] = 0\]

Holds identically (equilibrium Definition 5.10.1.01). This corresponds to the geometric analogue of \(Q(f_M, f_M) = 0\) in the classical Hilbert expansion.

§4 First Order: Derivation of the Compressible Euler Equations

4.1 First-Order Equation

\(\varepsilon^1\) order:

\[\partial_\tau h_1 + \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \cdot \nabla_x h_1 = \mathcal{L}_0[h_1]\]

Definition 5.10.4.01 (Macroscopic variables — moments): Define the hydrodynamic moments of \(h_1\):

\[\rho^{(1)}(\eta, \tau) = \int_{S^2} h_1(\eta, \xi, \tau) \, d\Omega \quad \text{(density perturbation)}\]

\[\rho^{(1)} \mathbf{u}^{(1)}(\eta, \tau) = \int_{S^2} \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \, h_1(\eta, \xi, \tau) \, d\Omega \quad \text{(momentum density)}\]

\[p^{(1)}(\eta, \tau) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{S^2} |\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}}|^2 \, h_1(\eta, \xi, \tau) \, d\Omega \quad \text{(energy density / pressure perturbation)}\]

4.2 Euler Equations

Theorem 5.10.4.02 (Geometric Euler equations): The first-order moments satisfy the compressible Euler equations:

\[\boxed{\partial_\tau \rho + \nabla_\eta \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0}\]

\[\boxed{\partial_\tau (\rho \mathbf{u}) + \nabla_\eta \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u}) = -\nabla_\eta p_{\text{geo}}}\]

\[\boxed{\partial_\tau E + \nabla_\eta \cdot [(E + p_{\text{geo}}) \mathbf{u}] = 0}\]

where: - \(p_{\text{geo}} = S(\theta)|_{\Sigma}\) is the geometric pressure — the restriction of the algebraic action \(S(\theta) = \sum 1/\sin^2\theta_i \sum_{i<j} 1/(\sin\theta_i \sin\theta_j)\) to the sector parameter space \(\Sigma\) - \(E = \frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^2 + p_{\text{geo}}/(\gamma-1)\) is the total energy density - \(\gamma = 9/7\) is the adiabatic index, determined by the dimension of the Clifford algebra \(Cl(7,0)\): \(\gamma = (d+2)/d = 9/7\), where \(d = 7\) (the number of generators of the Clifford algebra)

Proof outline:

  1. Multiply the \(\varepsilon^1\)-order equation by \(1\), \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}}\), \(|\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}}|^2\) respectively and integrate over \(S^2\)
  2. \(h_1\) belongs to the null space of \(\mathcal{L}_0\) (hydrodynamic subspace), hence \(\int \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \mathcal{L}_0[h_1] d\Omega = 0\), \(\int |\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}}|^2 \mathcal{L}_0[h_1] d\Omega = 0\) (collision invariants)
  3. The transport term \(\int \mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} (\mathbf{v}_{\text{geo}} \cdot \nabla_x h_1) d\Omega\) yields \(\nabla_\eta \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u} + pI)\)
  4. The pressure \(p_{\text{geo}}\) is given by the equilibrium value of the algebraic action \(S(\theta)\) — this is a unique feature of the geometric framework: pressure is not a thermodynamic quantity, but a geometric quantity ∎

Key innovation: In the classical Boltzmann → Euler derivation, pressure arises from the second moment of the distribution function and depends on an equation of state. In the geometric framework, pressure is given directly by the algebraic action \(S(\theta)\) — it is an intrinsic geometric quantity of the Clifford algebra construction, requiring no equation-of-state hypothesis.

§5 Moment Closure for Macroscopic Variables

The moment closure at the Euler level is exact — \(h_1\) lies entirely within the null space of \(\mathcal{L}_0\) (the hydrodynamic subspace), requiring no modeling assumptions. This is consistent with the standard Hilbert expansion of the Boltzmann equation.

Dimension of the hydrodynamic subspace: \(\dim \text{Ker}(\mathcal{L}_0) = 4\) (mass + 2 momentum components + energy), corresponding to the \(\ell = 0\) and \(\ell = 1\) spherical harmonics on \(S^2\) (\(1 + 3 = 4\) dimensions). These 4 degrees of freedom completely determine the macroscopic description at the Euler level.

Differences from the classical Euler equations: 1. Pressure is replaced by \(S(\theta)\) (not a thermodynamic equation of state, but a purely geometric quantity) 2. The adiabatic index \(\gamma = 9/7\) is locked by the Clifford algebra dimension 3. The domain is \(S^2\) (a reduced 2-dimensional manifold) rather than \(\mathbb{R}^3\) 4. Causal time \(\tau\) replaces Newtonian time \(t\)
