Chapter 3 Representation-Theoretic Classification of the 230 Space Groups

[Tool Layer Declaration] The group representation theory used in this chapter (Frobenius reciprocity theorem, character formulas, induced representations) is standard mathematical tooling, not derived from the Sole Axiom δ (Zero Motion) of Geometric Theory. It is introduced as an analytical language to classify crystallographic symmetries on the geometric sector parameter space. The rigorous connection between group representation theory and the Geometric Theory framework is an open research direction (see Theorem 1.4.3.01).

§3.1 Irreducible Representations of Point Groups

Proposition 10.19.3.01 (Point Group Representation Compatibility): The information field spherical harmonic modes on the geometric sector parameter space \(\Sigma \cong S^2\) are compatible with the irreducible representations of the 32 three-dimensional crystallographic point groups.

Argument: The information field modes on the coding orbit \(\Sigma \cong S^2\) are expanded in spherical harmonics \(Y_l^m\). Spherical harmonics transform under \(SO(3)\) rotations according to irreducible representations \(D^{(l)}\) (dimension \(2l+1\)). The 32 crystallographic point groups are discrete subgroups of \(SO(3)\). By Frobenius reciprocity, the \(SO(3)\) representation \(D^{(l)}\) decomposes under the point group \(G\) as a direct sum of irreducible representations of \(G\): \[D^{(l)}|_G = \bigoplus_\alpha n_\alpha^{(l)} D^{(\alpha)}\] where \(n_\alpha^{(l)}\) is the multiplicity of the irreducible representation \(D^{(\alpha)}\), determined by the character formula: \[n_\alpha^{(l)} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi^{(l)}(g)^* \chi^{(\alpha)}(g)\] The decompositions of low-order spherical harmonics (\(l=0,1,2\)) naturally cover the 32 point groups: \(l=0\) (\(D^{(0)}\), 1-dim) → totally symmetric representation \(A_{1g}\); \(l=1\) (\(D^{(1)}\), 3-dim) → decomposes as \(A_{2u} \oplus E_u\) (cubic group) or \(A_{2u} \oplus E_{1u}\) (hexagonal group); \(l=2\) (\(D^{(2)}\), 5-dim) → decomposes as \(E_g \oplus T_{2g}\) (cubic group) or \(E_{1g} \oplus E_{2g}\) (hexagonal group).

§3.2 Projective Representations of Space Groups

Proposition 10.19.3.02 (Projective Representations of Space Groups): The 230 space groups, as discrete subgroups of the Euclidean group \(E(3)\), have their projective representations jointly determined by point group representations and Bloch’s theorem for the translation group.

Argument: An element of the space group \(S\) is \(\{R|\mathbf{t}\}\), where \(R \in G\) (point group) and \(\mathbf{t}\) is a translation. By the Seitz representation, the space group acts on Hilbert space as: \[\hat{P}(R|\mathbf{t})\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \psi(R^{-1}\mathbf{r} - R^{-1}\mathbf{t})\] By Bloch’s theorem, the irreducible representations of the translation subgroup \(T\) are \(\exp(i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{t})\), so the space group representation can be reduced to representations of the “little group” of the point group at \(\mathbf{k}\). The complete classification of the 230 space groups is accomplished by induced representations from the little group representations.

§3.3 Geometric Constraint on Coordination Number

Proposition 10.19.3.03 (Geometric Constraint on Coordination Number): The coordination number (CN) is constrained by the irreducible representation dimensions of spherical harmonics on the coding orbit \(S^2\) under point group actions. The irreducible decomposition of low-order modes (\(l \leq 2\)) provides an upper-bound estimate of the number of independent geometric directions.

The coordination number describes the nearest-neighbor interaction count of a central atom and is a crystallographic geometric parameter. On \(S^2\), low-order spherical harmonic modes correspond to fundamental geometric directions: \(l=0\) (\(D^{(0)}\), 1-dim): 1 totally symmetric direction; \(l=1\) (\(D^{(1)}\), 3-dim): 3 independent spatial directions; \(l=2\) (\(D^{(2)}\), 5-dim): 5 independent quadrupole directions. Crystallographic point group symmetry further reduces the number of independent directions. This framework provides a qualitative constraint relationship between the number of geometric modes and the coordination number, not a quantitative equation.

§4.2 Representation-Theoretic Selection Rules

Proposition 10.19.4.01 (Representation-Theoretic Selection Rules for Orbital Activity): Under the point group \(G\), atomic orbitals are classified by irreducible representations \(D^{(\alpha)}\). The orbital degeneracy \(N^{\mathrm{orb}}\) equals the dimension \(d_\alpha\) of the irreducible representation.

Argument: The \(d\) orbitals (\(l=2\)) decompose under the \(O_h\) point group as \(e_g\) (2-dim) and \(t_{2g}\) (3-dim). The number of active electrons \(n^{\mathrm{act}}\) is the electron count in the partially filled representation: e.g., \(d^9\) (\(\mathrm{Cu}^{2}\)): \(e_g^4 t_{2g}^5\), active electron is the hole in \(t_{2g}\), \(n^{\mathrm{act}} = 1\); \(d^5\) (\(\mathrm{Mn}^{2}\)): \(t_{2g}^3 e_g^2\), high spin, \(n^{\mathrm{act}} = 5\). The orbital degeneracy \(N^{\mathrm{orb}}\) equals the irreducible representation dimension \(d_\alpha\). Hence \(F_{\mathrm{orb}}\) can be rewritten as: \[F_{\mathrm{orb}} = \sqrt{\sum_\alpha \frac{n_\alpha}{d_\alpha} \cdot \sum_\beta \frac{n_\beta}{d_\beta}}\] where \(n_\alpha\) is the electron count in representation \(D^{(\alpha)}\).

Chapter 5 Phonon Frequencies and Green Function Percolation

§5.1 Representation-Theoretic Selection Rules

Proposition 10.19.5.01 (Representation-Theoretic Classification of Phonon Modes): Optical phonon modes at the Brillouin zone center are classified by irreducible representations of the point group; active modes are determined by character projection.

Argument: Phonon modes correspond to normal modes of lattice vibrations, transforming under the point group according to irreducible representations. By the character projection operator: \[\hat{P}^{(\alpha)} = \frac{d_\alpha}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi^{(\alpha)}(g)^* \hat{P}(g)\] the dynamical matrix is projected onto irreducible representation subspaces, yielding symmetry-classified phonon modes. Infrared-active modes require that the direct product of the representation with the dipole moment (\(l=1\)) contains the totally symmetric representation; Raman-active modes require that the direct product with the polarizability (\(l=2\)) contains the totally symmetric representation.

§5.2 Character Projection and Wigner-Eckart: The phonon frequency Green function percolation formula, rewritten in representation-theoretic language, is (framework-level formula):

\[\omega_{\mathrm{phonon}}^{(\alpha)} = v_{\mathrm{geo}}^{\mathrm{alg}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1^{\mathrm{eff}}}{\bar{\rho}}} \cdot \frac{\Phi_G^{(\alpha)}(\Gamma)}{S_e} \cdot \frac{1}{\chi_T}\]

where \(\Phi_G^{(\alpha)}\) is the percolation function projection onto irreducible representation \(\alpha\), \(v_{\mathrm{geo}}^{\mathrm{alg}} = 71.832113\) is the algebraic self-consistent value of the geometric velocity, \(\lambda_1^{\mathrm{eff}} = 391.05\ \mathrm{rad}^{-2}\) is the effective eigenvalue in the spectral gap direction, \(\bar{\rho}\) is the reduced density parameter (dimensionless), and \(\chi_T = 3.6161912064 \times 10^{-17}\ \mathrm{s}\) is the geometric time scale. The factor \(1/\chi_T\) converts the rad⁻¹ dimension to s⁻¹, giving \(\omega\) the correct frequency dimension.

By the Wigner-Eckart theorem, matrix elements depend only on representation labels: \[\langle \alpha | \Phi | \beta \rangle = \langle \alpha \| \Phi \| \beta \rangle \cdot \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & \Gamma & \beta \\ m_\alpha & m_\Gamma & m_\beta \end{pmatrix}\] where the double-bar matrix element is the reduced matrix element, depending only on representation labels; the parentheses denote Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.

[Hierarchy Declaration] All “theorems” in this chapter are downgraded to “constructions” or “framework-level correspondences.” The construction of the complex line bundle \(L\) relies on the complexification of the percolation function \(\Phi\) (\(\Phi_{\mathbb{C}} = \Phi + i\Phi^\perp\)), where the rigorous geometric construction of \(\Phi^\perp\) has not yet been completed by Theorem 1.1.5.01. §6.1 Complex Line Bundles and U(1) Principal Bundles

Construction 6.1 (Fiber Bundle of the Fermi Surface): The metallic Fermi surface can be formally regarded as a holographic section of the \(U(1)\) principal bundle of a complex line bundle \(L\) over the coding orbit \(\Sigma \cong S^2\), with total space \(P\) being the percolation function level set, base space \(B = \Sigma\), and fiber \(F = U(1)\).

Construction Idea: The complex-valued percolation function \(\Phi_{\mathbb{C}} = \Phi + i\Phi^\perp\) (where \(\Phi^\perp\) is determined by the adjoint solution of the Theorem 1.1.5.01 percolation function analytic framework) takes the level set \(\Phi_{\mathbb{C}} = \Phi_F\) on \(\Sigma\). The level set is locally homeomorphic to \(S^1\) and thus can be regarded as a bundle with fiber \(S^1\). Gauge transformations \(U(1)\) correspond to the phase degree of freedom of the complex line bundle: \(\Phi_{\mathbb{C}} \to e^{i\chi}\Phi_{\mathbb{C}}\). The connection 1-form \(A = \Phi_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}d\Phi_{\mathbb{C}}\) defines parallel transport.

§6.2 Chern Classes and Topological Invariants

Construction 6.2 (First Chern Class): If the construction of the complex line bundle \(L\) is completed, its first Chern class \(c_1(L) \in H^2(\Sigma; \mathbb{Z})\) is determined by the winding number of the complex percolation function level set: \[c_1(L) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_\Sigma F = \frac{1}{2\pi} \oint_{\Phi_{\mathbb{C}}=\Phi_F} dA = n \in \mathbb{Z}\] where \(F = dA\) is the curvature 2-form and \(n\) is the number of times the level set winds around the North pole of \(S^2\).

Key Statement: The specific integer value of \(c_1(L)\) is determined by the homotopy class of the clutching function \([\Sigma, U(1)] \cong \mathbb{Z}\) and is not necessarily 2. A previous version claimed “if \(L\) is compatible with the complexified tangent bundle then \(c_1=2\)” — that “if” was never verified. \(c_1(TS^2) = 2\) is the Chern class of the complexified tangent bundle, a fixed value; but \(c_1(L)\) is the Chern class of the constructed complex line bundle \(L\), not the tangent bundle Chern class, and the two are conceptually distinct with not necessarily equal values.

Topological Invariants: The number of connected components \(N\) of the Fermi surface, the genus \(g\) of each component, and the Chern number \(c_1\), if well-defined, are topological invariants preserved under perturbations.

§6.3 Homological Form of the Area Formula

Framework 6.3 (Homological Form of the Area Formula): The Fermi surface area is placed in formal correspondence with the generator of the homology group \(H_2(S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}\) of \(S^2\): \[A_{\mathrm{FS}} \sim \langle [\Phi_F], [S^2] \rangle \cdot 4\pi = n \cdot 4\pi\] where \([\Phi_F] \in H^2(S^2)\) is the Poincaré dual of the region enclosed by the percolation function level set, \([S^2] \in H_2(S^2)\) is the fundamental class, and \(\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\) is the Kronecker pairing.

§6.4 Homotopy Analysis of Connectivity

Framework 6.4 (Homotopy Analysis of Connectivity): The number of connected components of the Fermi surface is determined approximately by the homotopy class corresponding to the pole structure of the source term \(Q\).

The maxima of the source term \(Q\) approximately correspond to the maxima of the percolation function \(\Phi\) (a rigorous correspondence requires consideration of the smoothing effect of the Green function convolution). Each maximum region forms an occupied “island” below the level set \(\Phi = \Phi_F\). The boundary of an island is a simple closed curve, homotopy-equivalent to \(S^1\) on \(S^2\). By the van Kampen theorem, the fundamental group of the union of \(N\) disjoint islands is the free product of \(N\) copies of \(S^1\): \(\pi_1(\mathrm{FS}) = \underbrace{\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} * \cdots * \mathbb{Z}}_{N\text{ copies}}\).

§7.1 APS Index Theorem Form for Surface States

§7.2 Unified Surface Bridge Theory
