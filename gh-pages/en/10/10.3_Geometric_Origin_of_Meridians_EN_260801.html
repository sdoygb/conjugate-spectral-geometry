\[ \rho_{\mathcal{C}}(r, \phi, z) = \sum_{n,m} A_{nm} \cdot J_n(k_{nm} \cdot r) \cdot \cos(n \cdot \phi + \phi_0) \cdot \cos(k_z \cdot z + \delta_z) \tag{10.3.2.02} \]

Proof. In cylindrical coordinates, \(\nabla^2 = \partial^2/\partial r^2 + (1/r)\partial/\partial r + (1/r^2)\partial^2/\partial\phi^2 + \partial^2/\partial z^2\). Separating variables \(\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(r, \phi, z) = R(r) \cdot \Phi(\phi) \cdot Z(z)\) and substituting into (10.3.2.01):

  • Radial equation: \(r^2 R'' + r R' + (\kappa^2\theta_\tau r^2 - k_z^2 r^2 - n^2)R = 0 \to R(r) = J_n(k_{nm} r)\), \(k_{nm}^2 = \kappa^2\theta_\tau - k_z^2\)

The radial boundary condition \(J_n(k_{nm} \cdot R) = 0\) determines the discrete wavenumbers \(k_{nm}\). Linear superposition of all modes yields (10.3.2.02). ∎

Note 2.1 (Physical meaning of the Bessel function order \(n\)). \(n = 0\) corresponds to axisymmetric modes (along the meridian trunk), \(n \geq 1\) corresponds to helical modes (the helical course of meridians). Different \(n\) values correspond to different types of meridian trajectories: \(n = 0\) for straight meridians, \(n = 1\) for single helices, \(n = 2\) for double helices, etc.

§3.2 Acupoint Phase Classification Theorem

Proof. Definition 10.3.4.01 (Meridian Curve). \[ \frac{d\ell}{ds} = \frac{\nabla \phi_{\mathcal{C}}}{|\nabla \phi_{\mathcal{C}}|} \tag{10.3.4.02} \]

§4.2 Critical Point Structure and Poincaré-Hopf Constraint

Definition 10.3.4.02 (Phase Field Critical Point). A critical point \(p\) of \(\phi_{\mathcal{C}}\) satisfies \(\nabla\phi_{\mathcal{C}}(p) = 0\). Classified by the signature of the Hessian Matrix \(H_\phi = \nabla^2\phi_{\mathcal{C}}(p)\):

  • Source point: \(H_\phi\) positive definite (\(\nabla^2\phi_{\mathcal{C}} > 0\)), index = +1, corresponds to meridian origin (well point)
  • Sink point: \(H_\phi\) negative definite (\(\nabla^2\phi_{\mathcal{C}} < 0\)), index = +1, corresponds to meridian terminus
  • Saddle point: \(H_\phi\) indefinite (signature indefinite), index = -1, corresponds to meridian branching point (connecting point)

Theorem 10.3.4.02 (Poincaré-Hopf Critical Point Constraint Theorem). The total number of critical points of the phase field \(\phi_{\mathcal{C}}\) on the body surface \(\nabla\Sigma\) satisfies the Poincaré-Hopf Index Theorem:

\[ \sum_{i} (-1)^{\text{index}(p_i)} = \chi(\nabla\Sigma) = 2 - 2g \tag{10.3.4.03} \]

where \(\chi\) is the Euler characteristic, and \(g\) is the genus. The human body surface is homeomorphic to the sphere \(S^2\) (\(g = 0\)), \(\chi = 2\).

Proof. \(\phi_{\mathcal{C}}\) is a smooth function on the compact smooth manifold \(\nabla\Sigma\), and its gradient field \(\nabla\phi_{\mathcal{C}}\) is a smooth vector field on \(\nabla\Sigma\). The Poincaré-Hopf Theorem (Hopf, 1927) asserts that for a smooth vector field on a compact smooth manifold, the sum of the indices of its isolated zeros equals the Euler characteristic of the manifold. The isolated zeros of \(\nabla\phi_{\mathcal{C}}\) are the critical points of \(\phi_{\mathcal{C}}\), with indices as in Definition 10.3.4.02. The genus of the human body surface \(\nabla\Sigma\) is \(g = 0\) (no holes), \(\chi = 2 - 2\times0 = 2\). ∎

Corollary 10.3.4.02a (Number difference between sources and sinks). The total number of sources (index +1) and sinks (index +1) minus the number of saddles (index -1) equals 2. Let \(N_{\text{source}}\) be the number of sources (well points), \(N_{\text{sink}}\) the number of sinks (termini), \(N_{\text{saddle}}\) the number of saddles (connecting points); then:

\[ N_{\text{source}} + N_{\text{sink}} - N_{\text{saddle}} = 2 \tag{10.3.4.04} \]

In the meridian system, each of the twelve regular meridians has 1 well point (source) and 1 terminus (sink), but some meridians share termini, so the actual number of termini is less than 12. Meanwhile, the fifteen collaterals provide 15 saddle points. Substituting: \(12 + N_{\text{sink}} - 15 = 2 \to N_{\text{sink}} = 5\). This is consistent with the actual situation of meridian termini in traditional Chinese medicine theory (some meridians terminate at the head, face, or trunk, with shared termini).
