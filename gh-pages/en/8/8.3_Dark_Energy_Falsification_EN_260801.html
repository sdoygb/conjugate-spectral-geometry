§1 Standard Explanation of Cosmic Accelerating Expansion

1.1 Standard Cosmological Framework

In the \(\Lambda\)CDM model, the accelerating expansion of the universe is driven by the cosmological constant \(\Lambda\):

\[\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho + \frac{3p}{c^2}\right) + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}.\]

A positive \(\Lambda\) term provides an effective negative pressure \(\rho_\Lambda = +\Lambda c^2 / (8\pi G)\), driving acceleration. Observations give \(\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.68\).

1.2 The Cosmological Constant Problem

The standard framework faces two fundamental difficulties: - Fine-tuning problem: \(\rho_\Lambda\) is \(10^{120}\) times smaller than the vacuum energy predicted by quantum field theory - Coincidence problem: \(\rho_\Lambda\) and the matter density are of the same order only at the current epoch

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, neither problem exists — because \(\Lambda = 0\), and accelerating expansion has a completely different geometric origin.

1.3 Additional Difficulties of the Dark Energy Hypothesis

In addition to the cosmological constant problem, the dark energy hypothesis also faces:

  • Unknown nature: Is dark energy a cosmological constant, quintessence, phantom, or k-essence? The standard framework cannot distinguish
  • Equation of state controversy: Observations allow \(w = -1\) (cosmological constant) to \(w < -1\) (phantom), and theory cannot uniquely determine
  • Incompatibility with quantum field theory: The gravitational effect of vacuum energy cannot be self-consistently treated in the standard framework
  • Relation between inflation and dark energy: Both are “accelerating expansion” but with different physical mechanisms; the standard framework cannot unify them

Conjugate Spectral Geometry provides a unified explanation of accelerating expansion from the ℐ-sector diffusion equation — none of the above difficulties exist.
