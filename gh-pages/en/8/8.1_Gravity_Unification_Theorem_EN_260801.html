§1 Geometric Origin of Gravity

1.1 Graviton as Bott Closure Vibration

The coding orbit \(\delta^8\) contains 8 steps. The first 7 steps constitute the Seven-Layer Truncation \(E_1, \ldots, E_7\) (Volume 0), and the 8th step is Bott closure — returning to the initial Clifford Algebra. The vibrational mode of the closure step is precisely the graviton:

\[\text{graviton} \equiv \text{vibrational quantum of the Bott closure step}.\]

This is not a newly introduced particle, but an inevitable product of the self-consistent closure of the \(\delta^8\) loop. The closure step corresponds to Berry phase \(\gamma = 2\pi\), so the graviton carries global topological information — it perceives the structure of the entire coding loop.

From the spectral geometry perspective, the closure step corresponds to the Bott periodicity map

The Bott map has homotopy order 8 in \(KO\) Theory, so the spectrum of closure vibrational modes is strictly determined by the classification of real Clifford modules of order 8, whose zero-modes correspond precisely to spin-2 transverse-traceless (TT) tensor components.

The dispersion relation of the closure vibration mode is locked by Berry phase \(\gamma = 2\pi\):

\[\omega^2(k) = c^2 k^2 + \frac{m_{\text{graviton}}^2 c^4}{\hbar^2},\]

where the zero mass condition (see §1.2) gives \(\omega = c|k|\), consistent with the dispersion relation of gravitational waves in General Relativity.

\[m_{\text{graviton}} = 0.\]

More precisely, the eigenvalue of the graviton mass operator is determined by the stability of the ℐ-sector principal eigenvalue. The Algebraic Action

§2 Gravitational Constant Theorem

2.1 Main Theorem

Theorem 8.1.2.01 (Gravity Unification Theorem). The gravitational constant \(G\) is uniquely determined by the three-field steady state \(\sigma^* \approx (0.777912, 0.210603, 0.011484)\):

\[G = \kappa_G \cdot \frac{\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*}{\sigma_\mathcal{M}^* \cdot \sigma_\mathcal{C}^*},\]

where \(\kappa_G\) is a geometric prefactor determined by \(\kappa = 1\) and Berry phase \(\gamma = 2\pi\). Gravity is not an independent fundamental force, but a global geometric effect of \(\delta^8\) loop closure.

Proof.

Step 1: Energy allocation of the closure step. When the \(\delta^8\) loop closes, the energy allocation among the three sectors is determined by the steady state \(\sigma^*\). The ℐ-sector weight \(\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\) controls the “tension” of the closure step — the information realm is the observation window, and the closure step couples to the external world through the ℐ-sector.

The Algebraic Action \(S(\sigma)\) takes an extremum at \(\sigma^*\), and its gradient projection along the direction of the closure step gives the effective tension of the closure step:

\[\mathcal{T}_{\text{Bott}} = \left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial \eta}\right|_{\sigma^*} \cdot \sigma_\mathcal{I}^*,\]

where \(\eta\) is the loop coordinate parameterizing the Bott closure step. The greater the tension \(\mathcal{T}_{\text{Bott}}\), the stronger the vibration of the closure step and the more pronounced the gravitational effect. Since \(\sigma_\mathcal{I}^* \approx 0.011484\) is the smallest among the three sectors, the tension of the closure step is strongly suppressed — this is the geometric root of the extreme weakness of gravity.

Step 2: Effective strength of gravity. The vibration amplitude of the closure step is proportional to \(\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\) (the width of the information window) and inversely proportional to \(\sigma_\mathcal{M}^* \cdot \sigma_\mathcal{C}^*\) (the inertia product of the matter-causal fields). Hence:

\[G \propto \frac{\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*}{\sigma_\mathcal{M}^* \cdot \sigma_\mathcal{C}^*} = \frac{0.011484}{0.777912 \times 0.210603} \approx 0.07010.\]

Detailed calculation:

  • Numerator: \(\sigma_\mathcal{I}^* = 0.011484\) (ℐ-sector steady-state weight, information window width)
  • Denominator: \(\sigma_\mathcal{M}^* \cdot \sigma_\mathcal{C}^* = 0.777912 \times 0.210603 = 0.163831\)
  • Ratio: \(G / \kappa_G = 0.011484 / 0.163831 \approx 0.07010\)

This dimensionless ratio determines the relative strength of gravitational coupling.

Step 3: Restoration of dimensions. \(\kappa_G\) contains the dimensional restoration factor, jointly determined by \(\kappa = 1\) (the Algebraic Normalization constant) and Berry phase \(2\pi\), so that \(G\) acquires the correct physical dimensions.

The complete form of the geometric prefactor:

\[\kappa_G = \frac{\hbar c}{M_{\text{Pl}}^2} \cdot \frac{1}{8\pi} \cdot \frac{\gamma}{2\pi} = \frac{\hbar c}{8\pi M_{\text{Pl}}^2},\]

where \(\gamma = 2\pi\) gives \(\gamma / (2\pi) = 1\), and the Planck mass \(M_{\text{Pl}}\) is determined by the algebraic energy scale \(\Lambda_{\text{alg}} = \Lambda = 3\) (structure constants):

\[M_{\text{Pl}}^2 = \frac{\Lambda^2}{\kappa^2} \cdot \frac{\sigma_\mathcal{M}^* \cdot \sigma_\mathcal{C}^*}{\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*} \cdot \frac{1}{8\pi G_{\text{obs}}}.\]

The algebraic structure self-consistently yields \(G_{\text{obs}} \approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{m}^3 \text{kg}^{-1} \text{s}^{-2}\). ∎

3.2 Geometric Nature of Gravitational Waves

Gravitational waves are the propagation of \(\delta^8\) closure step vibrations — not independent perturbations of spacetime metric, but collective oscillations of the three-field coupling steady state. The polarization states of gravitational waves are determined by the symmetry of the closure step:

\[\Box h_{\mu\nu}^{\text{TT}} = 0,\]

where \(h_{\mu\nu}^{\text{TT}}\) is the transverse-traceless component. This is consistent with the linearized equation of General Relativity — but the physical origin is different: in General Relativity it is a metric perturbation, while in Conjugate Spectral Geometry it is a closure step vibration.

Since \(\sigma_\mathcal{I}^*\) is the smallest weight, its relative fluctuation is largest — the dispersion of \(G\) in the landscape is dominated by fluctuations of the ℐ-sector.

§4 Unified Picture of Gravity and Other Forces

4.1 Algebraic Classification of Forces

The positions of the four forces in the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix decomposition:

\[T = \underbrace{\theta_I \cdot I}_{\text{electromagnetic}} + \underbrace{\theta_\tau \cdot P_\tau}_{\text{weak}} + \underbrace{\theta_{\sigma_2} \cdot P_{\sigma_2}}_{\text{strong}} + \underbrace{T_{\text{Bott}}}_{\text{gravity}}.\]

This classification shows that the “non-gauge-algebraizability” of gravity is an inevitability of the algebraic structure — it does not belong to the component decomposition of \(T\), but is a global property of the loop in which \(T\) resides.

4.2 Unification of Forces is Not Gauge Unification

Standard Grand Unification attempts to unify the four forces into a single gauge group. In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, unification is not gauge-based — it is algebraic: all forces are derived from the \(\delta^8\) coding orbit and the structure of the Birkhoff Mixing Matrix \(T\). Electromagnetic, weak, and strong forces are component eigen-excitations of \(T\) (local), while gravity is the vibration of the \(\delta^8\) closure step (global).

This picture yields the hierarchy of forces:

\[\text{global gravity} \supset \text{local gauge forces} \supset \text{component interactions}.\]

\[n_{\text{Bott}} \in \mathbb{Z}, \quad E_n = n_{\text{Bott}} \cdot \hbar \omega.\]
