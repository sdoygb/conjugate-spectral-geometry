§1 Standard Difficulties of Galaxy Rotation Curves

1.1 Predictions of Newtonian Gravity

In the standard gravitational framework, the Newtonian prediction for galaxy rotation velocity is:

\[v_{\text{Newton}}(r) = \sqrt{\frac{GM(r)}{r}},\]

where \(M(r)\) is the visible mass within radius \(r\). Outside the visible matter distribution (\(r > r_{\text{visible}}\)), \(M(r) \approx \text{const}\), hence \(v \propto r^{-1/2}\) — the velocity should decline.

1.2 Observations and Contradictions

Observations show that galaxy rotation curves tend to flatten at large radii: \(v(r) \approx \text{const}\). Standard cosmology explains this by introducing a dark matter halo — additional invisible mass making \(M(r) \propto r\), thus \(v \approx \text{const}\).

In Conjugate Spectral Geometry, the complete resolution of this contradiction requires two levels: the 𝒞-sector correction produces a \(21\%\) effective gravitational constant rescaling (\(G_{\text{eff}} \approx 1.21G\)), while rotation curve flattening itself is achieved by the permeation function mechanism in 8.18 — no dark matter particles are needed.

1.3 Difficulties of the Dark Matter Hypothesis

The dark matter hypothesis faces multiple difficulties:

  • No particles detected: Direct detection experiments for dark matter candidates such as WIMPs and Axions consistently yield null results
  • Small-scale crisis of halo models: The central density cusps predicted by dark matter simulations for dwarf galaxies are inconsistent with observed core-flat distributions
  • Satellite galaxy number problem: Dark matter simulations predict too many satellite galaxies
  • Diversity problem: Detailed differences in rotation curves of different galaxies are difficult to explain with a unified dark matter halo profile

The root of these difficulties lies in attributing “missing gravity” to “missing matter”. Conjugate Spectral Geometry approaches the problem from the opposite direction — gravity itself needs correction: the 𝒞-sector correction provides a \(21\%\) gravitational constant rescaling, and the 8.18 permeation function mechanism provides rotation curve flattening. The two jointly replace the dark matter hypothesis, rather than either alone completing the task.
